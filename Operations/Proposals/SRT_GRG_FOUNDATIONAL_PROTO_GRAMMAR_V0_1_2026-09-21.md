# SRT Generative Relational Grammar — Foundational Proto-Grammar v0.1

> Role: first coherent, reviewable GRG architecture after the recent SRT reconstruction.
>
> Status: noncanonical first version, completed for whole-architecture author review.
>
> This file is intentionally stronger than a loose proposal and weaker than a canonical or mature formal grammar. It consolidates the current programme into one surface so the author can review the architecture as a whole rather than through scattered intermediate records.

## 0. Compact definition

Working definition:

~~~text
GRG
= a revisable typed proto-grammar
  for studying recurring generative relations by which
  non-flat differentiation forms organization,
  formed organization remains effective or recurrent,
  local generative loci and operative positions arise,
  later generative conditions are reconstructed,
  relations compose across loci and scales,
  structural expectations and consequences become available,
  and inquiry revises the grammar under mismatch.
~~~

The three-layer distinction is mandatory:

~~~text
SRT ontology
!= GRG proto-grammar
!= domain-native mechanism
~~~

SRT supplies the generative ontological orientation and current canonical constraints.

GRG supplies a typed research architecture for extracting, comparing, composing and revising recurring generative relations.

Domain sciences and practices retain ownership of their mechanisms, measurements, causal claims, evidence and historical particulars.

## 1. Why GRG follows from the reconstructed SRT direction

The programme begins from the SRT inversion:

~~~text
not only:
given objects + laws -> explain change

but also:
generative differentiation
-> formation of objects / boundaries / scales / positions
-> stabilized effective laws
-> consequence / history / reconstruction
-> renewed generative conditions
~~~

This creates a research question upstream of ordinary object-level modelling:

> What recurring generative relations make stable objects, state spaces, positions and later transformations possible, and how do those relations themselves change?

GRG is the attempt to make that question researchable across domains without replacing domain mechanisms with one universal equation or vocabulary.

## 2. Horizontal and vertical research

### 2.1 Horizontal research

Horizontal research begins from a sufficiently stabilized objectification.

Typical givens include:

- objects;
- variables;
- categories;
- agents;
- organisms;
- particles;
- institutions;
- boundaries;
- scales;
- state spaces;
- model classes.

It studies transformations and relations among those admitted units.

Canonical working form:

~~~text
stabilized objectification O*
+ state / relation at t
-> mechanism / law / transformation
-> state / relation at t+1
~~~

Horizontal research can be dynamic, causal, mathematical, generative and highly predictive.

Its defining feature is not stasis.

Its defining feature is that the relevant object space is treated as sufficiently given for the research question.

### 2.2 Vertical research

Vertical research reopens the generative basis of those givens.

It asks:

~~~text
how did this object / boundary / scale / state space form?

what organization keeps it available?

what prior differentiation remains effective?

what recurrent structure conditions later generation?

when does a local generative locus arise?

when does a locus become an operative from-where?

how does current organization reconstruct later possibility / accessibility /
transition / viability / differentiation conditions?
~~~

Vertical research seeks recurring generative relations across multiple objectifications.

### 2.3 Question-relative status

Horizontal / vertical status is not permanently attached to an entity.

The same phenomenon may be:

- a horizontal variable for one question;
- part of a vertical formation or reconstruction question for another.

Therefore every GRG record must state:

~~~text
research question
stabilized objectification
what is treated as given
what is reopened
~~~

### 2.4 The main loop

The central programme image is:

~~~text
V -> H -> V'
~~~

Expanded:

~~~text
vertical generative organization
-> formation / stabilization of objectifiable units and capacities
-> horizontal laws / interactions / predictions
-> intervention / consequence / history
-> reconstruction of generative conditions
-> renewed vertical inquiry
~~~

GRG does not replace horizontal science.

It studies the continuity by which horizontal science becomes possible and by which horizontal consequences can alter future generative conditions.

## 4. Upstream constraints

### 4.1 C0 — non-exhaustion / non-preclosure

C0 protects the SRT commitment that current determinate organization should not silently be treated as an exhaustive inventory of future generative possibility.

Working burden:

~~~text
current determinate organization
does not by itself prove
a completed object inventory
or a pre-given final option menu.
~~~

C0 is not an ordinary relation-library entry.

It is a research guard.

Non-identities:

~~~text
C0 != randomness
C0 != hidden possibility warehouse
C0 != infinite option count
C0 != empirical proof that every domain is open-ended in the same way
~~~

### 4.2 C1 — differential manifestation / relative backgrounding

C1 carries the bridge from Selection to generative relationality.

Working burden:

~~~text
actual generative differentiation
-> operative non-equivalence
+ relative manifestation / backgrounding / support / exclusion
~~~

C1 is not yet a domain-specific relation family.

It tells GRG that a meaningful relation must pay more than trivial logical difference or generic change.

Domain records may later distinguish:

- inhibition;
- exclusion;
- support;
- resource competition;
- constitutive boundary;
- accessibility;
- activation;

where source-native mechanisms justify them.

## 5. Cross-cutting burdens

These fields are mandatory or frequently required, but v0.1 does not treat them as independent primitive relation families.

### 5.1 Formation

Every vertical relation record must answer:

> How did the relevant organization / relation form?

For v0.1:

~~~text
formation = mandatory cross-cutting burden
R-FORM = OPEN as independent family
~~~

A later R-FORM family requires a recurring generative structure more specific than the statement that organization forms.

### 5.2 Maintenance / regeneration

Every relation for which persistence matters must state:

- what maintains it;
- whether it persists continuously;
- whether it is regenerated;
- whether recurrence is role-level or token-level;
- what happens under withdrawal.

For v0.1:

~~~text
maintenance = cross-cutting burden
not an independent primitive family by default
~~~

### 5.3 Objectification

Every empirical GRG claim must state which objects and variables are stabilized by the research interface.

Relevant choices include:

- unit;
- boundary;
- grain;
- timescale;
- normalization;
- aggregation;
- proxy;
- representation;
- archive inclusion;
- periodization.

Hard guard:

~~~text
measured / decoded / represented trace
!= vertical generative organization automatically
~~~

### 5.4 Evidence

Evidence is heterogeneous.

Current families include E1-E10, from controlled intervention to conceptual / narrative material.

Evidence family is not relation type.

Evidence strength is not compressed into one universal score.

### 5.5 Failure

Every strong relation candidate must state:

- what would count as near-miss;
- what would narrow it;
- what would split it;
- what would retype it;
- what would retire the mapping.

## 6. R-HIST — history and re-entry

R-HIST studies how prior generative organization remains effective in later generation.

### 6.1 R-HIST-a — retained efficacy

Working form:

~~~text
prior differentiation / organization
-> remains materially effective
-> changes later generative conditions
~~~

Minimum burden:

- prior state or organization is not merely readable;
- it materially changes later generation;
- the claimed bridge survives an appropriate control / comparison.

Non-identities:

~~~text
trace != retained efficacy
decodability != causal organization
irreversibility != retained efficacy
history classification != re-entry
~~~

### 6.2 R-HIST-b — recurrent reconstitution

Stronger form:

~~~text
organization_t
-> conditions later generation
-> renewed organization_t+1
-> renewed organization can again carry the relevant conditioning role
~~~

R-HIST-b adds recurrence / reconstitution.

It does not require strict token identity unless a source-native claim specifically does.

Non-identities:

~~~text
R-HIST-a != R-HIST-b
one-shot conditioning != recurrent reconstitution
role recurrence != token identity
~~~

### 6.3 Current empirical lesson

The RNN work is retained mainly as a calibration guard.

A history-readable L2-facing / objectified trace failed the tested causal mediation burden.

Therefore:

~~~text
tested trace NULL
!= vertical efficacy NULL
~~~

but also:

~~~text
tested proxy failure
!= evidence that an unspecified deeper vertical cause exists
~~~

Any future empirical vertical claim must pay its own observation / perturbation / realization bridge.

## 7. R-LOC — local generative locus / individuation

R-LOC asks when a generative organization becomes relatively separable enough to function as a local unit of continued generation or reconstitution.

Working burden:

~~~text
distributed / relational generation
-> relatively separable local organization
-> local organization carries a continuing generative role
~~~

R-LOC is deliberately domain-neutral.

Possible source-native realizations may include:

- organismic organization;
- recurrent dynamical organization;
- institution;
- technical subsystem;
- local coordination regime;
- other individuated units.

No realization automatically becomes SRT One.

Hard guards:

~~~text
R-LOC != SRT One automatically
attractor != R-LOC automatically
analyst grouping != R-LOC
temporary pattern != continuing locus automatically
~~~

SRT One remains a stronger canonical specialization with its own admission burdens.

## 8. R-POS — operative positionality

For v0.1, R-POS is downstream of R-LOC.

Working route:

~~~text
R-LOC
-> formed local generative locus
-> where additional burden is paid,
R-POS
~~~

R-POS asks:

> Does the formed locus materially change what becomes manifest, reachable, backgrounded, selectable or actionable from that locus?

Working form:

~~~text
formed locus L
-> L-indexed organization of accessibility / manifestation / selection
~~~

Non-identities:

~~~text
coordinate != R-POS
observer label != R-POS
finite positionality != R-POS
R-LOC != R-POS automatically
R-POS != SRT Selection-position automatically
~~~

The dependency is provisional for v0.1 and remains open to author review.

## 9. R-RECON — reconstruction of generative conditions

R-RECON is the current core family most directly tied to the vertical question:

> How does present organization change the conditions under which later generation can proceed?

### 9.1 R-RECON-a — generative-field reconstruction

Working form:

~~~text
current activity / organization
-> materially changes a typed generative condition / accessibility field /
   transition structure / viability landscape / institutional condition
~~~

The change must be real at the declared grain.

It cannot be merely a speculative future consequence.

Examples of target variables may include:

- reachable action relations;
- environmental selective conditions;
- transition constraints;
- institutional permissions;
- technical compatibility conditions;
- resource / viability conditions.

Non-identities:

~~~text
state update != R-RECON-a automatically
long-term effect != R-RECON-a automatically
environment modification != generative-field reconstruction automatically
~~~

### 9.2 R-RECON-b — realized downstream reconstruction effect

Stronger form:

~~~text
R-RECON-a field / condition change
-> later process encounters changed generative conditions
-> realized later accessibility / transition / viability /
   differentiation differs through the claimed bridge
~~~

This distinguishes:

~~~text
the field was actually reconstructed
from
the later trajectory actually realized a consequence of that reconstruction
~~~

### 9.3 Recursive subtype

A recurrent reconstruction pattern may take the form:

~~~text
process P
-> reconstructs field F
-> F changes later P
-> later P further reconstructs F
~~~

This is currently treated as a composite strengthening of R-RECON, not an additional primitive family.

## 10. Composition

A grammar requires more than isolated relation types.

v0.1 includes two explicit composition families.

### 10.1 C-MULTI — multi-locus relation-level organization

Working form:

~~~text
locus / process A
+ locus / process B
+ interaction structure
-> relation-level organization R
-> R changes later conditions for A / B / others
~~~

A C-MULTI claim must state:

- component relations;
- interaction structure;
- what new dependency appears at the relation level;
- why the result is not merely a list of individual effects.

Guard:

~~~text
interaction != C-MULTI automatically
coordination != collective One / Bearer / subject
~~~

Use "multi-position" only when operative positions are independently paid.

Otherwise use multi-locus / multi-component.

### 10.2 C-XSC — cross-scale composition / recursion

Working form:

~~~text
lower / local relations
-> organization at another scale
-> that organization conditions later generation across scales
~~~

A C-XSC claim must identify:

- scale construction;
- component relations;
- higher / coarser organization;
- return / conditioning path;
- failure condition.

Guards:

~~~text
cross-scale correlation != C-XSC
coarse description != higher-order causal organization
higher-order conditioning != mystical downward force
micro-mechanism != complete explanation automatically
~~~

## 11. Working composition verbs

v0.1 is a proto-grammar, not a closed formal grammar.

The following are working composition verbs for relation analysis, not formal operators with complete algebraic semantics.

### FORM

~~~text
FORM(X -> O)
~~~

records formation of organization O from source-native generative dependencies X.

### RETAIN

~~~text
RETAIN(O_t -> later conditions)
~~~

corresponds to R-HIST-a where later efficacy is paid.

### RECONSTITUTE

~~~text
RECONSTITUTE(O_t -> generation -> O_t+1)
~~~

corresponds to R-HIST-b.

### LOCATE

~~~text
LOCATE(O -> L)
~~~

asks whether organization O forms a local generative locus L.

### POSITION

~~~text
POSITION(L -> accessibility_L)
~~~

asks whether L becomes an operative from-where.

### RECONSTRUCT

~~~text
RECONSTRUCT(P -> F')
~~~

asks whether process P materially reconstructs a typed future generative condition F.

### REALIZE

~~~text
REALIZE(F' -> later generative difference)
~~~

distinguishes R-RECON-b from field change alone.

### COMPOSE

~~~text
COMPOSE(R1, R2, ... -> R*)
~~~

builds a multi-locus or relation-level organization where a new burden is paid.

### SCALE

~~~text
SCALE(R_local -> O_scale -> later R_local)
~~~

records cross-scale formation and return.

### EXPECT

~~~text
EXPECT(R -> E)
~~~

derives a structural generative expectation from a typed relation.

### INDEX

~~~text
INDEX(consequence | position, scale, horizon, relation)
~~~

routes consequence and normativity without collapsing perspectives.

### REVISE

~~~text
REVISE(grammar, mismatch)
-> retain / narrow / split / merge / retype / retire
~~~

This is the reflexive methodological operation.

## 12. Consequence typing

Consequences must not be collapsed into one generic return channel.

### CONSEQ-H — generic history / conditioning consequence

~~~text
generated consequence
-> later generative condition
~~~

This can route into R-HIST.

### CONSEQ-P — locus / position indexed consequence

~~~text
generated consequence
-> returns differentially relative to a formed locus / position
~~~

This is stronger than generic historical conditioning.

### CONSEQ-B — same-One prospective exposure

This is SRT Bearer-facing and requires stronger canonical burdens.

~~~text
CONSEQ-B
!= automatic GRG consequence type
~~~

It is included only as an interface route where the same-One prospective exposure burden is independently paid.

## 13. Generative expectation

A strong GRG relation is not only retrospective description.

It should constrain relation-consistent continuation or transformation.

### 13.1 E-B — structural generative expectation

Working form:

~~~text
typed relation R
-> directional burden on what continuation / transformation
   would preserve, strengthen, weaken, substitute or destroy the relation
~~~

E-B need not be:

- conscious;
- explicit;
- model-mediated;
- statistically probabilistic.

It is a structural expectation derived from the relation.

### 13.2 Horizontal prediction vs vertical expectation

Horizontal generative prediction:

~~~text
given stabilized object space
-> expected next state / trajectory
~~~

Vertical generative expectation:

~~~text
given typed generative relation
-> expected formation / retention / reconstruction
   of generative conditions or accessible relation structure
~~~

They may coexist.

Neither is automatically superior.

### 13.3 E-C0 — embodied / enacted anticipation

Where a formed locus actually regulates / acts in relation to expected future conditions without explicit model mediation:

~~~text
E-B
-> embodied / enacted anticipatory realization
~~~

### 13.4 E-C1 — model-mediated anticipation

Where future conditions are explicitly represented, modelled or simulated by a formed locus:

~~~text
E-B
-> model-mediated anticipatory realization
~~~

Guards:

~~~text
E-C0 / E-C1 != consciousness
prediction != normativity
explicit model != better ontology automatically
~~~

## 14. Normativity interface

The current author direction remains:

~~~text
generative relation
-> generative expectation
-> actual trajectory
-> indexed comparison
-> normative direction becomes possible
~~~

v0.1 names this:

~~~text
N-INDEX
~~~

Required indexing includes:

- position;
- relation;
- scale;
- horizon;
- affected bearer(s), where independently established;
- externalized consequence;
- reversibility / future option effects where relevant.

Possible descriptive outcomes include:

~~~text
G-stronger
G-weaker
preservation
substitution
hollowing
destruction
~~~

Hard guards:

~~~text
stable != good
persistent != good
more options != good automatically
more order != good automatically
larger whole != morally prior
expected != morally legitimate
~~~

N-INDEX is not a complete moral theory.

## 15. Reflexive grammar learning

GRG must represent both world-side reflexive learning and its own methodological revision.

### 15.1 L-REFLEX-W — world / civilizational reflexive learning

Working form:

~~~text
formed positions
-> experience
-> externalized memory / theory / rule / institution
-> intervention
-> consequence
-> revision of future organization
~~~

Possible realizations include:

- science;
- engineering;
- institutional learning;
- legal revision;
- cultural learning;
- technological adaptation;
- AI-assisted inquiry.

This does not imply civilization is one subject.

### 15.2 L-REFLEX-M — methodological GRG revision

Working loop:

~~~text
source-native record
-> objectification declaration
-> candidate relation
-> comparison / test
-> expectation
-> mismatch / result
-> grammar revision
~~~

Allowed revision operations:

~~~text
ADMIT
RETAIN
NARROW
SPLIT
MERGE
RETYPE
RETIRE
~~~

The two reflexive layers may interact.

They are not identical.

## 16. Source-native / objectification discipline

Every GRG mapping begins with the source-native account.

Mandatory sequence:

~~~text
source-native phenomenon / mechanism
-> objectification declaration
-> candidate generative burden
-> GRG typing
-> expectation / failure
-> cross-domain comparison
~~~

Forbidden shortcut:

~~~text
GRG label
-> search world for examples that sound similar
~~~

A mature relation record must state:

- what the domain itself claims;
- what is measured;
- how the measurement object is constructed;
- what GRG abstracts;
- what GRG does not inherit from the source;
- what source result would defeat the mapping.

## 17. Horizontal-to-vertical bridge requirement

Question-relative verticality creates a specific risk: any failed horizontal model could be relabelled "deeper vertical".

v0.1 blocks that escape.

Every vertical empirical claim must state:

~~~text
horizontal observable / intervention
-> what objectified structure it reads or changes
-> what vertical relation is inferred
-> bridge assumption
-> alternative horizontal explanation
-> discriminating evidence
~~~

Therefore:

~~~text
horizontal evidence can constrain vertical hypotheses
but
horizontal proxy != vertical organization automatically
~~~

and:

~~~text
proxy failure
!= proof of an unspecified deeper vertical cause
~~~

## 18. Relation admission

A candidate relation becomes a serious proto-grammar entry only if it states:

1. source-native phenomenon;
2. architecture role;
3. formation burden;
4. maintenance / recurrence burden where relevant;
5. dependency direction;
6. verticality declaration;
7. objectification / proxy interface;
8. evidence type;
9. admission conditions;
10. non-identities;
11. failure / withdrawal conditions;
12. composition dependencies;
13. structural expectation;
14. revision state.

Cross-domain recurrence is useful only when semantic invariance survives.

~~~text
same word != same relation
same diagram != same relation
same mechanism != required for same GRG burden
same GRG burden != identical domain mechanism
~~~

## 22. Three worked structural patterns

These are not universal laws.

They illustrate how v0.1 types existing work.

### Pattern A — history / re-entry

~~~text
formation
-> organization O
-> retained efficacy
-> later generation changes
~~~

Type:

~~~text
R-HIST-a
~~~

If O is recurrently regenerated:

~~~text
R-HIST-b
~~~

### Pattern B — local locus -> operative position

~~~text
distributed generation
-> relatively separable locus L
-> L acquires operative from-where
-> accessibility / manifestation differs relative to L
~~~

Type:

~~~text
R-LOC
-> R-POS
~~~

This is not automatic.

### Pattern C — reconstruction

~~~text
process P
-> changes typed future condition F
-> F is now materially different
-> later process encounters F
-> later trajectory differs through F
~~~

Type:

~~~text
R-RECON-a
-> R-RECON-b
~~~

If the later trajectory further reconstructs F:

~~~text
recursive R-RECON composite
~~~

## 23. What v0.1 calls "grammar"

v0.1 does not claim a mature formal grammar.

The term proto-grammar means the architecture already contains:

- typed relation families;
- typed composition patterns;
- dependency ordering;
- source-native admission rules;
- non-identities;
- expectation derivation;
- failure conditions;
- revision operations.

It does not yet contain:

- a complete closed syntax;
- formal completeness;
- proof of universality;
- one mathematical representation valid for all domains;
- mature derivation semantics for all composition types.

Therefore:

~~~text
GRG v0.1
= revisable typed proto-grammar
~~~

is the current accurate status.

## 24. What success means at v0.1

v0.1 is successful as an architecture if it can do all of the following:

### Distinguish

Reject false equivalences:

~~~text
trace != organization
formation != recurrence
locus != position
field reconstruction != realized effect
representation != operative regime
stability != legitimacy
~~~

### Preserve

Keep source-native mechanisms intact.

### Connect

Show how vertical and horizontal inquiry can be related without reducing one to the other.

### Generate

Derive relation-specific structural expectations.

### Revise

Allow evidence to narrow, split, retype or retire the grammar.

### Scale

Represent individual, multi-locus, cross-scale and civilizational patterns without declaring them one mechanism.
