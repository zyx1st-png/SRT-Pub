## Selected option — MOBJ2-B manifest-interface insufficiency

```text
Any third-person model whose primitive interface already consists of
fully determinate observations, state variables, action labels or boundaries
is necessarily downstream of the manifestation event that made those distinctions available.

Such a model may be predictively / dynamically complete,
but by itself it cannot exhaust the pre-object constitution question
because it begins after the distinction whose genesis is at issue.
```

Important boundary:

```text
this is NOT:
no third-person formalism can ever represent constitutive differentiation.
```

A more general formalism could in principle model the constitutive process. The selected claim is only that **starting from already determinate observables cannot, by itself, explain their determinacy without transferring the debt into the primitives**.

---

## Why this survives the strongest state-space objection

Predictive-state and computational-mechanics approaches show that state need not be a hidden physical object. It can be relationally reconstructed from observation histories and predictions.

That blocks a weak critique of state-space models.

But MOBJ2-B asks an earlier question:

```text
what makes one determinate observation / action / test / boundary
available as a primitive interface item in the first place?
```

If the answer is simply taken as primitive, predictive closure does not discharge the constitutive debt.
