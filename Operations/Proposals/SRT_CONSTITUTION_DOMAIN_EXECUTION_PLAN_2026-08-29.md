## Phase 2 — AI divergence / author convergence

For one constitutional question at a time:

### AI role

Generate multiple genuinely different structural readings, including:

- strongest version;
- weakest version;
- hidden presuppositions;
- counterexample shape;
- alternative ordering;
- likely conflations;
- consequences for reader-entry operation.

The AI must not choose the author’s view for the author.

### Author role

The author explicitly selects, rejects, hybridizes or rewrites.

Only the author’s converged language becomes a Constitution candidate.

### Recording

Use the existing choice-trace discipline for `decision` / `intuition_mining` where practical. AI may record options, but fields representing the author’s choice/reason/boundary must remain authored by the author.

Hard stop:

> no repository theory write during the divergence part of the conversation. Write only after a real author convergence event.

## Phase 3 — internal red-team

Each converged thesis is pressure-tested for:

1. circularity;
2. hidden pre-given object/unit/subject;
3. conflation of ontological situatedness with participatory methodology;
4. God-view exemption / reflexivity failure;
5. epistemic-privilege drift;
6. covert formalization (state space, scalar, threshold, natural coordinate);
7. inability to supply a reader-entry operation;
8. overclaiming from one domain to all domains;
9. conflict with another converged Constitution item.

Use older C-ONE/C-RET/C-OBJ/C-BEAR/C-PHEN and dependency-map gaps as **pressure prompts**, not mandatory commitments.

Exit condition:

- author accepts the thesis after seeing red-team alternatives;
- remaining uncertainty is written as boundary/tension, not silently closed.

## Phase 8 — domain deep well

Use the seven-step domain template in the blueprint and produce a single coherent study, not scattered patches.

Mandatory pre-model artifact: Bearer–Objectification Declaration.

Then allow domain-native methods:

- literature;
- formal models;
- experiments;
- statistics;
- simulations;
- causal comparison;
- first/second/third-person methods where appropriate.

The claim must remain domain-local unless independent cross-domain evidence exists.

### Domain increment gate

Before claiming “SRT adds X”, run the field’s strongest baseline/rival review.

Reuse bounded-probe / Case A-STOP logic:

- existing field already supplies the same distinction/gain -> no SRT increment claim;
- SRT reorganization adds a genuinely new researchable distinction -> proceed;
- unclear -> keep as translation/question generation, not superiority claim.

---

## Phase 9 — productive-adequacy review

After the first deep well ask:

```text
Did the Constitution change how the Given One was cut?
Did that change reveal a nontrivial problem?
Did it organize existing evidence better?
Did it generate a testable or operationally useful distinction?
Did the field already have the same thing?
Did the Bearer–Objectification Declaration actually help?
```

Possible dispositions:

- productive: keep and deepen;
- translation-only: useful language, no increment claim;
- scope-limited: works only for a subproblem;
- unproductive: revise or drop in that domain.

Only after this review may the programme select a second deep well.

---

## Phase 10 — cross-domain synthesis

Do not start before at least two deep wells have independently produced usable results.

Cross-domain synthesis asks what recurs without assuming recurrence in advance.

Possible outputs:

- repeated perspective operation;
- repeated objectification loss pattern;
- repeated bearer/history relation;
- domain-specific divergence that shows the Constitution was too broad.

No universal equation is required for cross-domain unity.
