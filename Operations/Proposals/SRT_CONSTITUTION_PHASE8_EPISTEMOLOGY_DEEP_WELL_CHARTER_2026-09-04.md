## 0. Exact study question

When historians, mathematicians or philosophers treat two historically separated constructions as instances of the **same mathematical method**, what makes that re-identification warranted rather than a retrospective convenience?

The bounded target is the identity of a mathematical **method family** across changes in:

```text
notation / formal presentation
proof goal / theorem output
intermediate objects
historical stage
available repertoire
later reconstruction
```

The study asks whether a Constitution-guided re-cut changes the admissible evidence and classification in a way that survives strong philosophy-of-mathematical-practice and proof-identity baselines.

Compact form:

```text
same result or later intertranslatability
!= automatically same method

historically different presentation
!= automatically different method
```

The candidate operational question is:

> Can method identity be tracked by a historically available, representation-robust, load-bearing generative role, while keeping retrospective reconstruction separate from what the earlier stage could actually implement?

This charter does not assume that the answer is unique to SRT.

## 1. Domain-template pass

### 1.0 Who participates?

- historical mathematicians construct, communicate, judge and reuse methods;
- mathematical communities stabilize vocabularies, acceptable operations, training and standards;
- historians and philosophers reconstruct method families from surviving texts and later representations;
- the present analyst chooses archive boundaries, equivalence criteria and comparison grain;
- proofs, formulas, models and texts are epistemic artifacts / measurement objects here, not automatically bearers or subjects.

No strong collective-bearer, consciousness or agency thesis is required for this well.

### 1.1 Given One

The study-local Given One is:

> a named mathematical method or method family is treated as a stable public object whose identity can be read directly across historical documents and later equivalent formulations.

Typical compressions include:

```text
same proof goal -> same method
same theorem type -> same method
modern intertranslatability -> historically same method
shared notation -> same method
new notation / new intermediate object -> new method
textbook genealogy -> historical availability
```

The well does not claim that historians or philosophers universally make these mistakes. They are the objectification shortcuts to be tested against the strongest baseline.

### 1.2 Objectification / detachment

A public method object is produced by operations such as:

```text
retrospective notation normalization
proof-goal grouping
textbook compression
selection of canonical representatives
translation among formalisms
periodization
archive inclusion / exclusion
attribution of a construction to an author, school or method family
```

These operations can erase which resources were actually available at the earlier stage and which components were load-bearing in generating the result.

### 1.3 SRT re-entry

Reintroduce as explicit evidence burdens:

- **ARTICULATION**: where did this method-One come from; which grouping rule made these cases one family?
- **STILL-THIS**: which representation changes preserve the method classification and where does re-identification fail?
- **OWN-HISTORY**: which later reformulations count as this method's own development rather than an externally imposed reconstruction?
- **CURRENT PROCESSABILITY**: which operations were actually available to practitioners at the historical stage, rather than merely derivable in hindsight?
- **CONSEQUENCE ATTRIBUTION**: which result, obstruction, uptake or failure is attributed to the method rather than to background assumptions, representation, author choice or later infrastructure?
- **EVALUATIVE RE-RUN**: what basis makes a method count as new, explanatory, general, elegant, infrastructural or merely local, and does that judgement survive a changed comparison basis?

These are reader operations. They do not define mathematical method identity by constitutional fiat.

### 1.4 Reorganization

The study keeps separate:

```text
result identity
proof identity
method identity
method-family identity
historical availability
later representational equivalence
institutional uptake
```

It also preserves both granularity guards already frozen in the forcing–CH project:

```text
same proof goal does not entail same method
new intermediate object does not entail new method
```

### 1.5 New problem

The bounded problem is:

> How can a historian or philosopher individuate a method strongly enough to distinguish genuine operation change from result/notation change, but weakly enough not to split every new auxiliary object into a new method—and do so without importing later repertoire into the earlier stage?

### 1.6 Domain test

Use the existing forcing–CH evidence project as a pre-existing, adversarial test bed rather than as a source of constitutional definitions.

The test has two layers:

1. **representation-family layer** — use the already executed Cohen ramified / generic-filter / Boolean-valued comparison to test whether the same-method verdict survives representation change;
2. **independent-control layer** — complete the frozen archive gate, select Control A and Control B under the preregistered selection protocol, and only then run the six method-individuation tests.

The Constitution contribution is assessed separately from the pre-existing protocol result:

```text
protocol validates or fails
!=
Constitution adds epistemic content
```

A Constitution increment requires showing that its re-cut changes an admissible classification, evidence requirement or stopping decision not already supplied by the strongest baseline or by the pre-Constitution protocol itself.

### 1.7 Domain increment gate

Possible dispositions are fixed now:

```text
Case A:
strong philosophy/history-of-mathematical-practice baselines already supply the full distinction
-> stop SRT increment claim

Case B:
Constitution improves routing, declaration or audit discipline only
-> translation / process contribution

Case C:
Constitution exposes a bounded method-identity distinction that changes archive coding,
control classification or historical inference beyond the strongest baseline and pre-existing protocol
-> retain as a testable epistemology increment

unresolved:
archive / control evidence is insufficient
-> keep the well open without superiority language
```

No outcome of the forcing–CH paper may be silently upgraded into an SRT Case C.

## 2. Mandatory Bearer–Objectification Declaration

| Field | Declaration for this well |
|---|---|
| unit | a historically documented mathematical construction / method candidate, compared across declared representations and stages |
| boundary | 1938–1963 mathematical logic outside the CH / forcing / independence target line for the independent controls; forcing representations are used only for the pre-existing representation test |
| grain | operation / generative-resource level, not theorem statement alone and not every syntactic intermediate object |
| equivalence | same-method classification requires declared representation-neutral functional-role tests; later interderivability alone is insufficient |
| normalization | modern notation and reconstruction may be used for comparison only if source-native availability and role are recorded separately |

| position loss | present analysts see later equivalences and later canon formation unavailable to historical actors; this asymmetry must be declared rather than treated as neutral hindsight |

| participation | historical mathematicians act within a repertoire; communities transmit/evaluate; historians reconstruct; analysts define archive and tests; texts preserve partial traces |

The declaration is diagnostic, not an ontology of mathematical practice.

## 6. Falsifiers and withdrawal conditions

Downgrade or withdraw any claimed epistemology increment if:

1. the strongest baseline already distinguishes result/proof/method identity with the same historical and representation-neutral consequences;
2. the Constitution pass merely restates the pre-existing Method Individuation Protocol without changing admissible evidence or decisions;
3. same-method verdicts depend on present-day notation or textbook genealogy rather than contemporaneous availability;
4. the classification changes whenever the same construction is rewritten in an admissible equivalent formalism;
5. the rule is so coarse that same proof goal collapses distinct operations;
6. the rule is so fine that every new intermediate object becomes a new method;
7. independently selected Control A and B do not fall on opposite sides under the frozen protocol;
8. Part II archive adequacy cannot be attested inside the fixed boundary;
9. a Case C claim requires post-hoc changing the archive boundary, control criteria or method rule.

Negative, qualified and boundary-failure outcomes remain valid deep-well results.
