# SRT Generative Relational Grammar Research Framework v0.1

## 0. Role

This file is the programme-level execution architecture for GRG.

It is not:

- a completed ontology;
- a universal equation;
- a vocabulary replacement for mature disciplines;
- a claim that all domains instantiate one mechanism;
- a new canonical owner.

Its job is to make the author's GRG direction operational:

> preserve source-native knowledge, extract candidate generative relations, compare them across domains without erasing mechanism, use evidence and failure to revise the grammar, and extend the same architecture to social and civilizational learning.

## 1. Framework identity

Working definition:

~~~text
GRG
= a self-revising research architecture
  for learning recurring generative dependency patterns
  across heterogeneous domains, positions, scales and histories.
~~~

The unification target is not:

~~~text
one substance
one equation
one mechanism
one vocabulary
~~~

The target is a constrained grammar of relations such as:

~~~text
formation
retention
maintenance
re-entry
position
consequence
reconstruction
cross-scale recursion
anticipation
normative comparison
~~~

A relation belongs in the grammar only if it carries admission conditions, non-identities and failure conditions.

## 2. Two-level programme architecture

### Level A — mainline framework construction

The mainline builds:

1. the relation / burden library;
2. domain and civilizational mappings;
3. evidence typing;
4. objectification declarations;
5. generative-expectation routing;
6. cross-domain translation rules;
7. revision and retirement rules.

### Level B — calibration workstreams

Calibration includes:

- experiments;
- simulations when appropriate;
- archival / historical tests;
- source-fidelity close reads;
- causal perturbation;
- transfer pilots;
- adverse-result preservation.

Calibration asks whether the framework is too permissive or wrong.

It does not block unrelated framework work.

~~~text
calibration constrains mainline
but
calibration queue != mainline work queue
~~~

## 3. Reuse of the Domain Reconstruction Layer

The existing Domain Reconstruction Framework remains the preferred entry surface for one mature discipline.

Therefore the active stack becomes:

~~~text
canonical / author constraints
        ↓
GRG Research Framework
        ↓
Domain Reconstruction Framework
        ↓
domain-native theory / mechanism / historical account
        ↓
evidence / calibration / intervention
        ↓
writeback to domain + GRG relation library
~~~

GRG does not replace domain frameworks.

It compares what their independently preserved relations pay.

## 4. Core data object: the GRG Relation Record

The minimum comparison unit is not a word or analogy.

A GRG relation record must declare:

~~~text

participants / components
position / scale / grain
formation process
retained or maintained organization
later process being conditioned
causal / inferential direction claimed
evidence type
objectification choices
generative expectation
failure condition
cross-domain mapping status
revision status
~~~

## 5. Source-native layer

Every GRG mapping begins with a source-native account.

The source-native layer owns:

- field terminology;
- mechanisms;
- measurements;
- empirical regularities;
- formal models;
- historical particulars;
- local causal claims.

Mandatory guard:

~~~text
domain mechanism != GRG relation
~~~

A relation may abstract dependency structure.

It may not appropriate the mechanism as SRT ontology.

## 6. Objectification layer

Every mapping should state which public objects are being constructed and stabilized.

Relevant operations include:

- boundary;
- grain;
- timescale;
- equivalence;
- normalization;
- aggregation;
- proxy;
- canonical representation;
- archive inclusion;
- participant pooling;
- periodization.

Question:

> What does this objectification preserve, and what generative relation could disappear because of it?

HKB normalization work is a model example of why this declaration is mandatory.

~~~text
measured object
!= maintained organization automatically
~~~

## 7. Evidence architecture

GRG must accept heterogeneous evidence without pretending all evidence is equivalent.

### 7.1 Evidence families

At minimum distinguish:

~~~text
E1 controlled intervention / experiment
E2 natural or quasi-experimental causal evidence
E3 longitudinal / observational empirical evidence
E4 formal / mathematical constraint
E5 mechanistic model with independent validation
E6 historical-comparative / institutional evidence
E7 engineering / operational performance evidence
E8 structured lived / practice experience
E9 philosophical / conceptual convergence
E10 narrative / metaphor / heuristic
~~~

These codes are research-routing labels, not a universal epistemic ranking.

### 7.2 Evidence dimensions

Each record should assess separately:

- source reliability;
- causal leverage;
- replication / robustness;
- scale match;
- temporal depth;
- position diversity;
- measurement dependence;
- transportability;
- confounding;
- revision pressure.

A strong philosophical convergence may nominate a relation.

It does not substitute for a causal experiment where the claim itself is causal.

A randomized experiment may strongly establish one local mechanism.

It does not by itself establish a civilization-scale relation.

## 8. Relation / burden library

The existing G0–G11 programme burdens remain working slots.

They should now be managed as a **relation library**, not a hidden ontology ladder.

Each relation can be:

~~~text
candidate
active
split
narrowed
merged
domain-limited
retired
open
~~~

GRG-R1 supplies the first worked example of burden decomposition:

~~~text
R1a retained imprint
R1b maintained scaffold
R1c causal re-entry
R1d prospective reuse / transfer
~~~

Lesson:

~~~text
one compressed relation
may need to split
when calibration reveals distinct evidential burdens.
~~~

This pattern should be reusable for later G-burdens.

## 9. Cross-domain translation

Cross-domain translation asks:

> Do two source-native systems pay a comparable generative burden?

It does not ask:

> Are they secretly the same thing?

Required comparison fields:

- relation topology;
- dependency direction;
- maintenance provenance;
- component / whole relation;
- scale;
- intervention availability;
- consequence type;
- time structure;
- evidence family;
- failure mode.

### Mapping status

Use:

~~~text
M0 verbal resemblance only
M1 structural analogy
M2 burden-aligned candidate
M3 independently evidenced relation match
M4 transfer-generating match
M5 repeatedly cross-domain constrained grammar candidate
~~~

These are programme routing labels, not ontology Levels.

No relation progresses merely because several domains use similar words.

### 9.1 First civilizational pressure-test revision

The first three case records (Internet standards, antimicrobial resistance, Montreal Protocol) produced a productive false-convergence result:

~~~text
one-relation compression = FAIL
~~~

Two methodological fields are now required in civilizational / cross-domain comparison.

#### Expectation embodiment / enforcement provenance

When an expectation is externalized, state how it becomes causally operative.

Working routing labels:

~~~text
P-TECH  technical interface / compatibility
P-LEGAL legal / treaty obligation
P-MKT   market / price / contract incentive
P-ORG   organizational procedure / hierarchy
P-NORM  social norm / reputational enforcement
P-INFRA material / infrastructural constraint
P-MIXED mixed provenance
~~~

These are not equivalent mechanisms.

#### Position / horizon consequence split

When effects cross positions or timescales, state separately:

- immediate beneficiary;
- delayed affected position;
- local versus distributed consequence;
- time horizon;
- externalized cost;
- reversibility of future option loss.

`Operations/Audits/SRT_GRG_CIVILIZATIONAL_FALSE_CONVERGENCE_PASS1_2026-09-21.md`.

## 10. Generative expectation layer

A GRG relation may carry a structural expectation about its continuation or transformation.

Existing author adjudication remains:

~~~text
B = structural generative expectation
C = formed-locus anticipatory realization
C0 = embodied / enacted
C1 = model-mediated
~~~

Framework question:

~~~text
given relation R at declared position / scale,
what continuation, maintenance, transformation or failure
would count as relation-consistent?
~~~

This expectation must be typed.

Do not infer:

~~~text
expectation = prediction
expectation = goodness
expectation = moral legitimacy
~~~

## 11. Normativity interface

GRG supplies a possible source line for indexed normativity:

~~~text
formed relation
-> generative expectation
-> actual trajectory
-> indexed comparison
-> G-stronger / G-weaker / substitution / hollowing / destruction
~~~

The index must name:

- position;
- relation;
- scale;
- time horizon;
- affected bearers where established.

Social and institutional analysis must additionally ask:

- who receives benefits;
- who carries consequences;
- whose agency is reduced;
- which costs are externalized;
- which future options are closed;
- whether persistence is maintained by domination;
- whether the larger organization erases participant generativity.

Therefore:

~~~text
stable != legitimate
persistent != good
larger whole != morally prior
more options != always better
~~~

## 12. Civilizational learning architecture

Civilization is treated as a distributed historical grammar-learning process.

### 12.1 Input families

Civilizational experience includes:

- scientific results;
- technical artifacts;
- failed technologies;
- public health;
- markets;
- firms;
- states;
- law;
- institutions;
- wars and peace processes;
- ecological interventions;
- education;
- communities;
- family / interpersonal practice;
- moral and religious traditions;
- art and cultural memory.

These are not one homogeneous dataset.

### 12.2 Learning loop

~~~text
finite positions
-> experience
-> externalization / record
-> cross-position comparison
-> regularity / theory / institution
-> intervention
-> consequence
-> anomaly / conflict / failure
-> revision
-> improved or rejected grammar candidate
~~~

Institutions themselves can be understood as partially externalized generative expectations.

They stabilize some relations and suppress others.

This is a research hypothesis family, not a blanket normative endorsement of institutions.

## 13. Civilizational experience record

For social / historical use, record:

~~~text
actors / positions
institution / practice
historical context
initial relation
rule / norm / incentive
intervention / event
distribution of consequences
short-term outcome
long-term outcome
externalized costs
adaptation / counter-adaptation
which relation strengthened / weakened
what expectation failed
evidence quality
alternative explanation
transfer status
~~~

The record must preserve losers, excluded groups and background conditions where evidence permits.

Aggregate success must not erase position-specific harm.

## 15. Programme workstreams

### F1 — Relation Library

Maintain G-burdens and their splits, guards and retirement conditions.

### F2 — Domain Atlas

Build / update domain reconstruction surfaces using the existing template.

### F3 — Cross-domain Translation

Compare independently grounded relation records.

### F4 — Civilizational Learning

Ingest historical / institutional / social cases with explicit evidence typing and position indexing.

### F5 — Generative Expectation / Normativity

Study how structural expectation becomes indexed normativity and where stronger moral premises enter.

### F6 — Calibration

Run bounded experiments, source-fidelity tests, natural experiments and adverse-result audits.

### F7 — Reflexive Grammar Learning

Track how evidence changes the framework itself.

AI-assisted retrieval / synthesis belongs here as a tool, not as definition authority.

## 16. AI role

AI can help:

- retrieve sources;
- normalize terminology;
- generate candidate relation records;
- find cross-domain structural similarities;
- generate counterexamples;
- audit provenance;
- identify contradictions;
- update maps;
- propose tests.

AI cannot by model agreement alone:

- establish author intent;
- promote a relation to canonical ontology;
- settle contested empirical claims;
- assign moral legitimacy;
- turn analogy into evidence.

The framework should preserve machine-generated hypotheses as visible machine synthesis until supported.

## 17. Anti-collapse guards

### 17.1 Anti-tautology

If every event instantiates the relation, the relation is too weak.

Require exclusion / failure cases.

### 17.2 Anti-metaphor

Shared language is not shared structure.

### 17.3 Anti-domain-erasure

Do not flatten field mechanisms into SRT labels.

### 17.4 Anti-evidence-flattening

Historical experience, RCTs, formal proof and philosophical intuition have different inferential roles.

### 17.5 Anti-God-view

Every grammar learner is positioned and finite.

### 17.6 Anti-progress myth

Historical persistence is not evidence of moral superiority.

### 17.7 Anti-scale escape

A failed mapping cannot always be rescued by moving to a different scale.

## 18. Framework scorecard

Judge the framework itself on:

### Compression

Does it connect independently grounded relations without erasing mechanism?

### Constraint

Does it reject tempting mappings?

### Transfer

Does it generate a useful question / control / distinction before target outcome inspection?

### Revision

Does evidence actually change the grammar?

### Coverage

Can it represent natural, biological, cognitive, social and civilizational cases without reducing them to one evidence type?

### Position sensitivity

Can it represent differential consequences across positions?

### Normative discipline

Can it keep generative expectation distinct from moral legitimacy?
