## 1. Core construct

Working neutral definition:

> **Preformal coherence sensitivity** is the ability to discriminate relational or global coherence / incoherence before the person can provide a complete explicit justification for that discrimination.

Do not create a permanent acronym yet.

### Core temporal signature

~~~text
coherence discrimination
occurs before
explicit articulation.
~~~

The research target is the **gap** between:

~~~text
"I know / feel this is wrong"
and
"I can say exactly why it is wrong."
~~~

The gap must predict something externally checkable to count as more than retrospective narrative.

---

## 2. Research questions

### RQ1 — existence

Can people discriminate structural coherence above chance before explicit solution / rationale?

### RQ2 — universality

Is this capacity broadly distributed across people, or concentrated in experts / specific domains?

### RQ3 — individual differences

Which dimensions vary reliably?

Candidate dimensions:

- sensitivity;
- false-alarm rate;
- calibration;
- detection latency;
- articulation latency;
- root-level vs local-error sensitivity;
- cross-domain transfer;
- tolerance for unfamiliar vocabulary;
- resistance to locally fluent but globally inconsistent prose;
- revision quality after mismatch detection.

### RQ4 — mechanism

Which existing mechanisms explain the phenomenon?

Candidates:

- semantic-coherence accumulation;
- gist extraction;
- implicit / statistical learning;
- schema abstraction;
- pattern completion / attractor dynamics;
- processing fluency;
- metacognitive feeling-of-rightness;
- recognition-primed expertise;
- predictive / generative processing.

### RQ5 — trait vs learned geometry

Is there a stable domain-general individual factor, or are apparent differences mainly learned domain geometries?

### RQ6 — SRT incremental value

After strong existing cognitive models are applied, does an SRT mapping add:
- new discriminating predictions;
- better failure conditions;
- useful multiview reconstruction;
or only relabel known mechanisms?

## 3. Competing models

### M0 — explicit-only

People do not genuinely know before they can articulate.
Apparent early intuition is post hoc reconstruction, fluency or noise.

### M1 — familiarity / fluency

Early judgments reflect familiarity or processing ease, not structural coherence.

Prediction:
- disrupt familiarity while preserving relational structure -> performance collapses.

### M2 — domain-specific learned gist

People learn compressed meaning structures within domains.

Prediction:
- strong within-domain performance;
- weak unfamiliar-domain transfer;
- expertise / exposure explains most variance.

### M3 — implicit statistical / relational learning

People acquire regularities below explicit awareness.

Prediction:
- early coherence sensitivity can emerge even without verbalizable rules;
- task-aligned statistical-learning measures correlate with performance.

### M4 — partially domain-general coherence architecture

There is a broadly human architecture for accumulating weak relational constraint and detecting global fit, tuned by domain learning.

Prediction:
- modest cross-domain latent factor remains after knowledge / fluency controls.

### M5 — SRT gate-geometry interpretation

Candidate only after M1-M4 are tested.

Prediction candidate:
- early mismatch is strongest when a proposed representation violates high-level relational constraints that have become background gate geometry;
- later articulation corresponds to foreground objectification / recutting of the violated relation.

M5 is not privileged.

## 4. Literature anchors / strongest prior art

### 4.1 Bowers et al. 1990 — intuition in discovery

Bowers, Regehr, Balthazard & Parker, *Cognitive Psychology* 22(1), 72-110.
DOI: 10.1016/0010-0285(90)90004-N.

Critical result:
people could discriminate coherent from incoherent material before identifying the coherent solution; tacit coherence could later surface as a hunch / hypothesis.

This is the closest classical neighbor to the present target.

### 4.2 Sweklej & Balas 2024 — semantic coherence

Frontiers in Psychology 15.
DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1406811.

Useful because it directly manipulates semantic / perceptual priming in intuitive coherence judgments.

### 4.3 Fuzzy-Trace Theory

Reyna (2023), "A new intuitionism: Meaning, memory, and development in Fuzzy-Trace Theory."

FTT is a major rival / partial explanation:
- gist representations support advanced intuition;
- gist reliance can increase with age / expertise;
- gist can be adaptive and biased.

### 4.4 Kahneman & Klein 2009

"Conditions for intuitive expertise: a failure to disagree."
DOI: 10.1037/a0016755.

Mandatory calibration guard:

~~~text
subjective confidence
!= intuitive validity.
~~~

Reliable intuitive expertise requires:
- a sufficiently regular environment;
- sufficient opportunity to learn the regularities.

### 4.5 Statistical learning

Forest et al. (2023), *Nature Reviews Psychology*, "Changes in statistical learning across development."

Key relevance:
humans extract environmental regularities from infancy onward, but the architecture / representational outcome changes with development.

Individual-difference literature adds a major guard:
- measurable differences exist;
- task reliability is often weak;
- different statistical-learning tasks can correlate poorly.

Therefore no single task may be treated as a trait measure.

## 6. Phase 1 — naturalistic retrospective case study

Use repository / dialogue history as a **case**, not as proof.

### 6.1 Sampling rule

Predefine a date window and sample all eligible episodes, not only remembered successes.

Candidate window:

~~~text
2026-09-21 through 2026-09-26
~~~

Eligibility:

- AI / reviewer proposes a substantive theory structure;
- author gives an early accept / reject / correction;
- later explicit reasoning / review exists;
- timestamps / order are recoverable.

### 6.2 Coding dimensions

For each episode:

- initial judgment time;
- initial rationale length;
- later rationale time;
- whether rationale changed;
- local vs root-level correction;
- number of concepts touched;
- whether later independent review supported the early direction;
- whether later author reversed the early judgment;
- whether AI had adapted to prior author wording;
- whether the target was familiar vs novel terminology.

### 6.3 Key naturalistic index

Candidate:

~~~text
articulation lag
= time / dialogue distance between
initial coherence judgment
and
first adequate explicit structural explanation.
~~~

Do not interpret shorter / longer as better by itself.

### 6.4 Main bias controls

- include false alarms;
- include reversals;
- include overconfident mistakes;
- blind coders to later outcome where feasible;
- separate author quotes from machine paraphrases;
- no cherry-picked "best examples".

## 7. Phase 2 — preregisterable behavioral battery

Use multiple tasks because single intuition / statistical-learning measures are psychometrically weak.

### Task A — semantic coherence without solution

Adapt the Bowers-style paradigm.

Trial:

~~~text
related triad / tetrad
vs
matched incoherent set
~~~

Measure:
- coherence judgment;
- confidence;
- whether solution can be explicitly generated;
- reaction time.

Signature:

~~~text
coherence accuracy > chance
among trials with no explicit solution.
~~~

---

### Task B — local validity / global inconsistency

Create short theoretical passages where:

- every sentence is individually plausible;
- one hidden relation violates the global structure.

Conditions:
1. coherent;
2. local factual error;
3. global relational inconsistency;
4. fluent nonsense control.

First response:

~~~text
Does this whole structure feel coherent?
YES / NO / UNSURE
~~~

Only afterward ask:

> explain exactly why.

Primary measure:

~~~text
early global-discrimination accuracy
before adequate explanation.
~~~

This task is closest to "theory shape".

---

### Task C — structural perturbation

Show a coherent relation network / paragraph.

Then silently alter one:
- direction;
- dependency;
- level;
- necessary / sufficient relation;
- temporal order;
- identity relation.

Ask rapidly:
- original or corrupted?
- confidence?

Then ask for reason.

Key test:
can participants detect relation damage before naming the damaged relation?

---

### Task D — unfamiliar vocabulary / preserved structure

Replace familiar domain terms with arbitrary labels while preserving relational structure.

Purpose:

~~~text
knowledge / word familiarity control.
~~~

Compare:
- same structure, unfamiliar labels;
- familiar labels, corrupted structure.

This distinguishes terminology familiarity from relational sensitivity.

---

### Task E — root correction choice

Give 3 proposed repairs to an inconsistent theory:

1. local wording patch;
2. compensatory extra assumption;
3. root-level re-cut that removes several downstream problems.

Ask for fast preference before rationale.

Later score:
- which repair actually restores formal / expert-defined consistency?

This tests whether early intuition is sensitive to repair geometry, not only anomaly detection.

---

### Task F — transfer

Use several domains:

- verbal semantic;
- causal diagrams;
- simple social scenarios;
- spatial / relational structures;
- unfamiliar toy theories.

Do not assume a general factor.

Estimate:
- within-domain reliability;
- cross-domain correlations;
- latent factors only if psychometrics support them.

## 9. Strong falsification / downgrade conditions

Downgrade the construct if:

### F1

Early coherence judgments are at chance when explicit solution is unavailable.

### F2

Performance is fully explained by familiarity / fluency controls.

### F3

No reliable individual differences remain after measurement error is modeled.

### F4

There is no cross-task stability even within aligned relational tasks.

### F5

Author naturalistic episodes show high false-alarm / reversal rates once all cases are sampled.

### F6

"root-level" judgments cannot be scored independently of later author preference.

### F7

An SRT interpretation adds no prediction or discriminator beyond established gist / implicit-learning / expertise models.

## 10. What would count as an interesting positive result

A strong result would **not** be:

> people have intuition.

That is already known.

A more interesting result would be:

~~~text
people can detect global relational constraint violation
before explicit articulation;

this ability is measurably reliable when task structure is aligned;

individual differences remain after
knowledge / fluency / reasoning / confidence controls;

the strongest individuals show
early detection + low false alarms + later valid recutting;

some cross-domain factor survives
without erasing domain-specific learning.
~~~

That would justify a stronger model.

## 11. SRT / GRG downstream question

Only after neutral psychological work:

> Can SRT explain the temporal sequence from tacit coherence detection to explicit recutting more economically or predictively than existing models?

Candidate SRT mapping:

~~~text
sedimented L2 relational structure
-> L0-facing gate geometry
-> current proposal violates constraints
-> Ontological Friction / mismatch becomes load-bearing
-> early hunch / coherence judgment
-> later L1 explicit objectification of the violated relation
-> gate / model recut.
~~~

Possible GRG relevance:

> the phenomenon may itself illustrate why reconstruction can precede grammar: a person can detect that a proposed grammar is wrong before possessing the explicit replacement grammar.

This is a research bridge, not evidence for SRT.
