## 2. Mature source-side baseline retained

### 2.1 Rutherford & Lindquist 1998 — Hsp90 capacitance

Rutherford & Lindquist reported that impaired Hsp90 in *Drosophila* exposes background-dependent morphological variation produced by previously silent genetic determinants. They further reported that Hsp90 normally buffers that variation; when buffering is compromised, including under temperature stress, cryptic variants can become expressed and subsequently acted on by selection.

Source:

- Rutherford, S.L. & Lindquist, S. (1998), “Hsp90 as a capacitor for morphological evolution”, *Nature* 396:336–342.
- DOI: 10.1038/24550

Source-side minimum retained here:

```text
underlying genetic differences are present
+
ordinary Hsp90-dependent organization buffers phenotypic consequence
+
perturbing the buffering regime can reveal phenotype
+
revealed heritable phenotype can enter later selection
```

### 2.2 Queitsch, Sangster & Lindquist 2002 — cross-organism realization

In *Arabidopsis*, reduced Hsp90 function likewise exposed genotype-dependent morphological variation and altered responses to environmental cues.

Source:

- Queitsch, C., Sangster, T.A. & Lindquist, S. (2002), “Hsp90 as a capacitor of phenotypic variation”, *Nature* 417:618–624.
- DOI: 10.1038/nature749

This supports Hsp90 buffering / release as a biological mechanism family rather than a one-off fly description.

### 2.3 Paaby & Rockman 2014 — mature CGV framing

The mature CGV review defines cryptic genetic variation as variation with little or no ordinary phenotypic effect that can generate heritable phenotypic variation under atypical conditions. It explicitly treats buffering / canalization as one route by which such variation can remain cryptic and later become exposed.

Source:

- Paaby, A.B. & Rockman, M.V. (2014), “Cryptic genetic variation: evolution's hidden substrate”, *Nature Reviews Genetics* 15:247–258.
- DOI: 10.1038/nrg3688

### 2.4 Canalization / buffering scope guard

Later canalization literature treats robustness to genetic or environmental perturbation as a mature problem family tied to genotype–phenotype mapping and evolvability.

Important guard:

```text
Hsp90
!= universal canalization mechanism
```

Canalization can be trait-specific and arise through multiple developmental / genetic architectures. Mechanistic detail remains open in many systems.

Therefore this audit claims mature ownership of the **buffering / perturbation / release structure**, not a universal Hsp90 mechanism.

## 4. Latent reconstructive reach reality test — audit-local operational synthesis

The evolution case is still useful because it makes the existing GRG burden experimentally legible.

The following is an **M-level operational synthesis of already accepted owner burdens**, not a new canonical definition:

```text
LR-1  retained difference / capacity
      Evidence must show that the relevant difference or conditional capacity
      is present before re-expression.

LR-2  real bridge
      A bounded causal / organizational pathway must remain by which a later
      condition can reconnect the retained difference to consequence.

LR-3  intervention sensitivity
      Perturbing the claimed bridge / support / access relation must alter the
      ability of the difference to regain consequence, rather than merely alter
      our observation or description of it.

LR-4  world-side re-entry
      Under the declared reopening condition, the difference must actually
      regain downstream consequence / participation in later dynamics.
      Data retrievability alone is insufficient.

LR-5  scope declaration
      The claim must name the relevant condition, intervention family,
      consequence burden, grain and horizon.
```

This distinguishes:

```text
stored information
!=
latent reconstructive reach
```

because storage can satisfy LR-1 while failing LR-2 through LR-4.

It also distinguishes:

```text
mere future possibility
!=
currently real conditional organization
```

because the bridge must already be intervention-sensitive before the later actualisation.
