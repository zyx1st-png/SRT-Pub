## 2. P-INST result

The binding-versus-persuasive distinction shows:

~~~text
P-INST institutional authority role = CAUSALLY IDENTIFIABLE / PASS
~~~

The authority relation depends on hierarchy / jurisdiction.

Prior reasoning can remain available while institutional binding force differs.

Therefore informational persistence is not sufficient to explain the successor constraint.

## 4. Provenance framework revision

~~~text

~~~

~~~text

~~~

This prevents P-MIXED from becoming an uninformative bucket.

## 5. Binding versus persuasive near-control

Source-native comparison:

~~~text
binding precedent
vs
persuasive authority
~~~

is not a perfect matched experiment.

But it is a strong institutional contrast:

~~~text
legal reasoning may remain available
while
mandatory successor constraint differs with authority relation.
~~~

The contrast supports an independently causal P-INST component.

## 6. Case-of-first-impression near-control

A case of first impression lacks controlling precedent.

This functions as a second near-control:

~~~text
same legal institution / court system
but no specific inherited binding precedent on the issue
-> wider construction burden for successor court.
~~~

This is analogous to:

~~~text
greenfield
vs
installed-base
~~~

without asserting exact equivalence.
