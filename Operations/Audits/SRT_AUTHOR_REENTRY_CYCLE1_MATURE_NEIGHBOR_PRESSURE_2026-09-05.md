# Author Re-entry Cycle 1 — Mature-neighbor pressure pass 1

> **Role:** first source-native neighbor pressure pass on the live author structure recorded on 2026-09-05. This is not a novelty verdict, not a canonical ontology update, and not a substitute for the next author adjudication.
>
> **Method guard:** the author structure came first. Neighbor theories are used here to pressure, subtract, and sharpen it. They are not used as a permission gate and are not allowed to silently author SRT.

## 0. Author structure under pressure

The live author pass currently contains the following provisional commitments / intuitions:

```text
A1  Selection maintains difference ontologically.
A2  Cognition draws a boundary; that boundary can reinforce ontological difference-maintenance.
A3  Determinate actuality is not yet an ontological One, although cognition may treat it as a One.
A4  Persistence belongs to cognitive One-formation and to continued existence on the ontological side.
A5  Cognitive One and ontological One are different One-formations; cognitive One serves ontological One.
A6  Ontological One affects later cognitive definition/persistence, which feeds back into the One's later Selection and maintenance.
A7  Clarification: external cognitive One also helps determine ontological One because ontological One is, in the author's current intuition, sedimented cognitive One.
A8  Clarification: without an ontological bearer One there is no stable Selection.
```

Canonical constraints still remain in force:

```text
K1  determinate world-side manifest actuality is downstream of Selection;
K2  Selection itself does not require a pre-existing selector / bearer.
```

Therefore the most important compatibility problem is already visible:

```text
subjectless / bearerless Selection is possible
but
stable Selection requires bearer One
```

The distinction between primitive/event-level Selection and stable/historical Selection is an AI candidate only at this stage.

## 1. Common problem in non-SRT language

Before comparing vocabularies, the shared problem can be stated without SRT terms:

> **How can a transient distinction become a persisting unit that carries history and supports recurrent action, while the unit is simultaneously shaped by the way other systems distinguish, classify, coordinate with, and act upon it—without presupposing either the finished unit or a finished observer at the start?**

This common problem has at least four separable burdens:

```text
P1  pre-individual burden:
    avoid assuming finished individuals before individuation.

P2  persistence / autonomy burden:
    explain how a unit becomes able to sustain its own distinction through time.

P3  external-recognition burden:
    explain when an externally imposed / socially enacted / cognitively drawn distinction can become causally constitutive of the unit rather than merely descriptive.

P4  historical-recursion burden:
    explain how the formed unit changes later possibilities, actions, and selection conditions.
```

## 2. Strongest neighbor 1 — Simondon

### 2.1 Why this is a strongest relevant neighbor

The repository already contains an A-class full-book close-read of Gilbert Simondon's *Individuation in Light of Notions of Form and Information*. This is stronger than a new secondary-summary intake and directly targets the pre-individual / individuation burden.

The existing full-close-read records especially strong pressure from:

```text
operation -> structure -> operation

local result
-> condition for next local operation

individuation from metastable / preindividual disparity
rather than an already finished individual

coherence without homogeneity

internal history + external coupling
```

### 2.2 Source-native neighbor claim

Simondon's individuation programme treats the individual as the result / phase of individuation rather than the starting substance. Transduction propagates structure while each local resolution conditions the next, and the individual remains tied to a milieu and residual preindividual potential.

### 2.3 Pressure on Cycle 1

Simondon strongly pre-empts any broad SRT claim that:

```text
SRT uniquely begins before the finished individual;
SRT uniquely treats earlier formation as conditioning later formation;
SRT uniquely treats persistence as organized heterogeneity rather than static identity;
SRT uniquely makes history operative in later individuation.
```

He also sharpens the current `many` problem: a preindividual field cannot safely be redescribed as a plurality of already individuated alternatives.

### 2.4 What Simondon does not directly settle here

The currently tested author claim is stronger/different in one specific direction:

```text
external cognitive One
-> repeated boundary / recognition effects
-> sedimentation into ontological bearer One
-> stable historical Selection
```

Simondon gives a powerful operation-structure-operation architecture, but the current source-native comparison does not by itself establish the author's cognitive-sedimentation claim or the proposed bearer/stable-Selection dependency.

### 2.5 Structural item changed by current SRT candidate

If later author-adopted, the non-trivial candidate would not be `history matters`. It would be the explicit separation:

```text
pre-bearer Selection
!= bearer-supported stable Selection
```

plus a proposed route by which externally/cognitively stabilized distinctions become part of the bearer that supports the latter.

That is not yet an accepted SRT claim.

## 3. Strongest neighbor 2 — organisational autonomy / enactivism

### 3.1 Why this is a strongest relevant neighbor

For the bearer/self-maintenance burden, autonomy and enactivism are stronger than generic process philosophy because they explicitly ask what makes a system boundary intrinsic rather than observer-imposed and how a system sustains its own individuality.

Primary anchors checked for this pass:

- Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde (2009), *Defining Agency: Individuality, Normativity, Asymmetry, and Spatio-temporality in Action*, *Adaptive Behavior* 17(5):367–386. DOI 10.1177/1059712309343819.
- Mossio & Moreno (2010), *Organisational closure in biological organisms*, *History and Philosophy of the Life Sciences* 32:269–288.
- Di Paolo (2018), *The Enactive Conception of Life*, *The Oxford Handbook of 4E Cognition*.

### 3.2 Source-native neighbor claim

Barandiaran et al. require genuine agency to include self-defined individuality, interactional asymmetry, and normativity, and describe agency as autonomous organization that adaptively regulates environmental coupling while contributing to sustaining itself.

Mossio & Moreno argue that organisms instantiate organisational closure: mutually dependent constraints generate a distinctive causal organization.

Di Paolo treats organic self-individuation as materially precarious, open and unfinished, with autonomy, agency and sense-making emerging from self-production / self-distinction tensions.

### 3.3 Pressure on Cycle 1

These neighbors strongly pre-empt broad claims that SRT uniquely discovers:

```text
stable agentive unity requires self-maintenance;
an agent defines / produces its own individuality;
internal normativity emerges from maintaining the organization;
organism-environment coupling can feed back into continued existence;
persistence and agency are recursively linked.
```

Therefore the statement:

```text
without ontological bearer One there is no stable Selection
```

cannot count as a distinctive result merely because stable agency needs a self-maintaining unity. Enactivism already pays much of that bill.

### 3.4 Strongest pressure against the new author structure

Autonomy theory pushes in the opposite direction from a simple observer-first account:

> if a boundary is constitutive of an autonomous unit, why should its ontological unity depend essentially on an **external cognitive One** rather than on the system's own organization?

This is a serious pressure point. Cycle 1 must distinguish at least:

```text
intrinsic self-distinction
from
external cognitive / classificatory stabilization
```

rather than replacing one with the other.

### 3.5 Structural item changed by current SRT candidate

The possible SRT repartition is not `self-maintenance`. It is:

```text
minimal / subjectless Selection
-> can occur before bearer

stable / iterated / history-bearing Selection
-> requires bearer continuity

bearer continuity
-> may itself be partly constituted by accumulated cognitive / classificatory / interactional sediment
```

If this survives author adjudication and neighbor pressure, it is at least a Level-1-style structural repartition candidate because it changes the dependency relation among Selection, bearer, cognition and persistence. It is not yet a Level verdict.

## 4. Strongest neighbor 3 — participatory sense-making

### 4.1 Why relevant

The author uses `external cognition`, so cognition cannot be assumed to live only inside an already finished individual. De Jaegher & Di Paolo (2007) are therefore a strong comparator.

Primary anchor:

- De Jaegher & Di Paolo (2007), *Participatory sense-making: An enactive approach to social cognition*, *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 6:485–507. DOI 10.1007/s11097-007-9076-9.

### 4.2 Source-native neighbor claim

Their core claim is that an interaction process can acquire a form of autonomy and that meaning can be generated and transformed in the interplay between the interaction dynamics and participating individuals.

### 4.3 Pressure on Cycle 1

This blocks a weak version of the author's idea such as:

```text
external interaction can affect what a unit means / how it is individuated
```

That is established neighboring territory.

It also supplies one possible way to avoid assuming that every cognitive boundary must originate in a fully self-contained cognizer: relational interaction dynamics can themselves become partly autonomous.

But it does **not** by itself establish:

```text
cognitive One sedimentation
-> ontological bearer One
-> stable Selection
```

That extra dependency still requires an explicit causal / constitutive account.

## 5. Strongest neighbor 4 — niche construction / ecological inheritance

### 5.1 Source-native neighbor claim

Niche construction theory describes organisms as modifying selective environments, with those environmental modifications feeding back into later selection pressures. Ecological inheritance preserves some of those constructed conditions across time / generations.

### 5.2 Pressure on Cycle 1

This strongly pre-empts any broad claim that:

```text
formed One changes the future many;
agent action changes later selection conditions;
historical environmental modification feeds back into future selection.
```

That recursive causal structure is established in evolutionary theory.

### 5.3 What remains different in the live author claim

Niche construction does not make **external cognitive classification** the central sedimentation mechanism of the bearer. Its feedback usually runs through organism-induced environmental modification and inherited selective conditions.

Thus the author's cognitive-sedimentation claim must not be defended merely by pointing to environment/selection feedback; that would collapse a potentially distinctive claim into an established neighbor mechanism.

## 6. Strongest direct pressure on `external cognitive One -> ontological One` — Hacking's looping effects

### 6.1 Why this comparison became necessary

The latest author clarification is not only enactive or biological. It claims that an externally cognitive One can help **determine** what becomes the ontological One through sedimentation.

For human/social kinds, Ian Hacking is therefore a more direct prior comparator than autopoiesis.

Checked anchors:

- Hacking (1996), *The looping effects of human kinds*, in *Causal Cognition: A Multidisciplinary Debate*.
- Hacking (2006), *Making Up People*.
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Social Ontology*, discussion of Hacking-style looping.

### 6.2 Source-native neighbor claim

Hacking's looping effect concerns classifications of people that alter how classified people are treated / understand themselves / behave, thereby changing the properties of the classified population and eventually the classification itself.

Compactly:

```text
classification
-> causal/social treatment and self-relation
-> classified people change
-> classification changes
-> further loop
```

### 6.3 Pressure on Cycle 1

For social, institutional and human identity cases, this is a direct warning against claiming as distinctive:

```text
external cognitive classification
-> changes the thing classified
-> stabilizes a new real kind / identity
-> feeds back into later classification
```

That family of looping is established prior art.

### 6.4 Crucial scope boundary

Hacking's argument is domain-bounded. A rock does not normally change merely because humans classify it as a rock. The causal efficacy of classification depends on mediating mechanisms: treatment, institutions, self-understanding, resource allocation, intervention, coordinated behavior, and related loops.

Therefore the author claim now faces a hard scope fork:

```text
DOMAIN-LIMITED READING
external cognitive One is ontologically constitutive only in systems with a real coupling channel through which classification / recognition changes persistence and action.

GENERAL ONTOLOGICAL READING
all ontological bearer Ones are sedimented cognitive Ones.
```

The second reading would require a much broader account of `cognition` and risks making cognition so weak that any interaction becomes cognition. No such generalization is licensed by this audit.

## 7. First non-substitutability read — provisional only

No formal Level 0/1/2 verdict is issued yet because the author has not adjudicated the neighbor pressure. Still, the current structure can be pre-sorted.

### Likely Level 0 / already-paid territory

```text
individual is not simply a pre-given substance;
formation history alters later formation;
self-maintaining unity supports agency;
organism/environment feedback alters later selection conditions;
external social classification can loop back on human/social kinds.
```

### Possible Level 1 structural repartition

```text
Selection itself may be bearerless
while
stable / iterated / history-bearing Selection requires bearer continuity.
```

This changes dependency structure rather than merely vocabulary, if the author explicitly adopts it.

A second possible Level 1 candidate is:

```text
cognitive/objectifying One
!= ontological bearer One
but repeated causally coupled cognitive One-formation can sediment into the bearer conditions of the latter.
```

This is only coherent if `sedimentation` is given a real causal / constitutive mechanism and a bounded scope.

### Possible Level 2 route — not yet earned

A Level 2 consequence would require cases that neighbors classify together but the SRT repartition separates, for example:

```text
same current autonomy / self-maintenance
+ same apparent cognitive classification
but different depth/location of sedimentation
-> different resilience of bearer continuity
-> different stability or ownership of later Selection
```

or:

```text
same transient Selection capacity
but no cross-time bearer continuity
-> no stable historical Selection
```

Those are future discriminator sketches, not current results.

## 8. The bootstrap problem survives all neighbors

The strongest unresolved issue is now sharper:

```text
if ontological bearer One is sedimented from cognitive One,
and stable Selection requires ontological bearer One,
what supports the first cognitive One-formation?
```

Neighbor comparison does not automatically solve this:

- Simondon allows individuation before the completed individual, but does not thereby prove cognitive sedimentation.
- Autopoiesis gives intrinsic self-distinction, which actually pressures an external-cognition-first reading.
- Participatory sense-making allows partially autonomous interaction dynamics, but still does not establish bearer sedimentation from classification.
- Hacking supplies classificatory loops only after relevant social/cognitive coupling mechanisms exist.
- Niche construction supplies environment-selection recursion without requiring cognitive classification.

Therefore the bootstrap must remain `D`, not be smoothed into a diagram.

## 9. Author adjudication now required

The next author pass should answer only the following four forks; no new governance work is needed.

### Q-A — scope of cognitive sedimentation

When you say:

> `本体的 One 本质上是认知 One 的沉积`

is this intended as:

```text
A. a general ontology claim about all bearer Ones;
B. a claim only about higher-order / cognitive / social bearer Ones;
C. a graded claim: intrinsic differential maintenance can form a basal ontological One, while cognitive sedimentation thickens it into a stronger historical bearer;
D. something else.
```

### Q-B — first boundary problem

Before a stable bearer exists, can a cognitive One occur at all?

If yes, is it:

```text
an instantaneous boundary event;
a relational / interactional event not owned by a stable subject;
a weak proto-cognitive registration;
or something else?
```

### Q-C — stable Selection dependency

Do you explicitly endorse the distinction:

```text
Selection does not require a bearer;
stable / repeated / history-bearing Selection does require a bearer?
```

If yes, what does `stable` minimally add: persistence, same-history ownership, consequence return, re-identifiability, or another condition?

### Q-D — what makes cognitive sedimentation ontic rather than descriptive?

What must happen for an external cognitive One to become part of the ontological One?

A mere label is insufficient. Which of the following is closest to the author's intuition:

```text
repeated behavioral treatment;
resource / environmental reconfiguration;
internalization by the bearer;
institutional / material inscription;
constraint formation;
all of these are examples of a more general causal sedimentation;
or another mechanism?
```
