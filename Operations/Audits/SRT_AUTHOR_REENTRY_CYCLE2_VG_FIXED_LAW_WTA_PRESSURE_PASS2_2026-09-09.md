## 1. Strongest mature fixed-law rival family

The strongest current rival is not a passive bump attractor. It is a recurrent competitive-selection / winner-take-all family with:

```text
option representations
+ recurrent amplification / excitation
+ competitive or feedback inhibition
+ dynamically generated winner / non-winner separation
+ state transition when the option/input configuration changes
```

This is a mature family rather than an SRT-specific construction.

### 1.1 Mysore & Kothari: competitive selection as symmetry breaking

Mysore and Kothari (2020) explicitly frame competitive selection as **symmetry breaking** among multiple available options:

```text
one option is selected
while the others are temporarily rejected
```

Their canonical circuit analysis further decomposes WTA selection into comparison, flexible selection boundaries, multi-option selection and unitary output.

Most important for the present audit, they distinguish fast on-the-fly flexibility from learned flexibility and state that the fast flexible selection boundary **cannot rely on plasticity mechanisms**. The circuit-level candidate instead uses feedback inhibition among competing option representations, while high-gain competitive inhibition plus recurrent amplification selectively enhances the winning option and suppresses the others.

Therefore this source directly supports a fixed-circuit rival that actively generates a vertical winner/non-winner relation without requiring historical parameter writeback.

Reference:

- Mysore, S. P. & Kothari, N. B. (2020). *Mechanisms of competitive selection: A canonical neural circuit framework*. eLife 9:e51473. DOI: `10.7554/eLife.51473`.

### 1.2 Mao & Massaquoi: recurrent lateral-inhibition WTA dynamics

Mao and Massaquoi (2007) analyze recurrent neural networks with lateral inhibition and derive conditions for the existence and stability of multiple equilibria. They show that lateral inhibition sharpens competing outputs toward winner-take-all dominance, and that shifts in input levels can yield network state transitions, including hysteresis.

For this audit, the important point is:

```text
fixed recurrent architecture
+ changed current inputs
-> new competitive state / winner transition
```

No synaptic learning event is required for that transition.

Reference:

- Mao, Z.-H. & Massaquoi, S. G. (2007). *Dynamics of winner-take-all competition in recurrent neural networks with lateral inhibition*. IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks 18(1):55–69. DOI: `10.1109/TNN.2006.883724`.

### 1.3 Wang: recurrent excitation + feedback inhibition produces and retains choices

Wang's recurrent decision-circuit framework uses strong recurrent excitation within option-selective populations and feedback inhibition between competing populations. The competition drives one population upward while the other is suppressed, yielding a categorical choice; after stimulus removal, the chosen attractor can persist as working-memory activity.

Reward-dependent plasticity is an **additional** capability in the broader framework, not a prerequisite for the basic recurrent competition / categorical-choice mechanism.

Reference:

- Wang, X.-J. (2008). *Decision making in recurrent neuronal circuits*. Neuron 60(2):215–234. DOI: `10.1016/j.neuron.2008.09.034`.

## 2. Payment matrix against `VG*`

### V1 — differential production

Criterion:

```text

rather than merely reading a pre-existing ranking
```

Fixed recurrent WTA pays this role strongly enough for the audit.

```text

```

### V2 — manifest/background co-generation

Criterion:

```text
enhancement / realization of one direction is dynamically coupled to
relative suppression / backgrounding of alternatives
```

This is close to the native WTA circuit description itself:

```text
winner amplification
+
competitive suppression of non-winners
```

No translation into SRT vocabulary is required to obtain the relevant causal role.

```text
V2: PAID by mature rival
```

### V3 — regeneration

Criterion:

```text
after perturbation, winner replacement or context/input change,
the non-flat relation is generated again rather than merely preserving one winner
```

Mature WTA / competitive circuits pay this through dynamic selection boundaries and state transitions under changed option/input configurations. Mao & Massaquoi explicitly identify input-driven transitions among stable competitive states; Mysore & Kothari require on-the-fly flexible selection boundaries without plasticity.

Thus the rival need not preserve one fixed content.

```text
V3: PAID by mature rival
```

### V4 — transition consequence

Criterion:

```text
the generated relation changes which later transitions are amplified,
suppressed, accessible or selected
```

Competitive selection is not merely descriptive separation. The winning representation alone is allowed to drive the relevant downstream perceptual / behavioral output, while recurrent state can retain the choice and condition subsequent circuit evolution.

```text
V4: PAID by mature rival
```

### L — persistent / re-identifiable locus

Self-sustained recurrent choice states and attractor states provide a persistent, re-identifiable locus after transient input removal.

```text
L: PAID by mature rival
```

## 3. The crucial distinction: state dependence is not automatically SED-B

The author has already ruled that SED-B is not mere asymmetric occupancy. It is retained history-conditioned writeback that changes later transition / reconstitution conditions.

To prevent the audit from laundering ordinary state dependence into SED-B, separate:

```text
x_t      = current fast dynamical / activation state
θ        = fixed recurrent law / weights / transfer structure
m_t      = candidate retained historical writeback variable
```

A fixed-law WTA rival can be written schematically as:

```text
x_(t+1) = F_θ(x_t, u_t)
θ_(t+1) = θ_t
```

where history can matter through the current state `x_t`, including ordinary hysteresis, while the recurrent law `θ` is unchanged.

That is not enough to pay SED-B under the author's ruling.

### 3.1 Washout / reset discriminator

Use the following source-neutral diagnostic:

```text
History H1 -> current competitive state x1
History H2 -> current competitive state x2

then force both systems through the same locked fast-state reset / washout R:

R(x1) = x_reset
R(x2) = x_reset

hold fixed:
θ
current input u*
measurement regime

compare future trajectories.
```

If:

```text
Future(H1 | reset, θ, u*)
=
Future(H2 | reset, θ, u*)
```

then the apparent history dependence was carried only by the current dynamical state / hysteresis. No independently retained SED-B writeback has been demonstrated.

If instead a history-conditioned difference survives the standardized fast-state reset and changes later reconstitution under the same current conditions, then an SED-B-like retained variable is present.

This diagnostic is an **audit tool**, not a new SRT definition.

## 5. Second pressure: generic `VG -> writeback -> next-VG` is also not yet distinctive

Pass 1 suggested that the residual might move to:

```text
VG_t
-> Selection_t differentiation
-> SH_t retained writeback
-> changed conditions for VG_(t+1)
```

That shape is stronger than fixed-law competition, but mature adaptive competitive systems already place it under substantial pressure.

### 5.1 Plastic WTA

Binas et al. (2014) show recurrent excitatory/inhibitory circuits that self-organize stable WTA competition through interacting excitatory and inhibitory plasticity. Other WTA literature couples competition to synaptic learning and homeostatic plasticity so initially weakly differentiated networks develop selective structure.

Reference:

- Binas, J., Rutishauser, U., Indiveri, G. & Pfeiffer, M. (2014). *Learning and stabilization of winner-take-all dynamics through interacting excitatory and inhibitory plasticity*. Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience 8:68. DOI: `10.3389/fncom.2014.00068`.

### 5.2 Reward-dependent recurrent decision circuits

Wang's review explicitly notes that recurrent decision circuits endowed with reward-dependent synaptic plasticity can produce adaptive choice behavior.

Thus ordinary mechanism families can already instantiate a generic loop of the form:

```text
competitive outcome / choice
-> retained parameter change
-> changed future competitive dynamics
```

### 5.3 Bowler pressure returns here

The Bowler close read already paid an even broader history-shaping chain:

```text
structured experience
-> changed recurrent organization
-> changed attractor / eigenspectrum scaffold
-> changed later accessibility, strategy and generalization
```

Therefore the mere statement:

```text
past selective history changes future vertical generation
```

cannot carry SRT distinctiveness by itself.
