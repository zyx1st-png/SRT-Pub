# Pass 19 correction addendum — participant-enacted stability has explanatory priority over observer-read stability

> **Role:** correct the machine framing at the end of Pass 19. The correction preserves Pass-19 neighbor payments but changes the live problem from hidden-history-under-observational-equivalence to participant-side constitution of stability.

## 1. Error in the previous machine pressure

Pass 19 proposed VSEL-3:

```text
Can two systems have the same observable constraint topology
but differ in SRT vertical selectability because their Selection histories differ?
```

This is a legitimate downstream model-discrimination question, but the author correctly rejects it as the **ontological centre** of the current workline.

Why it was too traditional:

```text
it starts with a third-person stable observation as explanatorily primary;
it then searches behind that observation for a hidden difference;
it therefore risks turning SRT back into a theory of latent state under observational equivalence.
```

That is not the current author direction.

## 2. Corrected explanatory order

The controlling direction is now:

```text
subjectless Selection at the pre-subject layer
-> ontological verticalization / formation of Selection-position
-> formed centres participate in later Selection from their own positions
-> participant-side active Selection constitutes / maintains relations of selectability
-> those relations become cross-time / cross-space / cross-position / cross-subject stable
-> multi-subject order
-> third-person observable stability / regularity as downstream evidence or projection.
```

The key reversal is:

```text
NOT:
observed stability -> infer active selection structure

BUT:
active positioned Selection -> constitutes order -> observed stability is one readout.
```

## 3. First-person does not undo bearerless L0

A potential contradiction must be blocked explicitly.

SRT already fixes that primitive Selection does not require a pre-existing selector. Therefore Pass 19 may not rewrite the full ontology as `first-person chooser -> Selection`.

Current layered reading:

```text
pre-subject / L0 role:
Selection is subjectless.

post-formation role:
Selection-position / One formation makes positioned active participation possible;
subsequent selections can be first-person / position-indexed in the author's current sense.
```

Thus the theory can retain both:

```text
Selection precedes the first completed subject;
and
multi-subject order is actively constituted by selections enacted from formed positions.
```

## 4. Effect on the four-dimensional stability definition

The author's prior definition remains:

```text
more stable Selection
=
cross-time / cross-space / cross-position / cross-subject selectability.
```

But the correction changes its interpretation.

It is not primarily:

```text
a stable objective pattern that survives changes in observer coordinates.
```

It is:

> **the capacity of an actively constituted selectable relation to continue to be enacted, inherited, reopened, supported, inhibited, or transformed by selections from multiple situated positions across those dimensions.**

The relevant invariance is therefore not observer-invariant state description. It is **participant-re-enterable selective organization**.

## 5. Whole > parts is also reinterpreted

Pass 19 correctly rejected the generic slogan `whole > parts because whole constrains parts` as SRT-specific.

The new author correction sharpens what SRT might add:

```text
parts are not merely externally observed components;
formed parts / centres actively Select from their own positions;
the relations created by those selections become conditions for one another's later Selection;
the whole is constituted as this participant-enacted field of selectable relations;
third-person constraint topology is a downstream representation of that field.
```

Therefore a better SRT candidate is:

> **The surplus of the whole lies not merely in additional objective constraints among parts, but in a participant-enacted organization of mutual selectability: each positioned centre helps constitute the conditions under which other centres can continue to Select, and the resulting order is maintained by that ongoing active participation.**

This is stronger than the prior static/history-topology contrast but is still only a comparative candidate.

## 6. Strongest-neighbor consequences

The correction does NOT make Simondon or Barad weak neighbors.

### Simondon

Simondon remains strong because individuation and transindividual formation are not passive third-person aggregation. Collective individuation can be constitutive and processual.

Remaining SRT question:

```text
Does Simondon make active, positioned Selection by formed centres
constitutive of the persistence/stability of multi-subject order,
or does transindividual individuation pay the same burden without Selection-first placement?
```

### Barad

Barad remains strong because agential intra-action is constitutive, not observer-passive, and agency is not a property of pregiven subjects.

Remaining SRT question:

```text
Does Barad supply a participant-positioned first-person/selective-centre layer
whose active re-selection maintains multi-subject order,
or is agency kept at a different non-subject-centred constitutive level?
```

### Participatory sense-making / enactivism

This comparator becomes substantially stronger after the author's correction because it explicitly treats autonomous agents as mutually modulating participants and interaction as enacted rather than merely observed.

Its likely pressure point is now not participation but **genetic depth**:

```text
it starts with autonomous agents / sense-makers;
SRT attempts to derive the possibility of positioned first-person participation
from prior subjectless Selection and vertical structure formation.
```

That is now the most serious comparative boundary to test.
