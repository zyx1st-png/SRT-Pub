# Multiple-One localization / relative verticality pressure

## 1. Root problem

The current PR #931 reconstruction now has a candidate for how one localized vertical process can become a continuing One:

```text
pre-object relational field
-> vertical generation
-> Selection / asymmetric actualisation
-> retained field reconstitution
-> repeated lineage-relative regeneration
-> localized continuing vertical process-unit / One candidate.
```

A new burden follows immediately:

> Why does the field not simply become one increasingly complex global vertical organization / global One?

If the answer is not explicit, `localization` becomes a hidden primitive.

## 2. Author/source constraints already available

Current author-side material supplies four relevant guards.

### 2.1 Before Bearer, Selection sedimentation is in `多`

The author states:

```text
bearer出现以前选择沉积在多。
```

Therefore pre-bearer history must not be retroactively stored inside a completed One merely to explain its later emergence.

### 2.2 Historical effects can mutually support one another

Cycle-2 author direction records:

```text
historical consequences can mutually support one another;
mutual support is coupled to relative suppression of competing continuations.
```

### 2.3 Center and edge are co-generated

The author analogy is relational center-surround / lateral-inhibition-like:

```text
support inside a forming center
+
relative suppression of competing directions
```

with center and edge generated together rather than sequentially.

### 2.4 Relative suppression is not annihilation

Backgrounded / relatively suppressed possibilities remain available as later conditions. Therefore One formation cannot be modeled as a global winner deleting all other future centers.

These constraints already make a global winner-take-all reading unsafe.

## 3. The key distinction — verticality need not be one global total ranking

A hidden assumption in many simple diagrams is:

```text
one field
-> one vertical hierarchy
-> one center
-> one One.
```

That assumption is not licensed.

A stronger machine-side candidate is:

> **Verticality is relation-indexed and can be only partially ordered rather than globally total.**

Meaning:

```text
A relation / continuation may be foregrounded relative to one emerging lineage
while remaining backgrounded, neutral or differently weighted relative to another.
```

Therefore the same wider field can support multiple local non-flat organizations without requiring one universe-wide scalar hierarchy.

This is a C-layer pressure formulation, not a canonical mathematical claim about order theory.

## 4. Localization without spatial locality

`local` must not mean spatially compact.

A candidate local One can be distributed across components or places.

The relevant locality is:

```text
lineage-relative regeneration scope
```

That is, some subset / relational organization of the wider field participates preferentially in regenerating one continuation lineage rather than every lineage equally.

Possible realization-side carriers may include topology, coupling, boundaries, recognition, recurrence, constraints, developmental organization or other mechanisms. None is the ontology-level definition.

## 5. Why multiple centers can form

Current machine synthesis:

```text
Selection consequences are initially distributed across 多 / the wider field.

Repeated Selection
-> leaves heterogeneous history-dependent changes.

Some historical effects
-> mutually support a continuation pattern A
-> relatively suppress A-incompatible continuations.

Other historical effects
-> can mutually support continuation pattern B
-> relatively suppress B-incompatible continuations.

Because suppression is relative rather than annihilative,
and because verticality is relation-indexed rather than a global total ranking,
A and B need not collapse into one center.
```

If each pattern is repeatedly regenerated relative to its own prior vertical history:

```text
V_A(t) -> V_A(t+1) -> ...
V_B(t) -> V_B(t+1) -> ...
```

then multiple One candidates become possible inside one wider field.

## 6. Distinct One does not require isolation

A and B need not be causally independent.

Too strong:

```text
One_A exists only if nothing in B changes A.
```

This would exclude organisms, social relations and nested organization.

A weaker distinction is needed:

> **Two lineage candidates are distinct when their continuation / dissolution conditions are not obligatorily identical, even though they may strongly condition one another.**

Machine-side pressure cases:

```text
A perturbation can alter / dissolve A while B continues;
B can alter A without becoming numerically identical to A;
A and B can share substrate / environment / constraints;
A and B can later form a relation-level vertical organization.
```

This is relative separability, not absolute independence.

## 7. Avoid accidental Bearer collapse

A major guard follows from Cycle-1 PASS3.

If localization were defined as:

```text
historical consequences are already routed back into One_A as its own history,
```

then One formation would already satisfy the core transition toward Bearer, contradicting the author distinction:

```text
before Bearer:
Selection sedimentation -> 多

when historical traces sediment in 一:
一 -> Bearer.
```

Therefore One localization must remain thinner than Bearer.

### 7.1 Thin One localization

Allowed machine candidate:

```text
field-mediated history repeatedly regenerates a local vertical lineage A
relative to A's prior organization.
```

This can occur even if the relevant historical traces remain distributed across the wider field / 多.

### 7.2 Bearer strengthening

Stronger condition:

```text
consequences generated from A
become the own-history of the organization that reconstitutes A's later Selection-position.
```

That is the existing return–reconstitution / `from-where` to `to-where` coupling burden.

Thus:

```text
lineage-specific regeneration
!= own-history / consequence bearing.
```

This distinction is required to keep One upstream of Bearer.

## 8. Candidate multiplicity gate — pressure labels only

For internal testing only:

```text
M1 — RELATIVE VERTICALITY
No single global total ranking is presupposed; foreground/background relations are indexed to concrete relational / continuation organization.

M2 — DIFFERENTIAL REGENERATION
Field history can regenerate different local vertical lineages differently rather than uniformly rewriting the entire field as one unit.

M3 — RELATIVE SEPARABILITY
Two lineages can have non-identical continuation / dissolution conditions without requiring causal isolation.

M4 — NON-ANNIHILATION
Formation of one center does not eliminate all background potential required for other centers to emerge or reopen.
```

If M1-M4 fail, the architecture tends toward a hidden global One or global winner-take-all ontology.

These are not necessary-and-sufficient conditions and are not new canonical variables.

## 9. Relation to Selection-position

Once a local vertical lineage becomes a continuing One candidate:

```text
One_A
= the local vertical process-unit continuing through lineage-relative reconstitution;

Selection-position_A(t)
= the time-local non-flat operative locus from which Selection can proceed for that lineage at t.
```

Multiple Ones therefore imply multiple coexisting Selection-positions without requiring multiple pre-existing selectors at the bootstrap stage.

The positions can overlap, inhibit, support or transform one another.

## 10. Relation to multi-subject order

The multiplicity problem is logically prior to multi-subject order.

```text
one wider field
-> multiple continuing local vertical lineages
-> multiple Ones / positions
-> reciprocal Selection among positions
-> relation-level vertical organization
-> JFS and/or multi-subject order where additional conditions hold.
```

If multiple Ones cannot be explained without presupposing already individuated units, later multi-subject order remains circular.

## 11. Edge cases

### 11.1 Global lockstep

If every local change necessarily changes every candidate lineage identically, then apparent local units may be aspects of one global process rather than distinct Ones.

### 11.2 Shared substrate

Two lineages may share substrate and still be distinct if continuation conditions can diverge. Substrate partition is not the identity criterion.

### 11.3 Nested One

A local One can remain a component / condition of a higher-order One. Nestedness does not require that lower Ones disappear.

### 11.4 Temporary center

A transient local vertical center that does not enter lineage-relative regeneration remains a center-like event / organization, not yet a continuing One.

### 11.5 Strong domination

A dominant lineage may heavily suppress another while the suppressed lineage remains possible. Domination therefore does not automatically collapse two Ones into one; actual lineage continuation must be tested.

## 12. Current strongest synthesis

> **Multiple Ones become possible when Selection-generated verticality is relation-indexed rather than globally total, historical effects differentially regenerate more than one local continuation lineage, and relative suppression does not annihilate alternative lineages. Distinctness requires relative separability of continuation / dissolution conditions, not causal isolation. One formation remains thinner than Bearer because local lineage regeneration need not yet mean that consequences have become the One's own history.**

This is C-layer synthesis only.

## 13. New pressure created by this synthesis

The live question becomes sharper:

> What changes the field-side `多` from merely distributed Selection history into several separately regenerable continuation lineages, without already invoking One-owned history / Bearer closure?

Two candidate answers remain open:

```text
A — differential relational coupling / mutual-support topology is sufficient to localize lineages;

B — an additional non-spatial boundary / addressability relation is required before a lineage is genuinely localized.
```

Do not answer this by definition. A/B require further pressure and author adjudication.
