## 1. Author-owned E2 target; what cannot change to fit an attractive dataset

Current programme and 2026-10-08 author Option A specify **one bounded, model-relative predictive question**:

> Given lawful matched observations, declared structural learning permissions, a comparable data/complexity/computational budget and prespecified held-out tests, does a **named, implementable shared-geometry representation** improve **held-out predictions** of grouping, boundary, transition and related cross-probe organization against **named, capable H1/H2 implementations**?

H1 = implementation/constraint; H2 = latent/representation/model organization; H3 = effective reachability/differentiability **description or consequence**, not a third mutually exclusive model mechanism. Shared geometry may itself be a H2 representation. If named capable H2 accounts for the data, allow **NO LOCAL COMPARATIVE INCREMENT**; do not infer a universal H2 family reduction or SRT ontology conclusion.

**E2 pass: prespecified held-out predictive gain, not compression alone.** At similar predictive adequacy, compression is an optional *separate* score. **E3** prospective intervention causality and **E4** genuinely future recursive reconstitution cannot substitute for E2 and remain independently gated. The old toy / CN-3 experiment remains HOLD even if a task family is later chosen.

### P1–P6 readout meanings in the existing 09-26 E2–E4 candidate (no invented measurements)

| Probe | Existing target | Candidate output needs specifying before model selection |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | pairwise similarity/arrangement | e.g. an ordinal pair judgment or distances with an explicit participant/noise model; not just treating model embedding distance as observed human similarity |

## 2. Four author options (NONE selected; no machine-default experiment)

Each family below is a **task choice**, not a named-model pick. Models are examples of source-native competitors / pressures only; their runnable P1–P6 interfaces are **not** verified.

### A — consequence-driven multidimensional *concept reorganization* and cross-probe transfer

**Task:** use a fixed set of multifeature stimuli in two contexts with different **learned consequence contingencies**, rather than merely announcing a new category. After interaction, collect similarity, grouping, boundary, continuation, anomaly and transfer probes. The existing 09-26 E2–E4 candidate §2–4 proposes this kind of two-world setup, **but it is NOT a frozen runnable experimental protocol**.

**Source-native strongest-neighbor family — classic categorization / multidimensional psychological space (M1 correction):** The earlier version missed the **GCM/ALCOVE/SUSTAIN/Goldstone tradition**, directly relevant to fitting an identification/similarity representation and predicting categorization or perception. These are **prior scientific capabilities**, not evidence of an extra SRT mechanism:

| Named mature work | Source-native explanatory burden | Faithful boundary for option A |
|---|---|---|
| **Nosofsky (1986)**, *Attention, similarity, and the identification-categorization relationship*, J Exp Psychol Gen 115, DOI **[10.1037/0096-3445.115.1.39](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.115.1.39)**, PMID **2937873** | Generalized Context Model (**GCM**) uses an MDS-choice perceptual representation fitted on **identification-confusion data** and attention-weighted exemplar similarity to predict categorization on the same stimuli. **Identification != direct P1 free similarity ratings**; the observed cross-paradigm predictive relation is already mature science | A shared psychological space explaining identification and category responses is **not new**; generalization to consequence-history changes, P4/P5 and true held-out P6 cannot be assumed |
| **Kruschke (1992)**, *ALCOVE: an exemplar-based connectionist model of category learning*, Psychol Rev 99:22–44, DOI **[10.1037/0033-295X.99.1.22](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.99.1.22)**, PMID **1546117** | Error-driven category-feedback learning of **feature-selective attention weights** over exemplars, with learned association/readout weights. One powerful model of category-dependent representational distance and boundary behavior | Feedback-driven attention change is a **strong baseline**, not the same claim as experimentally demonstrating *whole-field consequence topology revision* or P1–P6 causal/recursive recutting |
| **Love, Medin & Gureckis (2004)**, *SUSTAIN: a network model of category learning*, Psychol Rev 111:309–332, DOI **[10.1037/0033-295X.111.2.309](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.111.2.309)**, PMID **15065912** | Adaptive clustering with attention/recruitment under surprise, supporting category learning, inference and task-dependent structure changes; **not merely fixed exemplar weights** | A model that can recruit new clusters must not be handicapped into fixed latent categories or a mere static attention reweighting rival |
| **Goldstone (1994)**, *Influences of categorization on perceptual discrimination*, J Exp Psychol Gen 123:178–200, DOI **[10.1037/0096-3445.123.2.178](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.123.2.178)**, PMID **8014612** | After category learning, same/different discriminability changes: acquired distinctiveness and some acquired equivalence | **Perceptual discrimination sensitivity != identical to P1 similarity report**; nevertheless, category feedback affecting perceived differences is an existing strong P1-adjacent empirical result |

**Mack, Love & Preston (2016)**, *Dynamic updating of hippocampal object representations reflects new conceptual knowledge*, PNAS 113:13203–13208, DOI **10.1073/pnas.1614048113**, PMCID PMC5135299, **explicitly uses SUSTAIN** to model feature-attention/cluster organization across two classification tasks with task-relevant stimulus features. Its fMRI representational-similarity result is **not** direct proof that a single training history predicts a matched P1–P6 behavioral battery or a second revaluation. **A can now name GCM, ALCOVE and SUSTAIN as candidate capable H2 comparators in principle**, but software/parameters/data rights and actual matching task-interface feasibility are **NOT YET CHECKED**. This is a task-family review, **not** a particular model-arm choice.

**Native probe coverage / debt (recalibrated):** The **identification / similarity–classification pairing and attention-weighted category boundaries** already have mature parametric paradigms and model-fit conventions (Nosofsky, ALCOVE, SUSTAIN). **P1-adjacent** identification/perceptual-discrimination judgments, **P2** exemplar-category membership and **P3** category-boundary behavior are therefore **plausible adaptations with existing methodological precedents**, **not six wholly novel measurement inventions**. But P1 *free similarity ratings* are not interchangeable with identification/confusion; Goldstone's sensitivity measure is not identical to P1; and a morph continuum/boundary requires dimension suitability. **P4** conditional transition forecasts, **P5** separately observed subjective anomaly, and especially **P6** an untouched novel *action/affordance* response after learned consequence revaluation are **not automatically present in those classics**. A task-specific observation/readout/likelihood and truly held-out novel combinations remain required; no ready-made source is verified as matching all six. Equal item counts alone do not equal comparable conditional histories or legally matched information.

**Benefit:** strongest match to the original *one learned organization -> several distinct probe predictions* question **and** a mature, well-defined category-learning/MDS experimental and computational tradition. The **P1-adjacent/P2/P3 entry burden is lower than previously stated**; GCM, ALCOVE and SUSTAIN provide **plausible named H2 comparison candidates**, subject to model/task fit, implementation, lawful data access and equal budgets. **Cost / strongest-neighbor absorption:** attention-weighted exemplar similarity, feedback-driven feature reweighting, learned clustering and category-induced perceptual changes already cover much of what a naive "shared space cross-predicts categorization" or "learning changes perceptual distance" success would show. **For any *additional* E2 claim, explicitly state what held-out predictive job exceeds a fairly capable GCM/ALCOVE/SUSTAIN (and relevant latent-task) implementation; if none, report NO LOCAL COMPARATIVE INCREMENT.** Feedback learning alone is not proof of the owner's distinct consequence-topology/recursive target. **Completing the specific two-world P1–P6 battery remains high-cost unless an existing corpus genuinely contains the needed events/actions and untouched holdouts**. Avoid task-label shortcuts, sensory/value confounds and premature CN-3 execution.

### B — relational sequence / graph navigation and structural *remapping*

**Task:** participants or models learn transitions over a graph/relation system, then encounter a changed context or local relation and make sequence predictions and relational-transfer choices. Only extend to perceptual grouping/similarity/morph boundaries if those judgments can be independently and sensibly defined for the same learned relations.

**Source-native neighbor:** Whittington et al. (2020), *The Tolman–Eichenbaum Machine*, Cell 183:1249–1263.e23, DOI **10.1016/j.cell.2020.10.024**, PMCID PMC7707106. TEM models space/relational-memory generalization, including remapping. **TEM code is a CN-2 reference, NOT an MG-1 result or an automatically selected E2 benchmark**; earlier repo audit #1109 found code provenance and static constraints, not task-equivalent reproduction or rights clearance.

**Native probe coverage / debt:** **P4 transition distribution** is close to source-native; relational/generalization aspects of **P6** may be naturally adapted, but **action affordance is not automatically TEM transfer**. P5 may be defined as sequence violation but needs human/behavioral observation. **P1/P2/P3** require substantial new readouts, and *P3 continuous morph boundary* can be particularly unnatural for a discrete graph unless an explicitly valid continuum is added. Without them this is **not the original six-probe cross-probe target**.

**Benefit:** existing mature relational models and identifiable sequence tasks provide concrete feasibility anchors; simple next-step prediction is easier to type. **Cost:** risks selecting the task only because TEM code exists, shifting the owner problem from *whole cross-probe reorganization* to ordinary graph generalization; creating P1–P3 could effectively become a new task/experiment.

### C — rapid instructed *task-set / control-policy switching*

**Task:** teach novel compositional rules, test control allocation/maintained goals under instruction and conflict, and examine transfer to new combinations.

**Source-native neighbor:** Cole, Laurent & Stocco (2013), *Rapid instructed task learning*, Cogn Affect Behav Neurosci 13:1–22, DOI **10.3758/s13415-012-0125-7**, PMCID PMC3557598. This is a reviewed cognitive-control framework, **not itself a fully specified runnable P1–P6 model**. PBWM / BG-PFC are concrete MG-1/MG-2 source-native models, but their Store/Ignore/Recall controls are a **different native task** from TEM and from the present full multi-probe E2 candidate.

**Native probe coverage / debt:** policy/transfer aspects of **P6**, and some rule-conditioned next-action aspects of **P4**, are conceivable; **P1/P2/P3/P5** need nontrivial new perceptual/grouping/boundary/surprise interfaces. A change in instructed rule is not automatically the learned *consequence-induced* geometry rewrite proposed by the original E2 candidate.

**Benefit:** strong control-theory alternatives, accessible task instructions and clear behavioral choices. **Cost:** chiefly addresses existing **MG-2 ACCESS -> CONTROL** and rule-set adaptation rather than CN-2 whole-field grouping/boundary/transition; risk of shifting the CURRENT NEXT and confusing instruction with causal field reorganization.
