# Generativity-first normativity pressure test

## 0. Scope

This audit implements the author adjudication in:

- `01_Source_Intuition/SRT_AUTHOR_ADJUDICATION_GENERATIVITY_POSITION_NORMATIVITY_2026-09-14.md`

It is noncanonical and does not authorize owner edits.

The central reconstruction question is now:

> Can SRT make `better / worse`, responsibility, and eventually `ought` constitutive of correctly typed generative relations without hiding the desired conclusion inside `generativity`, continuation, health, consequence-bearing, or non-outsourcing?

## 1. First separation: Selection-event vs recurrent generative position

A major pressure point is that a genuine Selection event must not require survival or recurrence.

Therefore distinguish:

```text
G-event
= a genuine actualising Selection that generates determinate asymmetry

G-position
= a formed position's capacity to participate in recurrent Selection
  across situations partly transformed by prior Selection
```

A terminal Selection may be a genuine `G-event` even if the selecting position never selects again.

Accordingly:

```text
Selection authenticity -/-> continuation
continuation -/-> Selection authenticity
```

This blocks restoration of the old primitive epsilon theorem through the back door.

However, once the object of evaluation is a **formed continuing position**, its ability to remain a participant in later generative relations can legitimately become position-relative evaluative material.

## 2. Non-evaluative core of recurrent generativity

To avoid defining `good` by another evaluative synonym, recurrent generativity should first be specified structurally.

Candidate decomposition for a formed position X:

### G1 — selective efficacy
X can participate in a non-trivial Selection whose occurrence changes the subsequent generative situation.

### G2 — re-entry
Differences partly generated through X's Selection can alter the conditions under which X subsequently participates in Selection.

### G3 — reorganisation
X can alter its own organisation in response to the transformed situation without merely being replaced by an unrelated Y.

### G4 — renewed selectivity
After reorganisation, a later Selection is still genuinely selectional rather than mere execution of a fully externally fixed trajectory.

These are structural predicates. None contains `good`, `healthy`, `valuable`, `correct`, or `ought`.

`Consequence return`, `reorganisability`, and `non-outsourcing` are therefore candidates for diagnosing G2-G4; they are not primitive value axioms.

## 3. Better/worse-for: revised O1 candidate

A non-circular O1 route is now:

1. establish a formed position X independently of evaluative language;
2. establish one or more structural generative dimensions G1-G4 for X;
3. compare transformations by how they alter X's capacity to instantiate those dimensions;
4. only then apply the indexed evaluative relation.

Candidate form:

```text
better-for(X | G_i, H)
worse-for(X | G_i, H)
```

where `H` is the relevant horizon / perturbation range.

This is not:

```text
more generativity = morally better simpliciter
```

It is:

> relative to a formed generative position X, a transformation can strengthen or weaken the very relations through which X remains a participant in generation.

The evaluative direction is therefore position-relative and relation-indexed.

## 4. Why this is stronger than mere continuation

Counterexample: a rigid institution can persist for centuries while all meaningful Selection is externally fixed or internally frozen.

Then:

```text
continuation: high
G4 renewed selectivity: low
```

So continuation is not sufficient for generativity.

Counterexample: a chaotic process can generate endless novelty but fail to preserve any history-bearing position that can re-enter and reorganise.

Then:

```text
variation: high
G-position: absent or weak
```

So novelty is not sufficient for generativity.

Counterexample: a system can receive feedback but have no capacity to reorganise except by following an externally fixed controller.

Then:

```text
feedback: present
independent renewed selectivity: weak
```

So feedback is not sufficient for generativity.

## 5. The label-swap test, rebuilt

The previous label-swap test should now be tied to generative structure rather than consequence-bearing alone.

Suppose y1 preserves X's capacity for G1-G4 under horizon H and y2 destroys G2-G4 while leaving only externally driven persistence.

If all those independently specified facts remain fixed, calling y2 `better-for X qua recurrent generative position` would require changing what counts as X's recurrent generative constitution.

Thus the evaluative direction is not freely swappable once the position and generative relation are fixed.

This establishes only an indexed O1 relation. It does not establish global moral priority.

## 6. Major objection: why privilege generativity at all?

This is the strongest anti-smuggling objection.

If SRT merely says:

> generativity is good because SRT values generation,

then the reconstruction fails.

The stronger answer is constitutive rather than axiological:

> X is being evaluated **as a recurrent generative position**. A transformation that eliminates the relations constituting X as such is internally negative with respect to that typed standing, just as a transformation that removes the relevant standing removes the basis of the indexed evaluation itself.

This yields:

```text
constitutive better/worse-for
```

not:

```text
universal goodness of maximum generation
```

The latter remains unproved.

## 7. Pathological generators do not refute O1

A tumour, coercive institution, exploitative firm, or predatory agent may genuinely have transformations that are better-for its own recurrent generativity.

This is not a defect in O1. It demonstrates that O1 is position-relative rather than globally moral.

Therefore:

```text
better-for X
-/->
better for Y

better-for X
-/->
better for a containing system

better-for X
-/->
morally better overall
```

A mature normative theory therefore requires relations among positions and scales, not only local O1.

## 8. Whole-as-position programme

The author has identified a future direction in which a sufficiently justified whole may itself become an evaluative position or scale.

This could potentially explain why a locally generative structure can be globally destructive.

But the whole must not be smuggled in as:

- a cosmic chooser;
- a hidden organism;
- a teleological subject;
- a moral super-agent;
- an automatically privileged scale.

Any whole-position construction must independently establish what makes the relevant whole a genuine generative unity / position rather than an observer-chosen aggregation.

Status:

```text
whole-relative O1: OPEN RESEARCH PROGRAMME
```

## 9. Outsourcing clarified

External influence is not itself outsourcing. All Selection is relationally situated.

`Outsourcing` should be reserved for cases in which a position retains nominal attribution as a Selection-position while the effective formation of Selection is persistently transferred elsewhere such that its own G1-G4 structure is hollowed out.

Therefore:

```text
being influenced != outsourcing
using tools != outsourcing
coordination != outsourcing
```

The diagnostic question is whether the position remains a genuine locus in the generative relation.

## 10. Responsibility: revised O2-R candidate

Responsibility should be reconstructed from the generative relation rather than from suffering or burden alone.

A candidate structure is:

```text
X participates as an attributable source in Selection
    +
that Selection helps transform the shared generative situation
    +
X has sufficient continuity / uptake / reorganisation for the transformed situation
  to become relevant to its subsequent Selection
    =>
answerability attaches to X's generative participation
```

The core is not punishment. It is closure between generative authorship and subsequent participation in the generated reality.

If X claims authorship/control while systematically excluding the generated relation from any later answerability, X is trying to retain the source-role while deleting part of the generative relation that made the source-role consequential.

This is a candidate constitutive basis for answerability.

It still does not establish blameworthiness or a unique required action.

## 11. Ought is not correct-option retrieval

Selection is not search over a pregiven answer key.

Therefore an SRT `ought` cannot begin as:

```text
given menu {A,B,C}, identify the objectively correct B
```

The prior candidate is instead a constitutive norm on generative Selection itself:

> do not systematically destroy, hollow out, or falsely externalise the generative relations required for the kind of Selection-position whose authority / agency is being claimed.

This yields a first candidate for **generative ought**.

But it remains typed and role-relative. It does not yet settle conflicts among positions, scales, horizons, or mutually incompatible generative futures.

## 12. Hume-gap reconstruction from B1

The generativity-first reconstruction changes the target of the Hume problem.

A standard neutral-fact picture can be represented as:

```text
completed fact F
+
external evaluative premise V
->
normative conclusion N
```

SRT instead proposes that a determinate fact-state is often only a cross-section of a thicker generative relation:

```text
Selection-generated differentiation
-> formed positions
-> later Selective relations partly conditioned by what prior Selection generated
```

The arrows denote dependency / constitution, not assumed primitive time.

Once a formed position exists, transformations are not neutral with respect to every description of that position. Some transformations preserve or enlarge the constitutive relations through which it remains a recurrent generative position; others remove or hollow out those relations.

Thus O1 does not have to be imported as a second substance called `value`. It can arise as an indexed ordering internal to a correctly typed generative standing.

At stronger levels, answerability can arise when a position is both attributable participant in generation and later participant in the reality so generated.

The resulting dissolution thesis is therefore:

> Hume's logical warning remains valid against deriving a completed deontic conclusion from premises deliberately typed as normatively neutral. SRT's challenge is that such neutral typing may already have projected away constitutive generative and positional relations from which weaker evaluative and answerability dimensions arise.

This is a metaphysical / type-level dissolution, not a formal derivation of morality from neutral propositions.

3. Is recurrent generativity a new owner-level construct, or only a cross-owner relation?
4. Can formed-position-relative continuation preference be stated without contradicting the rejection of strong primitive epsilon?
5. What independently qualifies a `whole` as an evaluative position or scale?
6. What additional relation is needed to move from local O1 to cross-position normative conflict resolution?
7. Can `generative ought` remain non-circular when agency claims, authority, and responsibility are added?
