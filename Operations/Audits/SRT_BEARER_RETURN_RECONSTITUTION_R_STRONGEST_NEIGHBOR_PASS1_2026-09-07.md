## 1. Common problem without SRT vocabulary

The common problem can be stated as:

> **How should a continuing agent or autonomous organization be individuated when its concrete components and control roles can change, the consequences of its activity feed back into its organization, and later action selection is generated from a history-sensitive internal organization?**

A fair account must distinguish at least:

```text
continued performance
continued self-maintenance
continued organization
history-bearing consequence attribution
and the organization that generates later action selection
```

without assuming in advance that these are the same unit.

This problem is not proprietary to SRT.

---

## 2. Strongest single mature neighbor — Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde (2009)

Primary source:

- Xabier E. Barandiaran, Ezequiel Di Paolo & Marieke Rohde (2009), “Defining Agency: Individuality, Normativity, Asymmetry, and Spatio-temporality in Action,” *Adaptive Behavior* 17(5):367–386. DOI `10.1177/1059712309343819`.

Why this is the strongest single neighbor for the present problem:

The paper does not merely say that organisms persist. It provides a **generative, explicitly non-circular** account in which an agent is an autonomous organization that adaptively regulates its coupling with the environment according to viability conditions.

Its source-native structure includes:

```text
individuality:
S is distinguished as a network of interdependent processes;
component processes depend on and enable other processes in the network;

adaptive modulation:
S alters constraints governing its coupling with E;

normative consequence:
the coupling change contributes to maintenance of processes constituting S;

boundary pressure:
what belongs to S is justified by constitutive interdependence,
not by an analyst's convenient box;

temporality:
a frozen state is insufficient; organization exists through process dependencies
and adaptive modulation unfolding over time.
```

This is a much stronger comparator than bare functionalism for the #921 problem because it already combines:

```text
higher-order unity
+ dynamic organization
+ adaptive change
+ consequence-to-maintenance feedback
+ an intrinsic individuality criterion
```

### 2.1 What Barandiaran et al. immediately absorb

The following cannot be claimed as an SRT increment by themselves:

```text
changing components can belong to one continuing organization;
organization can define its own relevant boundary;
activity consequences can feed back into self-maintenance;
future regulation can depend on internal adaptive organization;
agent/environment coupling can be actively modulated;
continuity is processual rather than snapshot identity.
```

Therefore the weak reading:

```text
R-A:
q_t changes O_t and thereby influences later P_(t+1)
```

is **strongly absorbed** as ordinary autonomous/adaptive organization unless an additional case-partitioning relation is shown.

## 3. Supporting strongest-neighbor pressure — organisational closure of constraints

Primary anchors:

- Matteo Mossio & Alvaro Moreno (2010), “Organisational closure in biological organisms,” *History and Philosophy of the Life Sciences* 32:269–288.
- Maël Montévil & Matteo Mossio (2015), “Biological organisation as closure of constraints,” *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 372:179–191. DOI `10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.02.029`.

Source-native pressure from the closure-of-constraints programme:

```text
processes and constraints are distinguished causal regimes;
biological organization realizes mutual dependence among constraints;
constraints depend on and contribute to maintaining one another;
closure can be used to pressure / mark system boundaries;
self-determination concerns organization contributing to the maintenance
of its own conditions of existence through effects of its activity.
```

This sharply pressures any claim of the form:

```text
past consequences alter the organization that enables its continuation
```

because reciprocal maintenance and effect-mediated self-determination are already central to organisational closure.

### 3.1 Consequence for `R`

`R` cannot survive merely as:

```text
feedback into the same organization
or
one internal constraint changes another internal constraint
or
activity affects conditions of later organization
```

Those shapes are neighbor-paid.

If `R` is to remain live, its burden must concern the **specific relation between consequence ownership and reconstitution of the later selective position**, not closure in general.

## 4. Generic-mechanism pressure — adaptive coevolutionary networks

Primary source:

- Thilo Gross & Bernd Blasius (2008), “Adaptive coevolutionary networks: a review,” *Journal of the Royal Society Interface* 5:259–271. DOI `10.1098/rsif.2007.1229`.

The source explicitly reviews systems in which:

```text
node state dynamics
<->
network topology dynamics
```

co-evolve and can generate robust topological self-organization from simple local rules.

This means the following are generic-mechanism territory:

```text
history-shaped topology;
controller / role migration;
feedback-dependent future organization;
self-organized differentiation;
state -> structure -> later-state recursion.
```

So even a successful demonstration that:

```text
q_t -> O_t -> P_(t+1)
```

is not enough. Without an independent unit / attribution consequence, that arrow can be ordinary recurrent credit assignment, adaptation, or network coevolution.

## 6. Why `V1 C1 R0` vs `V1 C1 R1` is useful but still dangerous

The contrast is conceptually cleaner than `V0C1` vs `V1C1` because it holds V and C fixed.

But a naive realization can collapse into:

```text
R0 = system does not learn from consequence
R1 = system learns from consequence
```

That would be too weak. Learning / plasticity / adaptive control are not bearer criteria.

A valid future pair must instead match, as far as possible:

```text
autonomy / self-maintenance
adaptive competence
current policy performance
V-like selective stratification
C-like consequence bearing
```

and vary only whether the candidate's **own borne consequences are non-outsourcably load-bearing in the reconstitution of its later Selection-position**.

Even then, the strongest-neighbor advocate must be allowed to say:

> the supposedly external update service or consequence sink is actually part of the autonomous unit.

If that boundary reselection dissolves the pair, the SRT residual weakens.

## 7. Three increasingly strong readings of `R`

### R-A — ordinary causal update

```text
borne consequence q_t
-> affects later P_(t+1)
```

Verdict:

```text
TOO WEAK / ABSORBED
```

Reason: ordinary adaptive regulation, recurrent learning and coevolutionary dynamics already supply this architecture.

### R-B — non-outsourcable consequence-to-reconstitution dependence

```text
q_t counts as borne by O_t;
q_t is non-outsourcably load-bearing in how O_t reconstitutes P_(t+1);
restoring / reloading the same apparent P while routing q_t's cost/history elsewhere
fails to preserve the same candidate continuation relation.
```

Verdict:

```text
LIVE AUTHOR-GATE CANDIDATE
```

This is stronger than generic learning because it ties the update to **consequence ownership / unit attribution**, but PH-IND06 and autonomy-boundary pressure may still absorb it.

### R-C — reciprocal attribution / reconstitution closure

```text
P_t helps determine which consequences accrue as O's own consequences;
those consequences alter O_t;
altered O_t reconstitutes P_(t+1);
therefore from-where and to-where mutually stabilize one another across time.
```

Verdict:

```text
DEEPER CANDIDATE / HIGH HIDDEN-PREMISE RISK
```

Potential gain: this better expresses bearer genesis as a reciprocal relation rather than one-way state update.

Risks:

```text
may simply rename organisational closure;
may smuggle in the bearer boundary through "own consequences";
may be circular if P is already used to identify O and O is used to identify P;
not author-confirmed;
not needed merely to preserve the accepted B+C reading.
```

## 8. Current common-problem extraction

After fair neighbor adaptation, the remaining problem is narrower than “how can a system maintain itself?” or “how can history affect later behavior?”

It is:

> **When a continuing autonomous organization changes over time, what makes the consequences of earlier action belong to the same continuing unit that generates later action selection, rather than to a replaceable service, an external controller, or a broader coupled system?**

This is where SRT's existing pieces can be brought together without claiming that any ingredient is new:

```text
PH-IND06:
non-outsourcing / same-unit consequence pressure

#921 V burden:
reconstitutable stratified Selection-position

#921 R question:
do the borne consequences make a difference to that reconstitution?
```

The possible contribution is a **typed relation among already-known burdens**, not a new mechanism.
