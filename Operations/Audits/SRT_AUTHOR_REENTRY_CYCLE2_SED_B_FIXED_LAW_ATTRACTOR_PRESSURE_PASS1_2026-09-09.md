## 1. Mature pressure family

### 1.1 Amari neural fields

Primary source:

- Shun-ichi Amari (1977), “Dynamics of Pattern Formation in Lateral-Inhibition Type Neural Fields,” *Biological Cybernetics* 27(2):77–87. DOI `10.1007/BF00337259`.

Source-native pressure:

```text
homogeneous lateral-inhibition field
+ fixed mutual connection law
-> localized excitation pattern
-> localized pattern can persist after the eliciting stimulus vanishes
```

The relevant point is not that the source proves endogenous SRT position formation. It shows that a persistent localized asymmetry can be sustained by current fixed-law field dynamics without first requiring learned / plastic rewrite of the connection law.

### 1.2 Continuous-attractor family

Mature review:

- Mikail Khona & Ila R. Fiete (2022), “Attractor and integrator networks in the brain,” *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 23:744–766. DOI `10.1038/s41583-022-00642-0`.

Supporting fixed-law / symmetry literature includes translationally symmetric neural fields that support a continuum of stationary bump solutions, including bumps in the absence of external input. The important structural feature is:

```text
fixed translation-invariant interaction law
-> multiple equivalent possible localized states
-> symmetry breaking selects one localized state
-> localized state persists / becomes the current causal locus
```

No claim is made here that the underlying units, field boundary or interaction law themselves emerge.

## 2. Why SED-B upgrades this family from semantic pressure to live counterexample candidate

Before SED-B, the following move was available:

```text
persistent bump
= persistent foreground/background asymmetry
= sedimentation
```

The author ruling now blocks that equivalence.

Under SED-B, the question becomes:

> Did prior differentiation leave a retained history-indexed change in the conditions governing later transition / reconstitution, beyond the fact that the network is currently occupying a localized asymmetric state?

For an ideal fixed-law attractor with:

```text
unchanged recurrent weights / kernel
unchanged thresholds
unchanged boundary conditions
no slow plasticity variable
no history-conditioned gating parameter
```

and only:

```text
current bump occupancy
```

carrying the localized asymmetry, the architecture is a prima facie candidate for:

```text
not S_H*(SED-B)
```

This is now a substantive rather than terminological pressure.

## 3. The candidate is not yet a successful counterexample

The remaining burden is `P*_endo`.

A localized persistent activity state is not automatically a Selection-position.

To qualify for the audit target, the candidate must satisfy at least:

```text
P1 finite locus:
   a bounded causal locus is identifiable after symmetry breaking;

P2 endogenous locus formation:
   the particular locus is not hard-coded as the only possible locus;
   it emerges from dynamics supporting multiple equivalent loci;

P3 recurrent causal origin:
   later selection-like transition / competition events
   repeatedly issue relative to that locus;

P4 re-identification:
   after perturbation or component-level variation,
   a causally equivalent locus can be re-identified
   without defining identity by SED-B itself.
```

Attractor literature robustly pressures P1/P2.

P3/P4 are not yet paid for an SRT-fair Selection-position interpretation.

Current verdict:

```text
not S_H*(SED-B): PLAUSIBLE IN IDEAL FIXED-LAW CASE
P*_endo: PARTIAL / NOT YET PAID
full counterexample: NOT YET ESTABLISHED
```

## 4. Strongest fair version of X2-A

The next fair candidate should remove avoidable loopholes.

### Architecture lock

```text
A. fixed translation-invariant recurrent law
B. multiple equivalent possible localized loci
C. no learning during the relevant formation / reconstitution interval
D. no slow hidden variable allowed to encode formation history
E. no externally fixed unique target location
F. perturbation / small noise may break symmetry but does not prescribe
   a unique locus in advance
```

### Formation requirement

```text
initial macrostate:
  no localized locus selected

then:
  one localized locus emerges dynamically

later:
  the localized locus persists and serves as the causal reference
  for a declared family of selection-like transitions
```

### SED-B exclusion requirement

After formation, audit all allowed state variables and parameters.

The candidate counts as `not S_H*` only if:

```text
formation history has not altered the later transition law,
threshold structure, boundary conditions, gating, coupling,
or another retained causal condition
beyond instantaneous / current attractor occupancy.
```

If an adaptation variable, refractoriness gradient, plastic trace, hysteretic threshold or other slow variable carries the formation history and changes later reconstitution, the candidate fails `not S_H*`.

## 5. Why spontaneous-bump variants are stronger than input-pinned bumps

An input-pinned bump leaves an easy objection:

```text
external input selected the locus;
therefore the relevant location may have been supplied rather than formed.
```

The stronger pressure uses translation / permutation symmetry and spontaneous symmetry breaking:

```text
no unique locus is privileged in the fixed law;
small fluctuations / instability select one among equivalent loci;
current localized organisation then persists.
```

This better matches Scope B because the particular higher-level locus is dynamically generated rather than preassigned.

But the audit must still distinguish:

```text
endogenous formation of the higher-level locus
from
endogenous formation of the underlying units / system boundary.
```

The former is the present pressure target. The latter remains outside what standard neural-field models pay.

It would not by itself refute SRT ontology globally. It would refute the strengthened structural-necessity claim at the tested target / scale.
