Civilizational false-convergence Pass 1 retained two M2 candidates:

~~~text

X4 present action reconstructs future option conditions
~~~

Before adding more supportive cases, both candidates are pressure-tested against mature source-native theory.

~~~text

X4 AS CURRENTLY PHRASED = TOO BROAD / SPLIT REQUIRED

source-pressure outcome = PRODUCTIVE REVISION

~~~

Neither candidate is promoted to M3.

## 2. X3 under Ostrom / institutional-analysis pressure

### 2.1 What mature institutional theory already owns

Ostrom's programme explains common-pool governance through configurations including:

- boundary / allocation rules;
- resource and community conditions;
- communication;
- monitoring;
- local sanctions;
- collective-choice / revision possibilities;
- polycentric institutional arrangements.

The Nepal irrigation evidence is especially important because written or formal institutional form is not the decisive variable.

Rules-in-use and participant practices matter.

### 2.2 GRG failure if unchanged

If X3 remains:

~~~text
externalized expectation
-> coordination
~~~

then any rulebook, norm, standard or policy document could be treated as causal merely because it states a future pattern.

That fails the framework's anti-tautology and anti-objectification guards.

### 2.3 Revision

Split X3:

~~~text
X3a — articulated / encoded generative expectation

A future relation is made explicit in a rule, protocol, norm,
schedule, model, plan or shared representation.

Burden:
representation / articulation only.

X3b — operative expectation regime

The expectation is materially / institutionally instantiated
such that it changes participant action possibilities through one
or more specified mechanisms:
interface compatibility,
monitoring,
sanction,
incentive,
organizational process,
social norm,
material infrastructure,
or mixed provenance.

Burden:
articulation or shared expectation
+ operative embodiment
+ changed action situation / accessibility.
~~~

Dependency:

~~~text
X3a does not entail X3b.
X3b need not always require a formal written X3a;
shared rules-in-use may be partly tacit.
~~~

This second clause is required by institutional evidence.

## 4. X4 under niche-construction pressure

### 4.1 What mature evolutionary theory already owns

Niche-construction literature distinguishes:

~~~text
organism modifies environment
-> environmental change affects selection pressures
-> stronger claim: evolutionary response follows
~~~

Ecological inheritance adds persistence of organism-modified environmental states into conditions encountered by descendants.

Important source-fidelity guard:

The status and explanatory importance of niche construction as a distinct evolutionary process is contested within evolutionary theory. GRG uses the explicit causal decomposition above as pressure, not as an adjudication of that broader debate.

### 4.2 GRG failure if unchanged

If X4 remains:

~~~text
present action changes future options
~~~

then almost every temporally extended causal effect qualifies.

This has insufficient exclusion power.

### 4.3 Revision

Split X4:

~~~text
X4a — reconstructed environmental / option condition

Current activity materially changes later environmental,
institutional or action conditions.

X4b — recursive selective / viability feedback

The reconstructed condition changes specified selection,
viability, accessibility or transition pressures on later
organisms / agents / positions.

X4c — inherited reconstructed condition

The reconstructed condition persists, is maintained or is
reproduced across a later cycle / generation such that later
participants inherit a changed generative field.
~~~

Guards:

~~~text
X4a != X4b
X4b != X4c
long-term consequence != recursive selective feedback
persistence != inheritance automatically
ecosystem engineering != evolution by niche construction automatically
~~~

## 6. Cross-domain gain after mature-source pressure

The source pressure does not make GRG more original by subtraction.

It makes GRG more disciplined.

### X3 possible cross-domain role

Not:

~~~text

~~~

But:

~~~text

into an operative constraint on later participant possibilities,
with explicit embodiment / enforcement provenance.
~~~

### X4 possible cross-domain role

~~~text

~~~

But:

~~~text

when that field feeds back on viability / selection / accessibility,
and whether the reconstructed condition becomes inherited across cycles.
~~~

These are still candidate grammar burdens.

Mature domain theories retain mechanism ownership.

## 8. Normativity consequence

Ostrom and niche-construction pressure jointly reinforce:

~~~text
operative / persistent
!= legitimate / good
~~~

A rule regime may be causally effective and unjust.

A niche-constructing activity may be evolutionarily successful and harmful to other positions.

Therefore generative expectation remains a source line for indexed normativity, not a moral shortcut.
