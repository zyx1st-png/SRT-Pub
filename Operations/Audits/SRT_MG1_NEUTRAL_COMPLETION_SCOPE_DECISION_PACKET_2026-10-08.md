## 1. Recover the actual MG-1 owner burden before specifying a finish line

Programme §14 labels MG-1 **"multilevel gating composition / strongest-neighbor map."** It asks for comparison across eight mature mechanisms and five specific questions **before freezing a new experiment**. Its current text does **not** say all eight must be reproduced in code, must beat one another in a P1–P6 task, or that proving SRT novelty is a condition of completing this **mapping task**. The 09-26 author source §10 frames the broader live mechanism question as:

> How do gates distributed across levels become coordinated into one effective action / objectification field, and how can a coarse conscious intention reconfigure that field without specifying the detailed execution?

That is a **research question**, not an already-solved mechanism identity. The 09-26 MG crosswalk §11–13 specifies the controlling **C2'** interpretation: `foreground_obj` target may recruit/reweight already-formed gates; **gate objectification under consequential unresolved conflict is a different route**. It does not prove `foreground_phen`, `manifest_OF`, Bearer ownership, or unique SRT causation. An **audit completion** cannot be mistaken for having solved all those substantive questions.

## 3. Conflict control and conscious conflict experience — genuine contested comparison

An MG-1 answer must not imply either exclusive direction:

~~~text
conflict detected -> control adjusted
  therefore: conscious gate-objectification NEVER matters

or:
conflict experienced -> subsequent adaptation
  therefore: all control adjustment REQUIRES conscious gate-objectification
~~~

**Original computational/normative neighbors and instructed control (not consciousness experiments by themselves):**

- **Botvinick et al. (2001),** *Conflict Monitoring and Cognitive Control*, Psychological Review 108:624–652, DOI [10.1037/0033-295X.108.3.624](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.108.3.624), PMID 11488380. The two computational modeling studies articulate conflict monitoring and simulate a conflict-to-control feedback loop. **A model omitting an explicit awareness variable does not empirically prove the modeled human control episodes were unconscious.**
- **Shenhav, Botvinick & Cohen (2013),** *The Expected Value of Control*, Neuron 79:217–240, DOI [10.1016/j.neuron.2013.07.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.07.007), PMCID PMC3767969. Explains control allocation in terms of estimated benefits/costs; **normative framework != automatically instantiated equal-task P1–P6 comparator**.
- **Cole, Laurent & Stocco (2013),** *Rapid Instructed Task Learning*, Cogn Affect Behav Neurosci 13:1–22, DOI [10.3758/s13415-012-0125-7](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13415-012-0125-7), PMCID PMC3557598. Instruction-to-task-set reconfiguration pressures claims that Bearer ownership or SRT-specific gate-objectification is necessary for basic effective CONTROL.

**Evidence involving awareness or subjective conflict, and an importantly different unconscious-control paradigm:**

| Citation and source-native paradigm | Source-level observed claim | Scope guard for Q4 / C2' |
|---|---|---|
| **Kunde (2003)**, *Sequential modulations of stimulus–response correspondence effects depend on awareness of response conflict*, Psychon Bull Rev 10:198–205, DOI [10.3758/BF03196485](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03196485), PMID 12747508. **Metacontrast masked/unmasked prime–target correspondence**, related to Eriksen/Simon sequential effects | Subsequent sequential modulation of prime-induced interference appeared when the prior conflicting prime was **consciously experienced**, not when fully masked, within those experiments | Evidence for **awareness-dependent trial-to-trial adaptation in this specific masked-priming design**; does not show that all kinds of control adaptation require conscious gate-objectification |
| **Desender, Van Opstal & Van den Bussche (2014)**, *Feeling the Conflict*, Psychological Science 25:675–683, DOI [10.1177/0956797613511468](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613511468), PMID 24395737. **Masked priming with reported subjective conflict**, tracked conflict-adaptation/error effects | Their observed adaptation followed **reported experience of conflict** even where that diverged from objective conflict under their task conditions | Experience/report of conflict **is not a validated SRT Gate objectification or phenomenality admission measure**; association need not identify causal necessity of consciousness |
| **van Gaal, Ridderinkhof, Scholte & Lamme (2010)**, *Unconscious Activation of the Prefrontal No-Go Network*, J Neurosci 30:4143–4150, DOI [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2992-09.2010](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2992-09.2010), PMCID PMC6632275. **Strongly/weakly masked go/no-go with fMRI** | Unconscious no-go cues can activate prefrontal inhibitory-control-related networks | **Inhibitory control under masking is not the same dependent variable as next-trial conflict adaptation** in Kunde/Desender; cannot settle their disagreement by treating tasks as identical |

**Active source-level rebuttal to a one-sided awareness reading:** Abrahamse & Braem (2015), *Experience a conflict—either consciously or not*, Front Psychol 6:179, DOI [10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00179](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00179), PMCID PMC4333772, explicitly argues that **a stronger latent conflict signal could independently increase both subjective conflict reports and later adaptation**, meaning Desender 2014's association does **not** alone establish a causal requirement for conscious experience. This is a **published commentary/alternative interpretation**, not proof that the original effects vanish or that the contrary model is established.

**Verdict for MG-1:** the necessity of awareness for particular kinds of conflict-triggered adjustment is **task- and measure-dependent and empirically/interpretively contested**. Keep separate: **(1) online response inhibition; (2) trial-to-trial adaptive control; (3) experienced/reported conflict; (4) reflective objectification and explicit revision of the constraint itself**. No publication above equates (3) with (4) or proves the current SRT C2' as a distinct mechanism. A fair MG-1 audit should inventory both evidential directions and the strongest alternative interpretations, allowing **OPEN / not identified** outcomes.

### Option A — define now a bounded source-comparison completion scope

**Choice content:** MG-1's **neutral review** may, after independent review and **explicit author completion declaration**, close once it faithfully covers all eight owner-listed families and Q1–Q5; identifies actual mechanism/native-task scope, both sides of conflict-awareness evidence (§3), strongest competitors, OPEN/NO-GAIN findings and evidential limitations; and keeps E2/CN-3 gates distinct. Not all eight models need to be reproduced and **no SRT novelty win is required merely to finish a literature review**.

- **Benefit:** clear, reviewable stopping point now; preserves a neutral scientific literature map and stops recurring requests to expand it without a concrete missing source.
- **Cost / risk:** a closed **review** could be misread as a validated mechanism. If Q5 is **NO-GAIN / no distinct prediction target**, then **the current CN-3 experimental rationale is not established by MG-1**: before any CN-3 proposal, separately reassess whether there is a defensible **neutral** test target and what it discriminates; if none, do not proceed on the empty premise. Existing CN-3 HOLD still applies regardless.

### Option B — require a concrete mechanism test before any global MG-1 completion

**Choice content:** keep **MG-1 overall** unresolved until the author specifies and independent review scrutinizes an actual same-target computational/empirical discrimination between the proposed coordination/revision account and a capability-strong mature control mechanism. Source comparison can be documented as a subdeliverable, but is not declared the whole MG-1 completion.

- **Benefit:** avoids treating an evidence map as completion of a substantive mechanistic question; demands an articulated potential test and a comparison that could fail.
- **Cost / risk:** blurs MG-1's *pre-experiment mapping* role with already separate **CN-2 E2 and CN-3 experimental** burdens; risks prolonged open status or circular blocking of the very experiment intended to resolve it. Must not create an additional executable gate or move CN-3 conditions without an explicit author decision.

### Option C — defer any MG-1 completion-line decision until CN-3 is actually being prepared

**Choice content:** leave the existing owner §14 MG-1 duties and HOLD unchanged. Use the current packet as **provisional issue/evidence inventory**, prioritize the **already-OPEN first CN-2 E2 task-family and P1–P6 readout decision** when appropriate, and revisit MG-1 completion meaning **only when a concrete CN-3 preparation request makes it operationally necessary**.

- **Benefit:** minimizes premature governance work and avoids freezing a completion definition for an inactive experiment line; preserves current research attention for identifying a lawful same-target E2 question.
- **Cost / risk:** no early explicit MG-1 closure rule; the same ambiguity may recur when CN-3 is later proposed, requiring review and author attention then. Deferral is **not MG-1 completion** or a waiver of any MG-1 owner duty.

**Why decide now versus later?** Now (A or B) gives consistent future agent/reviewer terminology and a known stopping point but spends author and review attention before the relevant experiment design exists. Later (C) keeps governance proportionate to actual activity but preserves completion ambiguity until CN-3 preparation. These were **genuine trade-offs at the time of presentation**. The author later **explicitly chose A**; B/C remain unselected historical alternatives, not new live requirements.
