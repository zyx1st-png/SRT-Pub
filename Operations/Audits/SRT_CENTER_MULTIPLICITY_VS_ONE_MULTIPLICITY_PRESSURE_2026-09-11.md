# Center multiplicity != One multiplicity

## 1. Why the previous A/B fork is still too coarse

The previous localization audit left:

```text
A — differential relational coupling / mutual-support topology is sufficient to localize lineages;

B — an additional non-spatial boundary / addressability relation is required.
```

Turing / activator–inhibitor pressure shows that this framing is too coarse.

A mature field mechanism can already generate:

```text
multiple peaks / centers;
local enhancement + surrounding inhibition;
endogenous positions;
pattern regeneration after perturbation;
slower field feedback affecting future organizer formation.
```

Therefore:

```text
multiple local centers
!= multiple Ones.
```

A field can contain many patterned maxima while still constituting one global pattern / one globally coupled history.

## 2. New three-step distinction

The localization problem should be decomposed into:

```text
L1 — CENTER FORMATION
A local vertical center / position-like asymmetry forms.

L2 — LINEAGE REGENERATION
A later local vertical organization is regenerated relative to the prior history of that local organization.

L3 — DIFFERENTIAL CONTINUATION
Two candidate lineages can diverge in continuation / transformation / dissolution without every such change being obligatorily the same change of one global lineage.
```

Only L1 is strongly paid by ordinary pattern formation.

L2 is the current One-lineage candidate.

L3 is the new multiplicity burden.

These are machine pressure labels only.

## 3. What L3 does and does not require

L3 does **not** require causal isolation.

Too strong:

```text
A and B are distinct Ones only if they do not influence one another.
```

Instead:

> **A and B are distinct One candidates if their continuation relations can become non-identical under some admissible history, even while they remain coupled.**

Examples of pressure-compatible shapes:

```text
A can be altered while B persists;
A can dissolve while B continues;
A and B can merge, but merger is then a change in both lineages rather than proof that they were always one;
A can constrain B without B becoming merely a state of A;
A and B can later participate in a higher-order relation-level One.
```

## 4. Why this is not Bearer yet

L3 concerns the separability of continuation relations.

Bearer concerns a stronger relation:

```text
consequences generated from A
-> become load-bearing own-history of the organization
-> alter reconstitution of A's later Selection-position.
```

Therefore:

```text
continuation separability
!= consequence ownership
!= own-history closure.
```

This preserves the author ruling:

```text
before Bearer:
Selection sedimentation -> 多

when historical traces sediment in 一:
一 -> Bearer.
```

A pre-bearer One can therefore have a distinguishable continuation lineage even if the relevant historical support remains distributed across the wider field.

## 5. Turing pattern as the critical negative control

Consider a multi-peak reaction–diffusion pattern.

```text
many peaks: YES
local center/surround relations: YES
regeneration: YES
```

But ask:

```text
Does peak A have a continuation relation that can diverge from peak B's,
or are A and B merely mutually constrained phases / positions of one globally organized pattern?
```

If every perturbation to A is resolved only by global pattern re-spacing such that no lineage-relative distinction survives, then:

```text
center multiplicity: YES
One multiplicity: NOT ESTABLISHED.
```

If distinct local histories can be regenerated, transformed or terminated differentially, then multiple-One pressure becomes stronger.

This is not a verdict on Turing patterns in general; it is a discrimination template.

## 6. Relative verticality becomes more precise

The previous audit proposed that verticality may be relation-indexed / partially ordered rather than one global total hierarchy.

L3 sharpens why this matters.

For multiple Ones, the field must permit at least:

```text
V_A has a continuation ranking / foreground-background organization relative to lineage A;
V_B has a continuation ranking / foreground-background organization relative to lineage B;
```

without requiring:

```text
ranking_A = ranking_B = one global ranking.
```

The same potential relation may therefore be:

```text
foreground / continuation-supporting for A;
background / irrelevant / threatening for B;
```

or vice versa.

This indexed plurality of vertical organization is a machine-side interpretation, not a new formal theorem.

## 7. One boundary as a consequence of differential continuation

Do not posit a boundary first.

A better order is:

```text
center formation
-> lineage regeneration
-> differential continuation becomes possible
-> a lineage-relative continuation boundary becomes detectable.
```

That boundary marks:

```text
changes that are changes of A
vs
changes that terminate / replace / merge / split A.
```

Thus the non-spatial boundary is better treated as an emergent consequence of differential continuation than as an independent extra primitive.

This demotes the previous B wording `additional boundary required`.

## 8. New preferred candidate

The previous A/B fork is replaced by a three-part machine candidate:

```text
C1 — differential coupling / mutual support generates local vertical centers;

C2 — repeated Selection-mediated reconstitution turns some centers into lineage-bearing local vertical processes;

C3 — multiple Ones exist when more than one such lineage acquires relatively separable continuation / dissolution conditions.
```

No additional boundary primitive is required in the candidate. Boundary emerges from C3.

## 9. Why one global field need not be one global One

A global field can carry the conditions of many local lineages without itself satisfying the same continuation organization as any of them.

The field may be:

```text
common condition / medium / distributed historical carrier
```

while local Ones are:

```text
lineage-relative process units regenerated within it.
```

The distinction is analogous in shape to:

```text
shared medium
!= one shared identity;
shared causal field
!= one shared continuation relation.
```

This must not be turned into a substance / container ontology.

## 10. Nested and overlapping Ones

The candidate allows:

```text
One_A and One_B share components;
One_A is nested in One_H;
One_A and One_B jointly help constitute One_AB;
One_A can cease while One_H continues;
One_H can cease while some lower-level Ones continue.
```

Therefore component overlap does not settle One identity.

The relevant pressure remains continuation organization.

## 11. New compact map

```text
DISTRIBUTED 多 / PRE-OBJECT FIELD
        ↓
Selection-generated vertical differentiation
        ↓
multiple local centers can form
        ↓
[some centers only = pattern components]
        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
if continuation / dissolution relations are relatively separable
        ↓
MULTIPLE ONE CANDIDATES
        ↓
if consequences become own-history and alter reconstitution
        ↓
BEARER strengthening where applicable
```

## 12. Remaining hard problem

The live burden now becomes:

> What source-neutral relation makes continuation sufficiently separable to count as more than multiple phases / parts of one global process, without smuggling in strict numerical identity or Bearer-level own-history?

Possible pressure dimensions include:

```text
selective perturbability;
branch-specific regeneration;
non-lockstep continuation;
merge / split behavior;
relative persistence under shared-field change.
```

These are probes, not a criterion set.

```text
multiple centers = multiple Ones: REJECTED
local coupling alone sufficient for One multiplicity: TOO WEAK
extra boundary as primitive: NOT PREFERRED
relative continuation separability: STRENGTHENED CANDIDATE
boundary as emergent from continuation separability: PREFERRED MACHINE READING
Bearer closure required for One multiplicity: NO
strict numerical identity solved: NO
canonical edit: NO
new Level: NO
HOLD change: NO
```
