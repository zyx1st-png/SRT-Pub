# CIV-004 — Nepal farmer-managed irrigation systems

## A. Case identity

~~~text
domain family = D-SOC / D-ECON / D-LAW
period = comparative Nepal irrigation studies summarized in Ostrom 2009
setting = farmer-managed and government-managed irrigation systems
record status = active source-pressure case
~~~

## B. Source-native account

Ostrom's Nepal irrigation research compared systems under different governance arrangements using measures including physical condition, tail-end water availability and agricultural productivity.

The Nobel lecture reports that, after controlling for environmental differences in the studied corpus, farmer-governed systems performed better on these measures.

Farmer-managed systems involved regular communication, local agreements, designated monitors and sanctions for rule violations.

Expanded studies preserved the broad comparative finding.

This does not establish that farmer management is universally superior in all irrigation or infrastructure settings.

## E. Initial relation

Irrigation requires users to coordinate contribution, maintenance and allocation of a common-pool water resource.

## F. Institutional mechanism

~~~text
shared resource
-> locally developed rules / agreements
-> participant communication
-> monitoring
-> sanctions / conflict handling
-> maintenance + allocation behavior
-> system performance
~~~

The operative object is not a written rule alone.

## G. Consequences

The source corpus links different institutional configurations with different irrigation-system outcomes.

The key GRG pressure is that the causal path passes through rules-in-use and participant enforcement / monitoring.

## I. Generative expectation

Rules and agreements specify expected future contribution / withdrawal / maintenance behavior.

Their causal relevance depends on participant practices that make those expectations operative.

## J. Normativity


No general political recommendation follows.
