# CIV-002 — antimicrobial use and resistance

## A. Case identity

~~~text
domain family = D-LIFE / D-TECH / D-SOC
period = ongoing
setting = human / animal / environmental antimicrobial use
record status = active schema-pressure case
primary source = WHO antimicrobial resistance fact sheet
~~~

Primary source:

https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/antimicrobial-resistance

## B. Source-native account

WHO defines antimicrobial resistance as microorganisms no longer responding to antimicrobial medicines.

WHO identifies misuse and overuse of antimicrobials as important drivers of resistant pathogens, while also emphasizing other contributors including inadequate infection prevention, poor water/sanitation/hygiene, insufficient diagnostics and vaccines, and unequal access to appropriate medicines.

Source-native mechanism includes:

~~~text
antimicrobial exposure
+ microbial variation / selection
+ transmission
+ health-system / agricultural / environmental conditions
-> changing prevalence of resistant pathogens
~~~

## C. Evidence

~~~text
evidence family = E3 large-scale surveillance / observational evidence
                  + E1/E2 mechanism and intervention evidence in broader AMR science
event evidence = very strong
mechanism evidence = strong
causal decomposition at civilization scale = multi-factor
distributional evidence = important / unequal burden
~~~

WHO reports that bacterial AMR was estimated to be associated with more than 4.7 million deaths globally in 2021 and that approximately one in six laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections worldwide was resistant to antibiotics in 2023.

These figures are public-health burden estimates, not GRG evidence by themselves.

## D. Objectification

~~~text
unit = pathogen-drug combination / infection / population
time horizon = immediate treatment vs multi-year resistance ecology
boundary = health system + community + agriculture + environment
aggregation = global surveillance hides local heterogeneity
important background = access, diagnostics, sanitation, infection control, prescribing,
                       agriculture, transmission networks
~~~

## E. Initial relation

Antimicrobials create immediate therapeutic possibilities for susceptible infections.

Their use also changes the selective environment in which resistant variants can spread.

## F. Intervention / institution

Relevant interventions include:

- prescribing / stewardship rules;
- diagnostics;
- infection prevention;
- vaccination;
- surveillance;
- agricultural use controls;
- research and drug development.

The case is not reducible to one policy lever.

## G. Consequences

The key cross-horizon structure is:

~~~text
local / immediate antimicrobial benefit
can coexist with
population-level / long-horizon erosion of treatment effectiveness
when resistance rises.
~~~

This is not an argument against appropriate antimicrobial use.

## H. GRG candidate

Candidate:

~~~text
X4 local success through background depletion
+
G8 reconstruction of future selectability
~~~

Working relation:

~~~text
effective present intervention
-> changes biological selective environment
-> alters future population composition
-> changes later treatment option space
~~~

What is paid:

- present action reconstructs later conditions;
- consequence crosses timescale and population boundaries.

Not paid automatically:

- a single-agent responsibility claim;
- simple tragedy-of-commons model;
- moral blame.

## I. Generative expectation

At a public-health / treatment-system scale, preserving future antimicrobial effectiveness creates a structural expectation that current use should be considered together with its effect on later resistance conditions.

This remains indexed to the treatment/ecological relation.

## J. Normativity

~~~text
normativity status = INDEXED / NOT FULL MORAL VERDICT
~~~

Relevant conflict:

~~~text
current patient's treatment need
vs
future population treatment effectiveness
~~~

Access inequity complicates any simple "use less" reading.

## K. Rival reading

Evolutionary biology, epidemiology, infectious-disease medicine and health policy already explain AMR.

GRG does not add a new resistance mechanism.

Possible contribution:

~~~text
make explicit the relation:
present successful intervention
-> reconstruction of future action space
-> multi-position / multi-timescale consequence.
~~~

## L. Cross-case links

Potential similarity to infrastructure lock-in or environmental depletion:

present success changes later options.

False-convergence risk:

biological evolution is not institution persistence or market lock-in.

## M. Failure condition

If GRG cannot preserve the biological selection mechanism and the access/stewardship tension, "future selectability" becomes an empty synonym for long-term effects.
