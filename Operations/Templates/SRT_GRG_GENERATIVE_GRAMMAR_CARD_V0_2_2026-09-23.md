# GRG Generative Grammar Card v0.2 — experimental fusion-case template

> Role: reusable noncanonical template for source-native GRG fusion cases.
>
> This template does not replace the current GRG foundational owner and does not define canonical SRT.
>
> Primary discipline: **source-native first, GRG second**.

## 0. Hard guards

A Card is not complete merely because every field contains text.

A valid Card may contain:

- empty fields;
- NOT ESTABLISHED;
- NOT APPLICABLE;
- mapping rejected;
- source/GRG type mismatch;
- unresolved ambiguity.

Do not force source vocabulary into GRG categories.

Do not infer:

`same word -> same role -> same mechanism -> same ontology`.

Use this progression instead:

`analogy -> explicit mapping -> candidate instantiation -> tested instantiation -> transfer candidate`.

### Selection hard guard

Source-native uses of “selection”, “choice”, “filtering”, “actualization”, “collapse”, “self-determination”, “negative selection” or similar vocabulary are not automatically SRT Selection.

SRT Selection remains a term of art.

### Card authority guard

The Card is:

- a research interface;
- a compression / comparison device;
- a provenance-preserving mapping surface.

The Card is not:

- a universal ontology inventory;
- a source replacement;
- a proof of SRT;
- a proof of GRG scientific distinctiveness;
- a license to create a new GRG foundational version.

# 21. Fusion-case execution sequence

Recommended order:

`source-native reading -> source reconstruction -> Card fill -> non-identity audit -> anti-metaphor test -> generative expectation -> failure/reopening condition -> Card outcome -> cross-card reconciliation`.

Do not begin with a target GRG conclusion.

Do not force a full Card in one pass.

Stop when the source evidence no longer supports the next field.

---

# 22. Cross-card reconciliation

After at least two sufficiently developed fusion cases, compare Cards on:

- fields that repeatedly carry the same generative role;
- fields that are repeatedly empty;
- false equivalences exposed by different sources;
- fields that are source-specific rather than GRG-general;
- newly required splits;
- opportunities to remove redundant fields;
- whether a transferable generative expectation survives without ad hoc retuning.

Cross-card reconciliation is allowed to:

- revise the Card;
- demote a GRG term;
- split a GRG term;
- reject a mapping;
- preserve domain-specific vocabulary instead of forcing unification.

Case count alone is not evidence of universality.
