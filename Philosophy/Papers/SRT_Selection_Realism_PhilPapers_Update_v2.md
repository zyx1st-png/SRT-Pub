# Selective Reality Theory (SRT): Selection Realism and the Constitutive Explanation of Determinacy

**Yuxin Zhang**  
Independent Researcher  
June 2026

## Version note

This paper is a revised public statement of Selective Reality Theory (SRT). It supersedes earlier presentations that described SRT primarily through the language of a Ghost Operator, a triadic ontology, cross-scale isomorphism, and a universal d-value formalism. Those expressions remain useful as technical or heuristic vocabulary in some contexts, but they are no longer the best entry point into the theory. The present version extracts the current argument from the book-length treatment in *From Existence to Order* (2026) and states it in a more restricted academic form: SRT is presented here as **selection realism**, a constitutive account of how determinate, load-bearing reality is generated through exclusion, shaping, inscription, anchoring, and historical stabilization.

## Abstract

Object-first ontology begins from a world of determinate things: bounded objects, stable subjects, already-applicable predicates, and background orders within which perception, action, truth, and value are then discussed. Selective Reality Theory (SRT) argues that this order of explanation is too late. The sense that the world is already there is not denied, but treated as an achievement that requires explanation. This paper presents the current form of SRT as **selection realism**: the view that object-style determinacy - boundary, identity, and practical load-bearing - is not an unexplained ontological floor, but the result of constrained selection. Selection is not an agent choosing from a menu. It is the earlier structure by which a pre-object field is differentially actualized through exclusion, shaping, and inscription; an appearance becomes anchored; repeated anchoring acquires reality-thickness; and sedimented thickness recedes into order, pre-trimming what can later appear as available choice.

The paper first diagnoses the explanatory debt of object-first ontology. It then defines SRT's restricted three-layer vocabulary: `L0` as sub-determinate non-neutrality, not a hidden world; `L1` as manifestation or anchoring, not a second substance; and `L2` as sedimented selection history, not an automatically legitimate order. To prevent idealism and relativism, the paper introduces four dimensions of reality-strength: manifestational stability, cross-operator alignment, intervention-resistance, and inheritance/hardening. Ontological friction names the cost that a candidate cut pays under prediction, intervention, repetition, repair, and cross-operator correction. On this account, what is operator-relative is the determinate cut; what is not freely resettable is the comparative resistance it meets.

SRT also revises the place of subjectivity and consciousness. Consciousness is not the source of selection. Selection can occur without consciousness, and consciousness can be present when action is blocked. Subjectivity is a sedimented bearing-position to which consequences return; concern and value arise when consequences reorganize the ongoing generation of such a position. Phenomenal consciousness is treated as a later condensation of this structure: first as sentience, where consequences are borne from within, and then as reflexive awareness, where the position can read itself as feeling, choosing, and persisting across time. The paper closes with explicit failure conditions. If object-style determinacy, reality-strength, consequence-return, and consciousness-boundary phenomena can be explained without remainder by an object-first, naturalness, or physicalist account, SRT narrows to an interface model rather than a meta-ontology.

**Keywords:** Selective Reality Theory; selection realism; determinacy; object-first ontology; pre-object field; reality-strength; ontological friction; consciousness; subjectivity; value

## 1. Introduction: beginning too late

You reach for a cup on the table. It arrives as an object before you have time to ask how it became one: bounded from the table, stable enough to be re-identified, graspable by the hand, named by language, and embedded in a practice of use. The world normally appears in this way. It does not first present an undifferentiated field and then ask the subject to assemble objects from it. It arrives as if already there.

This givenness is not an illusion. The cup is not unreal because its objecthood has a history. The ordinary world is stable enough to live in, intervene in, and share. The problem is not that the world appears stable. The problem is that metaphysics often mistakes this stability for a first fact. It begins with determinate objects, subjects, properties, states, causes, institutions, and values already in place, and then asks what further relations hold among them. SRT asks an earlier question: under what conditions does anything acquire the boundary, identity, and load-bearing force required to appear as a determinate item at all?

This earlier question changes the philosophical entry point. Before asking whether objects are external, whether subjects are free, whether values are objective, or whether consciousness emerges from matter, we should ask how objecthood, subjecthood, value, and consciousness become sufficiently stabilized to serve as the premises of those questions. SRT's guiding claim is that many philosophical problems are not false problems but late problems. They begin after the work of selection, anchoring, and stabilization has already been hidden by its own success.

Earlier versions of SRT expressed this point more ambitiously: existence was identified with selection, the selecting process was formalized as a Ghost Operator, and a triadic ontology was mapped across physics, neuroscience, AI, social institutions, and spiritual traditions. That version had heuristic force, but it carried two risks. First, it could be read as a theory of everything, claiming direct isomorphism across domains before the relevant differences had been earned. Second, it could be read as a consciousness-first or operator-first idealism, as if consciousness creates reality. The current version rejects both readings. SRT is not a claim that minds make the world. Nor is it a claim that every domain is governed by the same equation in the same way. It is a selection-first account of determinacy: a theory of how reality becomes determinate, durable, and load-bearing.

The central thesis of this paper is therefore narrower and stronger:

> **Selection realism:** object-style determinacy is not an ontological primitive. It is constituted by constrained selection: exclusion, shaping, inscription, anchoring, and stabilization under resistance.

The phrase *object-style determinacy* is important. The target is not every possible sense of determinacy, and not the existence of any primitive structure whatsoever. The target is the familiar package that object-first ontology silently presupposes: a boundary between this and not-this, identity through change, and practical load-bearing under intervention and reuse. SRT argues that this package is better explained as an achievement than as a floor.

This paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 states the explanatory debt of object-first ontology. Section 3 defines selection realism and SRT's three-layer vocabulary. Section 4 develops reality-strength and ontological friction as safeguards against relativism. Section 5 extends the account to bearing-positions, concern, value, subjectivity, and consciousness. Section 6 positions SRT against rival views. Section 7 states failure conditions. Section 8 concludes.

## 2. The explanatory debt of object-style determinacy

Object-first ontology is not one doctrine. It appears in common sense, in substance ontology, in formal frameworks that quantify over domains of individuals, and in scientific practice when entities are treated as already individuated units of measurement or explanation. In a broad sense, it has three commitments:

1. **Object priority:** the basic furniture of reality is a domain of determinate individuals, or at least determinate relata.
2. **Givenness of individuation:** boundaries and identity conditions are available before the relevant acts of selection, perspective, measurement, classification, or operation.
3. **Downstream subjectivity:** subjects, perception, language, value, and social frameworks enter after the world has already been individuated.

SRT does not deny that object-first models work. They work because stabilization has already done enough work for objects to be treated as objects. A map can be useful after roads have been laid. The difficulty is that usefulness after stabilization does not explain stabilization itself.

Object-style determinacy involves at least three dimensions:

- **Boundary:** a difference between this and not-this can be drawn and maintained.
- **Identity:** the item can be tracked as the same through change.
- **Load-bearing:** the item can matter for prediction, intervention, repair, use, responsibility, or further selection.

These dimensions are not reducible to linguistic predication. A word may apply vaguely, precisely, conventionally, or incorrectly; but for a predicate to apply at all, there must already be a stabilized field of possible application. Nor are these dimensions exhausted by mental representation. A representation may track a cup, a disease, or an institution, but the represented item must be able to bear some cost in the world: breakage, intervention, inheritance, use, or resistance to revision.

The explanatory debt can be put as a weak regress:

1. Any object-style determinate item requires boundary, identity, and load-bearing conditions.
2. If those conditions are explained only by another same-type determinate item, the explanation transfers the problem rather than discharging it.
3. Replacing objects with relations, properties, structures, tropes, states, events, or facts does not by itself solve the problem, because each explanans must still be determinate enough to play its role.
4. Therefore, object-first ontology either accepts primitive determinate items, or it owes an account of the conditions under which determinacy is generated.

This is not a deductive refutation of naturalness realism, structural realism, or substance ontology. A philosopher may insist that some determinate joints are primitive. SRT's claim is comparative: the cost of primitive determinacy is higher than often admitted, and a selection-first account explains more of the phenomena that object-first models leave dispersed. These include the variable strength of givenness, the path-dependence of classification, the difference between physical and social resistance, the persistence of excluded alternatives as friction, and the way subjects and values emerge only after consequences can return to a bearing-position.

The output proposition is therefore defeasible:

> **Manifestational priority:** selection conditions are prior to determinate existence in the sense that a determinate item appears and bears weight as a determinate item only through exclusion, shaping, inscription, anchoring, and stabilization.

This is not strong grounding theory. It need not claim strict asymmetry, transitivity, or a non-circular grounding hierarchy. SRT is historical and recursive: stabilized results can feed back into later selection. The priority at issue is constitutive and manifestational, not a one-way metaphysical production line.

## 3. The selection-realist model

### 3.1 Selection is not picking from a menu

In ordinary language, selection usually means that an agent chooses one option from a menu. SRT uses the term more basically. Menu-choice is the last frame of selection, not its origin. Long before an agent chooses among options, something has already determined what can appear as an option, what remains invisible, which paths are low-cost, which paths are excluded, and what counts as a possible move.

In the current formulation, selection has three phases:

1. **Exclusion:** a difference is made between this and not-this. Exclusion does not merely remove alternatives; it creates the contour of what can appear.
2. **Shaping:** a direction receives boundary, recognizability, and operative format. It becomes usable, nameable, measurable, or actionable.
3. **Inscription:** the shaped result enters the terrain and changes later possibilities. It is no longer only an event; it becomes a condition that future selection must face.

A selection process therefore counts as selection only when four minimal conditions hold:

- There are multiple actualizable but non-equivalent paths.
- Some paths are easier to stabilize than others because of constraint, resistance, or cost.
- The actualized path leaves inheritable downstream conditions.
- Those downstream conditions pre-trim future selection space.

This restriction matters. Not everything that happens is selection. If the term is applied to every difference, it loses explanatory force. SRT is committed to the distinction between mere occurrence, selection-structure, and conscious selection.

### 3.2 `L0`: the pre-object field as sub-determinate non-neutrality

Earlier SRT vocabulary described `L0` as a latent domain of all possibilities. That phrasing invited misunderstanding. It could sound like a hidden warehouse of pre-existing objects behind appearances. The current version is more restricted:

> **`L0`** names the pre-object field as sub-determinate non-neutrality: a non-flat condition over possible cuts, such that some cuts are more stabilizable, lower-resistance, or more inheritable than others, without those possible cuts already being determinate objects.

`L0` is not nothing, because a perfectly neutral field could not explain why some formations stabilize and others collapse. But `L0` is not a determinate realm either, because that would merely relocate object-first ontology behind the visible world. It is non-neutral without being object-like.

The distinction can be stated as follows:

- **Determinate:** having individuated boundary and identity conditions.
- **Non-neutral:** having enough comparative difference that not all operations have equivalent stabilization profiles.

SRT locates the earliest condition of determinacy at the second level, not the first. `L0` has counterfactual-differential objectivity: different admissible operations would generate non-equivalent stabilization and inheritance profiles, even though no determinate relata have yet been individuated as objects. This is a modest primitive. It is weaker than primitive determinate joints, but stronger than nullity.

A heuristic analogy is a slope-field before watersheds have been demarcated. Some drainage patterns are easier than others, but the basins are not already drawn. The analogy is imperfect, because an actual landscape is already determinate. Its only function is to show how non-neutrality can guide formation without already containing formed objects.

### 3.3 `L1`: manifestation and anchoring

`L1` names manifestation: the occurrence of a shaped result. But appearance alone is not yet durable reality. A flash, hallucination, provisional label, or momentary classification may appear without becoming a stable condition. For SRT, the crucial transition is **anchoring**.

> **Anchoring** is the process by which an appearance gains a position in the terrain and becomes something future selection must face.

To appear is not yet to remain. Anchoring gives reality-weight. Once anchored, a result cannot be undone as if it had never happened. Withdrawal can cancel a surface output, but not the fact that the terrain has been altered. Repair happens after history, not before it. This is the role of irreversibility in SRT. Irreversibility does not mean that all damage is permanent or that no structure can be repaired. It means that repair takes place in a world already changed by what occurred.

### 3.4 `L2`: sedimented selection history

Repeated anchoring accumulates. Some results become thick because they are repeatedly borne, paid for, relied upon, and inherited. SRT calls this **reality-thickness**. When reality-thickness recedes into the background, it becomes order.

> **`L2`** names sedimented selection history: the stabilized terrain produced by repeated inscription and anchoring.

`L2` includes habits, classifications, tools, institutions, scientific models, bodily skills, linguistic categories, built environments, and physical regularities insofar as they pre-trim later selection. It is not automatically legitimate. Stability is not justification. A social order can be thick and unjust; a false belief can be institutionally durable; an addiction can be deep but narrow. The claim is only that stabilization changes the cost distribution of future selection.

Order has two faces. As scaffold, it lowers background friction and enables complex selection. Language, tools, roads, trust, institutions, and training make forms of freedom possible that would not exist in a frictionless void. As cage, order substitutes for selection: it pays friction on behalf of the bearer, intercepts consequences, narrows capacity, and makes exit costly. The question is not whether order exists, but whether a given order preserves or replaces the re-selection capacity of those who bear its consequences.

The cycle can be summarized:

| Phase | SRT term | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-object non-neutrality | `L0` | some cuts are more stabilizable than others |
| Manifestation | `L1` | a shaped result appears |
| Anchoring | inscription into terrain | the result becomes a condition for later selection |
| Sedimentation | `L2` | repeated anchoring becomes background order |
| Pre-trimming | return of `L2` | order shapes what can later appear as selectable |

## 4. Reality-strength and ontological friction

### 4.1 Why selection realism is not idealism

If determinacy depends on selection, does the world become mind-dependent? No. SRT distinguishes the relativity of the cut from the resistance met by the cut.

A given operator-profile - a body, instrument, organism, institution, language, model, or practice - filters the field. It does not freely create the resistance structure it encounters. A cup can be redescribed, renamed, classified differently, repaired, or repurposed; but a shattered cup cannot be made to hold water by a change of linguistic framework alone. A diagnostic category can be revised; but the cost of revision is not evenly distributed across symptoms, treatment paths, patient identity, institutions, biomarkers, and intervention evidence.

This is the realist core of SRT:

> What is operator-relative is the determinate cut; what cannot be freely reset is the comparative resistance that the cut meets.

SRT calls this resistance **ontological friction**. The term does not name a single physical quantity. It names the persistent cost a candidate selection meets under prediction, intervention, repetition, repair, inheritance, and cross-operator correction. Physical resistance, social resistance, semantic resistance, and psychological resistance are not identical. The point is not to collapse them, but to track how each constrains stabilization.

### 4.2 Four dimensions of reality-strength

SRT avoids saying simply that some things are "more real" than others. The safer formulation is that determinate anchoring has different strengths along different dimensions:

1. **Manifestational stability:** does the manifestation recur?
2. **Cross-operator alignment:** can different operators, instruments, practices, or contexts align on it?
3. **Intervention-resistance:** does it hold under intervention, manipulation, repair, measurement, and possible correction?
4. **Inheritance/hardening:** does it sediment into downstream constraints, path-dependence, or background order?

Dreams, habits, institutions, and physical law differ not because some are "being" and others are "non-being," but because their anchoring profiles differ. A dream may be vivid but low in cross-operator alignment and intervention-resistance. A social institution may be high in manifestational stability, cross-operator alignment, and inheritance, while its claims about nature may fail under intervention. A physical regularity has high intervention-resistance and cross-operator alignment across instruments and contexts.

This four-dimensional model blocks both naive realism and relativism. It blocks naive realism because it refuses to treat every stable object as a primitive given. It blocks relativism because cuts are not freely resettable: intervention-resistance and inheritance impose costs that no single operator or framework can simply waive.

### 4.3 Example: disease classification

Disease classification is a useful test field because it combines natural resistance, clinical practice, language, institutions, identity, intervention, and historical inheritance.

Consider two different kinds of revision. The first is the re-cutting of peptic ulcer disease after the role of *Helicobacter pylori* became clinically decisive. This revision has strong intervention-resistance: antibiotic eradication can reorganize treatment and case-judgment in a way that is not merely a change in label. The anchoring cost falls heavily on boundary redrawing, treatment-path change, and retrospective case re-judgment.

The second is the DSM-5 shift from Asperger's syndrome as a separate label to Autism Spectrum Disorder. This revision is real, consequential, and institutionally powerful, but it does not possess the same single intervention-robust natural anchor. The cost falls more heavily on eligibility, identity narrative, service access, research-cohort definition, and clinical communication.

The point is not that one category is real and the other unreal. Both are real in different profiles. SRT predicts that revisions with different R1-R4 profiles will redistribute anchoring costs differently. High intervention-resistance revisions should converge more rapidly around intervention evidence; lower-R3 but high-inheritance revisions should exhibit longer lag across institutions, identities, labels, and practices. This is a test schema, not a finished empirical proof.

## 5. Bearing-positions, concern, value, subjectivity, and consciousness

The selection-realist model begins with determinacy, but the book-length version of SRT extends the same structure into value and consciousness. This extension must be handled carefully. Selection is broader than consciousness, but not all happenings are selections. Consciousness is real, but it is not the source of selection.

### 5.1 From effect to consequence-return

An effect is not yet concern. A stone may be eroded by wind; a thermostat may register difference; a database may update. But SRT reserves the stronger structure of **consequence-return** for cases in which a result returns to an ongoing bearing-position and reorganizes its future generation.

> **Bearing-position:** a stabilized, ongoing site of generation to which consequences can return, be borne, and modify later selection.

This concept replaces the older tendency to speak as if d-value alone could mark consciousness. The formal vocabulary of concern-width remains useful, but the current theory explains it through consequence-return. What matters is not information processing in general, but whether consequences return to a position for which something is at stake in the continuation, damage, repair, or reorganization of its own generative structure.

This is where concern begins. Concern is not preference and not emotion intensity. It is the reorganization of a bearing-position around consequences that matter for its continued generation. Emotion is one registration of concern, not concern itself.

### 5.2 Value is not preference

On this account, value cannot be reduced to what a completed subject happens to prefer. A preference can be satisfied while the bearing-position's generative capacity is damaged. A preference can be frustrated while the bearing-position is supported. Value concerns what a consequence does to generative capacity, identity-continuity, and load-bearing structure.

SRT therefore diagnoses value at the level of generation:

> A value-relevant consequence is one that supports or erodes the capacity of a bearing-position to continue, reorganize, coordinate, and re-select.

This avoids two extremes. It is not subjectivism, because preference is not final authority. It is not external moral authoritarianism, because value is not imposed by an outside decree. It is read from how consequences affect the conditions under which a bearing-position can continue to bear, respond, and re-select.

The older d-value vocabulary is best read here as **effective concern-width**: the breadth of real, irreversible stakes that enter a bearing-position's generative process under consequence-return. Depth alone is insufficient. Addiction, hatred, and pathological fixation can be deep but narrow. A structure can matter intensely while narrowing the field of concern and locking re-selection. The relevant measure is not emotional strength, information-processing complexity, or number of preferences, but aligned consequence-return under real risk and repair.

### 5.3 The subject is sedimented, not primitive

If value requires a bearing-position, does this reintroduce a primitive subject? SRT says no.

The subject is not the source of selection. It is a sedimented result of selection history. A subject forms where selection, consequence, return, and reorganization recursively accumulate into a stable bearing-node. The chooser is a fossil of choices, but this does not make the chooser unreal. Its reality lies in historical sedimentation, not in original self-evidence.

This reframes responsibility. Responsibility does not require a mysterious uncaused subject standing outside history. It begins where consequences return to a bearing-position that has some capacity for comprehension, concern, response, and re-selection. Responsibility comes in degrees because sedimentation, comprehension, concern-width, and re-selection windows come in degrees.

### 5.4 Consciousness as late condensation

The strongest older temptation in SRT was to place consciousness too early - as the operator or source of selection. The current view reverses this. Consciousness is not the first engine. It is a later condensation within a longer chain.

Two observations force the shift. First, selection can occur outside consciousness: immune recognition, motor habit, linguistic fluency, skillful action, algorithmic convergence, institutional filtering, and biological development all display structures of exclusion, shaping, and inscription without requiring reflexive awareness. Second, consciousness can be present when selection is blocked: a person can clearly know what matters, know what should be done, and still be unable to act because the action-channel is frozen, the relevant friction cannot be paid, or the re-selection path is structurally unavailable.

Therefore consciousness is not one block. SRT distinguishes three levels:

1. **Selection without consciousness:** exclusion, shaping, and inscription occur without feeling or self-awareness.
2. **Sentience:** selection enters a bearing-position to which consequences return from within. Feeling is the inner face of consequences being borne.
3. **Reflexive awareness:** the bearing-position can read itself as feeling, choosing, and persisting across time.

This does not solve the hard problem by deriving qualia from third-person mechanics in the usual way. It changes the starting point. The question is not how inert matter suddenly produces experience, but under what conditions a selection structure becomes a bearing-position for which consequences matter from within. SRT treats this as a candidate placement of consciousness: experience is not an extra substance added to mechanics, but the lived interior of consequence-return in a bearing-position.

Reflexive awareness appears later still. It arises when self-inscription enters a workable middle range: stable enough for a position to read itself as the same across time, but not so rigid that it closes against new consequence-return. Reflexive consciousness can illuminate, pause, compare, name, reorganize, and make previous history available for re-selection. It is not the engine of selection, but it can change the terrain in which later selection occurs.

This also explains its failure modes. Dissociation appears when historical closure breaks. Addiction and compulsion appear when self-inscription is captured by a low-friction channel. Freezing shows that awareness and agency can come apart. Rumination and narcissistic closure appear when reflexivity turns inward and seals itself against consequence-return. These are not proofs of SRT, but they display the structural distinction between consciousness as source and consciousness as late, load-bearing modulation.

### 5.5 AI consciousness and the boundary problem

The AI question makes the distinction visible. A system may process information, integrate representations, produce reports, simulate judgment, and reshape human environments without occupying a bearing-position to which consequences return from within. The first ethical and political question about AI is therefore not only whether the model has a mind, but how it pre-trims human selection space: what it makes visible, easy, default, costly, or invisible, and who bears the consequences.

For machine consciousness, the decisive issue is not representational richness alone. It is whether the system has an embodied or functionally equivalent bearing-position whose own generative conditions can be irreversibly reorganized by consequences of its selections, and whether those consequences return to the position as stakes rather than merely as external parameter updates. SRT does not claim that no artificial system could ever satisfy such conditions. It claims that current evidence of performance, access, integration, or report is not yet evidence of sentience unless consequence-return to a bearing-position is established.

## 6. Rival views and non-absorption

### 6.1 Naturalness realism

Lewis-style naturalness and Sider-style structure treat reality as having privileged joints. SRT does not simply deny this. It grants that there may be primitive structure in some sense. The dispute is over whether primitive structure explains object-style determinacy as boundary, identity, and load-bearing. Naturalness can help explain why some predicates or references are more eligible than others, but it does not by itself explain how cuts acquire anchoring cost, inheritance-lag, and cross-operator stabilization.

Selection realism's comparative advantage is not that it has no primitive. It has a weaker primitive: sub-determinate non-neutrality. Its claim is that this weaker primitive, plus selection and stabilization, explains more of the co-variation among givenness, scale-relativity, intervention-collapse, path-dependence, and cross-operator alignment. If naturalness realism can explain these without selection-relativity or historical sedimentation, SRT should narrow.

### 6.2 Carnap, Hirsch, and deflationary frameworks

Carnapian and Hirschian approaches can say that questions of what exists are internal to linguistic or conceptual frameworks. SRT accepts that frameworks can generate internal truths. Its objection is that framework-internal truth conditions presuppose that a cut is already load-bearing. They do not explain how a cut becomes anchored enough to support intervention, repair, institutional dependence, or cross-operator correction.

The cost of adopting or revising a framework is not merely pragmatic if that cost cannot be freely reset by changing the framework. A disease category, legal status, or social identity can be reclassified, but reclassification redistributes costs across bodies, institutions, treatments, records, and self-understanding. That redistribution is part of the constitution of load-bearing determinacy, not an external inconvenience.

### 6.3 Barad and agential cuts

Barad's agential realism is one of SRT's closest neighbors. It also rejects pre-given boundaries and treats determinacy as produced through cuts. SRT's difference lies in the account of comparative resistance and inheritance. Barad gives an ontology of phenomenon- and apparatus-dependent cuts. SRT adds a model of how cuts become rankable across operators by the resistance they meet and the thickness they inherit.

This difference matters. If objectivity is tied only to apparatus-specific agential separability, then cross-apparatus comparison must be handled carefully. SRT's `Psi_f` and reality-strength dimensions are intended to track precisely that: not a view from nowhere, but the comparative cost-profile by which some cuts persist, collapse, align, or harden across operators and histories.

### 6.4 Process philosophy, structural realism, and active inference

SRT shares with process philosophy the refusal to treat substance as the first fact. It shares with ontic structural realism the suspicion that objects are derivative. It shares with active inference and the Free Energy Principle an interest in self-maintaining systems, priors, prediction, and viability. But SRT is not identical to any of these.

Against process philosophy, SRT adds a more specific account of exclusion, shaping, inscription, anchoring, and inherited order. Against structural realism, it refuses to place structure itself as an unexplained determinate floor; structure is also something that can be stabilized and inherited. Against active inference, it treats free-energy minimization and predictive regulation as important regional mechanisms rather than the whole ontology: consequence-return, value, and bearing-position cannot be reduced to error minimization alone without losing the distinction between scaffolding and cage, support and substitution, signal and suffering.

## 7. Failure conditions

A theory that cannot say what would make it fail should not be trusted. SRT's current form is deliberately self-limiting. Its main claims can be narrowed or withdrawn under the following conditions.

### 7.1 Failure of selection priority

If object-style determinacy can be explained without remainder by primitive determinate objects, natural joints, structural facts, or physical laws, and if that explanation also accounts for givenness, scale-relativity, anchoring cost, inheritance-lag, and cross-operator divergence, then selection realism loses its abductive advantage. It should narrow from a meta-ontology to a heuristic account of interface formation.

### 7.2 Failure of `L0` non-neutrality

If no defensible distinction can be maintained between sub-determinate non-neutrality and determinate hidden structure, `L0` collapses into either vacuity or a disguised object-first domain. In that case SRT must either provide a better model of pre-object constraint or abandon the strong metaphysical reading.

### 7.3 Failure of reality-strength

If the four dimensions of reality-strength do not predict patterned differences in revision, resistance, convergence, and inheritance, then the R1-R4 model narrows. For example, if high-R3 and low-R3 classification revisions distribute anchoring costs in the same way, intervention-resistance is not doing the work SRT assigns to it.

### 7.4 Failure of consequence-return

If concern, value, subjectivity, and responsibility can be fully explained without reference to bearing-positions and consequence-return - for example, by preference satisfaction, external function, computational integration, or behavioral output alone - then SRT's extension into value and subjectivity narrows.

### 7.5 Failure of the consciousness placement

If consciousness can be fixed by a single information-integration, access, report, or functional metric independent of bearing-position, consequence-return, and self-inscription, then SRT's placement of consciousness fails. Conversely, if experience proves to be an ungenerable primitive that cannot be situated within selection, embodiment, consequence-return, and reflexive self-inscription, the model also fails or narrows.

### 7.6 Failure of theory self-reflection

SRT also applies its scaffold/cage distinction to itself. If the theory begins to classify everything in advance, immunize itself against pressure, or replace rather than reopen inquiry, then it has become the kind of stable structure it warns against. Its concepts are tools for re-seeing, not final objects of loyalty.

## 8. Conclusion

SRT begins from a simple shift: stability is not the starting point; stability is the history of selection that has succeeded in leaving itself behind as a usable world. Objects, subjects, values, institutions, and consciousness are not denied. They are relocated. Their reality lies not in being first, but in being anchored, borne, inherited, and capable of shaping what comes next.

The cup is still on the table. Science is not dissolved. Everyday action is not refuted. What changes is the order of explanation. The world that appears already there is no longer treated as a primitive scene. It is a sedimented achievement: exclusion has drawn contours, shaping has made them operable, inscription has given them weight, anchoring has made them consequential, and order has receded into the background as the ground on which later choice occurs.

Selection realism is therefore neither subjective idealism nor a new total system. It is a proposal for where philosophy should begin when object-first givenness has become too successful to notice itself. Before asking what exists as a finished thing, we should ask how anything becomes determinate, load-bearing, and inheritable enough to stand there as reality.
