# Before Objects: Selection Realism and the Constitutive Explanation of Determinacy

## Abstract

Object-first ontology takes the basic furniture of reality to be a domain of determinate individuals whose boundaries and identity conditions are given prior to any selection, perspective, or operation. This paper argues that *object-style determinacy*—the combination of boundary, identity, and practical load-bearing—should not be presupposed without explanation as the ontological floor, and is better understood as a grounded result of conditions of constrained selection: cutting, anchoring, and stabilization. I call the resulting view *selection realism*. The argument has two stages. Stage A is a weak transcendental regress yielding only the modest conclusion that object-first ontology owes an *explanatory debt* concerning the conditions of determinacy; it does not deductively defeat naturalness realism. Stage B is a defeasible inference to the best explanation that these conditions are best understood as constrained selectability, located as the disciplined middle between Sider/Lewis-style unique natural joints and Hirsch/Carnap-style framework-internal convention. To avoid idealism and relativism, reality-strength is recast as four dimensions—manifestational stability, cross-operator alignment, intervention-resistance, and inheritance/hardening—with intervention-resistance read dispositionally. The view is distinguished from Barad's agential realism, ontic structural realism, process philosophy, and Kant, and is given empirical purchase through a worked example in disease classification (peptic ulcer / *H. pylori* versus the DSM-5 reclassification of Asperger's syndrome). Every strong claim is paired with a withdrawal condition.

**Keywords**: selection realism; object-first ontology; determinacy; manifestational priority; constitutive explanation; reality-strength; natural kinds; anti-relativism

## 1. The Explanatory Debt of Object-Style Determinacy

### 1.1 The cup

You reach for the cup on the table. But "the cup" is never merely a patch of raw stimulus: it already comes with a boundary, a graspable shape, a use, a linguistic stability, and conditions for being re-identified across encounters. It already arrives as a *determinate item* (its graspability is, in Gibson's [1979] sense, an affordance). The world almost always arrives this way—as if already there, composed of finished, bounded things into which we subsequently step to perceive, name, evaluate, and act. This sense of givenness is so stable that most philosophical questions begin only after it: Is the external world real? Are we free? Are values objective? Are institutions legitimate?

These questions arrive too late. Each presupposes that objects, subjects, values, and orders are already stably in place, and asks only about their further properties. An earlier, routinely skipped question is: *how does anything come to be determinate, object-like, and bounded at all, such that subsequent perception and choice can take hold of it?* Before asking what objects are, one should ask how individuation is grounded.

### 1.2 Levels of determinacy, and the target

"Determinacy" carries several senses, and conflating them obscures what is at issue. I distinguish: **D1 boundary** (an operable distinction between a thing and what it is not); **D2 identity** (the thing can be tracked as the same through change); **D3 applicability** (a concept or classification applies stably); **D4 practical load-bearing** (the item bears weight in action, intervention, repair, and prediction); and **D5 ontological strength** (the item has enough reality to serve as a condition for subsequent selection).

The argument of this paper targets *object-style determinacy*, the conjunction **D1 + D2 + D4**: boundary, identity, and practical load-bearing. This is what object-first ontology sets, without explanation, as a floor. The stronger claim that *no* determinacy is fundamental is not a premise; it appears only as the defeasible output of the Stage B abduction (§2.3). This lowers the argumentative burden: I need not defeat every position about basic determinate items, only show that object-style determinacy requires explanation and that selection realism explains it better.

Two clarifications prevent a circularity worry. First, **D5 is not the target**; it is an extension layer. D5 is not a fifth, independent *kind* of determinacy competing with D1–D4, but the *level* of reality that the four reality-strength dimensions of §5.1 (R1–R4) characterize. The paper explains object-style determinacy *by* reality-strength, and does not define reality-strength *by* determinacy: the non-circularity claim is precisely that this level is *exhibited* by R1–R4 (recurrence, cross-operator alignment, intervention-resistance, hardening), not stipulated by the determinacy it helps to explain. Second, **D3 is not a grounding premise**. It recurs in the examples (linguistic labels, diagnostic predicates) because it is an *expression and feedback layer*: stable applicability is both a result of stabilized object-style determinacy and a mechanism that pre-trims subsequent cuts.

### 1.3 Object-first ontology

> **Object-first ontology** =df the view that the basic furniture of reality is a domain of determinate individuals (objects), satisfying:
> **(O1)** *Object fundamentality*—determinate objects are ontologically basic, and events, processes, facts, and perspectives are analyzed using objects as relata.
> **(O2)** *Givenness of individuation*—objects come, prior to and independently of any selection, perspective, or operation, already equipped with boundary and identity conditions.
> **(O3)** *Subjects enter later*—subjects, perception, value, language, and frameworks act on an already-individuated domain.

(O1) is the entry point of much theory and everyday ontology (quantificational logic sets the domain as a set of objects; neo-Aristotelian substance ontology takes substances as prior), though contemporary metaphysics already contains event ontologies, fact ontologies, structural realism, process philosophy, trope theory, and bundle theory as anti-object-fundamentalist lines. Three independent pressures bear on (O1): the indeterminacy of composition and individuation ([van Inwagen 1990]; [Unger 1980]); the suspicion of relata without relations (ontic structural realism, [Ladyman & Ross 2007]); and the scale- or concept-relativity of individuation (Putnam's [1987] conceptual relativity; Hirsch's quantifier variance).

But even granting objects, (O1) cannot explain the *production of givenness*: it offers only reification or reduction, with no third route. This is the bridge from the critique of (O1) to the deeper target. The genuine target is **(O2)**: not "what is basic," but *how object-style determinacy is grounded*. "Object-first" is the entry label; "before objects" is cashed out in the body as *before determinate individuation*.

### 1.4 Relation to the vagueness literature

The determinacy at issue here is *not* the vagueness literature's question of unsharp predicate boundaries or whether objects are fuzzy (semantic vagueness, ontic vagueness, epistemicism; e.g. [Keefe 2000]; [Williamson 1994]; [Barnes 2010]). It is the prior question of object-style determinacy conditions: how an item acquires boundary, identity, and load-bearing such that it can then be vaguely or precisely predicated at all. Semantic and ontic vagueness can be seen as downstream phenomena of cuts that are insufficiently stabilized or multiply and conflictingly anchored; but I neither reduce the vagueness literature nor commit to vague objects or ontic indeterminacy.

### 1.5 Four boundaries

The thesis is easily misread, so four boundaries are fixed at the outset. (i) *Not subjective idealism*: minds do not create the world; what can be constructed is the explanatory cut, while the *cost* of anchoring cannot be waived—a cut that cannot survive intervention, repetition, and cross-operator alignment is at most a local appearance. Construction has cost. (ii) *Not relativism*: reality comes in graded strengths (§5.1); dreams, habits, public institutions, and physical law are not equally real. (iii) *No hidden world*: the pre-objectual condition that does the explanatory work (written `L_0`) is not a warehouse of objects behind appearances, nor a Kantian unknowable noumenon, but only a non-neutrality over the space of possible cuts (§3.2). I use `L_0` throughout only as shorthand for *sub-determinate non-neutrality*—the fact that not all possible cuts are equally stabilizable—not as a name for an external system or a second domain. (iv) *Not a theory of everything*: value, subjecthood, consciousness, and politics are out of scope; each strong claim carries a withdrawal condition.

## 2. The Two-Stage Argument

The argument has two stages of deliberately different strength. Stage A (a weak transcendental regress) yields only a modest but secure conclusion: object-first ontology owes an *explanatory debt*. Stage B (a defeasible inference to the best explanation) argues that the debt is best discharged by constrained selectability. The ontological bite is earned by Stage B, not smuggled in as a theorem of Stage A.

### 2.1 Stage A: the regress to an explanatory debt

1. Any object-style determinate item *x* (bearing D1 boundary + D2 identity + D4 load-bearing) requires individuating conditions: a discernible boundary, identity conditions, and a scale–perspective that fixes "this-one / not-this-one."
2. For any non-primitive, non-self-evident determinate item *x*, its determinacy cannot be explained merely by another *same-type* determinate item *y*; otherwise the explanation only transfers determinacy from *x* to *y* without discharging what is to be explained.
3. *(Category-invariance.)* Re-describing *y* as a relation, structure, property, or trope does not change the situation, since these too are determinate only insofar as they have discriminability and applicability boundaries. The pressure is on *determinacy as such*, not on objecthood.
4. *(What "same-type" means.)* A same-type determinate item is any explanans whose explanatory role already presupposes the individuated boundary, identity, and *role-applicability* conditions of the sort at issue. ("Role-applicability" here is *not* D3 semantic-applicability as a target; it is only the minimal applicability an explanans needs to play its explanatory role.) Hence an opponent cannot escape by relabeling the explanans "structure/property/relation/trope": if the determinacy it relies on is exactly the kind the explanandum needs, it is same-type, and the debt is unpaid. A genuinely *different*-type explanans must *not* presuppose that determinacy—which is precisely the role `L_0` is meant to play (§3.2).
5. Therefore, if object-first ontology sets object-style determinacy as a starting point requiring no explanation, it *lacks an explanation of the conditions of determinacy*. This is the **explanatory debt**.

It is important what Stage A does *not* do. It does not deductively conclude that determinacy is non-fundamental. A naturalness realist may posit basic, primitive determinate items that carry their own individuation conditions (natural joints, basic substances or essences, determinate structural facts), claiming to have discharged the debt at that point. Stage A does not refute this by "regress on pain of incoherence"—that would beg the question. Stage A establishes only the disjunction: *either* accept basic primitive determinate items (and bear their cost) *or* leave the debt outstanding. Which is preferable is handed to Stage B's comparison of explanatory power.

This also shows in what sense calling it a "debt" is not question-begging. The term does not presuppose that whatever is fundamental must be explained—the naturalness realist is right that brute fundamentals can be explanatorily innocent. The debt's force is *earned* downstream, by Stage B's exhibiting *systematic* explananda—the co-variation of givenness, inheritance-lag, and cross-operator divergence with R3/`L_2` (§2.2)—that brute determinate primitives leave as unexplained coincidence. Absent such explananda the naturalness realist's "fundamentals need no explanation" would stand unrebutted; it is the systematic phenomena, not the regress, that convert a bare option into a debt.

### 2.2 Stage B: inference to the best explanation

Stage A leaves a debt and an open question: what best discharges the conditions of determinacy? Three candidates are on the table: (i) Sider/Lewis unique operator-independent natural joints; (ii) Hirsch/Carnap framework-internal convention; (iii) selection realism's constrained selectability—operator-relative cuts that pay resistance and are stabilized and inherited.

To prevent "unification" from collapsing into mere aesthetic preference, I fix six comparison criteria in advance: **(C1)** primitive burden (how many unexplained basic items are posited); **(C2)** explanatory unification (does one mechanism cover many phenomena, and does the unification yield new testable consequences); **(C3)** scope (givenness, scale-relativity, stabilization, inheritance); **(C4)** counterfactual/differential prediction (does it predict observable differences rather than retrofit); **(C5)** path-dependence (can it explain lag and historical sedimentation); **(C6)** cross-operator correction (can it explain alignment and divergence patterns). The governing principle: *unification counts as a theoretical virtue only insofar as it yields differential predictions (C2→C4), not by parsimony alone.*

The claim is that (iii) is the best explanation—defeasibly—on the strength of work that (i) and (ii) cannot do without remainder. The following table compares the three across five phenomena; each row carries a failure condition, so the comparison is checkable by a referee.

| Phenomenon | Naturalness realism (Sider/Lewis) | Deflationism (Hirsch/Carnap) | Selection realism | Differential prediction (C4) | Failure condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Givenness | explained at derivative cognitive/semantic levels | treated as framework-relative stability | anchoring after a cut recedes into the background | givenness-strength varies measurably with `L_2` sedimentation history (labels lag affordances) | givenness uncorrelated with sedimentation history |
| Scale/perspective relativity | judged derivative, owing a further derivation story | framework choice, no deep fact | multiple operator-cuts are natively relative | divergence patterns track R1–R4, not randomness | divergence pattern independent of R1–R4, purely random |
| Intervention-collapse (differential stability) | referred to causal/natural structure | referred to practical convenience | resistance (`Psi_f`) pays for differential stability | collapse pattern measurable and *framework-independent* | collapse varies with framework renaming |
| Inheritance-lag (path dependence) | single joint predicts no lag | convention predicts no lag | `L_2` hardening | revision leaves a *path-dependent* cost-redistribution and lag signature | revision leaves no lag, freely resettable |
| Cross-operator divergence/alignment | a unique joint predicts convergence | no cross-framework fact within a framework | R2 alignment + R3 anchor | alignment/divergence stratifies by R3 strength (high R3 converges, low R3 institution-relative) | alignment/divergence independent of R3 |

*Why unification is a virtue here, not an aesthetic.* The opponent can of course accept "dispersed explanation," and dispersion is not in itself ugly. The sharper point is that these five phenomena *co-vary empirically*: for a given cut, the strength of its givenness, its scale-divergence pattern, its intervention-collapse, its inheritance-lag, and its cross-operator alignment are bound together and move with the same `L_2` history. The testable form of the co-variation (connecting to §5.4): when a cut's R3 is high, its divergence should converge under intervention evidence and its revision should appear mainly as *boundary redrawing and case re-judgment*; when R3 is low but R2/R4 are high, the same cut's stability is borne mainly by institution and inheritance, and revision appears mainly as *lagged redistribution across institutional eligibility, identity narratives, labels, and treatment paths*. A division-of-labor explanation assigns these phenomena to mutually incommensurable domains (cognitive science, semantics, social practice, causal structure) and therefore *does not predict their co-variation with R3/`L_2`*; selection realism predicts the co-variation from a single cut–anchor–stabilize–inherit structure. **Withdrawal condition**: if the co-variation is falsified (the items vary independently, with no dependence on R3/`L_2`), the unification argument fails and selection realism narrows.

### 2.3 The output proposition and manifestational priority

> **(A, debt)** Object-style determinacy requires explanation: it cannot be discharged merely by a same-type determinate item.
> **(B, abduction)** The best way to discharge it is constrained selectability.
> Together: **selection conditions are manifestationally prior to determinate existence**. *If the Stage B abduction succeeds*, object/determinacy-first ontology has misplaced a grounded result as a foundation; if the abduction is defeated, the claim narrows to the defensive version (object-style determinacy at least owes an explanatory debt). Every "misplaced foundation" claim hangs under the abductive antecedent; none is asserted unconditionally.

On the nature of this priority I take the *weak route*. The contribution of this paper lies in selection-first determinacy, not in grounding theory; to reduce length and reviewing risk, I claim manifestational priority as a *constitutive explanation*: a determinate item can appear and bear weight as a determinate item in dependence on the cutting, anchoring, and stabilization of selection conditions. I do *not* use strong grounding language or commit to its formal features (asymmetry, transitivity, non-circularity, the partial/full distinction). The restraint has a reason: `L_2` stabilization, historical sedimentation, and the bootstrapping model (§3.3) involve feedback and hysteresis that may be in tension with standard grounding's non-circularity and asymmetry requirements. For a reader who accepts grounding, the constitutive explanation can be read as a grounding-friendly articulation; the full alignment with the grounding literature (e.g. [Fine 2012]) is optional further work, not load-bearing here.

## 3. The Selection-Realist Model

Section 2 leaves a direction: object-style determinacy is best understood as the achievement of constrained selectability. This section gives the positive model. Throughout, the mechanism is tied to the grounding claim: the following processes are constitutive conditions *on which* determinacy stands, not the causal history of something already determinate.

### 3.1 Selection, its minimal conditions, and three phases

"Selection" here does not mean an agent choosing from a menu.

> **Selection** =df constrained differential actualization—*not* intentional choosing by an already-individuated agent—whose phases are **cut, anchor, stabilize**.

To prevent the term from over-generalizing (otherwise every causal filtering, information compression, evolutionary process, classification, or institutional sedimentation becomes "selection," and explanatory power drops), I impose minimal conditions. A process counts as selection only if: **(S1)** there are multiple actualizable but non-equivalent manifestation paths; **(S2)** some paths are more easily stabilized owing to resistance, cost, or constraint; **(S3)** the actualized path produces *inheritable* downstream conditions; **(S4)** those downstream conditions in turn *pre-trim* the future selection space. Only when S1–S4 jointly hold is a process the structure "differential actualization–anchoring–inheritance–pre-trimming," rather than "some difference merely occurred." Selection in this sense is widespread but *not universal*—which avoids the over-generalization of calling all happening "selection."

The three phases: **cut** separates "this-one" from "not-this-one" and confers a recognizable operable format, while also being exclusion—the excluded becomes downstream friction that alters future cost distribution. **Anchor** gives a manifestation a position in the terrain: to appear is not to remain; reality-weight accrues only when a manifestation becomes a condition that subsequent selection must face. **Stabilize** lets repeated anchoring recede into the background, sedimenting into habit, language, norm, institution, and model. The non-trivial linkage: anchoring brings *irreversibility* (withdrawal can cancel a surface result but not the altered terrain), irreversibility accumulates into *reality-thickness*, thickness recedes into the background as *order*, and order *pre-trims* subsequent selection.

> **Three layers.** `L_0`: the non-neutral comparative order over cut-space (§3.2). `L_1`: post-selection manifestation/anchoring events (not a second substance). `L_2`: the stabilized history of selection (not automatically legitimate).

### 3.2 `L_0`: a comparative-topological model

If we do not appeal to basic primitive determinate items, where does the regress terminate? I fix a single primary model for `L_0` and do not let it slide: a *comparative-topological* model.

> **`L_0`** =df a non-neutrality over the space of possible cuts: some cuts are more stabilizable, lower-resistance, and more inheritable than others. `L_0` contains no determinate item (object/property/structure/relation); it is a *comparative order over possible cuts*, not an individuated property. It is **sub-determinate but not null**.

Two predicates must be distinguished: *determinate* = having individuated boundary + identity conditions; *non-neutral* = having comparative non-uniformity sufficient to make cuts non-equivalent. `L_0` is non-neutral without being individuated. This dissolves a dilemma: `L_0` is neither fully determinate (it contains no determinate item, so the regress does not restart) nor fully null (it contains a non-trivial comparative order, so there is something to constrain). Is the comparative order itself already determinate? What is ordered are possible cuts; the non-uniformity on which the ordering rests is *functional*—it exists only in the manifestable difference "some cuts are more easily stabilized," not as an independent determinate structure. Its determination is precisely the achievement of cut–anchor–stabilize.

An analogy and its limits. A *misleading* analogy is the complex plane: a quantity on the imaginary axis is not on the real axis yet affects subsequent transformations. It misleads because the complex plane is a *fully determinate* structure (i has exact identity conditions), whereas `L_0` is not; on that analogy `L_0` would be read as "a second determinate dimension behind appearances," the hidden world/noumenon rejected in §4.3. A better (also merely heuristic) analogy is a *slope-field over a landscape not yet partitioned into watersheds*: the slope makes some drainage-cuts more stable and lower-cost, but the slope is not itself a set of demarcated basins; the basins (determinate objects) are not given—the slope (non-neutrality) makes some partitions low-resistance achievements. The limit of the terrain analogy: a real landscape is already a determinate physical structure, while `L_0` is not.

A semi-formal display (a representational device, *not* a determinate-domain claim). Let **C** be cut-space, the space of possible differentiations (not presupposed as an individuated set of objects); let *c₁, c₂, … ∈ C* be candidate cuts; let **R(c)** be the resistance a cut meets under intervention/repetition/cross-operator alignment; **S(c)** its tendency to be anchored and recede; **H(c)** its tendency to sediment into downstream constraint. Then `L_0` *is not a subset of C* (not a set of objects) but the *non-uniformity of R, S, H over C*—their non-constancy across cuts, which makes some cuts non-equivalent. Two guardrails must be stated with the notation, or it will smuggle in determinacy: (i) C is not claimed to be a determinate domain—its elements' determinacy is an achievement of anchoring, not a given; the notation merely *displays* non-uniformity, it does not impose a metric on a pre-given domain. (ii) R/S/H are functional differentials, not determinate structures; reading them as determinate functions on C is a representational idealization whose only ontological commitment is "the comparative order is non-trivial."

> **Counterfactual-differential objectivity.** A sub-determinate condition has *counterfactual-differential objectivity* iff different admissible operations would generate non-equivalent stabilization and inheritance profiles, even though no determinate relata are yet individuated at the base level.

This is the objectivity `L_0` has—not object-style objectivity. Three objections, handled directly:

- **(O-a) Determinization.** If the comparative order can order, is it not already a determinate structure, restarting the regress? Reply: distinguish object-style determinacy (individuated boundary + identity) from counterfactual-differential objectivity. `L_0` has only the latter: it contains no item that can be tracked and re-identified as the same; it contains only a counterfactual difference—cutting *c* rather than *c′* yields different downstream stabilization/inheritance costs. Ordering does not presuppose that the ordered items are individuated, just as a slope-field's "steeper/shallower" does not presuppose demarcated basins. `L_0` is *not a structure but the non-uniformity of structured possibility*.
- **(O-b) Vacuity.** If the comparative order is not a determinate structure, how can it *constrain*? Reply: constraint does not require the constrainer to be a determinate object. `L_0` constrains by making some cuts' anchoring/inheritance costs systematically lower—a constraint cashed out in the manifestable difference "some cuts collapse under intervention, some persist" (§3.4). A cut-space with no non-uniformity would make all cuts equicostly, hence no differential stability, no givenness, no graded reality-strength; we observe these, so `L_0` is non-vacuous. Vacuity and determinization form a pincer; the position sits exactly between—enough to constrain (non-vacuous), not enough to individuate (non-determinate).
- **(O-c) Modal base, and the relata/truthmaker worry.** What gives the "possibility" of the possible-cut space C? If natural law, we return to a naturalness base; if operator ability, we lose operator-independence. Reply: read C's modality as *the counterfactual space of operations relative to `L_0` non-neutrality and sedimented `L_2`*—not actual operator ability, not bare logical conceivability, not a fully given book of natural law. And on the relata: before a cut is determined, what fixes the relata of "cutting *c* rather than *c′*"? The *c*, *c′* are *not object-style items* but *operational differential paths*; the counterfactual is not a comparison of two already-determinate objects but of two operational profiles' stabilization costs. Its truthmaker is not a pair of individuated relata but `L_0`'s non-neutrality itself—the *failure of operation-equivalence across possible stabilization histories*. The non-neutrality is not *inferred from* already-determinate relata but *displayed by* the ineliminable difference among admissible operations. If one insists a counterfactual must have object-style relata as truthmaker, then `L_0` reduces to an explanatory device and selection realism narrows to an interface reading (§5.4). On the reading defended here, operational profiles suffice as truthmaker and no narrowing follows; a truthmaker theorist who refuses operational profiles that role will instead read `L_0` as an explanatory device—again the interface reading of §5.4. I mark this as a fork in the dialectic rather than a closed objection: the position is defensible, not demonstrated.

### 3.3 "Selection needs a selector"

> *Objection*: if *x*'s determinacy is grounded in "selection," and selection needs an operator that does the cutting, and the operator is a determinate object, then the regress is covertly restarted.

Three replies. **(i)** An operator need not be a basic determinate object; it is a *constraint profile* (`theta`). That a cut is "operated" does not entail a pre-given "cutter." **(ii)** Operators are themselves grounded: any stable operator (subject, instrument) is a sedimented history of selection (`L_2`), so explaining determinacy via operators is not a vicious circle but *bootstrapping*. **(iii)** The regress terminates in the cut-space non-neutrality of §3.2, not in a first object and not in a hidden world.

A residual *tu quoque* must be met head-on, since reply (iii) invites it: does selection realism not simply trade one primitive (determinate joints) for another (a non-neutral cut-space), so that the charge of an unexplained starting point rebounds on it? Three asymmetries answer the charge. First, `L_0` non-neutrality is a *sub-determinate* primitive: it carries no individuated boundary or identity, so—unlike primitive determinate joints—it does not restart the Stage A individuation regress (§2.1). It is the *cheaper* posit, not a like-for-like swap. Second, it is a *single* primitive that predicts the empirical co-variation of five phenomena (§2.2), whereas primitive determinate joints discharge those explananda only by dispersing them across mutually independent layers, leaving their co-variation unexplained; lower primitive burden together with wider differential reach is exactly the theory-choice profile Stage B was built to adjudicate (C1, C2→C4). Third, the posit carries a withdrawal condition (§5.4): if a naturalness base can recover the co-variation without operator-relativity, `L_0` is demoted to an explanatory device—whereas a brute determinate joint is offered as needing no such accountability. The asymmetry is therefore not "primitive versus no primitive" but "a weaker, more unified, self-limiting primitive versus a stronger, dispersed, unconditional one."

### 3.4 Filtering, resistance, and the realist core

The most dangerous slide—reading "determinacy grounded in selection" as "minds project the world"—is blocked by cost. An operator (`theta`, a constraint profile) *filters*; a manifestation must *pay resistance* (`Psi_f`).

> **`Psi_f`** =df the resistance a candidate selection persistently meets under prediction, intervention, repetition, and cross-operator alignment. What can be constructed is the explanatory cut; what cannot be waived is the anchoring cost.

The realist core, stated precisely: selection realism is realist about minimal non-neutrality and resistance in the sense that they are *not created by, nor exhausted by, any given operator's projection*; what is operator-relative is the determinate cut. This is steadier than "operator-independent": it does not claim non-neutrality/resistance is a ready-made structure free of all operators (which would slide back into object-first), only that no single operator can create or exhaust them.

Three kinds of resistance must be kept apart, lest `Psi_f` over-broaden: (i) physical/intervention resistance (a shattered cup cannot hold water); (ii) social/institutional resistance (institutions resist reclassification); (iii) semantic/practical resistance (the cost of conceptual revision). All are resistance, but the anti-relativist function is borne mainly by type-(i) R3—only its counterfactual constraint cannot be freely reset by any operator group's agreement. This yields a middle formula, more precise than "operator-independent" and harder than "not exhausted by a single operator":

> *Resistance is not operator-independent as a ready-made structure, but it is operator-transcendent in the limited sense that no actual or possible operator-profile can freely reset its comparative constraints.*

This blocks the strong-constructivist reply ("collective practice creates it"): a collective `L_2` can *move* the cost distribution but cannot *freely reset* type-(i) R3's counterfactual constraint. Social reality-strength (high R1/R2/R4) and physical resistance (R3) are thereby separable (§5).

## 4. Rival Views and Non-Absorption

This section does not survey; it cuts against the nearest neighbors, answering "selection realism is just a renaming." The differentiation rests on the account selection realism gives of how cuts acquire constraint, stabilization, and inheritance.

### 4.1 Sider and Lewis

Sider ([2011]) holds that reality has a privileged *structure*, set as a primitive, cross-categorial notion—a generalization of Lewis's ([1983]) "perfectly natural properties" carving nature at the joints. A preliminary distinction: the uniqueness of naturalness *about basic structure* does not directly entail the uniqueness of *ordinary-object cuts*—Sider's privileged structure does not by itself yield a unique domain of ordinary objects, nor do Lewis's perfectly natural properties entail a unique everyday object-partition.

The weaker, primary critique: naturalness realism may grant the scale-relativity of higher/ordinary/social/functional objects and hand them to derivative structure, reference magnetism, semantics, or practice—but this is precisely to *disperse* the explanation of object-style determinacy across physical, semantic, psychological, and practical layers; selection realism offers a *unified* selection–stabilization account. This is a comparison on theory-choice criteria (unification, primitive burden, scope), not a dogmatic refutation: selection realism does not refute naturalness realism by demanding an explanation it refuses to give; it offers an alternative with lower primitive burden and greater explanatory reach regarding scale-relative individuation, givenness, stabilization, and inheritance.

The pinpoint hinge: Sider sets structure as primitive ideology. Selection realism *accepts* the concession that basic structure may be primitive, but observes that primitive structure does not by itself *predict the formation pattern* of object-style determinacy (D1+D2+D4)—the variation of givenness-strength with sedimentation history, the inheritance-lag under revision, the stratification of cross-operator divergence by R3 strength (§2.2). Lewis's naturalness can, via reference magnetism (Lewis [1984]), explain why reference "gravitates" to more natural properties, but reference magnetism does not explain the *cost structure* of a cut being anchored, repaired, or abandoned (§4.2). The disagreement is not over whether there is a primitive base, but over what explains the *formation pattern* of D1+D2+D4. The stronger critique (that naturalness, even with derivative layers, cannot explain the generation of determinacy *as* determinacy, since it must somewhere presuppose determinate joints) is sharper but harder to establish, and is left as future work.

*Why primitive structure does not yet explain object-style determinacy.* To press the point past mere position-taking, grant the naturalness realist the entire concession. Let basic structure be primitive (Sider's privileged ideology), and let ordinary, social, and functional objects be *derivative*—settled at some non-fundamental level rather than carved at a perfectly natural joint (one can defend ordinary objects without requiring privileged joints; cf. [Thomasson 2007]). Even then the question selection realism presses is untouched: by what process does a *derivative* object acquire D1+D2+D4—operable boundary, re-identifiable identity, practical load-bearing? "Derivative" names a rank in a dependence ordering; it does not name a *mechanism of formation*. Naturalness underwrites reference magnetism—reference gravitates toward more natural properties—and magnetism explains why some terms latch more stably than others. But magnetism is a fact about word–world fit *given an already-determinate property*; it does not explain the cost structure exhibited when a cut is first anchored, later repaired, and sometimes abandoned. These are different explananda. The selection-realist claim is therefore not "fewer primitives, hence better"—a parsimony slogan the naturalness realist can simply match—but that one cut–anchor–stabilize–inherit mechanism *predicts a co-variation* that dispersed derivation does not: where R3 intervention-resistance is high, revision should converge under intervention evidence and leave a *short* inheritance-lag; where R3 is low but R2/R4 are high, the same revision should leave a *long*, path-dependent lag borne by institution and identity (§2.2, §5.3). Naturalness-plus-derivation can accommodate either pattern after the fact, but it does not predict their *linkage* to a cut's R3/`L_2` profile, because it assigns givenness, reference-stability, and institutional sedimentation to mutually independent layers. That linkage, not primitive-count, is the hinge.

### 4.2 Hirsch and Carnap

Hirsch's ([2011]) quantifier variance and Carnap's ([1950]) internal/external distinction reply that there is no deep fact about "*the* cut": choose a linguistic framework, let it hold internally, and the question of how determinacy is grounded is a pseudo-question.

The reply must avoid reading deflationism as "denying all factual difference." Selection realism does not deny that deflationary frameworks can state internal truths. Its objection is that the cost of adopting, maintaining, shifting, repairing, or abandoning a cut is not itself exhausted by framework-internal truth conditions. The argument skeleton (bridging to §5): (i) framework-internal truth conditions explain *when a proposition is true*; (ii) but they do not explain how a cut acquires *load-bearing capacity* (becomes a determinate item); (iii) load-bearing capacity shows up as a *cost structure* in intervention, repair, inheritance, and cross-operator correction; (iv) hence anchoring cost is not an external pragmatic cost of adopting a framework but a *constitutive condition* of object-style determinacy—precisely the layer Carnap's linguistic framework and Hirsch's quantifier variance leave untouched.

This is the genuine disagreement: if anchoring cost is fully externalized as pragmatic cost, one cannot explain why some cuts collapse under intervention (a broken cup, regardless of what we call it), why cost distribution is *path-dependent* (a repair history changes later identity judgments) rather than freely resettable with the framework, and why R3 resistance is *independent of any single framework's choice*. These are conditions on whether a cut can keep bearing weight as a determinate item—constitutive conditions of determinacy, not its external accompaniment.

*Anchoring cost as constitutive, not merely pragmatic.* The deflationist has a natural rejoinder: granted there are adoption costs, but these are *pragmatic* costs of choosing a language, external to the truth-conditional content a framework fixes—Carnap's own line between the practical question of *adopting* a framework and the theoretical questions *internal* to it. The reply turns precisely on this boundary. (i) Framework-internal truth-conditions settle when a sentence counts as true *once a cut is in place*; they presuppose, and so cannot explain, that the cut is already load-bearing. (ii) Whether a cut is load-bearing is not settled by stipulation; it is exhibited in what the cut costs under intervention, repair, inheritance, and cross-operator correction—costs that are not freely resettable by re-choosing the framework. (iii) A cost that cannot be reset by reframing is, by the deflationist's own lights, no longer a merely pragmatic cost of language-choice but a constraint on whether the chosen language *has determinate items to quantify over at all*. (iv) Hence anchoring cost belongs to the *constitution* of object-style determinacy, not to its external pragmatics: quantifier variance can vary *what* we count, but not the differential cost-structure by which some counts stay anchored and others collapse. The disagreement with Hirsch is thus not over whether internal truths obtain—they do—but over whether the anchoring cost that makes a cut load-bearing is itself a framework-internal convention. Selection realism says it is not: it is the layer at which frameworks are answerable to `L_0` non-neutrality and sedimented `L_2`.

### 4.3 Barad

Barad ([2007]) holds that determinacy is produced by an *agential cut* within *intra-action*, and that *phenomena* are the basic ontological units—on the surface almost a synonym for selection realism. I accept the core direction: determinacy is constituted through cutting, and boundaries are not pre-given. I rewrite the agential cut as a constrained selection that pays `Psi_f`. To stay fair and sharp, I keep one strongest difference: *Barad gives an ontology of cut-dependent phenomena; selection realism adds a general account of how cuts acquire comparative resistance, cross-operator rankability, and inheritable reality-thickness.*

Three textual hinges (page references to Barad 2007). **(1) Core definitions**: *phenomena* are the ontological inseparability of intra-acting agencies, the basic ontological units (33, 139); *apparatuses* are material-discursive boundary-making practices, not mere observational instruments (140–148); the *agential cut* enacts a contingent resolution of the ontological indeterminacy *within* the phenomenon (140); objectivity rests on *agential separability*—"exteriority-within-phenomena" (175). **(2) The difference in objectivity**: Barad's account ties determinacy and objectivity to specific material-discursive apparatuses (objectivity is *agential separability* enacted within a phenomenon); selection realism adds a further *comparative* question across apparatuses—*cross-operator rankability* and *inheritable reality-thickness*, whereby the resistance of cuts can be compared and ranked across apparatuses/operators and sediments as `L_2` hardening. Barad gives "cutting produces determinacy within an apparatus"; selection realism gives "how cuts acquire comparable resistance and historical thickness across apparatuses." **(3) Whether `Psi_f` is absorbable**: I judge that it is not, without remainder—`Psi_f` requires a cross-apparatus, rankable general measure of resistance, whereas Barad deliberately binds objectivity to the specific apparatus (a core commitment of her onto-epistemology); introducing a cross-apparatus rankable `Psi_f` would change the ontological structure (from apparatus-bound objectivity to comparative-inheritance objectivity). So selection realism is not a renaming of agential realism but a substantive modification of its "apparatus-relative" commitment.

### 4.4 Other neighbors, briefly

*Ontic structural realism* ([Ladyman & Ross 2007]): I accept the demotion of object-fundamentality (structure first), but rewrite structure as itself a stabilized selection-result (`L_2`) rather than an ungrounded floor—OSR retains (O2) by treating structural facts as pre-given, and §2.1(3) shows that swapping the base for relations or structure does not dissolve the determinacy question. *Process philosophy* (Whitehead, Seibt): I accept becoming-before-substance but add operators, `Psi_f`, withdrawal conditions, and an accountable agency-logic, constraining process metaphysics into a checkable structure. *Kant*: I accept that experience has conditions but replace fixed a priori categories with a dynamic, revisable, embodied, historically sedimented constraint `theta`; the crucial cut is `L_0` versus the noumenon—the thing-in-itself is a negatively characterized something-or-domain behind appearances, whereas `L_0` is not an object but the non-neutrality of cut-space. There is no unknowable X behind appearances, only cost-paying conditions of selectability.

## 5. Reality-Strength and Disease Classification

### 5.1 Four dimensions

If everything selected is real, are dreams, hallucinations, institutions, and physical law equally real? Reply: *for object-style determinacy, reality-strength shows up as differences in anchoring stability* (not degrees of being in any general ontological sense). It must be characterized along four dimensions rather than a single linear rank.

> **Reality-strength** =df the strength of determinate anchoring along four dimensions: **(R1)** *manifestational stability*—does the manifestation recur; **(R2)** *cross-operator alignment*—can it be aligned across operators, instruments, contexts; **(R3)** *intervention-resistance*—does it hold under intervention; **(R4)** *inheritance/hardening*—does it sediment into downstream constraint (path dependence, lag).

E1–E4 are typical *combinations* of these, not a strict ladder: E1 local manifestation (low R1 only, e.g. dreams/hallucinations); E2 stable reality (R1 + partial R4, e.g. habits); E3 cross-operator reality (R1+R2+R4, e.g. institutions/public facts); E4 paradigm physical reality (all four high, R3 robust under possible intervention, e.g. physical law). Reality-strength is not degrees of *being* but the strength of *determinate anchoring*—which avoids the "being comes in degrees" controversy. Thus, *for object-style determinacy*, objectivity shows up as stable alignment under resistance and cross-operator correction; I do not here redefine truth in general, only how the objectivity of object-style determinacy is exhibited.

### 5.2 The modal basis of "possible intervention"

R3 takes a dispositional criterion: a structure's disposition to resist under *possible* intervention, repetition, measurement, and cross-operator correction, not its current consensus magnitude. The modal space of "possible intervention" must be specified, or R3 loses ontological weight. It is *not* current technical ability (else unobserved facts lose independent R3), *not* bare logical conceivability (else it is too weak), *not* a prior reduction to a complete book of natural law (else R3 collapses into a naturalness base); it is *the counterfactual space of operations relative to `L_0` non-neutrality and sedimented `L_2`*. A cut has high R3 iff it robustly resists within this counterfactual operational space.

This avoids circularity by being an *explicit bootstrap* (an explanatory interlock), not a vicious circle: possible intervention is *jointly constrained* by sub-determinate non-neutrality and inherited stabilization profiles. "Jointly constrained" is not circular definition—it restricts the modal space to *manifestable differences*: R3 and `L_0` are not mutually defined but co-determined on the shared measure "manifestable difference in stabilization cost." If this interlock can be shown to reduce to a one-way determination by natural law, R3 collapses into a naturalness base and selection realism narrows (§5.4).

Four boundary cases follow naturally. *Unobserved facts* can have high R3 (robust under possible intervention) even with current R2 zero. *Stable ideology/myth* can have high R1/R2/R4 (social reality-strength) without its claims *about the physical world* thereby being true: social reality-strength and physical truth must be distinguished. *Scientific revolution*: an old theory may have had high R1/R2/R4 descriptive standing while the resistance-structure (R3) it aimed at was not actually hit—distinguish "theory stability" from "stability of the resistance hit." *Mathematical/logical objects*: R2/R4 high while physical-intervention R3 does not directly apply; the physical reading of E1–E4 does not transfer directly to mathematics, a scope limitation.

### 5.3 Worked example: disease classification

The cup of §1 is good for intuition but too easily absorbed by affordance theory or ordinary-language philosophy. Disease classification bears more weight, because it simultaneously involves natural resistance (R3), classificatory practice, cross-operator stability, historical inheritance, and intervention effects. The general prediction: diagnostic categories show trackable differences along R1–R4; and when a category is revised (a syndrome split or merged), selection realism predicts not mere lexical replacement but a *patterned redistribution of anchoring cost*—diagnostic hesitation, re-judgment of past cases, changes in treatment paths, shifts in patient identity and institutional eligibility—whose pattern depends on the specific R1–R4 combination. Two contrasting real revisions instantiate this.

*High R3: peptic ulcer → Helicobacter pylori infection.* After Marshall and Warren ([1984]; Nobel Prize 2005), peptic ulcer was re-cut from a "stress/acid" framework into a category with bacterial infection as principal cause. On the selection-realist reading this cut has *strong R3*: antibiotic eradication therapy works robustly under intervention, and the cure pattern is *framework-independent*. The predicted cost-redistribution is dominated by *boundary redrawing + case re-judgment + treatment-path switch* (acid-suppression → eradication), not primarily by institutional eligibility; R2 alignment converges rapidly with intervention evidence.

*Low R3 / high R2·R4: Asperger's syndrome → Autism Spectrum Disorder (DSM-5, [APA 2013]).* This revision merged a standalone label into a spectrum. On the reading, this cut has *weaker R3* (no single intervention-robust natural joint) but *high R2/R4* (borne by institution and inheritance). The predicted cost-redistribution is dominated by *institutional eligibility changes, identity narratives (the "Aspie" identity), service/education eligibility, research-cohort redefinition, and label/treatment-path lag*, not by intervention-driven case re-judgment.

Laid side by side along the four dimensions, the two revisions display systematically different anchoring profiles:

| Dimension | Peptic ulcer → *H. pylori* | Asperger's → ASD |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** manifestational stability | symptoms and clinical presentation recur stably | presentation recurs, but category boundaries are more continuous |
| **R2** cross-operator alignment | endoscopy, culture, and treatment-response align rapidly | clinical, educational, familial, and identity-community operators align more loosely |
| **R3** intervention-resistance | high—antibiotic eradication yields a robust intervention-anchor | low/diffuse—no single mechanism locks the anchor |
| **R4** inheritance/hardening | re-judgment of past cases, treatment-path reorganization | label-lag, service eligibility, identity narrative, research-cohort redefinition |
| **Selection realism predicts** | revision appears mainly as a natural-resistance structure being *re-hit* | revision appears mainly as *redistribution* of institutional/identity/clinical anchoring cost |

Rival accounts each capture part of this: HPC ([Boyd 1991], [1999]) captures causal homeostasis; Khalidi ([2013]) intervention-supporting kinds; Haslanger ([2012]) and Hacking ([1999]) social construction and the looping/institutional reality of human kinds; Epstein ([2015]) the metaphysics of social facts; medical pragmatism goal-relative classification (on psychiatric classification specifically, [Cooper 2005]; [Kendler et al. 2011]). Selection realism's additional claim is not that these are false, but that *revision costs distribute according to R1–R4 profiles*—so that, for the same kind of classification revision, the cost-redistribution form differs systematically between high-R3 and low-R3 cuts. This is what the side-by-side table contributes: the two cases are predicted to differ not in *whether* revision incurs cost but in *where* the cost lands—case re-judgment and treatment-path switch for high R3, institutional and identity redistribution for low R3 / high R2·R4. Two cases cannot, however, by themselves *establish* a co-variation: the cases differ in many respects at once, so a single contrast underdetermines which difference does the work, and two data points cannot fix a law. The table therefore provides a *test schema* rather than a finished test. Selection realism predicts the *shape* of the difference, and the schema is discharged or defeated by a *within-domain gradient*—a series of higher- and lower-R3 revisions *inside one classificatory system*, whose redistribution profiles are then compared. The **withdrawal condition** is correspondingly concrete: if such a gradient shows no R3-dependent difference in where revision cost lands—if revisions distribute their costs across case re-judgment, treatment paths, institutional eligibility, identity labels, and research cohorts in the *same* proportions regardless of R3—then R3 does no work in this domain and the four-dimensional model narrows accordingly.

Two safeguards against oversimplification. First, the *H. pylori* case is *not* a naïve-realist story of "discover the bacterium → classification fully determined by natural fact": even with high R3, natural resistance must still be stabilized into object-style determinacy through diagnostic practice, treatment paths, case re-judgment, and operator correction; high R3 means this stabilization is *more constrained by intervention evidence*, not that it bypasses stabilization. Second, the "low R3" of Asperger's → ASD does *not* mean "no natural resistance" or "purely socially constructed": its natural, behavioral, clinical, institutional, and identity dimensions heavily overlap; the anchoring cost of revision falls more on classificatory boundaries, identity labels, service eligibility, clinical communication, and historical inheritance—this does not make the classification unreal but locates its reality-strength more in R2/R4 and D3 feedback.

### 5.4 Self-limitation and conclusion

Selection realism attaches to each bold claim a level, cost, threshold, failure mode, and withdrawal condition. This is argumentative discipline: a metaphysical claim that cannot say what would narrow it does not deserve to be treated as a claim. The **principal withdrawal condition**: if some physicalist or naturalness account can explain, without remainder, manifestation, anchoring, first-person access, update-cost, givenness, and differential stability, then selection realism should narrow from a meta-ontology to an interface/compression framework. Two qualifications: this would be a *narrowing of scope rather than a total refutation*; and "without remainder" is an ideal limit, approached in practice by checkable intermediate milestones (can a naturalness account recover the scale-relativity of individuation without introducing operator-relativity? can it predict the lag signature of `L_2` hardening?), not a one-shot binary verdict.

This paper does not prove that selection realism is established, nor that it explains everything. It defends two independently assessable claims. *(Defensive)* Object-style determinacy should not be presupposed without explanation by object-first ontology; selection realism offers a more unified constitutive explanation. *(Extended, supported by the Stage B abduction, defeasible)* If that explanation holds, it supports a stronger selection-first metaphysics—object-style determinacy, and perhaps determinacy in general, is not the ontological floor.

If objects are not philosophy's first fact, philosophy should not begin by asking what exists as a finished thing; it should ask under what conditions of selection, anchoring, friction, and stabilization anything comes to be determinate, load-bearing, and inheritable as reality.
