## 1. Introduction

The continuity of a sentence does not settle the continuity of the research problem organized around it. The continuum-hypothesis question existed before forcing, and its direct-answer core remained `CH` or `not-CH` across the four stages examined here [EVD-D03-0001]. Forcing did not create that sentence-level question and did not enlarge its direct-answer core. Nor should an independence result be treated as a direct yes-or-no answer to CH in a unique universe [EVD-D03-0003]. The starting point of this paper is therefore continuity, not rupture.

The proposed rupture lies elsewhere. This paper argues, in its weak and default form, that forcing reconstructed the research regime organized around CH [EVD-D03-0002; EVD-D05-0001; EVD-D06-0001]. A research regime includes not only the sentence under investigation but also the backgrounds from which work proceeds, the operations regarded as available, the outputs those operations can generate, and the rules by which those outputs are classified as answers, diagnoses, divisions of a problem, or forms of progress. The thesis is not that mathematical truth was historically created. It is that the historically available organization of inquiry can change even while the direct-answer core remains fixed.

That thesis requires a four-stage comparison. The pre-Gödel stage must be reconstructed on its own terms; the Gödel stage must be treated as a distinct intervention rather than folded into an undifferentiated “before forcing”; the Cohen event must be separated from the later institutionalization of forcing; and the mature forcing tradition must be evaluated for inheritance, non-local reuse, and scaffold formation [EVD-D03-0004]. These stages cannot be reduced to a simple before-and-after story without prejudging the central question.

Two further separations govern the argument. First, operation novelty is not institutionalization. Failure of C5-op shows only that a later background–operation–output relation lacks a conservative reproduction in the earlier system. A one-off construction or local technique may satisfy that description. Regime innovation requires, in addition, independent evidence that the operation became heritable, non-local, and scaffold-forming. Second, a change in the CH-local method regime does not by itself establish a reconstruction of the global update regime of set theory. The latter claim requires evidence beyond CH, including cross-problem inheritance and infrastructure [EVD-D06-0002].

The paper also distinguishes a research-regime conclusion from a semantic one. Strong semantic re-individuation would require evidence that the later response-role structure cannot be conservatively translated back into the Gödel-stage role structure. That is the function of C2. Changes in background theory, method, evaluation, or institutional uptake cannot substitute for this evidence. Because the C2 dossier is incomplete, the present draft uses the weak title and does not claim strong semantic re-individuation [EVD-D09-0001].

The contribution is therefore deliberately narrow. Section 2 distinguishes object-level forcing accessibility from the historical institutionalization of an accessibility operation. Section 3 separates semantic question identity from a research-problem regime and states the relevant conservative-translation conditions. Section 4 develops the four-stage case. Section 5 calibrates the criterion against a result innovation and a local technical innovation, both still to be selected through evidence-led screening. Section 6 offers a detachable SRT bridge at P3 level. Section 7 groups the principal objections and their associated withdrawal rules. Sections 8 and 9 state the limited implications and conclusion.

## 2. Forcing Potentialism and the Missing Historical-Practical Level

### 2.1 Object-level accessibility

Forcing and width-potentialist approaches provide the nearest conceptual neighbor to this paper because they analyze relations between a model and its forcing extensions [EVD-D01-0001]. In modal notation, the relevant relation may be represented as

\[
M\preceq_F M[G].
\]

The same core relation can be stated without making a modal vocabulary load-bearing:

\[
R_F(M,N)
\iff
\exists\mathbb P\in M\,
\exists G\subseteq\mathbb P\,
\bigl(
G\text{ is }M\text{-generic for }\mathbb P
\land
N=M[G]
\bigr).
\]

This paper does not attempt to settle which forcing-potentialist semantics is correct, whether strict or loose accounts should be preferred, or whether divergent systems should be subordinated to convergent ones. Any characterization of width, height, class, or arithmetic potentialism remains subject to verification in Dossier D01 [EVD-D01-0002].

### 2.2 From accessibility to an inherited operation

The object-level relation does not by itself answer the historical-practical question. A model may stand in an extension relation to another model without that relation already being an operation that researchers can publicly reconstruct, learn, reuse, and place inside later method chains. The paper's central positioning distinction is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{model accessibility}
\neq
\text{historical institutionalization of an accessibility operation}
}
\]

The left side concerns a structured relation among models, worlds, or formula truth. The right side concerns a mathematical practice: how an operation enters a shared repertoire, how its admissible uses are stabilized, how later work depends on it, and how it changes what counts as a productive response to a problem. The distinction is substantive only if historical evidence shows that the relevant operation was reconstructed, transmitted, reused, and made consequential for subsequent work [EVD-D06-0001]. If the two sides differ only by vocabulary, the paper fails; this distinction is load-bearing rather than an optional positioning claim.

### 2.3 Limited comparison with adjacent approaches

The paper's target is not a survey of potentialisms. Height, class, arithmetic, strict, loose, convergent, and divergent forms matter only insofar as they prevent overgeneralization from forcing accessibility to a single universal access relation [EVD-D01-0003]. Likewise, philosophy of mathematical practice may already explain historical path dependence, method inheritance, and shifting standards of progress [EVD-D01-0004]. The present proposal must earn its narrower contribution by showing how an explicitly reconstructed accessibility operation alters a problem-specific research regime. General claims that mathematics has a history or that practices change are insufficient.

### 2.4 Representation and ontological neutrality

Because the central inference can be stated through `R_F(M,N)`, it does not depend on a uniquely modal presentation. Modal machinery may be illuminating, but its eliminability would not eliminate the historical question. Conversely, a diversity of forcing methods or model extensions does not by itself establish a multiverse ontology [EVD-D01-0005]. Universe and multiverse positions may disagree about truth while still recognizing changes in the methods used to analyze CH, generic extensions, absoluteness, or new principles [EVD-D01-0006]. The research-regime claim is intended to remain neutral between those ontological positions.

## 3. Semantic Question Identity and Research-Problem Regimes

### 3.1 A stable direct-answer core

Let

\[
Q_{CH}=\text{“Is CH true?”}
\]

and let its direct-answer core be

\[
D(Q_{CH})=\{CH,\neg CH\}.
\]

The proposed analysis begins by holding this core fixed. C1 records continuity of the direct answers under translation. C1 is not evidence that the problem changed; it is the condition that prevents the paper from manufacturing change by replacing the original question with a different sentence.

### 3.2 Semantic question identity

The semantic identity of a question is represented as

\[
Q_t^{sem}(Q_\varphi)=\langle D(Q_\varphi),R_t(Q_\varphi)\rangle,
\]

where `D` is the direct-answer core and `R_t` is the response-role structure available at time `t`. Response roles may include direct answer, partial answer, metamathematical diagnosis, dissolution, or problem splitting. Question semantics and inferential erotetics are asked to support only this distinction between direct answers and structured responses [EVD-D02-0001]. They do not, by themselves, show that historical backgrounds, methods, or progress rankings constitute a research regime.

Strong semantic re-individuation requires a non-conservative change in `R_t`. The decisive condition is C2:

\[
\pi_R E_{t+1}(\tau_{\mathcal O}(o))
=
\sigma_R\bigl(\pi_R E_t(o)\bigr),
\]

with a corresponding reverse condition. A later response role without a conservative earlier image would be one strong witness of failure, but non-surjectivity is not stipulated as the only form of non-conservativity. Most importantly, the relevant comparison in this paper is the Gödel stage against institutionalized forcing, not the pre-Gödel stage against an undifferentiated later period.

### 3.3 The research-problem regime

The wider unit of analysis is

\[
Q_t^{research}(Q_\varphi)
=
\langle
Q_t^{sem},B_t,M_t,E_t
\rangle.
\]

Here `B_t` comprises default backgrounds and live competing extensions; `M_t` is the set of methods available for the problem; and `E_t` maps candidate outputs into response roles and progress statuses. This structure distinguishes what an output is from how a practice classifies its contribution. The claim that `B_t`, `M_t`, and `E_t` have constitutive historical force must be supported by evidence from mathematical practice and history, not borrowed from question semantics [EVD-D04-0001].

### 3.4 Conservative translation and operation novelty

Comparing two regimes requires translations of outputs, backgrounds, methods, response roles, and value structures. In the main text, three conditions do most of the work. C1 preserves the direct-answer core. C2 tests response-role conservativity. C5-op tests whether background–operation–output generation is preserved:

\[
(b,m)\leadsto_t o
\Longrightarrow
\bigl(\tau_B(b),\tau_M(m)\bigr)
\leadsto_{t+1}
\tau_{\mathcal O}(o),
\]

with a corresponding reverse requirement.

Failure of C5-op establishes only operation-level novelty. It does not establish regime innovation. Evaluation-order change under C3 and inferential-function change under C4 may corroborate a regime argument, but neither suffices alone. C6 controls the strength of translation through structural quasi-inverses relative to the output equivalence defined in the formal note.

For the historical audit, a conservative reproduction counts only if the translation maps the later relation into a Gödel-stage `B_t`, `M_t`, and `\mathcal O_t` profile documented in Dossiers D03 and D04. The mere retrospective availability of component mathematics or later notation does not itself supply a conservative reproduction. This is a filling discipline for the existing C5-op test, not a new modal criterion and not a substitute for the separate institutionalization filter.

### 3.5 Institutionalization and scope

The institutionalization filter is fixed as

\[
\operatorname{Inst}(m^*)
:=
H(m^*)\land N(m^*)\land S(m^*),
\]

where `H` is heritability, `N` is non-locality, and `S` is scaffold formation. These are conjunctive conditions, not a score. Each requires independent evidence [EVD-D06-0003; EVD-D06-0004; EVD-D06-0005]. The strategy's working criterion for regime innovation is therefore failure of C5-op together with successful institutionalization. If only C5-op is supported, the permitted verdict is “local technical innovation.”

Local and global scope also remain separate:

\[
M_t(Q_\varphi)=\mathfrak U_t\!\restriction_{Q_\varphi}.
\]

Thus a change in the CH-local method set does not entail a change in the global update regime. The default thesis requires the local comparison only. A global conclusion becomes eligible only after Dossier D06 verifies cross-problem inheritance and scaffold formation.

The first stage asks how CH was posed, what backgrounds and methods were available, what kinds of outputs were recognized, and how progress was assessed before the Gödel intervention [EVD-D03-0005]. The goal is not to reconstruct a single consensus. It is to identify a documented range of live practices without projecting later forcing vocabulary backward. The CH question already existed at this stage, so the later story cannot be one of problem creation [EVD-D03-0001].

This stage must establish a baseline for `B_t`, `M_t`, `R_t`, and `E_t`. It must also record disagreements, minority positions, and retrospective distortions. A later textbook summary cannot by itself establish what earlier practitioners regarded as an answer, a diagnosis, or progress.

### 4.4 Institutionalized forcing

Institutionalized forcing is not identical with the Cohen event [EVD-D03-0004]. The later stage must be documented through three independent lines. Heritability requires evidence that other researchers could publicly reconstruct, learn, and reuse the operation [EVD-D06-0003]. Non-locality requires documented use across distinct problems, model families, or proof tasks rather than a list of CH-adjacent applications [EVD-D06-0004]. Scaffold formation requires evidence that forcing entered later method chains, iteration and preservation practices, forcing axioms, generic-absoluteness programs, standard terminology, textbooks, training, or durable technical infrastructure [EVD-D06-0005]. Every item in that list is an evidence obligation, not an established result of this draft.

The three conditions cannot substitute for one another. Textbook presence might support scaffold formation but does not by itself prove non-locality. Multiple applications might support non-locality but not heritability. A technically reconstructible operation may be heritable without forming infrastructure. Only an independently supported conjunction licenses the strategy's institutionalization verdict.

Strong semantic re-individuation asks whether the institutionalized-forcing response-role structure has a conservative translation back into the Gödel stage. ### 5.4 Formal constraints and technical accuracy

The historical argument operates under mathematical constraints. The final draft must distinguish syntactic independence, relative consistency, model existence, model truth, interpretability, conservative extension, proof-theoretic strength, and constructive feasibility [EVD-D03-0012]. It must state all assumptions behind model-existence claims, maintain the distinction among ground model, generic extension, and external metatheory, and verify every assertion about generic filters and Boolean-valued models [EVD-D03-0013].

Consistency-strength structures may supply evidence that mathematical development is constrained rather than arbitrary [EVD-D04-0004]. They do not identify the source or ontological ownership of those constraints. They therefore cannot decide Platonism, potentialism, universe/multiverse ontology, or the SRT bridge.

### 5.5 Two non-commutativities

Modal non-commutativity concerns the order of model or world extensions. Historical non-commutativity concerns whether one operation is available, teachable, or composable before another has become part of a practice. Neither form entails the other. A claim about the order of height and width extensions requires technical evidence [EVD-D01-0007]; a claim that forcing iteration was unavailable before forcing became an inherited operation requires historical evidence [EVD-D06-0006]. The paper uses the distinction only to prevent a category error.

## 6. The SRT Bridge — P3 and Detachable

The main paper does not depend on SRT. Historical path dependence, research-regime change, method inheritance, and institutionalization are not uniquely SRT claims [EVD-D01-0004]. The bridge is retained only as a P3 mapping and must be removable without damage to Sections 2–5 and 7.

Under the frozen mapping, `L_0^{math}` refers to candidate mathematical paths that have not stabilized and are not uniformly reachable; `L_1^{math}` refers to manifest proofs, models, counterexamples, independence results, or method innovations; and `L_2^{math}` refers to inherited and backgrounded methods, proof libraries, training, and institutions. These are domain mappings, not replacements for canonical definitions and not claims that `L_0` is a hidden mathematical object-domain.

The Level A candidate is selective second-order write-back: some successful mathematical manifestations may become inherited scaffolds that asymmetrically lower the costs of compatible future paths. The preregistered local comparison is

\[
\Delta c_t(p_{compatible}\mid L_2^k)
<
\Delta c_t(p_{incompatible}\mid L_2^k).
\]

Compatibility must be specified in advance through method reuse, proof tools, background axioms, translation interfaces, intermediate results, or training libraries. The claim allows local scaffolds, branching, and incompatible paths; it does not posit global historical convergence. Evidence for forcing's inheritance and scaffold formation would be relevant to this bridge but would not by itself establish the directional comparison [EVD-D06-0007].

## 7. Objections and Failure Conditions

### 7.1 Question identity and historical constitution

**Objection:** The direct answers did not change, so the question did not change.  
**Reply:** The paper accepts the premise about the direct-answer core. Its default conclusion concerns the wider research regime, not the sentence-level question. A strong semantic conclusion would require independent C2 evidence. If D09 does not establish non-conservative response-role change relative to Gödel, semantic re-individuation is withdrawn.

**Objection:** A research regime is merely context around the real mathematical problem.  
**Reply:** That objection succeeds unless D03–D06 show that backgrounds, operations, output generation, evaluation, and problem generation altered what researchers could do with CH. The framework cannot answer the objection by definition alone. If the historical evidence shows only a change of convenience or vocabulary, the reconstruction claim is withdrawn.

**Objection:** Universe theorists need not accept a changed problem identity.  
**Reply:** The weak thesis does not require them to do so. It is compatible with disagreement about truth and ontology, provided the CH research regime changed. Universe/multiverse neutrality itself requires evidence from both sides [EVD-D01-0006].

### 7.2 Institution, novelty, and calibration

**Objection:** Every new method makes C5-op fail, so the criterion overgenerates.  
**Reply:** C5-op asks whether a type-correct translation exists into the documented earlier `B_t`, `M_t`, and output profile; it is neither a test over every mathematical resource retrospectively available nor a claim that novelty was already institutionalized. It identifies operation novelty only. A regime verdict also requires `H`, `N`, and `S`. Control B is designed to show a real C5-op failure that does not pass the institutionalization filter. If that control cannot be established, the criterion remains uncalibrated.

**Objection:** Any important result can retrospectively be described as a new operation.  
**Reply:** Control A requires an important result within a stable operation regime. The dossier must actively search for evidence that a supposedly result-only case did introduce a new operation. If such evidence is found, the candidate is rejected rather than forced into the control profile.

**Objection:** Institutionalization is being inferred from influence.  
**Reply:** Influence is not the criterion. Heritability, non-locality, and scaffold formation must be independently evidenced. The Cohen event and later forcing institution are therefore separate stages [EVD-D03-0004].

### 7.3 Potentialism, modality, and ontology

**Objection:** Fixed-background discovery can accommodate the entire story.  
**Reply:** It may accommodate the mathematical results. The narrower question is whether it preserves the historically available operations, output roles, and evaluation structure under conservative translation. The paper does not infer from a negative answer that truth was created.

**Objection:** The proposal is merely philosophy of mathematical practice or an appendix to forcing potentialism.  
**Reply:** That objection succeeds unless D01 establishes a genuine gap between model accessibility and institutionalized operation, while D03–D06 show that the distinction explains the CH case. General practice vocabulary cannot be treated as deficient by assertion. If the gap is merely terminological, the paper stops rather than retaining a weakened positioning claim.

**Objection:** If the modal formulation can be eliminated, the account loses its subject matter.  
**Reply:** The non-modal `R_F(M,N)` formulation preserves the object-level relation. The historical-institutional question remains. Representation neutrality fails, however, if a central inference turns out to depend on a specific modal semantics.

**Objection:** Consistency hierarchies support a realist ontology rather than the proposed historical account.  
**Reply:** Consistency strength constrains the space of admissible claims. It does not by itself determine what the source of constraint is. The paper leaves that ontological question open.

### 7.4 The SRT increment

**Objection:** The SRT section redescribes ordinary method inheritance and imports a convergence story.  
**Reply:** The bridge does not require global convergence. Its only candidate increment is a local directional comparison of compatible and incompatible path-cost changes. If the comparison is unsupported, Level B removes the prediction. If the remaining mapping adds no content, Level C removes the section. No defense of SRT may alter the historical argument or strengthen its conclusion.

- If C5-op cannot be established relative to the Gödel stage, stop the paper.
- If C5-op fails but `H`, `N`, and `S` are not all independently supported, describe only technical innovation.

## 8. Implications

If the CH-local evidence is completed, the paper will show that sentence continuity can coexist with research-regime reconstruction. That conclusion would allow philosophers of mathematics to discuss historical change without claiming that a mathematical sentence or its direct answers were created by history. It would also isolate two objects often run together: accessibility among models and the inheritance of an operation within a research practice.

The analysis would not automatically generalize beyond CH. Application to another mathematical problem would require a fresh reconstruction of backgrounds, methods, response roles, evaluation, C5-op, and institutionalization. Application to set theory globally would require the separate D06 evidence gate. Application to semantic question identity would require D09. This division of burdens is itself a methodological implication: local, global, and semantic conclusions should not borrow evidence from one another.

The framework is also representation-neutral in a limited sense. Its historical thesis can be posed whether forcing accessibility is expressed modally or by a non-modal extension relation. This neutrality is conditional on the completed argument not relying on a specific modal logic [EVD-D01-0005]. It does not imply neutrality about every philosophy of mathematics.

## Formal Note: C1–C6 and the Two Non-Commutativities

The comparison uses the translations `tau` and `rho` specified in v0.7 for outputs, backgrounds, methods, response roles, and evaluation structures. C1 preserves the direct-answer core in both directions. C2 requires response-role classification to commute with output translation, again in both directions. C3 requires evaluation order to be preserved on the translation image. C4 requires preservation of support, blocking, problem generation, problem differentiation, and method-transfer functions. C5-op requires preservation of the background–operation–output generation relation. C6 requires structural quasi-inverses.

For C6, outputs satisfy

\[
o\approx_t o'
\]

when they are equivalent in response role, evaluation status, inferential function, and background–operation generation profile. The quasi-inverse requirements are

\[
\rho_{\mathcal O}\circ\tau_{\mathcal O}
\approx_t
\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal O_t}
\]

and

\[
\tau_{\mathcal O}\circ\rho_{\mathcal O}
\approx_{t+1}
\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal O_{t+1}}.
\]

C1 records continuity. C2 alone carries the strong semantic verdict. C3 and C4 corroborate but do not independently establish regime innovation. C5-op identifies operation novelty; institutionalization determines whether that novelty rises to regime innovation. C6 controls the strength of the conservative comparison.

Modal non-commutativity compares the order of extension operations over models or worlds. Historical non-commutativity compares transformations of a practice state, including whether a later operation is available only after an earlier operation has become institutionalized. These are distinct claims with distinct evidence burdens [EVD-D01-0007; EVD-D06-0006].
