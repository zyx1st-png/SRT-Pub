## 1. Scope and comparator

This dossier reconstructs the Gödel-stage baseline against which later dossiers may eventually compare the Cohen event and institutionalized forcing [EVD-D03-0006; EVD-D03-0008]. It does **not** compare the two operations, establish Cohen-stage novelty, adjudicate C5-op, establish institutionalization, or adjudicate C2. It therefore supplies no verdict about CH-regime reconstruction, global set-theoretic reconstruction, or strong semantic re-individuation.

The analytical stage is not assumed to be homogeneous. The primary record includes three distinguishable moments: the 1938 announcement, the 1939 proof sketch and 1940 full exposition, and Gödel's 1947 discussion of what consistency and possible undecidability would mean for the continuum problem [GODEL1938-AC-GCH, pp. 556–557; GODEL1939-GCH, pp. 220–224; GODEL1940-MONOGRAPH, pp. 1–2, 35–61; GODEL1947-CH, pp. 519–524]. The Bernays and Torrance 1941 reviews and Carnap's 1940 private notes supply narrow contemporaneous reception items, not proxies for consensus [BERNAYS1941-REVIEW, p. 112; TORRANCE1941-REVIEW, reprint p. 102; VANATTEN-KENNEDY2009-GODEL, pp. 303–304]. Later technical and historical accounts are used as retrospective checks and are not treated as evidence of what practitioners already believed in the 1938–1947 window.

The comparator to be carried forward is deliberately limited:

- an exact technical intervention;
- the output it proved;
- the assumptions needed to move between model-theoretic and syntactic presentations;
- a source-attributed range of evaluations;
- a provisional, partly unresolved `B_t`, `M_t`, `O_t`, `R_t`, and `E_t` profile.

## 3. Technical record

### 3.1 Original background theories

The original sources do not all use one modern background formulation [EVD-D03-0006]. In 1938 Gödel lets `T` be the result of removing choice from a von Neumann set/class system and states that, if `T` is consistent, adding four propositions simultaneously preserves consistency [GODEL1938-AC-GCH, p. 556]. His items 1 and 2 are choice and GCH; items 3 and 4 are descriptive-set-theoretic existence statements [GODEL1938-AC-GCH, p. 556].

The 1939 paper states in n. 1 that it sketches the proof of the first two 1938 propositions for Zermelo's system, with or without substitution, under an explicit identification of “definite property” with a propositional function over all sets [GODEL1939-GCH, p. 224 n. 1]. The 1940 monograph instead uses a Bernays/von Neumann class-set framework and calls the base system `Σ`; its introduction states the conditional contradiction transformation, while Bernays independently describes its axiom groups and the role of choice [GODEL1940-MONOGRAPH, pp. 1–2; BERNAYS1941-REVIEW, p. 112].

Modern authoritative accounts normalize the result as follows:

\[
\operatorname{Con}(ZF)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{Con}(ZF+AC+GCH),
\]

and hence also

\[
\operatorname{Con}(ZFC)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{Con}(ZFC+CH)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{Con}(ZFC+GCH).
\]

This normalization is supported by Kanamori's explicit summary and Bagaria's modern presentation [KANAMORI1996-DEVELOPMENT, p. 37; BAGARIA2019-SET-THEORY-SEP, §§6.3, 7]. It is not attributed verbatim to Gödel's original notation.

### 3.2 Constructible-universe construction

In modern notation, the constructible hierarchy begins with the empty level, takes at a successor stage the subsets first-order definable over the preceding level using parameters from that level, and takes unions at limit stages:

\[
L_0=\varnothing,\qquad
L_{\alpha+1}=\operatorname{Def}(L_\alpha),\qquad
L_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}L_\alpha,
\qquad
L=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}}L_\alpha.
\]

Kanamori gives this retrospective `Def`-presentation and immediately notes that Gödel's 1940 rigorous exposition used a different set-by-set construction through eight binary operations [KANAMORI1996-DEVELOPMENT, pp. 36–38]. Kennedy and Bagaria give the same modern hierarchy, including the facts that `L` is a proper class, transitive, contains all ordinals, and satisfies the required axioms [KENNEDY2025-GODEL-SEP, §2.4.2; BAGARIA2019-SET-THEORY-SEP, §7].

Accordingly, the modern formula above is an expository reconstruction. It is **not** evidence that `Def(L_α)` notation or a later packaged method belonged to the Gödel-stage working repertoire.

### 3.3 Exact outputs in the primary proof record

Gödel's 1938 theorem says that, conditional on the consistency of `T`, the four listed propositions may be adjoined simultaneously [GODEL1938-AC-GCH, p. 556]. The CH-relevant package is `AC + GCH`, with CH following as an instance of GCH. His proof sketch describes a model made from the “mathematically constructible” sets and states that the axiom asserting every set constructible is true in that model [GODEL1938-AC-GCH, pp. 556–557].

The 1939 proof sketch separates the steps more explicitly [EVD-D03-0006]:

- Theorem 7: the displayed constructible models satisfy the relevant set-theory axioms, subject to the paper's stated formulation [GODEL1939-GCH, p. 223].
- Theorem 8: the constructibility proposition `A` implies the paper's choice statement `R` and generalized-continuum statement `C` [GODEL1939-GCH, p. 223].
- Theorem 9: `A` is true in the constructed models [GODEL1939-GCH, p. 224].
- Theorem 10: `R` and `C` are true in those models [GODEL1939-GCH, p. 224].
- The final paragraph states that the construction can be carried out in the corresponding formal systems so that a contradiction from the extension would yield a contradiction in the unextended theory [GODEL1939-GCH, p. 224].

That last step is the explicit bridge from the model construction to the relative-consistency conclusion. It does not assert that the base theory proves GCH.

The 1940 exposition makes the architecture explicit at monograph scale: construction of the model `Δ` in Chapter V, verification of the base axioms in Chapter VI, verification of `V=L` in Chapter VII, and the deductions of AC and GCH in Chapter VIII [GODEL1940-MONOGRAPH, pp. 35–61]. Its introduction describes the metamathematical output as a transformation of a contradiction in `Σ+AC+GCH` into one in `Σ` [GODEL1940-MONOGRAPH, pp. 1–2].

### 3.4 Relative-consistency content and assumptions

The result is conditional and one-directional [EVD-D03-0006; EVD-D03-0007]. Gödel's own 1938 n. 5 says that the model is constructed using essentially transfinite methods and therefore gives “only a relative proof of consistency,” requiring the consistency of `T` [GODEL1938-AC-GCH, p. 557 n. 5]. The verified result licenses the following conditional claims:

- if the specified base theory is consistent, then the specified extension with choice and GCH is consistent;
- in the constructible inner model, choice and GCH hold;
- therefore CH cannot be refuted from the relevant ordinary axioms if those axioms are consistent.

It does **not** license any of the following:

- `ZF ⊢ CH` or `ZFC ⊢ CH`;
- unqualified truth of CH in the intended universe;
- `V=L` for the intended universe;
- consistency of `ZF + not-CH` or `ZFC + not-CH`;
- full independence of CH;
- a countable transitive model of ZFC from bare `Con(ZFC)`;
- a historical verdict that Gödel “solved half” of CH.

### 3.5 Model truth, syntax, consistency, and independence

| Claim form | Type | What is verified here | What does not follow |
|---|---|---|---|
| `L ⊨ GCH` | model-relative truth in the constructible inner model | The model satisfies GCH [GODEL1939-GCH, pp. 223–224, Ths. 7–10; GODEL1940-MONOGRAPH, pp. 53–61; BAGARIA2019-SET-THEORY-SEP, §7]. | GCH is true in an unqualified intended universe. |
| `ZF + V=L ⊢ GCH` | syntactic consequence in a strengthened theory | Modern reconstruction of the formalized constructibility result [KENNEDY2025-GODEL-SEP, §2.4.2; PAULSON2003-RELATIVE-CONSISTENCY, §2.7, p. 11]. | `ZF ⊢ GCH`. |
| `Con(ZF) → Con(ZF+AC+GCH)` | external relative-consistency assertion, or a formalized implication once a metatheory is fixed | The modern summary of Gödel's result [KANAMORI1996-DEVELOPMENT, p. 37]. | Absolute consistency, truth, or the opposite consistency direction. |
| `ZF ⊬ not-CH`, conditional on `Con(ZF)` | syntactic non-refutability consequence | Bagaria states the consequence explicitly [BAGARIA2019-SET-THEORY-SEP, §6.3]. | `ZF ⊬ CH`; both directions are needed for independence. |
| `CH` independent of `ZFC` | two-sided syntactic claim | **Not established by this dossier.** Koellner explicitly attributes it to the combined Gödel and Cohen results [KOELLNER2013-CH-SEP, §1.1]. | It cannot be attributed to Gödel's one-directional result alone. |

The symbol `Con(T)` in the table must be read either as an external metatheoretic assertion or as a particular arithmetized consistency sentence in a stated metatheory. The two readings are not silently interchangeable [EVD-D03-0007].

### 3.6 Model existence, countability, transitivity, and external metatheory

For a first-order theory in a countable language, completeness connects syntactic consistency with the existence of some model, and Löwenheim–Skolem yields a countable model when a model exists [KENNEDY2025-GODEL-SEP, §2.1.3, opening two paragraphs; MARKS2026-SET-THEORY-NOTES, §18, p. 75, Th. 18.1]. This does not add transitivity, well-foundedness, or standardness: internal Foundation is compatible with external ill-foundedness, and an externally well-founded extensional model requires a separate collapse argument before it is represented transitively [MARKS2026-SET-THEORY-NOTES, §18, p. 75, Ths. 18.2–18.3]. None of the verified Gödel 1938 or 1939 statements uses countability as a premise for the inner-model relative-consistency result [GODEL1938-AC-GCH, pp. 556–557; GODEL1939-GCH, pp. 223–224].

The safe hierarchy of claims is therefore:

1. `Con(T)` may yield existence of **a** first-order model of `T` through completeness, in an adequate external metatheory.
2. For a countable language, model existence may yield a **countable** first-order model through Löwenheim–Skolem.
3. Neither step supplies a **transitive**, **well-founded**, or **standard** model.
4. A countable transitive model is a stronger premise and is not inferred anywhere in this dossier.

When the construction is described relative to a model `M`, the object is the internally computed `L^M`; when `L` is described as the proper-class inner model of the ambient universe, that is a different presentation. Ordinary set-model satisfaction does not simply become an internal satisfaction predicate for proper-class `L`; a modern ZF treatment uses relativization and theorem schemata in the external metatheory [PAULSON2003-RELATIVE-CONSISTENCY, §§2.1–2.4, pp. 7–10; MARKS2026-SET-THEORY-NOTES, §18, p. 76]. Any later comparison must say which presentation is being used and what the external metatheory assumes [EVD-D03-0007].

### 4.2 What can and cannot be said historically

The verified sources support a range rather than a single stable response role [EVD-D03-0008]. Gödel in 1938 simultaneously presented a relative-consistency result and tentatively described constructibility as a possible completion [GODEL1938-AC-GCH, p. 557]. Bernays in 1941 treated the construction as a substantial, well-designed mathematical task, which defeats the claim that it was regarded **only** as a minor technical lemma—at least for this named reviewer [BERNAYS1941-REVIEW, p. 112]. Torrance independently classified the result as conditional compatibility but did not assign it a broader role [TORRANCE1941-REVIEW, reprint p. 102]. Carnap's 1940 notes provide one signal that the intervention was conceptually linked to incompleteness and unresolved problem classes, while their private status and indirect transmission block any uptake inference [VANATTEN-KENNEDY2009-GODEL, pp. 303–304]. In 1947 Gödel classified his result as one line of attack toward likely undecidability while denying that even full undecidability from the then-current axioms would settle the continuum question for a realist [GODEL1947-CH, pp. 519–520].

Cohen's much later participant recollection pulls in the other direction: he remembers little direct follow-up and describes an early impression of technical and philosophical isolation [COHEN2002-DISCOVERY, pp. 1086–1088, 1099–1100]. Because that account is retrospective and explicitly personal in places, it cannot establish general neglect. It does, however, prevent positive technical assessments from being promoted into institutionalization or broad uptake.

This record does not establish that “partial answer,” “metamathematical diagnosis,” or “relative-consistency response” was a shared, stable `R_t` category. Those phrases may be used only with a named source or retained as unresolved candidate codings.

## 6. Counterevidence and competing interpretations

### 6.1 Against “independence-type responses were wholly absent before Cohen”

Gödel's 1947 paper explicitly lists demonstrability, disprovability, and undecidability as the three possibilities under consistency, calls undecidability the most likely, describes its proof as a promising attack, and identifies his earlier result as already establishing non-disprovability [GODEL1947-CH, p. 519]. It then asks what even a completed two-sided result would mean and denies that it would settle the question definitively for someone who takes set theory to describe a determinate reality [GODEL1947-CH, pp. 519–520].

This is verified counterevidence to a total-absence narrative. It does **not** prove that bilateral independence already functioned as a stable community response role; that remains a D09 question.

### 6.2 Against “Gödel's contribution was regarded only as a technical lemma”

Bernays devotes a multi-page contemporary review to the monograph, reconstructs its axiomatic and constructive architecture, and on p. 112 praises the arrangement's elegance and suitability [BERNAYS1941-REVIEW, p. 112]. Kanamori later calls the relative-consistency result a field-launching metamathematical achievement [KANAMORI1996-DEVELOPMENT, pp. 34–38]. These sources defeat the universal “only a technical lemma” claim. They do not prove a single shared assessment.

### 6.3 Against a homogeneous Gödel stage

Gödel's 1938 “natural completion” language and his 1947 suspicion that CH would ultimately be disproved by new axioms pull in different evaluative directions [GODEL1938-AC-GCH, p. 557; GODEL1947-CH, p. 524]. Kennedy explicitly treats the 1938 phrase as ambiguous between correctness and mere definiteness [KENNEDY2025-GODEL-SEP, §2.4.4]. Kanamori's broad progress narrative is also marked by his declared Whiggish standpoint [KANAMORI1996-DEVELOPMENT, p. 1].

The safe historical conclusion is therefore heterogeneity: several evaluations are documented, and their relation and uptake are incomplete.
