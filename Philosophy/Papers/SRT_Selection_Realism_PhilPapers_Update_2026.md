# Selective Reality Theory (SRT): Selection Realism and the Constitutive Explanation of Determinacy

**Yuxin Zhang**  
Independent Researcher  
June 2026

## Version note

This paper is a revised public statement of Selective Reality Theory (SRT). It supersedes earlier presentations that described SRT primarily through the language of a Ghost Operator, a triadic ontology, cross-scale isomorphism, and a universal d-value formalism. Those expressions remain useful as technical or heuristic vocabulary in some contexts, but they are no longer the best entry point into the theory.

The present version extracts the current argument from the book-length treatment in *From Existence to Order* (2026) and states it in a more restricted academic form: SRT is presented here as **selection realism**, a constitutive account of how determinate, load-bearing reality is generated through exclusion, shaping, inscription, anchoring, and historical stabilization. The guiding claim is not that reality is an illusion, nor that consciousness creates the world, but that stability is not the original floor. Stability is a history: a result of selection that has become sufficiently anchored to appear as already given.

## Abstract

Object-first ontology begins from a world of determinate things: bounded objects, stable subjects, already-applicable predicates, and background orders within which perception, action, truth, and value are then discussed. Selective Reality Theory (SRT) argues that this order of explanation is too late. The sense that the world is already there is not denied, but treated as an achievement that requires explanation.

This paper presents the current form of SRT as **selection realism**: the view that object-style determinacy—boundary, identity, and practical load-bearing—is not an unexplained ontological floor, but the result of constrained selection. Selection is not an agent choosing from a menu. It is the earlier structure by which a pre-object field is differentially actualized through exclusion, shaping, and inscription; an appearance becomes anchored; repeated anchoring acquires reality-thickness; and sedimented thickness recedes into order, pre-trimming what can later appear as available choice.

The paper first diagnoses the explanatory debt of object-first ontology. It then defines SRT's restricted three-layer vocabulary: `L0` as sub-determinate non-neutrality, not a hidden world; `L1` as manifestation or anchoring, not a second substance; and `L2` as sedimented selection history, not an automatically legitimate order. To prevent idealism and relativism, the paper introduces four dimensions of reality-strength: manifestational stability, cross-operator alignment, intervention-resistance, and inheritance/hardening. Ontological friction names the cost that a candidate cut pays under prediction, intervention, repetition, repair, and cross-operator correction. On this account, what is operator-relative is the determinate cut; what is not freely resettable is the comparative resistance it meets.

SRT also revises the place of subjectivity and consciousness. Consciousness is not the source of selection. Selection can occur without consciousness, and consciousness can be present when action is blocked. Subjectivity is a sedimented bearing-position to which consequences return; concern and value arise when consequences reorganize the ongoing generation of such a position. Phenomenal consciousness is treated as a later condensation of this structure: first as sentience, where consequences are borne from within, and then as reflexive awareness, where the position can read itself as feeling, choosing, and persisting across time. The paper closes with explicit failure conditions. If object-style determinacy, reality-strength, consequence-return, and consciousness-boundary phenomena can be explained without remainder by an object-first, naturalness, or physicalist account, SRT narrows to an interface model rather than a meta-ontology.

**Keywords:** Selective Reality Theory; selection realism; determinacy; object-first ontology; pre-object field; reality-strength; ontological friction; consciousness; subjectivity; value

## 1. Introduction: beginning too late

You reach for a cup on the table. It arrives as an object before you have time to ask how it became one: bounded from the table, stable enough to be re-identified, graspable by the hand, named by language, and embedded in a practice of use. The world normally appears in this way. It does not first present an undifferentiated field and then ask the subject to assemble objects from it. It arrives as if already there.

This givenness is not an illusion. The cup is not unreal because its objecthood has a history. The ordinary world is stable enough to live in, intervene in, and share. The problem is not that the world appears stable. The problem is that metaphysics often mistakes this stability for a first fact. It begins with determinate objects, subjects, properties, states, causes, institutions, and values already in place, and then asks what further relations hold among them. SRT asks an earlier question: under what conditions does anything acquire the boundary, identity, and load-bearing force required to appear as a determinate item at all?

This earlier question changes the philosophical entry point. Before asking whether objects are external, whether subjects are free, whether values are objective, or whether consciousness emerges from matter, we should ask how objecthood, subjecthood, value, and consciousness become sufficiently stabilized to serve as the premises of those questions. SRT's guiding claim is that many philosophical problems are not false problems but late problems. They begin after the work of selection, anchoring, and stabilization has already been hidden by its own success.

Earlier versions of SRT expressed this point more ambitiously: existence was identified with selection, the selecting process was formalized as a Ghost Operator, and a triadic ontology was mapped across physics, neuroscience, AI, social institutions, and spiritual traditions. That version had heuristic force, but it carried two risks. First, it could be read as a theory of everything, claiming direct isomorphism across domains before the relevant differences had been earned. Second, it could be read as a consciousness-first or operator-first idealism, as if consciousness creates reality. The current version rejects both readings. SRT is not a claim that minds make the world. Nor is it a claim that every domain is governed by the same equation in the same way. It is a selection-first account of determinacy: a theory of how reality becomes determinate, durable, and load-bearing.

The central thesis of this paper is therefore narrower and stronger:

> **Selection realism:** object-style determinacy is not an ontological primitive. It is constituted by constrained selection: exclusion, shaping, inscription, anchoring, and stabilization under resistance.

The phrase *object-style determinacy* is important. The target is not every possible sense of determinacy, and not the existence of any primitive structure whatsoever. The target is the familiar package that object-first ontology silently presupposes: a boundary between this and not-this, identity through change, and practical load-bearing under intervention and reuse. SRT argues that this package is better explained as an achievement than as a floor.

This paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 states the explanatory debt of object-first ontology. Section 3 defines selection realism and SRT's three-layer vocabulary. Section 4 develops reality-strength and ontological friction as safeguards against relativism. Section 5 extends the account to bearing-positions, concern, value, subjectivity, and consciousness. Section 6 positions SRT against rival views. Section 7 states failure conditions. Section 8 concludes.

## 2. The explanatory debt of object-style determinacy

Object-first ontology is not one doctrine. It appears in common sense, in substance ontology, in formal frameworks that quantify over domains of individuals, and in scientific practice when entities are treated as already individuated units of measurement or explanation. In a broad sense, it has three commitments:

1. **Object priority:** the basic furniture of reality is a domain of determinate individuals, or at least determinate relata.
2. **Givenness of individuation:** boundaries and identity conditions are available before the relevant acts of selection, perspective, measurement, classification, or operation.
3. **Downstream subjectivity:** subjects, perception, language, value, and social frameworks enter after the world has already been individuated.

SRT does not deny that object-first models work. They work because stabilization has already done enough work for objects to be treated as objects. A map can be useful after roads have been laid. The difficulty is that usefulness after stabilization does not explain stabilization itself.

Object-style determinacy involves at least three dimensions:

- **Boundary:** a difference between this and not-this can be drawn and maintained.
- **Identity:** the item can be tracked as the same through change.
- **Load-bearing:** the item can matter for prediction, intervention, repair, use, responsibility, or further selection.

These dimensions are not reducible to linguistic predication. A word may apply vaguely, precisely, conventionally, or incorrectly; but for a predicate to apply at all, there must already be a stabilized field of possible application. Nor are these dimensions exhausted by mental representation. A representation may track a cup, a disease, or an institution, but the represented item must be able to bear some cost in the world: breakage, intervention, inheritance, use, or resistance to revision.

The explanatory debt can be put as a weak regress:

1. Any object-style determinate item requires boundary, identity, and load-bearing conditions.
2. If those conditions are explained only by another same-type determinate item, the explanation transfers determinacy rather than explaining it.
3. Replacing objects with structures, properties, relations, or facts does not by itself solve the problem if those structures, properties, relations, or facts are already determinate in the relevant sense.
4. Therefore, object-first ontology either accepts primitive determinate items as brute or owes an explanation of how determinacy is constituted.

This is not a deductive refutation of naturalness realism or primitive structure. A defender of natural joints may say that some determinate structure is basic and needs no explanation. SRT's argument is abductive rather than coercive: it asks whether more phenomena become intelligible if object-style determinacy is treated as generated rather than primitive.

Those phenomena include the felt givenness of objects, the scale-dependence of individuation, the difference between dreams and public facts, the path-dependence of classifications, the cost of revising institutions, and the way consciousness appears as source only after it has already condensed upon prior selection histories. Object-first ontology can accommodate many of these one by one. SRT claims that they belong to one pattern: the pattern by which selection becomes anchored, thickened, and inherited.

## 3. Selection realism

### 3.1 Selection is not choosing from a menu

In ordinary language, selection suggests an agent choosing between already listed options. That is precisely what SRT does not mean. Menu-choice is a late and derivative case. Before a subject chooses among options, something must already have made some things available as options, some paths visible, some paths costly, and some paths unthinkable.

SRT defines selection as **constrained differential actualization**. A process counts as selection in the relevant sense when four conditions are met:

1. There are multiple actualizable but non-equivalent manifestation paths.
2. Some paths are easier or harder to stabilize because of resistance, cost, or constraint.
3. The actualized path leaves inheritable downstream conditions.
4. Those downstream conditions pre-trim future selection space.

This definition avoids overextension. Not every event is a selection. A mere happening becomes selection only when exclusion, shaping, inscription, and inheritance jointly alter the terrain of what can later occur.

SRT describes selection through three phases:

- **Exclusion:** a difference is made between this and not-this. Exclusion is not merely loss; what is excluded can remain as shadow, tension, and later friction.
- **Shaping:** the selected direction receives boundary, format, and operability. Something becomes capable of being used, tracked, named, or acted upon.
- **Inscription:** the result enters the terrain and changes future possibility distributions. Without inscription, manifestation passes without weight.

Selection therefore begins before conscious choice. It is the structure by which a field becomes cuttable, a cut becomes operable, and an operable result becomes part of subsequent reality.

### 3.2 `L0`, `L1`, and `L2` as restricted vocabulary

Earlier SRT used a triadic ontology: Latent Domain, Manifest Domain, and Convergence Domain. The current version keeps the notation but restricts its meaning to avoid metaphysical inflation.

**`L0`: sub-determinate non-neutrality.** `L0` is not a hidden world behind appearances and not a warehouse of unmanifest objects. It names the fact that the pre-object field is not perfectly flat. Some cuts are more stabilizable, lower-cost, and more inheritable than others. `L0` is sub-determinate because it does not contain already individuated objects, properties, or relations. It is non-neutral because not all possible cuts are equally viable.

**`L1`: manifestation and anchoring.** `L1` is not a second substance or a private mental screen. It is the event-layer in which a selection becomes manifest and may acquire an address in the terrain. Appearance is not yet anchoring. Something can flash, dissolve, or remain thin. Anchoring begins when a manifestation becomes a condition subsequent selections must face.

**`L2`: sedimented selection history.** `L2` is not automatically legitimate order. It is the hardening of repeated anchoring into background constraint: habit, language, institution, body schema, law, memory, affordance, or model. `L2` lowers the cost of some future selections and raises the cost of others. It can be scaffold or cage.

The three-layer movement can be summarized:

> pre-object non-neutrality (`L0`) → manifestation/anchoring (`L1`) → sedimented history (`L2`) → pre-trimming of later selection.

This is not a cosmological map of three realms. It is a grammar for describing how determinacy is generated and inherited.

### 3.3 Anchoring, irreversibility, thickness, and order

SRT's main sequence is:

1. **Appearing is not remaining.** A manifestation may occur without entering subsequent terrain.
2. **Anchoring gives appearance a position.** The manifestation becomes something later selection must take into account.
3. **Anchoring introduces irreversibility.** Withdrawal may cancel a surface result but not the fact that the terrain has been changed.
4. **Repeated anchoring produces reality-thickness.** What has been borne, paid for, relied upon, and re-entered becomes thick.
5. **Thickness recedes into order.** Once thick enough, selection history becomes background and appears as the way things simply are.
6. **Order pre-trims future choice.** It makes some paths visible, available, cheap, and default; it makes others invisible, costly, or excluded before deliberation begins.

This sequence is the backbone of SRT's current form. Stability is not illusion. Stability is history. The mistake is to treat this history as if it had no history.

## 4. Reality-strength and ontological friction

If reality is constituted by selection and anchoring, a familiar objection arises: does this make everything equally real? Are dreams, hallucinations, institutions, mathematical objects, and physical objects merely different selections?

SRT answers no. Reality-strength is not a simple on/off property, but it is also not arbitrary. It can be described along four dimensions:

1. **Manifestational stability:** does the manifestation recur or persist?
2. **Cross-operator alignment:** can it be aligned across subjects, instruments, contexts, and practices?
3. **Intervention-resistance:** does it hold under intervention, manipulation, repair, or prediction?
4. **Inheritance/hardening:** does it sediment into downstream constraint, producing lag, cost, and path-dependence?

These dimensions explain why a dream, a habit, a public institution, and a physical law are not equally real. A dream may have local manifestation but low cross-operator alignment and low intervention-resistance. A social institution may have high cross-operator alignment and inheritance but limited physical intervention-resistance. A physical object typically has higher intervention-resistance: it breaks, blocks, supports, weighs, and resists independently of any single operator's framing.

SRT calls the cost of anchoring **ontological friction** (`Psi_f`). This is not a mystical force. It is a general name for the resistance a candidate cut meets under prediction, intervention, repetition, repair, and cross-operator correction. What can be selected is not whatever an operator wishes. A cut must pay its anchoring cost. A classification that cannot survive use, intervention, or inheritance remains thin; a cut that repeatedly survives becomes thick.

This blocks subjective idealism. Minds do not make reality at will. What is operator-relative is the cut: the way a field is divided, named, operationalized, or entered. What is not freely resettable is the comparative resistance the cut meets. A community may rename a disease, but it cannot by renaming alone change whether a treatment works. A legal system may reclassify a status, but it cannot make the inherited costs of the old classification vanish. Construction has cost.

## 5. From consequence-return to consciousness

### 5.1 Bearing-position

The same selection sequence can be extended from objecthood to subjectivity, but only if one more structure is introduced: consequence-return.

A consequence is not automatically a matter of concern. Many effects occur without returning to a position that must bear them. SRT calls such a position a **bearing-position**: a structured locus whose ongoing generation can be reorganized by what happens to it. A bearing-position is not necessarily a reflective human self. It is any position to which consequences can return in a way that alters anchoring, inscription, and future selection conditions.

This distinction explains why an impact on a stone and a word that changes a person are not merely different in intensity. They differ in the level at which the consequence enters. In one case, the effect alters properties; in the other, the consequence may reorganize a bearing-position's ongoing generation.

### 5.2 Concern and value

Concern is not liking. Nor is it emotional intensity. A person may feel strongly about something without reorganizing around it, and may structurally care about something without strong emotion. In SRT, concern occurs when a bearing-position's generative process reorganizes around a consequence.

Value is not preference. Preference says what an already-formed subject wants. Value concerns what supports or erodes the bearing-position's capacity to continue generating, integrating, and reselecting. Something can satisfy a preference while damaging generative capacity; something can frustrate a preference while supporting it.

This gives SRT a non-subjective but non-authoritarian account of value. Value is not decided by mere desire, nor imposed by an external authority. It is diagnosed at the level of generation: does this structure support, narrow, consume, outsource, or reopen the conditions under which a bearing-position can continue?

### 5.3 Subjectivity as sediment

SRT does not begin from the subject. The self is not the source of selection. It is a sedimented node of selection history.

A subject forms when repeated selections, consequences, returns, repairs, and re-selections acquire enough continuity to be borne as one position. The subject is therefore real, but its reality is historical rather than primitive. The selector is a fossil of selection.

This view preserves responsibility without requiring a mysterious origin-self. Responsibility begins where consequences return to a bearing-position capable of comprehension, concern, and re-selection. Degrees of responsibility depend on sedimentation, understanding, available alternatives, and the realistic window for re-selection.

### 5.4 Consciousness as late condensation

SRT's revised account of consciousness is deliberately not consciousness-first. Consciousness is not the engine of selection. Two observations force this revision.

First, selection often occurs without consciousness. Immune recognition, motor skill, habit, perceptual parsing, institutional default, and algorithmic convergence all select, trim, and write without reflective awareness. Second, consciousness can be present while action is blocked. A person in a freeze state may know what matters, know what should be done, and still be unable to move. Consciousness is online; selection-action is locked.

These cases show that consciousness is not a single block. SRT distinguishes three layers:

1. **Selection without consciousness.** Exclusion, shaping, inscription, anchoring, and inheritance can occur without experience.
2. **Sentience.** Feeling arises when consequences are borne from within a bearing-position. Pain is not merely a signal; it is what it is like for a consequence to return as bad-for-this-position.
3. **Reflexive awareness.** A further layer condenses when a position can read itself as feeling, choosing, and persisting across time. It can say: I know that I am in pain; I know that I am choosing; I can examine my own history.

This placement reframes the hard problem. SRT does not solve it by deriving qualia from matter in a single jump. It shifts the explanatory order. The question is not how inert matter suddenly produces subjectivity. The question is how selection, embodiment, consequence-return, bearing-position, and self-inscription form a structure for which things matter from within.

Reflexive consciousness is late, but not useless. Once present, it can illuminate, pause, compare, name, and reorganize the selection terrain. It can raise friction on old defaults and return occluded paths to view. It does not create choice from nothing, but it changes the terrain in which future selection occurs.

## 6. Rival views and non-absorption

### 6.1 Naturalness realism

SRT is close to views that posit natural joints, privileged structure, or mind-independent kinds. It does not deny resistance or objectivity. Its disagreement concerns formation. Naturalness realism may explain why some predicates latch better than others, but it often leaves unexplained how object-style determinacy acquires boundary, identity, and practical load-bearing across scale, intervention, revision, and inheritance.

SRT's claim is comparative. If naturalness realism can explain givenness, scale-relativity, anchoring cost, inheritance-lag, and cross-operator divergence without appealing to selection-like formation, then SRT narrows. Until then, selection realism offers a unified mechanism: cuts acquire reality-strength by being anchored under resistance and inherited through subsequent terrain.

### 6.2 Deflationism and framework conventionalism

Carnapian and Hirsch-style deflationary approaches can say that object questions are internal to linguistic or conceptual frameworks. SRT accepts that frameworks matter, but argues that framework-internal truth conditions presuppose a more basic load-bearing question: what makes a cut usable, revisable, costly, and inheritable?

A community may adopt a framework, but the cost of adopting, maintaining, abandoning, or repairing it is not exhausted by framework choice. The distinction between a useful classification and an idle notation lies in anchoring cost. Frameworks do not merely classify reality; when stabilized, they become part of the terrain through which later selection runs.

### 6.3 Barad and agential cuts

Barad's agential realism is one of SRT's closest neighbors. It also rejects pre-given boundaries and treats determinacy as produced through cuts. SRT's difference lies in the account of comparative resistance and inheritance. Barad gives an ontology of phenomenon- and apparatus-dependent cuts. SRT adds a model of how cuts become rankable across operators by the resistance they meet and the thickness they inherit.

This difference matters. If objectivity is tied only to apparatus-specific agential separability, then cross-apparatus comparison must be handled carefully. SRT's `Psi_f` and reality-strength dimensions are intended to track precisely that: not a view from nowhere, but the comparative cost-profile by which some cuts persist, collapse, align, or harden across operators and histories.

### 6.4 Process philosophy, structural realism, and active inference

SRT shares with process philosophy the refusal to treat substance as the first fact. It shares with ontic structural realism the suspicion that objects are derivative. It shares with active inference and the Free Energy Principle an interest in self-maintaining systems, priors, prediction, and viability. But SRT is not identical to any of these.

Against process philosophy, SRT adds a more specific account of exclusion, shaping, inscription, anchoring, and inherited order. Against structural realism, it refuses to place structure itself as an unexplained determinate floor; structure is also something that can be stabilized and inherited. Against active inference, it treats free-energy minimization and predictive regulation as important regional mechanisms rather than the whole ontology: consequence-return, value, and bearing-position cannot be reduced to error minimization alone without losing the distinction between scaffolding and cage, support and substitution, signal and suffering.

## 7. Failure conditions

A theory that cannot say what would make it fail should not be trusted. SRT's current form is deliberately self-limiting. Its main claims can be narrowed or withdrawn under the following conditions.

### 7.1 Failure of selection priority

If object-style determinacy can be explained without remainder by primitive determinate objects, natural joints, structural facts, or physical laws, and if that explanation also accounts for givenness, scale-relativity, anchoring cost, inheritance-lag, and cross-operator divergence, then selection realism loses its abductive advantage. It should narrow from a meta-ontology to a heuristic account of interface formation.

### 7.2 Failure of `L0` non-neutrality

If no defensible distinction can be maintained between sub-determinate non-neutrality and determinate hidden structure, `L0` collapses into either vacuity or a disguised object-first domain. In that case SRT must either provide a better model of pre-object constraint or abandon the strong metaphysical reading.

### 7.3 Failure of reality-strength

If the four dimensions of reality-strength do not predict patterned differences in revision, resistance, convergence, and inheritance, then the R1-R4 model narrows. For example, if high-R3 and low-R3 classification revisions distribute anchoring costs in the same way, intervention-resistance is not doing the work SRT assigns to it.

### 7.4 Failure of consequence-return

If concern, value, subjectivity, and responsibility can be fully explained without reference to bearing-positions and consequence-return—for example, by preference satisfaction, external function, computational integration, or behavioral output alone—then SRT's extension into value and subjectivity narrows.

### 7.5 Failure of the consciousness placement

If consciousness can be fixed by a single information-integration, access, report, or functional metric independent of bearing-position, consequence-return, and self-inscription, then SRT's placement of consciousness fails. Conversely, if experience proves to be an ungenerable primitive that cannot be situated within selection, embodiment, consequence-return, and reflexive self-inscription, the model also fails or narrows.

### 7.6 Failure of theory self-reflection

SRT also applies its scaffold/cage distinction to itself. If the theory begins to classify everything in advance, immunize itself against pressure, or replace rather than reopen inquiry, then it has become the kind of stable structure it warns against. Its concepts are tools for re-seeing, not final objects of loyalty.

## 8. Conclusion

SRT begins from a simple shift: stability is not the starting point; stability is the history of selection that has succeeded in leaving itself behind as a usable world. Objects, subjects, values, institutions, and consciousness are not denied. They are relocated. Their reality lies not in being first, but in being anchored, borne, inherited, and capable of shaping what comes next.

The cup is still on the table. Science is not dissolved. Everyday action is not refuted. What changes is the order of explanation. The world that appears already there is no longer treated as a primitive scene. It is a sedimented achievement: exclusion has drawn contours, shaping has made them operable, inscription has given them weight, anchoring has made them consequential, and order has receded into the background as the ground on which later choice occurs.

Selection realism is therefore neither subjective idealism nor a new total system. It is a proposal for where philosophy should begin when object-first givenness has become too successful to notice itself. Before asking what exists as a finished thing, we should ask how anything becomes determinate, load-bearing, and inheritable enough to stand there as reality.
