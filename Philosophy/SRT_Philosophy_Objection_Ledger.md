4. Which canonical file owns the underlying term?
5. Does the claim explain more than existing frameworks, or merely rename them?

## 2. O-Phil-1 — Physicalist reduction

### Strongest form

A physicalist can argue that all apparently distinct `L_1` phenomena will eventually be explained by sufficiently detailed physical, computational, or neuroscientific description. If so, SRT’s triadic ontology may be only a vocabulary overlay on physicalism.

### What it targets

- `L_1` as more than a reportable state.
- explanatory gap claims.
- SRT as meta-ontology rather than interface theory.

### SRT response

SRT should not merely say “physicalism misses experience.” It must specify **which operation** is missed:

1. **Manifestation** — how a latent possibility becomes determinate for an operator.
2. **Anchoring** — how a candidate state becomes stable enough to count as real in a domain.
3. **First-person access** — how an event is not merely described but lived as an occurrence.
4. **Update cost** — how changing a world-model requires paying `\Psi_f` rather than just rewriting symbols.

### Narrowing condition

If physicalist models can account for manifestation, anchoring, first-person access, and update cost without remainder, SRT should be narrowed from **meta-ontology** to **interface / compression / modeling framework**.

### Editing rule

Avoid writing “physicalism is false.” Prefer:

> SRT treats physicalism as a powerful `L_2` stabilization and asks whether that stabilization also explains the `L_0 \to L_1` anchoring operation.

## 3. O-Phil-2 — Subjective idealism collapse

### Strongest form

If existence depends on selection, critics may read SRT as saying that reality is created by a subject or observer.

### What it targets

- `Existence ≡ Being Selected`.
- pan-selectionist slogans.
- participatory observation language.

### SRT response

Always pair selection language with cost language:

\[
\text{Filter Shape}=f(\theta),\qquad \text{Resistance Cost}=\Psi_f(L_0,\hat G_\theta)>0
\]

`\theta` helps determine the cut, but the cut must survive resistance. A fantasy, hallucination, failed model, or unstable interpretation may be selected locally but fail to stabilize across time, action, and other operators.

### Withdrawal condition

If `\Psi_f` is not operationally distinguishable from subjective confidence, then SRT loses its anti-idealist guardrail and must weaken claims about objective resistance.

### Editing rule

Every high-level “selection creates reality” statement needs a nearby “selection pays resistance” qualifier.

## 4. O-Phil-3 — Panpsychist combination problem

### Strongest form

If micro-events involve selection, why is SRT not just panpsychism? If every selection has an “inside,” how do micro-insides combine into a unified subject?

### What it targets

- micro-scale selection language.
- electron / seed / proto-interiority metaphors.
- consciousness continuity claims.

### SRT response

SRT should distinguish **selection event** from **subjecthood**.

A selection event becomes a candidate subject only when further criteria are met:

1. nonzero and structured `d-value`;
2. integrated selection bandwidth;
3. minimal `L_2` closure or memory-like stabilization;
4. subject-boundary criteria;
5. failure-sensitive update under `\Psi_f`.

Micro-selection alone is not consciousness.

### Narrowing condition

If SRT cannot operationalize the transition from micro-selection to subject-boundary formation, then it should avoid claiming to solve the combination problem and instead say it **reframes** it.

### Editing rule

Use:

> micro-selection is not macro-subjecthood

not:

> every micro-entity has consciousness.

## 5. O-Phil-4 — Sophisticated representationalism

### Strongest form

Representationalists need not claim that the brain stores a perfect inner picture. They can argue that task-specific internal content vehicles, with specificity, invariance, and downstream consumption, explain action better than coupling language.

### What it targets

- anti-representational coupling axiom.
- protocol-first language.
- “map vs lock-opening” metaphors.

### SRT response

SRT should not reject representation globally. It should say:

- representation is real and useful in some task regimes;
- representation is not the universal primitive of cognition;
- what matters is whether a structure lowers anchoring cost and improves selection.

### Withdrawal condition

If specificity / invariance / downstream-consumer evidence predicts task success better than payability or coupling-efficiency measures across a task family, protocol-first SRT should be narrowed to online-action, embodied-control, or high-cost contexts.

### Editing rule

Avoid “representation is false.” Prefer:

> representation is one stabilized `L_2` strategy among others, not the first principle of cognition.

## 6. O-Phil-5 — Language expansion against ineffability

### Strongest form

New languages, mathematics, training regimes, and shared practices may expand expressive capacity enough to dissolve many “ineffable” phenomena.

### What it targets

- explanatory gap.
- dimensional mismatch.
- saturated phenomena.

### SRT response

Keep `H-Phil-Ineffability` typed:

| Type | Survives what? |
|---|---|
| Interface insufficiency | survives only relative to current language and concepts |
| Dimensional compression | survives only if projection loss remains under improved encoding |
| High-cost approximation | survives if approximation exists but is not payable under current conditions |

### Withdrawal condition

If language expansion and shared training remove the relevant loss, withdraw principle-level ineffability. Keep only local interface or cost claims.

### Editing rule

Never write “experience is unsayable” without saying whether this means current-interface insufficiency, compression loss, or high-cost approximation.

## 7. O-Phil-6 — Formal self-reference already exists

### Strongest form

Logic, type theory, set theory, fixed-point theorems, Gödel coding, and semantic hierarchies already distinguish safe and unsafe self-reference. SRT may be reinventing known formal work.

### What it targets

- paradox as boundary failure.
- Liar/Russell/Zeno generalization.
- “topological short-circuit” language.

### SRT response

SRT should not claim to solve all paradoxes. It should claim to provide a **layer diagnosis** for why certain paradoxes arise when a system attempts unstratified closure.

Safe self-reference includes:

- indexical self-reference;
- quotation and token-reference;
- object-language / meta-language separation;
- delayed feedback;
- Gödel-style encoded self-reference.

The target is pathological closure at one flat level.

### Withdrawal condition

If a given paradox is better explained by a domain-specific formal treatment with no useful `L_0/L_1/L_2` layer distinction, SRT should defer to that treatment.

### Editing rule

Use “diagnose” or “retype,” not “solve,” unless a formal derivation is supplied.

## 8. O-Phil-7 — Is–ought gap

### Strongest form

Even if norms are stabilized selection histories, it does not follow that they are morally justified. SRT may confuse descriptive emergence with normative legitimacy.

### What it targets

- `L_2` as normative closure.
- ethics and political philosophy extensions.
- virtue and responsibility claims.

### SRT response

Separate three levels:

| Level | Claim |
|---|---|
| Real constraint | a norm constrains future selection because it is stabilized |
| Legitimacy | a norm is justified because it preserves/expands selectable futures under acceptable cost |
| Pathology | a norm survives by hiding, exporting, or inheriting friction asymmetrically |

### Withdrawal condition

If SRT cannot define legitimacy conditions beyond stabilization, its ethics must remain descriptive social ontology, not normative theory.

### Editing rule

Do not let `L_2` closure silently imply “good.” Add legitimacy/pathology tests.

## 9. O-Phil-8 — Political legitimation problem

### Strongest form

If institutions are stabilized `L_2`, then oppressive institutions may appear ontologically legitimate simply because they persist.

### What it targets

- political philosophy.
- collective selection.
- institutional stability.

### SRT response

Political stability must be filtered through:

1. friction export — who pays the hidden `\Psi_f` cost?
2. future-selectability — does the structure preserve future possible choices?
3. occlusion — does the structure narrow `d-value` or hide alternatives?
4. reversibility — can affected operators modify or exit the institution?
5. inheritance — are costs passed to weaker or future operators?

### Withdrawal condition

If these diagnostics are not defined, political SRT risks becoming a theory of power stabilization rather than legitimacy.

### Editing rule

Institutional `L_2` reality is not institutional justification.

## 10. O-Phil-9 — Neuroscience underdetermination

### Strongest form

Many SRT philosophical claims can be mapped to neuroscience in multiple ways. Neural correlates may underdetermine the philosophical interpretation.

### What it targets

- consciousness / emotion / agency mappings.
- predictive processing / FEP comparisons.
- d-value proxies.

### SRT response

Every neural mapping should be typed:

| Mapping type | Status |
|---|---|
| analogy | explanatory comparison only |
| bridge hypothesis | plausible mapping, not yet decisive |
| operational proxy | measurable stand-in with failure condition |
| strong prediction | P4 claim requiring empirical risk |

### Withdrawal condition

If neural evidence supports multiple incompatible SRT readings equally well, the relevant claim must be marked underdetermined.

### Editing rule

Do not write “SRT predicts neural X” unless the proxy and falsification condition are explicit.

## 11. O-Phil-10 — Mathematical metaphor charge

### Strongest form

Equations in philosophy files may be decorative: they can create the appearance of rigor without adding testable structure.

### What it targets

- formulas for explanatory gap, paradox, gift, normativity, saturation, etc.
- theorem labels in bridge files.

### SRT response

Every formula should have a declared role:

| Formula role | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Definition | fixes the meaning of a term inside SRT |
| Model | gives a candidate structure for analysis |
| Analogy | clarifies an intuition but carries no formal burden |
| Proxy | names a measurable or operational stand-in |
| Placeholder | marks a future formalization slot and must not be overclaimed |

### Withdrawal condition

If a formula cannot be classified into one of these roles, remove it or rewrite it as prose.

### Editing rule

Prefer one honest prose sentence over an equation with unclear burden.
