# SRT Ethics — PH-SS Guardrails Companion

> **Purpose**: Harden the ethics layer against the is-ought gap, stability-as-goodness, d-value absolutism, and poetic-math overclaim.  
> **Status**: Companion bridge file. It does not replace `SRT_Philosophy_Ethics.md` or `SRT_Ethics_Agency.md`.  
> **Use rule**: Read this before upgrading any ethics claim involving `d-value`, moral progress, virtue, love, grief, responsibility, agency, normativity, or social `L_2`.

---

## 0. Core ethics guardrail

SRT ethics should distinguish three things:

```text
moral reality      = a value/norm/concern becomes real as a constraint;
moral intensity    = the concern has high d-value or high affective / existential weight;
moral legitimacy   = the concern or norm survives further tests of future selectability,
                     friction distribution, cross-subject bandwidth, and correction.
```

Minimal warning:

> `d-value` expansion is not automatically moral progress. `L_2` stabilization is not automatically moral legitimacy.

**PH-SS addressed**: `PH-SS-06`, `PH-SS-08`, `PH-SS-12`.

---

## 1. Moral legitimacy ladder

| Level | Name | Meaning | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | Local preference / local d-intensity | a system strongly cares about something | morally relevant but not yet legitimate |
| L1 | Stabilized concern | the concern repeatedly anchors behavior and interpretation | ethically real but still not justified |
| L2 | Shared norm | concern becomes socially recognized, taught, enforced, or ritualized | socially real; legitimacy still open |
| L3 | Legitimacy-candidate norm | preserves or expands future selectability without hidden friction export | candidate for moral justification |
| L4 | Pathological norm | persists by occlusion, coercion, shame, inherited cost, or asymmetric friction export | real but ethically suspect |

Use rule:

> Ethics claims should specify whether they refer to moral intensity, moral reality, or moral legitimacy.

---

## 2. Legitimacy tests

A stabilized norm or high-`d-value` concern becomes a **legitimacy candidate** only if it tends to pass these tests.

| Test | Question | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Future-selectability test | Does it preserve or expand future selectable possibilities for affected agents? | moral closure / lethal `L_2` |
| Friction-export test | Does it lower real friction, or merely export `Psi_f` to weaker agents? | oppression, blame shifting, hidden labor |
| Cross-subject d-bandwidth test | Does it widen shared `d-value` recognition beyond the self / in-group? | narcissistic or tribal value expansion |
| Subject-generation test | Does it protect the emergence of future subjects and agency? | paternalism, infantilization, subject suppression |
| Reversibility / correction test | Can affected agents contest, exit, repair, or revise the norm? | frozen moral `L_2` |
| Occlusion test | Does it hide alternatives, costs, or affected subjects? | structural evil / invisible harm |
| Suffering-signal test | Does it suppress signal-type suffering instead of learning from it? | blind pain-minimization / structural blindness |

## 3. Reading `T-Eth-1` safely

Legacy / current ethics language may say:

```text
Moral progress iff d-value increases.
```

This is too strong if read literally. Safer reading:

> `d-value` expansion can be a necessary signal of moral widening, but it is not sufficient for moral legitimacy.

### Why not sufficient?

A system can expand `d-value` in pathological ways:

- it can care more intensely about domination;
- it can widen in-group concern while excluding outsiders;
- it can expand agency for one group by exporting friction to another;
- it can intensify moral feeling while reducing future selectability;
- it can stabilize a powerful norm through shame, fear, or punishment.

### Replacement formula role

If retaining a formula, mark it as:

```text
Formula role: phenomenological / directional model, not a sufficient condition for moral legitimacy.
```

Safer formulation:

```text
Moral legitimacy requires d-expansion + non-exported friction + future-selectability + correction channels.
```

## 4. Friction-export test

### Definition

A norm, institution, relationship, or moral demand exports friction when it reduces apparent `Psi_f` for one operator by increasing hidden `Psi_f` for another.

```text
apparent moral order = low visible friction for dominant operator
hidden moral cost    = displaced Psi_f borne by weaker / invisible operators
```

### Examples

| Pattern | Hidden mechanism |
|---|---|
| “obedience as virtue” | transfers uncertainty and agency-cost to subordinate agents |
| “harmony at all costs” | suppresses conflict signals and exports suffering to those harmed |
| “sacrifice as moral purity” | converts one group’s future selectability into another group’s stability |
| “responsibility without power” | assigns blame where `theta_acc` / selectable action-space is absent |

### Ethical guardrail

> A moral norm that survives by exporting `Psi_f` is `L_2`-real but not thereby legitimate.

---

## 5. Ought-implies-selectable refinement

Legacy formula:

```text
Ought(sigma) => exists theta_acc: P(sigma | theta_acc) > epsilon
```

Safe reading:

> An ethical demand is illegitimate or must be recalibrated if the demanded action is not selectable under the agent’s actual embodied, social, cognitive, and institutional constraints.

### Additions

The accessible parameter set should include:

```text
theta_acc = embodied capacity
          + cognitive bandwidth
          + social permission
          + institutional access
          + material resources
          + temporal opportunity
          + trauma / occlusion state
```

### Responsibility recalibration

Responsibility should be reduced or redistributed when:

- selectable action-space is structurally blocked;
- `Psi_f` is beyond payable range;
- `L_2` norms hide the actual constraint;
- agents are assigned responsibility without agency;
- the only selectable options are all harmful.

## 6. Love / grief / gift formulas: poetic-math guardrails

Ethics files may use powerful formulas for love, grief, gift, virtue, or infinite friction. These are valuable but should not be overread.

| Theme | Safe formula role | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Love | phenomenological / existential-weight model | not literal infinity unless operationalized |
| Grief | coupling-loss / phantom-anchoring model | not proof that `Psi_f` mathematically diverges |
| Gift | phase model of moral/economic transition | not claim that all gifts instantly become exchange |
| Virtue | d-calibration / theta-calibration model | not automatic moral superiority |
| Suffering | signal vs structural diagnostic | not suffering-maximization |

Use label:

```text
Formula role: phenomenological model / not literal divergence unless operationalized.
```

---

## 7. Responsibility-bandwidth refinement

Responsibility should not simply scale with `d-value`. High `d` can increase the moral weight of a situation, but responsibility depends on selectable agency.

Safer decomposition:

```text
moral weight      ~ d-value / affected future-selectability
responsibility    ~ accessible meta-selection capacity
culpability       ~ responsibility - structural blockage - unpayable friction
repair obligation ~ capacity to reduce future harm / restore selectability
```

### Guardrail

> High concern does not automatically imply high blame. High harm does not automatically imply the agent had selectable alternatives.

---

## 8. Ethics claim checklist

Before approving an ethics claim, ask:

```text
[ ] Is this about moral intensity, moral reality, or moral legitimacy?
[ ] Does it confuse stable L2 with justified L2?
[ ] Does it treat d-value expansion as sufficient for moral progress?
[ ] Does it check whether friction is reduced or exported?
[ ] Does it preserve future selectability for affected agents?
[ ] Does it include correction / exit / reversibility channels?
[ ] Does it distinguish responsibility from culpability?
[ ] Does it mark poetic formulas as model / analogy / placeholder?
[ ] Does it link to occlusion and suffering guardrails where relevant?
```

## 10. Minimal conclusion

SRT ethics becomes stronger when it says:

```text
Norms can be real without being good.
Values can be intense without being legitimate.
Responsibility requires selectable agency.
Moral progress requires future-selectability, non-exported friction, and correction channels.
Love and grief can carry ontological weight without requiring literal mathematical infinity.
```
