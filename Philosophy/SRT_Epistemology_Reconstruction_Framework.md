# SRT Epistemology Reconstruction Framework v0.1

> **Role**: active Layer-2 problem map for epistemology under Architecture v2. It sits between `SRT_Constitution_V1.md` and specific epistemic theories, philosophical models, historical studies, formal protocols or deep wells.
>
> **It is not** a general SRT theory of knowledge, a proof that all truth is position-relative, a philosophy-of-mathematics rewrite, or a retroactive constitutionalization of the pre-existing forcing–CH project.
>
> **Claim ceiling**: this framework may reorganize epistemic questions, inherited assets and evidence burdens. It does not by itself establish a new theory of truth, justification, method identity, objectivity, collective knowledge or a Case-C epistemology increment.

### 0.1 Scope

This framework covers epistemology-facing questions about:

- how claims, evidence items, methods, proofs, models, concepts and public knowledge objects become identifiable units;
- when an epistemic object or method remains the same across representation, historical change and reconstruction;
- how evidence is attributed, measured, normalized, admitted and withdrawn;
- which historical resources were available to a knower, community or method at a declared stage;
- how reasons, learned biases, commitments, denials, uncertainty and revision organize inquiry;
- how knower / analyst / community position affects access without automatically collapsing objectivity;
- how public institutions, archives, textbooks, standards and naming practices stabilize knowledge objects.

It does **not** settle:

- a correspondence / coherence / pragmatic theory of truth;
- mathematical realism versus anti-realism in general;
- consciousness or phenomenal subjectivity;
- a universal social construction thesis;
- collective subjecthood or moral responsibility;
- the canonical ontology of `L0`, `L1`, `L2`, `d`, `Psi_f` or `T_dir`;
- whether any specific SRT formalism is the privileged realization of knowledge.

## 1. Domain starting picture / Given-Ones inventory

Epistemic practice legitimately requires portable public objects. The constitutional question is not whether such objects are false, but which construction, equivalence and history conditions are hidden when they are treated as self-sufficient.

| Given One | Domain-native meaning | Typical evidence / operation | What this framework keeps open |
|---|---|---|---|
| proposition / claim | content that can be asserted, denied, justified or evaluated | argument, testimony, proof, observation, text | whether formulation, inferential role and historical use are being collapsed into one content identity |
| evidence item | observation, datum, source, proof step, document or result admitted as support | measurement, archive, citation, replication, derivation | who defined the item, what was normalized away, what target it supports, and withdrawal conditions |
| reason | consideration that bears on belief, inference or action | argument, rule, testimony, salience, learned knowledge | represented reason != operative bias != justified uptake != agent-owned commitment |
| method | repeatable family of operations used to generate / test / transform results | proof technique, measurement protocol, experimental method, historiographic procedure | same goal/result/notation/intertranslatability != automatically same method |
| proof | public inferential artifact establishing a theorem relative to rules/background | derivation, formal proof, explanatory proof | proof identity != theorem identity != method identity |
| model / representation | structured surrogate or formal description used for inference | equations, diagrams, coordinates, encodings, simulations | redescription != object change; representation equivalence != historical availability |
| fact / result | stabilized outcome treated as portable across contexts | theorem, measurement result, empirical regularity | result identity may survive while method/evidence/consequence attribution changes |
| concept / quantity | stable public category used to identify phenomena | definition, operational standard, measurement convention | constitutive role can be revisable and constrained rather than arbitrary or ontologically creative |
| knower / agent | individual who learns, infers, acts, reports or revises | reasoning, testimony, expertise, decision | access position, causal efficacy, responsibility and subjecthood are non-identical roles |
| community / discipline | distributed practice that transmits standards, methods and records | teaching, peer review, canon, archive, institutional uptake | public stabilization != collective subjecthood; community availability != individual availability |
| historical stage | declared repertoire and record at a time | contemporaneous texts, reviews, correspondence, textbooks | later reconstructability != earlier processability |
| consensus / canon | publicly stabilized classification or accepted result | textbook naming, review literature, standards, citation networks | stability != truth by itself; dissent != deficit by itself |

Mandatory guard:

```text
publicly stabilized != naturally pre-given
constructed != arbitrary
position-relative access != no objectivity
later equivalence != historical availability
successful result != faithful reason / method / mechanism
consensus != truth by definition
```

## 2. Constitution-to-epistemology translation

| Constitution operation | Epistemology-native question | Existing object pressured | What becomes visible | Boundary / non-entailment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ARTICULATION | How did this claim, fact, method, proof, evidence item or concept become the unit being evaluated? | proposition, method, datum, concept | archive cut, operational definition, coding rule, naming, grouping, representative case | does not imply epistemic objects are unreal or arbitrary |
| STILL-THIS | Under what transformation is a claim/proof/method/model still counted as the same epistemic object? | method family, proof identity, representation, theory | equivalence grain, invariant role, load-bearing operation, translation, re-identification | Constitution supplies no universal identity criterion |
| OWN-HISTORY | Which later reformulations, sources, revisions or consequences count as this method/claim/community's own history? | genealogy, attribution, canon, concept history | source provenance, development line, external reconstruction, anachronism, ownership of revision | chronology alone does not establish epistemic ownership |
| CURRENT PROCESSABILITY | What evidence, concepts, operations and alternatives were actually available to the knower or community at that stage? | hindsight, method choice, rational reconstruction | repertoire, training, resource/access constraints, live alternatives, historical admissibility | later derivability != earlier availability; bounded agents need not exhaust logical possibility |
| CONSEQUENCE ATTRIBUTION | Which success, error, uptake, exclusion or revision belongs to the claim, method, evidence procedure, agent, community or institution? | proof success, empirical confirmation, consensus, error | result vs method vs infrastructure vs analyst contribution; consequence landing level | successful output != faithful mechanism; community outcome != collective bearer proof |
| EVALUATIVE RE-RUN | By what declared criterion is an account better justified, more objective, explanatory, robust or progressive? | justification, objectivity, explanation, theory change | invariance, convergence, counterevidence sensitivity, local-validity recovery, revision cost, excluded alternatives | no scalar `d` or universal ranking is licensed here |

These are executable burdens, not answers.

## 4. Bearer / position architecture

| Candidate entity / role | Epistemic object | Historical actor | Evidence bearer / source | Consequence bearer | Evaluator / analyst | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| proposition / theorem | yes | no | may be content of evidence | inferential consequences attach to uses, not phenomenally to proposition | evaluated by agents/community | public object only |
| proof / document / dataset | yes | artifact | preserves partial trace | may change later inquiry | analyst interprets boundary/provenance | not a subject/bearer by default |
| method family | yes, analyst/community individuated | enacted by historical actors | evidenced by documents/practice | affects reachable results / training | identity partly reconstructed | no automatic agency |
| individual knower | yes, as study unit | yes | testimony/reasoning/source | belief/action/reputation/responsibility may return | can self-evaluate and be evaluated | primary agent-level candidate |
| historical mathematician/scientist | yes | yes | contemporaneous production/use | success/failure/uptake may return | later analyst has asymmetric hindsight | actor-native repertoire must be declared |
| community / discipline | public object and social structure | distributed | maintains archives/standards | institutional consequences | analysts reconstruct | no collective bearer/subjecthood assumption required |
| archive / journal / textbook | object/infrastructure | no | selection/filtering surface | shapes future accessibility/canon | editors/analysts choose cuts | infrastructure, not epistemic subject |
| experimenter / historian / present analyst | yes | yes | creates coding and comparison procedures | inferential consequences | reflexive position must be declared | cannot be treated as neutral view from nowhere |
| model / AI system | possible epistemic artifact/actor-like tool | usually not historical knower in this framework | output may be evidence only under declared procedure | task consequences can occur | human/model roles must be separated | capability or trace != justified knower/subjecthood |

Mandatory guard:

```text
source != claim
artifact != bearer
method != agent
community != collective subject by default
analyst reconstruction != actor-native availability
having a reason representation != owning/revising the reason
```

## 7. Epistemology problem families

### E1 — Epistemic object formation and constrained objectification

**Core question**: How does something become a stable claim, datum, concept, fact, representation or evidence item without assuming either passive copying or arbitrary invention?

**Constitution operations**: ARTICULATION, STILL-THIS, EVALUATIVE RE-RUN.

**Objectification pressure**: proposition normalization, operational definition, archive cuts, model construction, public naming.

**Position pressure**: analyst/knower procedures help identify objects but are themselves constrained by invariance, repeatability, counterevidence and world resistance.

**Inherited assets / materials**: DiSalle/PH-METH02, Blind Spot, PH-MEM01, PH-SEM01, Schulte/PH-MR01.

**Mature neighbors**: philosophy of scientific representation, measurement theory, scientific realism/constructivism, inferentialism, operationalism, pragmatism, historical epistemology.

**Possible domain-local gain**: an explicit objectification declaration changes how an evidence object is coded or what independent convergence is required.

**Translation-only collapse**: if this only restates mature measurement/representation practice.

---

### E2 — Method, proof, model and representation identity

**Core question**: When are historically or representationally different epistemic operations still instances of the same method / proof family / model, and when does identity actually change?

**Constitution operations**: STILL-THIS, ARTICULATION, OWN-HISTORY.

**Objectification pressure**: proof-goal grouping, canonical representatives, modern notation, model equivalence, auxiliary-object inflation.

**Position pressure**: later analysts can see equivalences and substitutions unavailable to historical actors.

**Inherited assets / materials**: forcing–CH method-individuation protocol, D05b, DiSalle redescription guard, Rosenhagen identity pressure, Schulte adjacent-case audit.

**Mature neighbors**: identity of proofs, philosophy of mathematical practice, model equivalence, scientific representation, historiography of mathematical methods.

**Possible domain-local gain**: a method-identity rule changes a classification while surviving representation invariance, historical-availability and granularity controls.

**Translation-only collapse**: if mature proof/method historiography already supplies the same classification consequences.

### E3 — Evidence attribution, admissibility and measurement

**Core question**: What makes an observation, document, proof, output or metric evidence for this claim rather than merely available information?

**Constitution operations**: CONSEQUENCE ATTRIBUTION, ARTICULATION, EVALUATIVE RE-RUN.

**Objectification pressure**: measurement rules, scoring, source extraction, performance metrics, archive inclusion, selection of controls.

**Position pressure**: the analyst defines coding/target relations; evidence must retain source-native scope and withdrawal conditions.

**Inherited assets / materials**: PH-METH02, PH-METH01, forcing control-selection and archive-adequacy protocols, AI wrong-reasons negative control, Gödel card as weak formal-closure caution.

**Mature neighbors**: confirmation theory, measurement theory, robustness/triangulation, causal inference, evidence hierarchies, philosophy of experiment, source criticism.

**Possible domain-local gain**: separating objectification/bearer/target roles changes what evidence is admissible or causes an honest STOP/NO-GO.

**Translation-only collapse**: if this is simply competent source criticism and measurement practice.

### E4 — Historical availability, ownership and genealogy

**Core question**: Which prior resources and later developments count as an epistemic object's own history, and what was actually available at the stage being judged?

**Constitution operations**: OWN-HISTORY, CURRENT PROCESSABILITY, STILL-THIS.

**Objectification pressure**: textbook compression, retrospective notation, canonical lineages, archive survival, hindsight substitution.

**Position pressure**: present reconstruction is asymmetrically informed by later success.

**Inherited assets / materials**: forcing archive and baselines, Wang/PH-MEM01, DiSalle historical conceptual change, Nagase learning pressure.

**Mature neighbors**: historical epistemology, history of science/mathematics, genealogy, contextualist historiography, path dependence.

**Possible domain-local gain**: typed historical availability changes a method/claim classification relative to a hindsight-equivalence grouping.

**Translation-only collapse**: if ordinary historiography already forces exactly the same source discipline and result.

### E5 — Situated knower, participant and community position

**Core question**: How do access, embodiment, expertise, attention, institutional role and participation condition inquiry without turning all truth into private perspective?

**Constitution operations**: ARTICULATION, CONSEQUENCE ATTRIBUTION, OWN-HISTORY.

**Objectification pressure**: view-from-nowhere description, community-as-single-agent compression, disagreement-as-deficit, expert/nonexpert flattening.

**Bearer pressure**: individual knower, participant, institution and community are distinct roles; public stabilization does not establish a collective subject.

**Inherited assets / materials**: Blind Spot, Amornbunchornvej, Wu, Sulic, PH-CONSC02 as adjacent access guard.

**Mature neighbors**: social epistemology, standpoint/situated knowledge, expertise, distributed cognition, disagreement epistemology, philosophy of testimony.

**Possible domain-local gain**: declaring position/access roles prevents an invalid transfer or changes evidence interpretation while preserving cross-position constraints.

**Translation-only collapse**: if standard social/situated epistemology already supplies the same distinction.

### E6 — Reasoning, commitment, uncertainty, revision and justification

**Core question**: How are reasons made operative, how can inquiry remain epistemically open while action closes, and what makes a belief/position revisable rather than merely stable?

**Constitution operations**: CURRENT PROCESSABILITY, EVALUATIVE RE-RUN, CONSEQUENCE ATTRIBUTION, OWN-HISTORY.

**Objectification pressure**: answer correctness, confidence scores, reasons-as-static-premises, binary assertion/denial, certainty as closure.

**Position pressure**: a finite agent may have multiple valid continuations and incomplete guarantees; revisability and reason ownership remain separate from mere transition efficacy.

**Inherited assets / materials**: Wu/PH-AG02, Sulic/PH-AG03, Simonelli/PH-SEM01, Wang/PH-MEM01, Georgatos, Sawyer method audit, AI wrong-reasons control.

**Mature neighbors**: epistemology of reasons, virtue epistemology, Bayesian/uncertainty frameworks, belief revision, inferentialism, disagreement, bounded rationality.

**Possible domain-local gain**: a typed separation among reason representation, operative constraint, commitment, consequence and revision changes an actual reasoning/evidence classification.

**Translation-only collapse**: if it reduces to familiar bounded-rationality / belief-revision distinctions.

---

### E7 — Public stabilization, infrastructure and epistemic institutions

**Core question**: How do archives, journals, standards, textbooks, naming conventions and communities stabilize knowledge objects, and when does stabilization improve or distort future inquiry?

**Constitution operations**: ARTICULATION, OWN-HISTORY, CURRENT PROCESSABILITY, EVALUATIVE RE-RUN.

**Objectification pressure**: canon selection, publication survival, standard setting, aggregate consensus, institutional uptake.

**Bearer pressure**: institution/community effects can be real without treating the institution as a conscious or morally unified bearer.

**Inherited assets / materials**: forcing archive/corpus protocols, DiSalle conceptual history, weak disunity-of-science pressure, manuscript/canon interfaces.

**Mature neighbors**: social epistemology, sociology/history of science, peer-review studies, institutional epistemology, bibliometrics, canon formation.

**Possible domain-local gain**: infrastructure selection changes what future inquiry can process, and a declared archival/canonical cut alters a downstream historical inference.

**Translation-only collapse**: if ordinary archival/social-epistemology practice fully explains the effect.
