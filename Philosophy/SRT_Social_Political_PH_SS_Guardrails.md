# SRT Social / Political PH-SS Guardrails Companion

> **Purpose**: Harden SRT social ontology and political philosophy against two errors: mere social construction and institutional reification.  
> **Status**: Companion bridge file. It does not replace `SRT_Social_Economics_CompactCore.md` or `SRT_Political_Philosophy.md`.  
> **Use rule**: Read this before upgrading claims about society, market, money, law, institution, legitimacy, inequality, recognition, or political order.

---

## 0. Core social-political guardrail

SRT should distinguish:

```text
social reality       = a collective L2 structure is stabilized enough to constrain behavior;
institutional force  = a rule or structure has enforcement / consequence-return power;
legitimacy           = the structure preserves or expands future selectability without hidden friction export;
pathology            = the structure survives by occlusion, coercion, or asymmetric cost transfer.
```

Minimal warning:

> Social `L_2` reality is not social legitimacy. Institutional persistence is not political justification.

**PH-SS addressed**: `PH-SS-06`, `PH-SS-09`, `PH-SS-12`.

---

## 1. Collective `L_2` definition

### Definition-like guardrail

> A social fact is a collective `L_2` structure stabilized by repeated recognition, symbolic encoding, enforcement, memory, and consequence return.

This prevents two reductions:

| Bad reduction | Why insufficient | SRT correction |
|---|---|---|
| social facts are just private beliefs | cannot explain enforcement, institution, money, law, durable constraint | collective `L_2` with consequence return |
| institutions are self-justifying because they persist | cannot distinguish legitimacy from domination | add future-selectability and friction-export tests |

### Minimal mechanism

```text
recognition -> repetition -> symbolic encoding -> enforcement -> memory -> consequence return -> L2 stabilization
```

## 2. Social object table

| Social object | SRT reading | Legitimacy guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Money | exchange pathway stabilized by shared trust, pricing, enforcement, and memory | does it distribute agency or extract future selectability? |
| Law | normative `L_2` backed by authority, procedure, enforcement, and contestation channels | can affected subjects contest and revise it? |
| Identity | self-selection and other-recognition jointly stabilized across time | does recognition expand or trap subjecthood? |
| Culture | cross-generational selection preferences hardened into shared forms | does it preserve reselection capacity? |
| Organization | role, responsibility, and action pathways stabilized as collective agency | are agency and blame aligned? |
| Market | distributed selection process with price / metric protocols | does it reveal value or hide externalized `Psi_f`? |
| Institution | formal + informal constraints plus enforcement and memory | does stability come from legitimacy or coercion? |

## 3. Institutional legitimacy ladder

| Level | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| I0 | coordination pattern | people repeatedly act in compatible ways |
| I1 | stabilized convention | pattern becomes expected and taught |
| I2 | institutionalized rule | pattern gains enforcement and memory |
| I3 | legitimacy-candidate institution | affected agents can contest, exit, revise, and share benefits / costs |
| I4 | pathological institution | persists by occlusion, coercion, shame, cost export, or blocked exit |

Use rule:

> Institution = structured constraint. Legitimacy = structured constraint that remains corrigible, non-occlusive, and future-selectability-preserving.

---

## 4. Friction-export test for institutions

A social structure exports friction when it lowers visible cost for one group by increasing hidden `Psi_f` for another.

| Institution pattern | Hidden friction export |
|---|---|
| low-price market with invisible labor cost | consumer `Psi_f` reduced by worker / supplier `Psi_f` increase |
| bureaucratic compliance | institutional uncertainty shifted to applicants / weaker agents |
| punitive morality | group stability bought by shame, exclusion, or suppressed alternatives |
| extractive institution | elite future-selectability expanded by reducing others’ selectable futures |
| pseudo-open institution | participation is allowed symbolically but cannot change gate rules |

### Diagnostic question

```text
Who pays the Psi_f that makes this institution look stable, efficient, or moral?
```

---

## 5. Future-selectability test

A social or political order is healthier when it preserves and expands legitimate reselection capacity.

| Healthy sign | Pathological sign |
|---|---|
| affected subjects can revise rules | rules can only be obeyed, not modified |
| exit is possible without destruction | exit causes loss of identity, livelihood, or safety |
| dissent creates information | dissent is treated as disloyalty |
| costs are visible and contestable | costs are hidden or naturalized |
| gate rules can be reselected | gate rules are immune to affected agents |

Short formulation:

> Political legitimacy requires reselection capacity, not merely stable order.

## 6. Dehumanization as d-exclusion

Existing social economics formula:

```text
Dehumanization(i -> j) = d_i[theta_j] -> 0
```

Safe interpretation:

> Dehumanization is the social operation by which another subject is removed from the agent’s effective `d-value` field and treated as non-stake-bearing background.

### Political significance

Dehumanization enables:

- friction export without guilt;
- responsibility without recognition;
- punishment without mutual subjecthood;
- institutional exclusion with moral cover;
- violence treated as maintenance.

### Guardrail

`d_i[theta_j] -> 0` is not just lack of empathy. It is a structural removal of another operator from the space of morally relevant selection.

## 7. Market / money guardrails

SRT social economics may say:

```text
Money = L2 metric
Market = distributed selection operator
Value = stabilization expectation
```

These are useful but need guardrails.

| Claim | Risk | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Money as `L_2` metric | price becomes truth | price is a social metric protocol, not final value |
| Market as distributed selection | market outcome becomes legitimate | market selection can hide friction export |
| Value as stabilization expectation | stable demand becomes moral value | value prediction is not ethical justification |
| Trust as friction reduction | low-friction order becomes good | friction can be lowered by suppression or invisibilization |

Short warning:

> Efficient selection is not automatically legitimate selection.

---

## 8. Political responsibility and agency alignment

A political system becomes pathological when responsibility and agency are misaligned.

| Misalignment | SRT diagnosis |
|---|---|
| responsibility without selectable agency | blame assigned where `theta_acc` is blocked |
| power without consequence return | decision-makers do not pay downstream `Psi_f` |
| rule without revision channel | `L_2` hardens without reselection capacity |
| representation without update | public input does not modify gate rules |
| welfare without subjecthood | care becomes management rather than agency expansion |

### Test

```text
Does the person/group asked to pay, obey, or sacrifice also have access to revision, exit, and consequence-return channels?
```

---

## 9. Anti-relativism in social theory

SRT does not say that any socially stabilized belief is truth. Social objectivity requires constraint and correction.

Social truth / validity should be tested by:

- cross-operator alignment;
- intervention outcomes;
- visible cost distribution;
- affected-agent testimony;
- reversibility / correction capacity;
- resistance to propaganda and pure power stabilization;
- longitudinal effects on future selectability.

Short formulation:

> Social reality is selected, but not arbitrary; it is constrained by recognition, enforcement, cost, resistance, memory, and correction.

## 12. Minimal conclusion

SRT social / political theory becomes stronger when it says:

```text
Society is collective L2, not mere belief.
Institutions are real constraints, not automatically legitimate constraints.
Markets are distributed selection, not moral truth machines.
Money is an L2 metric, not final value.
Legitimacy requires reselection capacity, non-exported friction, and future-selectability.
Dehumanization is d-exclusion.
Political pathology begins when power, responsibility, and consequence-return are separated.
```
