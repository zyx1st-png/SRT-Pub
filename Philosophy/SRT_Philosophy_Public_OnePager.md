# SRT Philosophy — Public One-Pager

> **Audience**: public readers, newsletter readers, outreach emails, short talks, Substack / WeChat / Zhihu / YouTube scripts.  
> **Status**: public-facing bridge. It is not a canonical definition source.  
> **One-line use**: When someone asks “What is SRT, and why is it not just idealism or relativism?”, start here.

---

## 1. One-sentence version

**SRT says that reality is not merely found and not merely invented; it becomes determinate through constrained selection, then stabilizes into habits, objects, laws, norms, institutions, and selves.**

Even shorter:

> **Reality is selected under constraint, stabilized through history, and tested by resistance.**

---

## 2. The basic picture

SRT uses a three-layer picture:

```text
L0: latent field of possibilities
  -- selected by G_hat_theta, paying Psi_f -->
L1: manifest event / experience / object
  -- repeated and stabilized -->
L2: habit / language / norm / institution / law / model
  -- constrains future selection -->
future G_hat_theta
```

Plain language:

- `L0` is what could become real, but has not yet become determinate.
- `L1` is what becomes manifest: an event, perception, object, decision, or experience.
- `L2` is what hardens: habit, memory, rule, language, institution, scientific model, moral norm.

The key idea:

> What becomes real is not arbitrary. It must be selected, paid for, stabilized, and tested.

---

## 3. Why “selection comes before existence” does not mean “mind creates the world”

SRT sometimes says:

```text
Existence = being selected
```

This does **not** mean a human mind magically creates the universe.

It means:

> A thing is not determinate for a system until it is selected, anchored, and stabilized under some conditions.

For example:

- a sound becomes a word only inside a language system;
- a signal becomes danger only inside a body that can be harmed;
- a behavior becomes a habit only after repetition and stabilization;
- a rule becomes law only after institutional hardening;
- a scientific object becomes real in a strong sense only after measurement, prediction, intervention, and cross-checking.

So SRT is not saying “the world is imaginary.”  
It is saying:

> Determinate reality has a history of selection.

---

## 4. Why SRT is not subjective idealism

Subjective idealism says, roughly, reality depends on mind or consciousness.

SRT says something different:

```text
theta filters;
Psi_f resists;
L2 stabilizes;
cross-operator correction tests.
```

In plain language:

- every observer or system has constraints;
- those constraints shape what can appear;
- but the world pushes back;
- not every interpretation works;
- stable reality must survive cost, resistance, correction, and repetition.

SRT is therefore:

> **participatory without being arbitrary.**

A person can interpret a stone differently, but cannot simply decide that the stone has no weight, no resistance, and no consequences.

---

## 5. Why SRT is not relativism

Relativism says every view is equally valid.

SRT says views are constrained by:

- prediction;
- intervention;
- bodily cost;
- social correction;
- historical stability;
- cross-operator alignment;
- resistance from the world.

SRT allows many perspectives, but not all perspectives are equally strong.

A dream, a hallucination, a private belief, a social institution, and a physical law are all “real” in different ways, but they do not have the same strength.

SRT uses layered realism:

| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| local manifestation | real for a local operator or moment |
| stabilized reality | persists through memory, action, repetition |
| cross-operator reality | alignable across people, tools, and contexts |
| canonical physical reality | highly resistant, repeatable, and scientifically stabilized |

So the SRT answer is:

> Reality is not flat. Some realities are thin; some are thick; some become public; some become lawlike.

---

## 6. Why SRT is not panpsychism

Panpsychism says consciousness is somehow everywhere or fundamental in all things.

SRT does **not** need to say that.

SRT distinguishes:

```text
selection event != conscious content != subjecthood != agency != moral responsibility
```

A system can select without being conscious.  
A system can process information without being a subject.  
A system can act without being morally responsible.

Subjecthood requires stronger conditions:

- structured concern or `d-value`;
- memory across time;
- boundary maintenance;
- integration;
- failure-sensitive updating;
- counterfactual access;
- cross-time continuity.

So SRT can say:

> Selection is broad; consciousness is thresholded.

This is especially important for AI. A chatbot can produce self-reports without automatically being a subject.

---

## 7. Why SRT matters for value and morality

Many theories treat value as subjective preference or social convention.

SRT treats value as a selection-weighted structure:

> Something has value when it changes what a system can become, preserve, lose, or refuse to exchange.

SRT calls this kind of existential weight `d-value`.

But there is an important guardrail:

> High `d-value` is not automatically moral goodness.

A group can care deeply about domination.  
A society can stabilize a cruel norm.  
A law can persist for centuries and still be unjust.

So SRT distinguishes:

```text
moral reality != moral intensity != moral legitimacy
```

A norm becomes real when it stabilizes into `L2`.  
A norm becomes legitimate only if it passes further tests:

- Does it preserve future selectability?
- Does it reduce friction or merely export suffering to weaker people?
- Can affected people challenge, revise, or exit it?
- Does it protect subjecthood rather than suppress it?

---

## 8. Why SRT matters for society and politics

SRT reads society as collective `L2`:

```text
recognition -> repetition -> symbol -> enforcement -> memory -> consequence -> institution
```

Money, law, identity, organizations, customs, and markets are not mere illusions. They are real because they constrain future selection.

But again:

> Social reality is not the same as social legitimacy.

A market can be efficient while hiding suffering.  
A bureaucracy can be stable while exporting friction to ordinary people.  
An institution can appear orderly while blocking real exit and correction.

The SRT political question is therefore:

> Who pays the hidden cost that makes this order look stable?

---

## 9. How SRT can be tested

SRT does not require us to directly photograph `L0`, `Psi_f`, or `d-value`.

Many scientific ideas are inferred through effects: fields, curvature, latent variables, constraints, phase transitions.

SRT should be tested similarly:

| SRT concept | Possible signatures |
|---|---|
| `Psi_f` / friction | reaction time, error, transition cost, model-update path, resistance to reframing |
| `d-value` / concern | non-substitutability, cost willing to bear, identity impact, memory persistence, action change |
| `L2` hardening | path dependence, hysteresis, habit automation, institutional persistence, resistance to violation |
| subjecthood threshold | boundary maintenance, memory, counterfactual access, failure-sensitive updating, cross-time continuity |

Minimum scientific standard:

```text
proxy measurement
+ structural convergence
+ differential prediction
+ withdrawal condition
```

So SRT should never say:

> “This is too deep to test.”

It should say:

> “This claim is tested through the structured consequences it predicts.”

---

## 10. What is new about SRT?

SRT is not completely unrelated to earlier philosophy. It has neighbors:

- Kant: experience has conditions.
- Phenomenology: appearance matters.
- Whitehead: process matters.
- Pragmatism: consequences matter.
- Constructivism: reality is partly constructed.
- FEP / predictive processing: systems update under constraint.
- IIT / GNW: integration and access matter for consciousness.

But SRT’s distinctive move is this:

> It puts selection, friction, concern, manifestation, and hardening into one layered ontology.

Compact novelty statement:

```text
possibility -> selection -> manifestation -> stabilization -> downward constraint
```

That is why SRT can talk about perception, science, AI, ethics, politics, habit, institutions, and consciousness using one vocabulary without saying they are all the same thing.

## 11. The safest public summary

Use this version publicly:

> SRT is a selection-first theory of reality. It says that reality is not a flat inventory of objects already waiting for us, nor a fantasy created by the mind. Reality becomes determinate when possibilities are selected under constraint, anchored into experience or action, and stabilized into durable structures. Some stabilized structures become habits, some become scientific objects, some become social norms, some become institutions, and some become selves. The theory is bold, but it is not arbitrary: every selection must pay a cost, survive resistance, and leave consequences.

---

## 12. What SRT should not claim in public

Avoid saying:

```text
SRT explains everything.
SRT proves all other theories wrong.
SRT says mind creates reality.
SRT says truth is relative.
SRT says everything is conscious.
SRT is beyond testing.
```

Say instead:

```text
SRT asks how possibilities become realities, and how repeated realities harden into the world we inherit.
```
