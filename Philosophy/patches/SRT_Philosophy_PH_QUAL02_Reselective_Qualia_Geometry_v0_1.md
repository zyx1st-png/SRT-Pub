# SRT Philosophy Patch PH-QUAL02: Reselective Qualia Geometry v0.1

> **Status**: P3/P4 philosophy-of-mind bridge hardening patch.
>
> **Canonical caution**: this file does **not** define qualia, consciousness, `d`, `T_dir`, bearer, subjecthood, or reselectability canonically. It does not claim that reselectability proves phenomenal presence, and it does not close the phenomenal-necessity / SRT-zombie pressure point. Its narrower purpose is to test whether SRT can explain part of **phenomenal character** through bearer-indexed, history-bearing deformation of future reselectability.

---

## 0. Why this patch exists

Existing SRT work already contains four ingredients that have not yet been tightly integrated:

1. `Philosophy/Foundations_Annex/06_Qualia_Interface_Batch.md` places broad qualia mainly at `L1` and introduces a relational quality-space bridge:

   ```text
   Q_L1 = (E, Delta_ij)
   ```

   where phenomenological structure is studied through experienced similarity, neighborhood, clustering and deformation rather than through isolated ineffable atoms alone.

2. `PH-CONSC03` separates:

   ```text
   Selector != Bearer != Concern Domain != Experiencer
   ```

   and proposes that a strong structural-bearing candidate requires irreversible consequence return, history-bearing writeback and non-trivial change in the same bearer's future-selectability space.

3. `PH-CONSC04` preserves the hard boundary:

   ```text
   structural bearing != phenomenal bearing proved
   ```

   and leaves phenomenal necessity open.

4. The reselectability line treats system health / objecthood not as raw option count but as the capacity to remain consequence-bearing, reorganizable and reopenable across history.

The missing bridge is therefore not:

```text
reselectability -> consciousness
```

but the narrower question:

> **Once phenomenal presence is granted or independently supported, can the qualitative organization of experience be partly explained by how an event changes the same bearer's future reselectability?**

This patch records that hypothesis.

---

## 1. Main distinction: phenomenal presence versus phenomenal character

Current SRT should split the residual hard problem at least one step further:

```text
HP-presence:
Why is there phenomenal presence at all rather than a phenomenally empty but structurally complete bearer?

HP-character:
Given phenomenal presence, why does an event feel like this rather than that,
why does it belong to this bearer,
and why does it have this intensity, valence, neighborhood and temporal thickness?
```

Current SRT does **not** close `HP-presence`.

The candidate contribution of this patch concerns `HP-character`.

Therefore:

```text
reselective qualia geometry
!= proof of phenomenal necessity
```

but it may be a substantive bridge for explaining qualitative organization once phenomenality is in play.

---

## 2. Core bridge: qualia as first-person organization of future-selectability deformation

Let `B` denote a candidate continuing bearer and let `Omega_B(t)` denote, at bridge level only, the set / structure of futures, states, actions or selections still reachable for that bearer at time `t`.

Following `PH-CONSC03`, a consequence that is genuinely borne can leave a history-dependent rewrite such that:

```text
Omega_B(t + dt) != Omega_B(t)
```

This patch introduces a **test-local mnemonic**, not a canonical symbol:

```text
D^B_q : Omega_B(t) -> Omega_B(t + dt)
```

where `D^B_q` denotes the bearer-indexed reselective deformation associated with experiential event `q`.

The proposed constitutive bridge is:

> **Qualitative character may be partly understood as the first-person organization of a concern-weighted, history-bearing deformation of the same bearer's future reselectability.**

In compressed form:

```text
qualia_character(q, B)
~
first-person mode of D^B_q
```

The `~` is deliberate. It marks a bridge hypothesis, not an identity theorem.

---

## 3. Why this is stronger than “qualia are internal representations”

A representation-first account can say that a system encodes red, pain, sweetness, threat or bodily damage.

The SRT question is different:

> What changed for the continuing bearer because this event occurred?

A phenomenally relevant event is therefore not treated merely as a state label. It can reorganize:

- which candidates enter attention;
- which actions remain reachable;
- which paths become suppressed or privileged;
- what is remembered and later retrieved;
- what counts as danger, opportunity or concern;
- which previous paths become reopenable or unreopenable;
- how the same bearer updates future policy, expectation and orientation.

This gives the experience temporal thickness:

```text
past history
-> present consequence-bearing event
-> future-selectability rewrite
```

The hypothesis is that at least part of qualitative character tracks this structured transformation rather than an isolated instantaneous code.

## 4. Reselective Qualia Structure Hypothesis

### Claim PH-QUAL02-A — similarity bridge

For a bearer already admitted as a serious phenomenal candidate, experienced similarity between two qualia should systematically covary with similarity between their bearer-indexed reselective deformation profiles.

Bridge form:

```text
Delta_phen(q_i, q_j)
~
Dist(D^B_qi, D^B_qj)
```

where:

- `Delta_phen` is the experiential dissimilarity relation already motivated by the quality-space line;
- `Dist(...)` is an intentionally unspecified distance / comparison operator over reselective deformation profiles;
- neither side is claimed to reduce without remainder to the other.

The testable content is relational:

> If two experiences are phenomenally close, then — after controlling for obvious sensory and task confounds — their effects on attention, memory, candidate admission, action preparation, concern weighting and future reselection should tend to be correspondingly close. Large systematic dissociations would count against a strong version of the bridge.

This does not require qualia to be reducible to behavior. The relevant object is the history-bearing reorganization of the bearer, not merely overt output.

## 5. First-person ownership / for-me-ness

One central advantage of the bridge is that it gives SRT a non-representational route into ownership.

The important index is:

```text
D^B_q
```

not merely:

```text
representation("ME", q)
```

An event has a stronger `for-me` profile when:

1. its consequences close on the same continuing bearer;
2. it leaves history-dependent writeback in that bearer;
3. it changes that bearer's own reachable future-selection structure;
4. the relevant change is not fully resettable, transferable or externally absorbed while leaving `B` unchanged.

Thus a candidate route to ownership is:

```text
same-bearer consequence return
+ history writeback
+ future-selectability deformation
-> stronger structural basis for for-me-ness
```

Guardrail:

```text
structural basis for for-me-ness
!= phenomenal presence proved
```

This remains compatible with `PH-CONSC03` and `PH-CONSC04`.

## 6. Qualia dimensions that may be re-described in reselective terms

The bridge suggests the following decomposition.

### 6.1 Qualitative character

Different experiences correspond to different deformation profiles over the bearer's future candidate / action / attention / memory / concern structure.

```text
red != blue != pain != sweetness
```

need not mean only that different internal labels are activated. They may differ in how they reshape the bearer's next reachable possibilities.

### 6.2 Phenomenal similarity

Phenomenal neighborhood may partly reflect similarity of deformation profile.

This links the existing `quality space` program to SRT's future-selectability language.

### 6.3 Intensity

Intensity should **not** be identified with stimulus strength alone.

A stronger candidate read is that phenomenal intensity may covary with some combination of:

- breadth of affected future directions;
- degree of candidate reprioritization;
- persistence of writeback;
- irreversibility / recoverability burden;
- stake coupling;
- attentional capture;
- inability to externalize the consequence.

No scalar intensity formula is proposed here.

### 6.4 Temporal thickness

An experience is not necessarily a timeless point-event. Its qualitative identity may depend on how current state, acquired history and anticipated future alternatives are bound together.

This is especially relevant to pain, fear, grief, relief, recognition and insight.

### 6.5 Ineffability

A bearer-specific, high-dimensional deformation profile may be only partially compressible into `L2` verbal labels.

Therefore:

```text
ineffability
may partly reflect
lossy L1 -> L2 compression of high-dimensional bearer-specific structure
```

This does not prove that ineffability is exhausted by compression.

## 7. Valence must be separated from reselectability

A central guardrail is:

```text
Valence != Reselectability != Reselectability health
```

The theory must reject the naive mapping:

```text
pleasure = more options
pain = fewer options
```

because locally compressive experiences can protect long-horizon reselectability.

Example:

```text
acute pain
-> severe local narrowing of current action space
-> withdrawal / protection
-> preservation of bodily integrity
-> possible increase in long-horizon future reselectability
```

Conversely:

```text
strong positive reward
-> locally attractive expansion / repetition
-> habit capture or addiction
-> progressive long-horizon foreclosure
```

Therefore negative valence can accompany adaptive local closure, while positive valence can accompany pathological long-term foreclosure.

This is consistent with the broader reselectability rule that option count is not the target variable.

---

## 8. Pain as an instructive case

Pain is useful because it makes the local / global distinction visible.

A reselective reading of acute pain is not:

```text
pain = Psi_f
pain = d
pain = T_dir
pain = low reselectability
```

Instead:

> Pain can be modeled as a high-priority phenomenal event that strongly reorganizes the same bearer's near-term candidate space in response to stake-bearing bodily consequence.

It may:

- suppress competing actions;
- privilege withdrawal and protection;
- alter attention;
- create durable memory;
- change future threat weighting;
- make a previously neutral path difficult to re-enter.

This makes pain a candidate example of **local reselection compression in the service of longer-horizon reselection preservation**.

### Trauma contrast

The pathology appears when a once-adaptive local closure fails to reopen:

```text
acute protective compression
-> persistent historical writeback
-> overgeneralized candidate exclusion
-> reduced recoverability / reopening
-> chronic pathological foreclosure
```

This gives SRT a natural distinction between protective closure and maladaptive lock-in without identifying either with valence alone.

## 9. Relation to canonical `d`

Canonical `d` remains the scalar summary of stake-coupled concern / irreversible-risk sensitivity. This patch does not redefine it.

Therefore:

```text
d != qualia intensity
d != qualia richness
d != consciousness level
d > threshold != phenomenality proof
```

The useful bridge role is narrower:

> `d` helps distinguish ordinary discriminable changes from changes that strongly enter the bearer's stake-coupled concern structure.

Thus a phenomenological deformation may have:

```text
low stake coupling
```

while remaining richly discriminable, as in many ordinary perceptual contrasts.

Another deformation may be strongly:

```text
d-weighted
```

because it threatens irreversible bodily, social, identity or other concern-relevant loss.

`d` therefore weights concern relevance; it does not generate phenomenality.

---

## 10. Relation to `T_dir`

`T_dir` is also not qualia.

Its current v0 role is whether the system can read back and use its own selection direction for reorientation.

The present bridge suggests only:

```text
experience
-> reselective deformation
-> possibly self-readable directional pressure
```

Examples:

- pain may bias toward withdrawal / protection;
- fear may reorganize escape, freeze and vigilance candidates;
- curiosity may open exploratory candidates;
- insight may suddenly reopen previously inaccessible inferential paths.

But:

```text
valence != T_dir
behavioral bias != T_dir
qualia != T_dir
```

A deformation can have strong causal directionality while remaining poorly readable to the bearer. Conversely, a bearer can explicitly report a direction without that report establishing the relevant underlying phenomenal structure.

## 11. Relation to `Psi_f`

This patch does not identify felt pain, discomfort or phenomenal difficulty with canonical `Psi_f`.

The safe relation is:

```text
Psi_f-related burden
may contribute to a consequence structure
whose same-bearer writeback shapes future reselectability
```

but any phenomenal read remains a domain projection requiring an explicit bridge.

Do not write:

```text
Psi_f = pain
Psi_f = phenomenal intensity
high Psi_f automatically creates qualia
```

### Weak bridge — preferred current formulation

```text
Phenomenal similarity should systematically covary with similarity
of bearer-indexed reselective deformation profiles.
```

Status: **P3 bridge candidate**.

This can motivate empirical comparison without claiming reduction.

### Strong constitutive hypothesis

```text
Qualitative character is the first-person mode of a concern-weighted,
history-bearing deformation of a bearer's future reselectability.
```

Status: **P4/H constitutive hypothesis**.

This stronger version directly addresses phenomenal character but must survive `PH-CONSC04`'s deletion / zombie pressure test. It must not be cited as if already established.

## 13. Falsification / discrimination program

The bridge becomes scientifically useful only if it risks failure.

### Test A — similarity covariance

Construct an empirical quality space from pairwise subjective similarity judgments and independently estimate reselective deformation profiles using measures such as:

- subsequent candidate admission / exclusion;
- attentional reprioritization;
- memory rewrite / retrieval bias;
- action affordance changes;
- counterfactual choice changes;
- recovery / reopening dynamics.

Prediction:

```text
closer qualia neighborhoods
-> closer deformation profiles
```

beyond trivial sensory-feature similarity.

### Test B — valence dissociation

Compare events matched for reported valence but differing in long-horizon future-selectability effects.

A strong valence-only account predicts similarity; the reselective bridge predicts potentially different deformation geometry.

### Test C — local compression / global preservation

Acute protective pain should show strong local candidate narrowing while preserving or restoring longer-horizon action capacity. Chronic maladaptive pain / trauma should show persistent unreopened foreclosure.

### Test D — same representation, different bearer consequence

Match systems or conditions for representational content while varying whether consequences close on the same continuing bearer and alter its future-selectability profile.

If phenomenal ownership tracks only explicit self-representation and not bearer-indexed consequence structure, the strong ownership version of this bridge is weakened.

### Test E — deletion pressure

Remove the `phenomenal character = first-person deformation` constitutive statement while preserving all structural reselective dynamics.

Ask:

> What explanatory target is lost beyond re-description?

If no independently specified phenomenal-character constraint disappears, the strong version should remain philosophical compression rather than explanatory closure.

## 14. What this bridge may explain — and what it does not

### Potential explanatory gain

The bridge may help explain:

- why qualitative character is relational rather than atomistic;
- why experiences have bearer-specific ownership;
- why phenomenology is history-sensitive;
- why intensity is not reducible to stimulus magnitude;
- why local negative experience can preserve long-term agency;
- why trauma / addiction can be understood as pathological reselection dynamics;
- why phenomenological similarity may have a structured geometry;
- why `L2` verbal reports incompletely compress lived structure.

### Still open

It does not explain:

- why phenomenal presence exists at all;
- why a fully structurally matched SRT-zombie is impossible;
- why a specific physical / computational realization must instantiate a specific quale;
- whether the constitutive first-person / third-person identity claim is necessary rather than optional;
- whether reselective geometry outperforms neighboring frameworks after matched operationalization.

## 17. One-paragraph abstract

SRT should not infer phenomenality directly from selection, stake, bearing or reselectability. The stronger and safer bridge is narrower: once phenomenal presence is independently granted or supported, qualitative character may partly reflect the first-person organization of a concern-weighted, history-bearing deformation of the same bearer's future reselectability. On this view, experiences are not merely internal labels but events that reorganize what the bearer can attend to, remember, approach, avoid, reopen and select next. Phenomenal similarity may therefore covary with similarity of bearer-indexed reselective deformation profiles, linking SRT's existing quality-space interface to consequence return, future-selectability writeback, `d`-weighted concern and `T_dir`-style reorientation without identifying qualia with any of those variables. The proposal remains P3/P4 and does not solve phenomenal necessity.
