# SRT Philosophy Patch PH-AG01: Agency Ladder and Selection-Control Architecture v0.1

> Status: philosophy of mind / agency bridge note.  
> Canonical caution: this document does not modify SRT primitive axioms. It uses Pacherie's *Agency* as a high-quality bridge to sharpen SRT's agency ladder, pending full-text close reading.

## 0. Source anchor

Primary source:

- Elisabeth Pacherie. (2026). *Agency*. Cambridge Elements in Philosophy of Mind. Cambridge University Press.  
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/agency/8786A777CD1BCD54553F6DAE12ECBB18

Accessible publisher metadata and summary indicate that the Element begins with classical philosophical focus on intentional action, then moves to minimal forms of agency, conscious agency, and shared agency.

---

## 1. Why this matters for SRT

SRT often uses selection, anchoring, action, concern, and subjecthood across many levels. This creates a risk: if every selection is called agency, SRT becomes too broad; if only reflective human intention is called agency, SRT loses the ability to describe biological, pre-reflective, and collective agency.

Pacherie's *Agency* is useful because it foregrounds the need to move from classical intentional action to minimal agency, conscious agency, and shared agency. This supports a graded account rather than a single all-or-nothing definition.

SRT-compatible lesson:

```text
agency is not mere causation and not only reflective intention.
it is a graded selection-control architecture.
```

---

## 2. Main SRT bridge claim

### Claim PH-AG01

SRT should distinguish general selection from agency by adding control, counterfactual sensitivity, action-readiness, concern, and integration into a future-oriented selection field.

### Core formulation

> Agency begins when a system does not merely undergo transitions, but modulates reachable transitions in a way that preserves or advances its own viability, concern structure, or action trajectory.

SRT translation:

```text
causal process < selection process < controlled selection < minimal agency < conscious agency < shared agency
```

---

## 3. Agency ladder

| Level | Description | SRT interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Causal activity | state changes occur | no agency; ordinary dynamics |
| Selection process | one trajectory is realized among alternatives | selection without agency may occur |
| Controlled selection | system modulates transition probabilities through internal organization | proto-agential control |
| Minimal agency | system maintains action-readiness and can regulate behavior toward viable transitions | low-level d/Psi_f-sensitive agency |
| Intentional agency | action is structured by goals, intentions, plans, or practical reasons | L1 action commitment under goal-model constraints |
| Conscious agency | action is accessible as one's own, reportable, or reflectively integrated | thick L1 self-coupled agency |
| Shared agency | multiple agents coordinate intentions, norms, roles, and commitments | intersubjective L2 / collective selection architecture |

This ladder prevents two errors:

```text
Overextension: every physical selection is agency.
Overrestriction: only reflective human intention is agency.
```

## 5. Formal bridge

A minimal SRT-style agency index can be sketched as:

```text
Agency_Level(x,t) = F(Control(x,t), Counterfactual_Sensitivity(x,t), Action_Readiness(x,t), d(x,t), Psi_f(x,t), Self_Coupling(x,t), Social_Coupling(x,t))
```

where:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Control` | capacity to modulate transition probabilities |
| `Counterfactual_Sensitivity` | capacity to behave differently under relevant changes |
| `Action_Readiness` | available transition density toward viable action |
| `d` | degree to which outcomes matter to the system |
| `Psi_f` | cost/friction of anchoring or transitioning |
| `Self_Coupling` | integration into self-model / ownership / report |
| `Social_Coupling` | coordination with other agents, norms, and shared commitments |

This is not a final equation. It is a bridge scaffold for distinguishing agency levels.

## 6. Relation to SRT core terms

### 6.1 Relation to G_hat_theta

`G_hat_theta` is broader than agency. It can describe selection/anchoring processes that do not yet count as agency. Agency appears when `G_hat_theta` is embedded in a system capable of regulated control and future-directed transition modulation.

```text
G_hat_theta alone != agency
G_hat_theta + control + d-bearing regulation -> agency candidate
```

### 6.2 Relation to d-value

`d-value` is crucial for distinguishing agency from mere optimization. An agent is not merely a system that changes state; it is a system for which possible transitions matter relative to viability, self-maintenance, goals, norms, or shared commitments.

```text
salience makes options stand out;
d-value makes options matter;
agency modulates transitions among mattering options.
```

### 6.3 Relation to Psi_f

`Psi_f` helps distinguish capacity from accessible agency. A system may have abstract ability but fail to act when transition friction is too high.

```text
agency failure = control capacity present, but adaptive transition not reachable at current Psi_f
```

This connects directly to attractor capture and action-readiness models.

### 6.4 Relation to L1 and L2

Agency unfolds across L1 and L2:

```text
L1: present action commitment / selected trajectory
L2: stabilized habits, intentions, norms, skills, roles, and shared practices
```

Shared agency especially depends on L2 because joint action requires durable roles, expectations, language, trust, norms, and mutual predictability.

## 7. New SRT claim cluster

### Claim PH-AG01a: Selection is broader than agency

Not all selections are agency. Agency requires a system-level capacity to regulate or modulate transitions in relation to viability, concern, control, or commitment.

```text
selection does not imply agency
```

### Claim PH-AG01b: Minimal agency requires controlled reachability

Minimal agency begins when a system can maintain and modulate reachable transitions, not merely react mechanically.

```text
minimal agency = controlled transition-readiness
```

### Claim PH-AG01c: Conscious agency is thick L1 action ownership

Conscious agency arises when action selection is not only controlled but available as self-related, reportable, or reflectively integrated.

```text
conscious agency = controlled L1 action + self-coupled ownership
```

### Claim PH-AG01d: Shared agency is intersubjective L2 coordination

Shared agency requires more than simultaneous behavior. It depends on L2-like coordination structures: roles, norms, joint commitments, shared attention, and mutual expectation.

```text
shared agency = coordinated selection field across agents
```

## 8. Experimental / operational consequences

SRT can test agency levels by separating:

1. causal responsiveness;
2. action control;
3. counterfactual flexibility;
4. subjective ownership;
5. d-value modulation;
6. Psi_f-sensitive failure;
7. joint-coordination stability.

Possible operational markers:

| Agency level | Candidate measure |
|---|---|
| controlled selection | policy flexibility, reversal, adaptive switching |
| minimal agency | persistence of viable action-readiness under perturbation |
| conscious agency | sense-of-agency report, intentional binding, ownership, confidence |
| agency failure | high initiation latency, attractor capture, transition inaccessibility |
| shared agency | joint task coordination, role stability, mutual prediction, norm repair |

## 9. Boundary cautions

1. This patch is based on publisher-accessible metadata/summary, not a full close reading of the complete Element.
2. Do not claim Pacherie endorses SRT.
3. Do not define agency so broadly that rocks, planets, or ordinary physical constraints become agents.
4. Do not define agency so narrowly that pre-reflective biological action disappears.
5. Do not collapse agency into consciousness: minimal agency and conscious agency must remain distinguishable.
6. Do not collapse shared agency into aggregate individual choices; joint agency requires coordination structure.

## 11. One-paragraph abstract

Pacherie's *Agency* gives SRT a high-value philosophy-of-mind bridge for sharpening the distinction between selection, minimal agency, conscious agency, and shared agency. SRT should not call every selection agency, but it also should not restrict agency to reflective human intention. A graded agency ladder is more stable: causal activity, selection, controlled selection, minimal agency, intentional agency, conscious agency, and shared agency. In SRT terms, agency begins when a system can regulate reachable transitions in relation to viability, concern, control, and future action. Conscious agency adds self-coupled L1 ownership, while shared agency depends on intersubjective L2 structures such as norms, roles, joint commitments, and mutual expectations. This patch is non-canonical and should be strengthened through full-text close reading before any canonical merge.
