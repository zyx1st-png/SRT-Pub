# SRT Philosophy Patch PH-SELF01: Diachronic Self-Binding, Commitment and Self-Alienation v0.1

> **Status:** bounded P3 philosophy/agency bridge and hardening note.  
> **Canonical caution:** this patch does not redefine `L_2`, `d-value`, `Psi_f`, subjecthood, agency, free will, commitment or reselectability. It registers a narrower diachronic interface: how historically sedimented self-constraints can support agency, and when the same structure becomes self-alienating because present consequence-bearing can no longer revise the inherited gate.

---

## 0. Empirical trigger and source boundary

Lower-level empirical trigger:

```text
Kai Lu et al.
Instinct to insight: Neural correlates of ethological strategy learning
bioRxiv (2023), DOI 10.1101/2023.09.11.557240

COSYNE 2025 update:
Instinct vs Insight: Neural Competition Between Prefrontal and Auditory Cortex Constrains Sound Strategy Learning
```

Current SRT source integration:

```text
Neuroscience/patches/SRT_Neuro_NEURAL25_Memory_as_Historical_Selection_Bias_v0_1.md
Neuroscience/hooks/NEURAL25_Memory_Historical_Selection_Bias_Integration_Hook.md
```

The Lu line supports only the lower-level proposition that prior strategy history can bias future control and that representation availability is not identical to behavioral control authority.

It does **not** establish human commitment, reflective self-binding, identity, self-alienation, free will or diachronic autonomy. PH-SELF01 uses Lu only as a minimal empirical trigger for the more general question:

```text
How can a past selection acquire authority over future selection,
and when does that authority cease to be generative?
```

The stronger philosophical distinctions below are SRT bridge proposals constrained by existing repo architecture, not claims attributed to Lu et al.

---

## 1. What is already covered — do not relabel as novelty

PH-SELF01 exists only because one specific diachronic gap remains. The following neighboring claims are already covered elsewhere.

### Existing coverage A — history becomes future ground

Q16 already states that repeated selection and consequence return sediment into the subject's internal terrain:

```text
past active selection
-> retained trace / writeback
-> later passive ground
-> new selection from that ground
```

Q16 also already distinguishes two failures:

```text
too little writeback -> weak continuity
writeback too rigid -> closure / repetition
```

Therefore PH-SELF01 does not newly claim that identity, habit or subject continuity is historically sedimented.

### Existing coverage B — stable closure is not generative health

`Core/SRT_Core_21c_Bridge_Hypotheses.md P2/P3-B13` already separates:

```text
formed process
!= structurally stable ISP
!= generatively healthy ISP
```

The stronger health criterion is consequence-sensitive revisability of comparison rules, boundaries or candidate-generation conditions.

Therefore PH-SELF01 does not redefine generative reselectability.

### Existing coverage C — pathological closure begins at the gate

`Core/SRT_Closure_Compatibility_Hardening.md` already distinguishes:

```text
channel presence
-> gate-rule revisability
-> appeal / pushback availability
```

and identifies the early lesion of pathological closure as gate-rule rigidity / non-revisability rather than simple option-count loss.

It also distinguishes:

```text
burden recorded
!= burden counted as effective input
!= burden able to alter the gate
```

Therefore PH-SELF01 inherits, rather than duplicates, the closure pathology architecture.

### Existing coverage D — meta-selection requires the subject-position to re-enter scope

`Philosophy/SRT_Philosophy_Ethics.md` already notes that a formally second-order choice can still be empty if an existing identity or role simply reproduces itself. The subject-position must itself be able to re-enter the scope of revision, and meta-selection must not buy present stability by locking future reselection capacity.

Therefore PH-SELF01 does not introduce a new theory of meta-selection.

### Existing coverage E — value is not preference

`_SRT_D_VALUE_Value_Non_Substitutability_Addendum.md` and Q14 already connect value with concern-weighted non-substitutability, future continuity and consequence return rather than reward or preference intensity.

Therefore commitment is not treated here as a synonym for high preference strength.

## 2. Stable PH-SELF01 delta — diachronic authority asymmetry

The remaining gap is not whether history constrains the future. It is how the authority of a past self relates to the authority of the present consequence-bearing self.

A diachronic subject contains at least two non-equivalent sources of normative / control weight:

```text
PAST-SELF AUTHORITY:
prior commitment
+ historical writeback
+ prior stake-bearing
+ continuity-preserving constraint

PRESENT-SELF AUTHORITY:
current consequence-bearing
+ newly returned evidence / burden
+ current reachability changes
+ present capacity to revise the inherited rule
```

These two authorities should not be collapsed.

Compact bridge claim:

```text
past autonomy
!=
unlimited authority over the future self
```

and equally:

```text
present preference
!=
unlimited authority to erase prior commitment
```

The PH-SELF01 problem is therefore one of **diachronic authority asymmetry**:

> A past selection may legitimately constrain a later self because otherwise no commitment, planning or historical subject continuity could exist. But a later self bears consequences that the past self could not fully contain in advance. A generative diachronic structure must therefore preserve both historical binding and an effective route by which later consequences can re-enter rule revision.

This is the main increment of PH-SELF01.

---

## 3. Self-binding — local foreclosure can support global future selectability

Self-binding should not be equated with loss of freedom.

Examples include:

- removing access to an addictive option;
- committing to savings or training;
- accepting role-specific duties;
- maintaining a long-horizon project despite short-horizon preference fluctuation;
- making relational commitments that intentionally remove some alternatives from ordinary competition.

A bounded SRT bridge characterization is:

```text
SELF-BINDING CANDIDATE:
chosen local foreclosure
+ historical writeback
+ stake / concern coupling
+ preservation of effective consequence return
```

The point is not that every self-binding improves the subject. The structural claim is narrower:

```text
local option reduction
can coexist with
greater global / later reselectability
```

For example, repeatedly reopening every local commitment can destroy the higher-order reachability created by skill, trust, joint planning or long-term investment.

Therefore:

```text
reselectability != maximum option count at every moment
```

Self-binding can be generative when local foreclosure functions as a scaffold for wider future agency.

---

## 4. Commitment — high revision threshold, not infinite revision threshold

PH-SELF01 does not offer a canonical definition of commitment. It proposes a bridge characterization sufficient to distinguish commitment from preference, one-shot choice and pathological closure.

```text
COMMITMENT CANDIDATE:
chosen local foreclosure
+ durable historical writeback
+ stake-coupled non-substitutability
+ conditional revisability under sufficiently strong consequence return
```

This yields the crucial distinction:

```text
commitment
!=
irrevocability
```

A mature commitment can have a high revision threshold:

```text
theta_revision >> 0
```

without requiring:

```text
theta_revision = infinity
```

The high threshold protects long-horizon structure from being dissolved by every transient preference shift. Conditional revisability protects the subject from becoming permanently subordinated to a past rule whose consequence structure has materially changed.

This bridge should not be read as implying that every commitment must remain easy to exit, or that every present discomfort justifies revision. The relevant distinction is whether consequence return retains **effective standing** to trigger review when the commitment's generative role itself has changed.

---

## 5. Self-alienation — inherited rule control plus blocked effective consequence return

The central PH-SELF01 pathological transition is:

```text
historical self-constraint
+ current consequence-bearing
+ blocked effective consequence return to the inherited rule
-> self-alienation candidate
```

Compact form:

```text
SELF-BINDING
= constraint + revisability

SELF-ALIENATION
= constraint + effective non-revisability
```

These are bridge mnemonics, not equations.

### 5.1 Why this is stronger than simple persistence

Persistence alone cannot distinguish:

- principled commitment;
- habit;
- sunk-cost behavior;
- coercion;
- addiction;
- identity-protective rigidity;
- mature long-horizon self-binding.

The discriminating question is:

> Can consequences currently borne by the same continuing subject alter the gate that keeps the inherited constraint in force?

If yes, historical constraint may still be functioning as self-binding.

If no — especially when the subject's burden is repeatedly registered yet prevented from changing the gate — the structure becomes a candidate for self-alienation.

### 5.2 Identity as scaffold, not sovereign

An identity rule such as:

```text
"I am the kind of person who never admits error"
```

may begin as a locally adaptive scaffold. It becomes self-alienating when current evidence can only be processed as a threat to identity and cannot become evidence for revising the identity rule itself.

Then the direction of service reverses:

```text
early:
identity scaffold -> supports bearer continuity

later pathological candidate:
bearer -> must increasingly serve identity scaffold
```

This is a candidate instance of operator alienation: a historically produced selection scaffold comes to monopolize the terms under which it may itself be questioned.

Blocked identity equation:

```text
subject = identity
```

The subject is the continuing consequence-bearing, history-bearing process. Identity is one sedimented scaffold within that process.

## 6. Diachronic autonomy — history must bind, consequences must still count

PH-SELF01 proposes the following P3 bridge characterization:

```text
DIACHRONIC AUTONOMY CANDIDATE
= historical binding
+ effective consequence return
+ rule revisability
```

This should be read as a structural decomposition, not a canonical equation.

It blocks two symmetrical reductions:

```text
PRESENTISM:
current preference erases all prior commitment

HISTORICAL SOVEREIGNTY:
past commitment permanently overrides all later consequence-bearing
```

A stronger formulation is:

> The past self has historical authority because without durable writeback there is no commitment or diachronic subject continuity. The present self has consequence authority because it is the position currently bearing outcomes that could not be fully specified by the earlier self. Diachronic autonomy requires a governance relation between these authorities rather than the absolute sovereignty of either one.

---

## 7. Self-faithfulness is not identity consistency

A further consequence is:

```text
self-faithfulness
!=
identity consistency
```

If the subject is historically reconstituted through consequence return, then revising an identity need not be self-betrayal.

A safer SRT bridge is:

> Self-faithfulness is fidelity to the continuing consequence-bearing process, not unconditional fidelity to one frozen historical self-description.

This allows two different cases to be distinguished:

```text
A. transient preference shift
-> commitment remains generative
-> persistence may be self-faithful

B. durable consequence reversal
-> commitment now compresses broader future selectability
-> refusal to review may become identity-faithful but subject-alienating
```

The patch therefore rejects both:

```text
change = authenticity
```

and:

```text
consistency = authenticity
```

Authenticity / self-faithfulness remains a higher philosophical interpretation and should not be used as a primitive SRT criterion.

## 8. Exit / revision / recomposition — inherited pathology ladder

PH-SELF01 imports the existing three-level reselection architecture from closure hardening:

```text
EXIT:
Can the later self leave the inherited closure?

REVISION:
Can the later self modify the operative parameters, boundaries or gate rules?

RECOMPOSITION:
Can the later self reopen the higher-order frame that determined which identity, value hierarchy or rule counted as authoritative in the first place?
```

Applied diachronically, this gives three depths of self-alienation candidate:

```text
LEVEL 1 — action lock:
"I cannot do otherwise."

LEVEL 2 — rule lock:
"This commitment / rule itself cannot be revised."

LEVEL 3 — generator lock:
"Even the framework that says what counts as a legitimate commitment, identity or reason cannot be reopened."
```

The third level is the strongest candidate for selection-operator alienation.

Do not identify every difficult-to-exit commitment with Level 3. The classification depends on actual gate structure and effective consequence-return pathways.

## 9. Relation to `d-value`, stake and non-substitutability

High stake can rationally raise the revision threshold because deeply anchored structures may organize broad future continuity.

Therefore:

```text
high d-value / non-substitutability
!=
pathology
```

and:

```text
high switching cost
!=
self-alienation
```

The pathology candidate arises only when preserving the historically weighted structure requires suppressing the effective return of consequences that would otherwise bear on its own revision.

This keeps four distinctions separate:

```text
preference strength
!= stake
!= commitment durability
!= gate non-revisability
```

A commitment may be costly, deeply non-substitutable and still generative if consequences retain standing to revise its form, boundaries or continuation conditions.

## 10. Relation to Lu / NEURAL25 — mechanism floor only

The Lu strategy-learning paradigm supplies a bounded lower-level template:

```text
prior successful strategy history
-> stronger future control bias / switching cost
-> alternative strategy may be represented before it controls behavior
```

NEURAL25 correctly hardens this into:

```text
content representation
!= candidate accessibility
!= control authority
!= historical path restructuring
```

PH-SELF01 must not add the following identities:

```text
mPFC strategy persistence = commitment
win-stay = self-binding
strategy switching = free choice
mPFC inhibition = liberation
neural competition = self-alienation
```

The philosophical bridge begins only after additional structures are present:

```text
history-bearing bearer
+ stake / concern
+ durable self-constraint
+ consequence return
+ revisability of the inherited gate
```

Thus Lu is an empirical trigger for the question of historical control, not evidence for the full diachronic-self thesis.

## 11. Differential tests / adjudication prompts

PH-SELF01 should remain testable at the bridge level rather than becoming a purely interpretive vocabulary.

### P4-A — high revision threshold versus eliminated revision channel

Compare two long-horizon commitments matched on:

- current preference conflict;
- duration;
- effort / sunk cost;
- identity centrality;
- material switching cost.

Distinguish:

```text
MODEL H — high-threshold commitment:
consequence evidence can trigger review once sufficiently strong / persistent

MODEL G — gate-rigid commitment:
consequence evidence is repeatedly registered but structurally prevented from changing the governing rule
```

Candidate discriminators:

- whether accumulated consequence evidence changes review probability;
- whether alternative interpretations become admissible;
- whether the subject can alter boundary conditions rather than only endure them;
- whether burdens are re-attributed as personal failure so the rule remains insulated;
- whether perturbation produces revision versus defensive restoration of the same identity rule.

### P4-B — same current state, different commitment history

Borrow the matched-current-state logic from NEURAL25.

Match groups or longitudinal states on:

- current expressed preference;
- current reward / cost;
- current behavior;
- current identity endorsement;
- current perceived alternatives.

Vary prior depth / mode of self-binding history.

Ask whether history predicts residual differences in:

- future revision threshold;
- alternative-path accessibility;
- gate-rule contestability;
- recovery after commitment violation;
- hysteresis after attempted reversal.

A history effect alone is not SRT-specific. The stronger PH-SELF01 signature requires a relation between historical binding and **effective consequence-return revisability**.

### P4-C — attribution-layer cut

Where burden is high, test whether subjects can distinguish:

```text
"I am failing the rule"
```

from:

```text
"the rule itself may require revision"
```

If identity-protective reinterpretation systematically blocks the second transition even under sustained consequence evidence, this is a candidate marker of gate insulation.

## 12. Failure / downgrade conditions

PH-SELF01 should be weakened or absorbed into neighboring theories if any of the following holds:

1. Commitment persistence is fully explained by ordinary habit strength, sunk cost, reward history or current preference, with no incremental role for consequence-return or gate revisability.
2. `effective consequence return` cannot be operationally distinguished from simple discomfort, preference change or post-hoc rationalization.
3. High-threshold but genuinely revisable commitments cannot be empirically distinguished from gate-rigid commitments.
4. History-depth effects disappear after matching current behavior, preference, identity strength, switching cost and available alternatives.
5. The proposed self-alienation architecture adds no predictive or diagnostic value beyond established identity-protective cognition, cognitive dissonance, addiction or commitment-device models.

If these failures occur, retain the phenomenological vocabulary only as interpretation and do not present PH-SELF01 as a distinct SRT bridge.

## 14. Compact synthesis

The full bounded chain is:

```text
selection
-> historical writeback
-> sedimented scaffold
-> durable self-constraint
-> commitment candidate
-> two possible diachronic trajectories

A. historical binding
   + effective consequence return
   + rule revisability
   -> generative self-binding candidate

B. historical binding
   + present consequence-bearing
   + blocked effective return to the inherited gate
   -> self-alienation candidate
```

The load-bearing question is therefore:

> **Can consequences still revise the rule that history has made authoritative?**

PH-SELF01's main theoretical increment is not that the past shapes the future. SRT already contains that claim. The increment is that diachronic agency requires a non-absolute relation between two forms of authority:

```text
historical authority of prior commitment
+
consequence authority of the presently bearing self
```

A subject with no historical binding cannot sustain commitment or continuity. A subject whose historical bindings cannot be revised by later consequence return risks turning its own sedimented history into an internal externality: a rule generated by the self but no longer answerable to the self that now bears its consequences.
