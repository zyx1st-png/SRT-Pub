## 2. Target A — Phenomenal Structure Interface

Preferred neighborhood:

```text
stake-gated phenomenal structure / concern-weighted salience / future selectability
```

Suggested bounded paragraph:

Historical learning can change the organization of later perception before explicit report or final action selection. Nagase's McDowellian account makes the intermediate structure more explicit: training can alter how features are grouped and which features become salient, thereby changing later discrimination and attention. In SRT this can be treated as one candidate route from historical constraint to later `L_1` organization, but neither perceptual salience nor task relevance should be identified with canonical `d-value`, and conceptual capacity should not be identified with `L_2`.

Do not insert until a synthesis pass can compare this route with current neuroscience salience / memory / objectification bridges.

## 3. Target B — Social Cognition

Preferred neighborhood:

```text
Ax-Cog-2b Invitation Gating
SOC-COG03 Norm / Script / Affordance / Constraint Reselection
```

Suggested bounded paragraph:

Invitation gating need not be produced only by explicit norms or active social scripts. Perceptual learning provides a complementary route: repeated training and learned conceptual relations may change grouping and salience so that some action opportunities become easier to notice and others recede before deliberation. This should be read as a perceptual-learning companion to SOC-COG03, not as a second affordance architecture.

Compact crosswalk:

```text
SOC-COG03:
role + script + history
-> social invitation gating

PH-PER01:
learning + conceptual/perceptual training
-> grouping / salience reorganization
-> candidate accessibility
```

## 4. Target C — Agency / Subjecthood

Preferred neighborhood:

```text
S5 reflective self-integration / policy revision
S6 norm access / answerability / repair
responsibility-position trace
```

Suggested bounded paragraph:

Language can thicken agency without defining subjecthood. By stabilizing and de-contextualizing patterns, linguistic articulation can make a previously transparent constraint available for joint attention, criticism and possible revision. In interpersonal contexts, conversation can also let a bearer participate in interpreting, correcting and identifying with the footprint of an action. These capacities can support reselectability and responsibility-position formation, but language is neither necessary for S4 subjecthood nor sufficient for S5 agency or S6 responsibility.

## 5. Two-axis hardening to preserve

Any future synthesis should keep these axes separate:

| Axis | Diagnostic |
|---|---|
| systematic integration | Can capacities / cues / policies coordinate and improve one another? |
| reselectability | Can the constraints shaping the live field be inspected, inhibited, contested, revised or reopened? |

Required non-identity:

```text
systematic integration != reselectability
```

A highly coherent and fluent system may still be rigid at the level of constraint reopening.

## 8. Do not include

- Do not say Nagase or McDowell proves SRT.
- Do not import McDowellian conceptualism as a canonical SRT theory of perception.
- Do not convert conceptual capacity into `L_2` or perceptual salience into `d-value`.
- Do not infer full agency or responsibility from language use.
- Do not deny non-linguistic subjecthood or agency merely because conversation is limited.
- Do not turn systematic integration into a scalar measure of freedom.
- Do not treat the radiologist example as settled neural evidence for early cognitive penetration.
- Do not open a new second-order-selection or meta-selectability mechanism parallel to the existing reselectability / H3-I5-J5 architecture.
