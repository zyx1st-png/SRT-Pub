## Suggested compressed paragraph

Discriminatory cognition can be treated as a narrowed cross-subject update gate. When a social agent's tolerance for out-group surprise is low, and group priors carry high precision, counter-evidence is more likely to be processed as threat, noise, shame, or identity attack than as information for belief revision. In SRT terms, ZBS is only an active-inference bridge proxy: it helps describe update bandwidth and `L_2` rigidity, but it does not define `d`, `\Psi_f`, or `T_dir`.

## Suggested mapping

| ZBS / active-inference phrase | SRT compression |
|---|---|
| narrow ZBS | low cross-group update bandwidth |
| high prior precision | rigid group `L_2` |
| discriminatory mind | biased evidence weighting plus in-group self-evidencing |
| dehumanising mind | effective `d`-exclusion / recognition break |
| bystander silence | `S_sig` suppression and `S_str` externalization |

## Do not include

- Do not turn active inference into a canonical SRT proof.
- Do not imply that all in-group preference is dehumanization.
