## 3. Suggested paragraph

Social constraints can act before final action selection by shaping the effective invitation field itself. Role-indexed, historically learned scripts may make some interpersonal actions salient, normal or soliciting while rendering others practically unavailable. These scripts need not be localized in one explicit internal rule: their effective constraint can be distributed across embodied skill, language, spatial organization, public artifacts and other agents. Such automation is not intrinsically pathological; the relevant distinction is whether sedimented scripts release resources for higher-level live choice or replace the capacity to reopen and revise the constraints that generate the choice field.

## 4. Suggested second paragraph

Script disruption provides a bounded route from transparent background constraint to explicit contestability. The social-domain revision pathway is `Visibility -> Contestability -> Alternative generation -> Enactability -> Historical writeback`; this pathway is not a second gate parallel to H3/I5. It only describes how a tacit constraint may become revisable and historically effective. To call a resulting change a second-order-selection candidate, the stricter I5 conditions still apply: consequence return to the same historical bearing position, comparison-scale or generation-rule rewrite, changed future selectable space, and persistent later efficacy. J5 then classifies reopening depth across R1 weight, R2 category/boundary, R3 candidate-generation and R4 concern levels.
