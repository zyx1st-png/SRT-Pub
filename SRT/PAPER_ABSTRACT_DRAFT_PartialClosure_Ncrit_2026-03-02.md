# 摘要草案 v1

## Title
From Partial Closure to Stable Closure: Minimal Embodiment Threshold in SRT

## Abstract
A persistent gap in origin-of-life theorizing is the transition from intermittent replication to autonomous self-maintaining closure. We formalize this gap in SRT by introducing **Partial Closure** (\(\mathcal{C}_{partial}\)) and a **Minimal Embodiment Threshold** (\(N_{crit}\)). Partial Closure denotes systems that can execute local replication mappings \(\hat{G}_\theta[L_0]\to L_1\) only with environmental compensation, while failing to sustain seamless self-input chaining. Stable closure emerges only when embodied constraint information satisfies \(I(\theta)\ge N_{crit}(env,\Psi_f)\) under payable friction conditions.

This framework reframes early replicator observations (e.g., near-self-replicating short RNA systems) as pre-closure critical states rather than full autonomous life. We derive phase-gate and fallback-gate criteria, separating (i) structural autonomy transitions from (ii) phenomenological concern emergence. Crucially, we show that crossing \(N_{crit}\) is **necessary for structural self-maintenance** but **insufficient for \(d>0\)** in SRT’s agency sense.

The model yields testable predictions: reduced environmental compensation should sharply increase replication-chain failure in \(\mathcal{C}_{partial}\) systems; systems near threshold should exhibit bifurcation-like sensitivity to small constraint-information gains. We propose a falsification protocol based on replication continuity, compensation dependence, and friction-payability signatures. The result is a unified, non-teleological bridge from chemical pre-closure dynamics to stable autonomous closure, with explicit anti-panpsychist boundaries.
