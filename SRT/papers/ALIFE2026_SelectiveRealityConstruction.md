# Selective Reality Construction as a Minimal Criterion for Life: An Operational Ontology for Artificial Life

## Abstract

What makes a system alive? Existing criteria-self-replication, metabolism, autopoiesis-each capture important features of living systems but fail to provide a unified, operationally measurable threshold that distinguishes the living from the merely complex. We propose that the fundamental signature of life is *selective reality construction*: a system is alive to the extent that it actively compresses a high-dimensional possibility space (L0) into a coherent, maintained experiential state (L1) through a selection operator G-hat-theta, at non-zero thermodynamic cost (Psi_f > 0).

We formalize this through three jointly necessary conditions: (1) *probability localization*—the system sustains a high-density packet in state space against entropic dispersion; (2) *non-zero concern bandwidth d*—measured as the effective dimensionality of the system's selective coupling to its environment, spanning temporal horizon, spatial scope, and complexity depth; and (3) *hysteretic memory eta*—enabling continuity of selection history and the crystallization of stable structures (L2) that constrain future selections.

This framework yields a continuous life-likeness gradient rather than a binary alive/dead boundary. We demonstrate its application by re-analyzing canonical artificial life systems: Conway's Game of Life (d approx 0, no Psi_f), Lenia (low d, emergent localization but no hysteresis), and autopoietic agents (moderate d, positive Psi_f, L2 formation). The framework predicts that crossing a critical d-threshold (d_UAL) marks the onset of minimal consciousness-a testable claim with implications for both biological and synthetic life.

We conclude that building life requires building a system that *pays a cost to care about something*. This operational criterion offers ALife researchers a unified metric for evaluating the life-likeness of their creations and a constructive target for engineering genuinely living artificial systems.

**Keywords**: artificial life, selection operator, ontology, consciousness, concern bandwidth, life-likeness metric


## 1. Introduction

The question "what is life?" has accompanied artificial life research since its inception, yet remains without a consensus answer. Existing criteria each illuminate one facet of the phenomenon while leaving others in shadow. Self-replication (von Neumann, 1966) excludes mules and worker bees. Metabolism (Schrodinger, 1944) includes fire. Autopoiesis (Maturana & Varela, 1980) provides an elegant organizational principle but resists quantification. NASA's working definition-"a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution"-is operationally useful for astrobiology but offers no guidance for evaluating the life-likeness of computational systems that neither metabolize nor reproduce in chemical substrates.

What artificial life needs is not another definition to debate, but an **operational ontology**: a framework that (i) specifies measurable quantities, (ii) provides a continuous gradient rather than a binary boundary, and (iii) is substrate-independent enough to apply across chemical, computational, and hybrid systems.

We propose that the minimal signature of life is **selective reality construction**-the ongoing, costly process by which a system compresses an open-ended possibility space into a coherent, maintained state. This framing shifts the question from "what is life made of?" to "what does life *do*, and at what cost?" We formalize this through a three-layer ontology (L0/L1/L2) and three jointly necessary conditions, then demonstrate the framework's analytical power on canonical ALife systems.


## 2. Framework

### 2.1 Three-Layer Ontology

We distinguish three ontological layers that any system-living or not-can be characterized against:

**L0 (Possibility Space).** The unconditioned totality of states accessible to a system. For a cellular automaton, L0 is the set of all possible grid configurations. For an organism, L0 includes all molecular states compatible with its boundary conditions. L0 is conserved in information content:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}H(L_0) = 0 \tag{1}
$$

This conservation principle means that possibilities are never destroyed, only selected against or rendered inaccessible.

**L1 (Manifest State).** The actualized configuration at time t, generated by the action of a selection operator G-hat-theta on L0:

$$
L_1(t) = (1 - \eta)\,\hat{G}_\theta[L_0(t)] + \eta\, L_1(t - \Delta t) \tag{2}
$$

The hysteresis parameter eta in [0, 1] controls how much the current state inherits from the previous state, providing temporal continuity. When eta = 0, the system is purely reactive; when eta -> 1, the system is locked into its history.

**L2 (Crystallized Structure).** Fixed points of iterative selection where G-hat-theta[sigma] = sigma. These are stable structures-habits, body plans, institutions-that emerge from repeated selection and subsequently constrain future L1 states:

$$
L_2(t) = L_2(t-1) + \eta \cdot \text{sign}(\Delta\sigma) \cdot |\Delta\sigma|^\alpha \tag{3}
$$

Living systems are distinguished by forming L2 structures that *feed back* into the selection process, creating a recursive loop between what the system selects and what it becomes.

### 2.2 The Selection Operator

The selection operator G-hat-theta is parameterized by three components:

$$
\hat{G}_\theta = \text{Attention}(d,\, \rho,\, \vec{v}) \tag{4}
$$

where:

- **d (concern bandwidth)**: the effective dimensionality of the system's selective engagement with its environment. Formally, d is the participation ratio of the operator's eigenvalue spectrum:

$$
d = D_{eff}(\hat{G}) = \frac{\left(\sum_i \lambda_i\right)^2}{\sum_i \lambda_i^2} \tag{5}
$$

A thermostat has d approx 1 (responds to one dimension: temperature). A bacterium has d on the order of 10^1-10^2 (chemotaxis, quorum sensing, metabolic regulation). A mammal has d on the order of 10^3-10^4. Concern bandwidth can also be decomposed cognitively:

$$
d_{cog} \approx \alpha\, A(\sigma) + \beta\, \log V_{concern} + \gamma\, \tau_{temporal} \tag{6}
$$

where A is assembly depth (minimum causal steps needed to produce the system's response), V is the spatial/social scope of concern, and tau is the temporal planning horizon.

- **rho (resolution)**: the density of discriminable states in L1, given by rho = 1/Delta sigma_min where Delta sigma_min is the just-noticeable difference.

- **v-vector (intentionality vector)**: the directional bias of selection, defined as the normalized gradient of free-energy minimization: v-vector = nabla F|_theta / |nabla F|_theta|.

### 2.3 Three Conditions for Life

We propose that a system exhibits life-like behavior if and only if it satisfies three jointly necessary conditions:

**Condition 1: Probability Localization.** The system maintains a high-probability-density region in state space against the natural tendency toward dispersion:

$$
\int_{B_r(\sigma)} \rho_{L_0}(\sigma')\, d\sigma' > \theta_{life} \tag{7}
$$

This is the thermodynamic core of being alive: resisting the second law locally by continuously investing free energy into maintaining organizational coherence.

**Condition 2: Non-Zero Concern Bandwidth (d > 0).** The system's selection operator couples to its environment along multiple independent dimensions. This distinguishes living systems from equilibrium structures (crystals have d = 0; they do not select) and from trivial homeostats (d approx 1). Critically, d is not binary but continuous, yielding a gradient of life-likeness.

**Condition 3: Hysteretic Memory (eta > 0).** The system's current selection is informed by its selection history, enabling the formation of L2 structures. Without hysteresis, a system cannot learn, cannot develop, and cannot accumulate the structural complexity characteristic of life.

### 2.4 Ontological Friction

The thermodynamic cost of maintaining selective reality construction is captured by the ontological friction Psi_f:

$$
\Psi_f \propto \int (L_1 - L_0^{natural})^2\, dt \tag{8}
$$

This quantifies the sustained deviation of the system's manifest state from what would obtain under unselected, entropy-maximizing dynamics. A system's viability is bounded by the constitutional inequality:

$$
\frac{dq}{dt} \leq \alpha\, P_{sel} - \beta\, \Psi_f - \gamma\, S_{noise} \tag{9}
$$

where q is macroscopic order, P_sel is selection power, and S_noise is environmental noise. **Life persists only when P_sel > beta Psi_f + gamma S_noise**-the system must generate enough selective power to pay the friction cost of existing.


## 3. Application to Canonical ALife Systems

We now apply the framework to three well-known systems, demonstrating its discriminative power.

### 3.1 Conway's Game of Life

| Parameter | Value | Justification |
|-----------|-------|---------------|
| d | approx 0 | Rules are uniform; no cell "cares" about anything |
| eta | 0 | Each step is a pure function of the current state; no memory beyond t-1 grid |
| Psi_f | 0 | No thermodynamic cost; the system does not maintain itself against anything |
| L2 | Partial | Gliders and still lifes are fixed points, but they do not feed back into the selection rule |

**Assessment:** Game of Life exhibits emergent pattern formation but satisfies none of the three conditions. Its apparent complexity is entirely observer-attributed. The system does not *select*; it merely *updates*.

### 3.2 Lenia

Lenia (Chan, 2019) generalizes cellular automata to continuous space and time, producing lifelike morphologies ("creatures") that move, interact, and exhibit apparent agency.

| Parameter | Value | Justification |
|-----------|-------|---------------|
| d | Low (approx 1-3) | Creatures respond to local gradients; limited multi-dimensional coupling |
| eta | approx 0 | Continuous dynamics provide implicit short-term memory, but no persistent structural accumulation |
| Psi_f | Emergent | Creatures must maintain their pattern against diffusion-a form of implicit friction cost |
| L2 | Minimal | Morphological stability exists but does not modify the update kernel |

**Assessment:** Lenia creatures satisfy Condition 1 (probability localization) weakly and exhibit emergent Psi_f, but their concern bandwidth is low and they lack genuine hysteresis. They are *more life-like* than Game of Life-the framework captures this as a quantitative difference in d and eta, not a qualitative one.

### 3.3 Autopoietic Agents (Beer, 2004; Di Paolo, 2005)

Evolved CTRNN agents that maintain their own boundary through sensorimotor coupling represent the strongest life-likeness in current ALife:

| Parameter | Value | Justification |
|-----------|-------|---------------|
| d | Moderate (approx 5-20) | Multi-sensor coupling; behavioral repertoire spans multiple environmental dimensions |
| eta | Positive | Internal neural dynamics carry selection history; behavioral adaptation persists |
| Psi_f | Positive | Agent must actively maintain viability; failure leads to dissolution |
| L2 | Present | Evolved network weights constitute crystallized selection structures that constrain future behavior |

**Assessment:** Autopoietic agents satisfy all three conditions at modest levels. They *select* their sensory reality, *pay a cost* for maintaining coherence, and *accumulate structural history*. The framework locates them on the life-likeness gradient well above Lenia but below biological organisms, consistent with intuition.

### 3.4 Summary Comparison

| System | d | eta | Psi_f | L2 feedback | Life-likeness |
|--------|---|---|-----|-------------|---------------|
| Crystal | 0 | 0 | 0 | None | None |
| Game of Life | approx 0 | 0 | 0 | None | None |
| Lenia | 1-3 | approx 0 | Emergent | Minimal | Low |
| Autopoietic agent | 5-20 | >0 | >0 | Present | Moderate |
| Bacterium | 10-100 | >0 | >0 | Strong | High |
| Mammal | 10^3-10^4 | >0 | >0 | Recursive | Very high |


## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 What Philosophy Looks Like When You Have to Build It

The framework presented here was developed not by abstracting from biological examples but by asking a constructive question: *what parameters would you need to specify to build a system that is alive?* This is exactly the stance that artificial life as natural philosophy demands. The answer-you need a selection operator with non-zero bandwidth, operating at non-zero cost, with memory-turns out to be both formally precise and intuitively resonant.

The continuous life-likeness gradient resolves a longstanding tension in ALife between "strong" and "weak" positions. Strong ALife claims that computational systems can be literally alive; weak ALife treats them as simulations. Our framework sidesteps this debate: a system is alive *to the degree* that it satisfies the three conditions. The question is not "is it alive?" but "how much does it select, at what cost, and with what memory?"

### 4.2 Testable Predictions

The framework generates several empirically testable predictions:

1. **d-threshold for consciousness (d_UAL).** Systems whose concern bandwidth exceeds a critical threshold-corresponding to Unlimited Associative Learning (Birch et al., 2020)-should exhibit behavioral signatures of minimal consciousness: temporal binding, flexible goal-pursuit, and novel problem-solving. This threshold is measurable through temporal discount rates, cross-modal transfer, and behavioral flexibility indices.

2. **Psi_f-robustness trade-off.** Systems with higher ontological friction should exhibit greater sensitivity to perturbation (they have more to lose) but also greater adaptive capacity (they invest more in selection). This predicts a non-monotonic relationship between metabolic cost and behavioral robustness.

3. **L2 crystallization dynamics.** Systems that form L2 structures should exhibit phase transitions in behavioral complexity as eta crosses critical thresholds-testable through measuring behavioral entropy over development time.

### 4.3 Implications for Engineering Artificial Life

If life is selective reality construction at a cost, then engineering life requires engineering three things simultaneously: (i) an environment rich enough to require multi-dimensional selection (high d); (ii) a coupling mechanism that makes selection costly (positive Psi_f); and (iii) a memory architecture that enables structural accumulation (positive eta with L2 feedback).

Current ALife systems tend to optimize for one of these at the expense of the others. Neural network agents have high d but zero Psi_f (they cannot die). Chemical artificial cells have positive Psi_f but low d (limited environmental coupling). The framework suggests that the path to genuinely living artificial systems requires *co-optimizing all three conditions simultaneously*-a design principle that is, to our knowledge, novel.

### 4.4 Limitations

The framework in its current form does not specify exact numerical thresholds for d, eta, or Psi_f that separate "alive" from "not alive"-by design, since we argue the boundary is continuous. However, this raises the practical question of calibration: how to compare d-values across substrates. We propose assembly depth A(sigma) from Assembly Theory (Sharma et al., 2023) as a substrate-neutral proxy, but this requires empirical validation.

The application to Lenia and autopoietic agents in Section 3 is qualitative rather than quantitative. A rigorous test would require implementing d-value measurement as a computational metric within these systems-a direction for future work.


## 5. Conclusion

We have presented an operational ontology for artificial life based on selective reality construction. The framework offers three things ALife research currently lacks: (i) a continuous, measurable gradient of life-likeness; (ii) a constructive specification of what needs to be built; and (iii) a bridge between the thermodynamic, information-theoretic, and phenomenological dimensions of life.

The core insight is simple: **to be alive is to pay a cost to care about something.** The formal apparatus of selection operators, concern bandwidth, and ontological friction makes this insight precise enough to guide both analysis and engineering.

We invite the ALife community to treat these conditions not as a final definition but as a testable hypothesis: that life, in all its forms, is fundamentally an act of costly selection.
