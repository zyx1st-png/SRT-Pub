# SRT Meta-OS Paper: JCS Reframe Memo

## 0 Recommended Target

**Primary target:** `Journal of Consciousness Studies`

**Why this journal fits better than Neuroscience of Consciousness**

- JCS is explicitly multidisciplinary and regularly publishes conceptual, philosophical, and meta-theoretical work on consciousness.
- The current paper is primarily a theoretical integration paper, not an empirical or methods paper.
- `Neuroscience of Consciousness` accepts theoretical work, but its scope is centered on the biological basis of consciousness and it explicitly notes that it will not normally publish papers proposing alternative physical foundations for consciousness. The present manuscript still reads that way.

**Working verdict**

- Do **not** submit the current version as-is.
- Reframe the paper as a **candidate translation framework** rather than a demonstrated new ontology or new physical foundation.

## 1 The Core Repositioning

### Current framing

The current manuscript presents SRT as:

- a meta-ontological operating system,
- a triadic ontology,
- a rigorous demonstration of a substrate-independent metric,
- a framework that yields necessary and sufficient conditions for consciousness.

### JCS-friendly framing

For JCS, the same paper should instead present SRT as:

- a **candidate meta-theoretical translation framework**,
- a structured way of comparing why major consciousness theories succeed on different explanatory dimensions,
- a proposal for a **provisional construct** (`d`) that may help organize future empirical work,
- a bridge paper that clarifies disagreements without claiming final resolution.

### One-sentence positioning

This paper argues that the proliferation of consciousness theories may reflect partially different explanatory targets, and proposes SRT as a candidate translation framework for relating those targets within a shared comparative vocabulary.

## 3 JCS Submission Summary

JCS requests a short summary at the beginning of the paper.

### 150-word summary draft

Consciousness science has produced an unusual situation: strong local theories, weak global convergence. This paper uses Robert Lawrence Kuhn's recent taxonomy of consciousness theories as a starting point for asking whether the field's fragmentation reflects simple disagreement or partly different explanatory targets. I propose that Selective Reality Theory (SRT) is best read not as a further first-order theory of consciousness, but as a candidate translation framework for comparing those targets within a shared vocabulary. On this reading, SRT distinguishes latent conditions, manifest experiential organization, and convergent public structure, and uses this triadic distinction to reinterpret major approaches such as IIT, global workspace theory, predictive processing, panpsychism, and idealism. I then introduce a provisional construct, existential stake (`d`), and argue that it may help generate empirically tractable hypotheses when combined with existing measures such as perturbational complexity. The paper closes by specifying limits, objections, and falsification conditions for the framework.

## 4 JCS Abstract Draft

### Abstract draft

Consciousness science continues to generate influential theories without approaching stable theoretical convergence. Using Robert Lawrence Kuhn's recent landscape of consciousness as a starting point, this paper argues that at least part of this proliferation reflects a structural problem: major theories often target different explanatory dimensions of consciousness rather than offering directly commensurable accounts of a single level of description. I propose that Selective Reality Theory (SRT) is most useful when read not as a further first-order theory of consciousness, but as a candidate translation framework for relating these dimensions within a shared comparative vocabulary. On this interpretation, SRT distinguishes latent conditions, manifest experiential organization, and convergent public or normative structure, and uses this triadic contrast to reinterpret recurrent tensions among IIT, global workspace theory, predictive processing/free energy approaches, panpsychism, idealism, and challenge theories. I then introduce the notion of existential stake (`d`) as a provisional construct intended to capture the depth and scope of concern-sensitive selection, and sketch several empirical hypotheses that could help test whether it adds explanatory value beyond existing structural measures. I conclude by identifying the framework's current limits and the conditions under which it should be revised or rejected.

### Abstract notes

- Keep under `250` words.
- Avoid citations in the abstract.
- Avoid `rigorous demonstration`, `necessary and sufficient`, `universal physical quantity`, and `proof`.

## 7 Tone Shifts Needed

### Replace high-commitment language

Use these replacements systematically.

- `rigorous demonstration` -> `conceptual argument`
- `proves` -> `suggests`
- `establishes necessary and sufficient conditions` -> `proposes a joint condition`
- `universal physical quantity` -> `candidate cross-domain descriptor`
- `beyond analogy` -> `stronger than a purely analogical reading`
- `dissolves contradictions` -> `helps reinterpret some apparent contradictions`
- `is` -> `can be read as`

### Example rewrites

**Current style**

SRT provides the meta-ontological operating system required to reveal the deep complementarities among these theories.

**Recommended style**

SRT can be read as a candidate translation framework for articulating complementarities among theories that otherwise appear to conflict at the level of first-order description.

**Current style**

This section rigorously demonstrates the substrate independence of the `d`-value.

**Recommended style**

This section develops a conceptual argument for treating `d` as a substrate-flexible construct whose usefulness depends on whether it supports clearer comparison and future empirical discrimination.

**Current style**

Consciousness emerges only when these conditions are jointly satisfied.

**Recommended style**

The framework proposes that conscious experience may require the joint co-occurrence of these conditions, a claim that remains open to empirical failure.

## 10 Recommended Opening Paragraph

Consciousness science is now rich in theories but poor in convergence. This is usually treated as a sign either of premature theory formation or of deep unresolved disagreement about the nature of consciousness itself. In this paper I explore a third possibility: that at least part of the field's persistent fragmentation reflects a mismatch between explanatory targets. Some theories primarily describe access and reportability, some structural integration, some adaptive regulation, and some the metaphysical status of experience. If so, non-convergence may not simply indicate that most theories are false, but that they are often speaking past one another. Using Robert Lawrence Kuhn's recent taxonomy as a starting point, I argue that Selective Reality Theory (SRT) is most productively read as a candidate translation framework for comparing these explanatory targets, rather than as a further first-order theory competing to replace them.
