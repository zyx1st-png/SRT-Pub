# Scope Statement for Frontiers in Neuroscience

This manuscript is submitted as a **Hypothesis and Theory** article and is most directly aligned with the **Translational Neuroscience** section of *Frontiers in Neuroscience*. It addresses a specific neuroscience problem: executive breakdown in situations where action knowledge is preserved but action implementation fails. The paper proposes a testable cross-modal control-cost framework that links behavior, autonomic physiology, language-derived markers, and optional neural or biochemical measures within a shared selection-budget model. Its translational value lies in a low-cost validation chain built from task switching, HRV/SCR, and speech-derived indicators, plus disorder-differentiating predictions for depression, obsessive-compulsive disorder, and Parkinson's disease. No new empirical dataset is reported. Instead, the manuscript provides explicit equations, preregistration-ready hypotheses, falsification rules, and a staged roadmap for biomarker-oriented studies and clinical cohort testing.
