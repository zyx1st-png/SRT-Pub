# Contribution to the Field Statement

People with neurological or psychiatric disorders often know what they should do but still struggle to turn that knowledge into action. This paper offers a new way to study that gap by treating it as a hidden control cost that can be tracked across behavior, body signals, and speech. The framework combines standard cognitive tasks with low-cost physiological measures such as heart-rate and skin-conductance recovery, and with a simple language marker based on how often people speak in terms of obligation versus possibility. It then shows how this approach could be tested in depression, obsessive-compulsive disorder, and Parkinson's disease. No new data are reported. The main value of the paper is that it turns an everyday but poorly explained clinical problem into a set of clear, testable studies with explicit rules for being supported, revised, or rejected. If validated, the framework could help connect basic neuroscience with practical tools for identifying and tracking executive dysfunction.
