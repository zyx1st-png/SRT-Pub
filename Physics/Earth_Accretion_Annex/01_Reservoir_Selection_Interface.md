## 1. External cosmochemistry summary

The Nature Astronomy paper analyzes ten nucleosynthetic isotope anomaly systems across planets and meteorite parent bodies. It argues that Earth's bulk silicate isotopic composition is consistent with an extension of the non-carbonaceous inner Solar System reservoir, rather than requiring substantial carbonaceous outer Solar System material.

The headline result:

```text
Earth formed, to first order, from inner Solar System material.
Outer Solar System material contributed little to none of Earth's mass.
```

The authors also infer that Jupiter likely acted as a material barrier during early Solar System formation, maintaining a separation between inner non-carbonaceous and outer carbonaceous reservoirs.

## 2. Reservoir-selection / accessibility-limitation analogy

This material is useful for SRT not because it concerns consciousness directly, but because it offers a physical example of a selection landscape shaped by historical barriers, reservoir separation, and constrained accessibility.

SRT's ontology often emphasizes:

```text
manifest outcome = what becomes reachable and stabilized under constraints
```

The Earth-accretion result provides a cosmochemical analogue:

```text
planetary composition = what becomes reachable and accretable under disk dynamics, reservoir structure, and gravitational barriers
```

This is a physical-scale example of selection without intention. It shows how a final manifest object can emerge not from all available cosmic material, but from the subset of material made accessible by historical constraints.

## 4. Volatile-boundary problem

The article highlights an important unresolved question: if Earth formed almost entirely from inner Solar System material, then how did Earth acquire sufficient water and carbon for life?

SRT should treat this not as a contradiction, but as a boundary-condition problem:

```text
Earth's bulk accretion reservoir may be inner-origin,
but volatile acquisition and retention still require additional explanation.
```

Possible interpretations:

1. water and volatiles were already present in inner Solar System material;
2. small late additions from outer Solar System material supplied disproportionate volatile content;
3. inner disk chemistry allowed more volatile retention than previously assumed;
4. impact and differentiation history altered volatile preservation.

SRT-facing lesson:

```text
majority material selection and minority catalytic boundary conditions must be distinguished.
```

A system's bulk structure may be selected from one reservoir, while a small boundary-condition component changes its future possibility space dramatically.

## 6. Public-facing paragraph

Recent cosmochemical work on Earth's provenance suggests that Earth formed almost entirely from inner Solar System material, with little or no mass contribution from outer Solar System carbonaceous reservoirs. The result matters for SRT not because planetary accretion is conscious, but because it gives a physical example of constrained selection: the manifest object is not assembled from all possible material, but from the subset made dynamically accessible by historical barriers such as Jupiter's early formation. In SRT terms, total possibility should be distinguished from accessible possibility. Earth's isotopic composition is a material record of this selection path, while the remaining water/carbon question shows that small boundary-condition components may have outsized effects on later biological possibility space.

Recent work by Sossi and Bower argues that Earth accreted almost entirely from inner Solar System non-carbonaceous material, with little or no contribution from outer Solar System carbonaceous reservoirs.
