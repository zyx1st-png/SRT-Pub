## 1. Why this matters for SRT

SRT's physics layer often treats manifest time as a stable ordering regime rather than as an unquestioned primitive container. This material is useful because it addresses a concrete boundary: when the clock itself has quantum motion, the passage of proper time may acquire quantum signatures that cannot be fully captured by classical relativistic time dilation alone.

This helps SRT avoid two extremes:

```text
classical time as final ontological floor
vs
loose metaphysical claims that time is merely subjective
```

The useful middle position is:

```text
time is operationally recorded through physical clocks, and the structure of that recording may depend on the quantum state of the clock.
```

## 12. One-paragraph abstract

Quantum signatures of proper time in optical ion clocks provide a strong physics-facing bridge for SRT's treatment of time as an operationally manifest layer. The PRL work by Sorci, Foo, Leibfried, Sanner, and Pikovski proposes that state-of-the-art trapped-ion clocks can probe regimes where classical proper-time dynamics is insufficient because the clock's own motion is quantum-controlled. Effects such as vacuum second-order Doppler shifts, squeezing-related clock shifts, and motion-clock entanglement may become observable through proper-time interferometry. For SRT, the point is not that subjective time is quantum proper time, nor that SRT is proven. The value is structural: temporal order becomes manifest through physical interfaces, and classical timekeeping is a stable L2-like regime whose limits can be tested when latent quantum clock states become experimentally accessible.
