## Suggested paragraph

Optical-ion-clock proposals for detecting quantum signatures of proper time sharpen SRT's distinction between manifest temporal order and deeper physical access conditions. Proper time is not merely an abstract parameter; it is operationally recorded by clocks whose motion, state, and coupling determine the recorded temporal relation. When the clock's motion is quantum-controlled, the classical proper-time layer can acquire measurable quantum corrections. SRT should not identify this with subjective time, but it can use the result structurally: time becomes manifest through an interface, and the stability of classical timekeeping is an effective L2 regime that may require extension at quantum precision.
