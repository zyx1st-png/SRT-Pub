# SourceCard: Pio-Lopez, Jawanda & Levin — *Theories of Aging: From Damage and Programmed Theories to Goal-Directedness*

## 1. One-line summary

This 2026 review preprint organizes aging theories into damage-based, programmatic, and collective-intelligence / goal-directedness families, then proposes that loss of large-scale anatomical goal alignment may be an aging mechanism: morphogenesis and regeneration are treated as homeostatic, goal-seeking processes, with bioelectric and other tissue-level pattern memories as candidate implementations of anatomical setpoints.

## 2. Source boundary

This is a **non-peer-reviewed review and theoretical synthesis**, not a new primary aging experiment. Its value for SRT is therefore:

```text
conceptual organization
+ explicit theoretical claims
+ routes to cited primary literature
+ candidate experimental interfaces
```

not direct validation of SRT, goal-directed aging, bioelectric rejuvenation, or any canonical SRT variable.

Whenever this card mentions Xenopus, planaria, Hydra, biobots, Yamanaka factors, parabiosis, senescence, stem-cell exhaustion or other empirical examples, the preprint is acting as a literature synthesis. Strong empirical use requires verification of the cited primary source.

## 3. Source-backed claims

### 3.1 Three-family theoretical map

The abstract states that the authors review damage-based and programmatic aging theories and add a third option grounded in a cognitive / collective-intelligence perspective on multiscale biological systems.

**PDF anchor:** pp. 1–2, title / abstract.

Safe compression:

```text
the paper proposes goal-directedness loss as an additional organizing framework,
not as a demonstrated replacement for all existing aging theories
```

### 3.2 Loss-of-goal-directedness model

The authors propose that aging can arise from dynamical-systems-level disruption of homeostatic alignment in cellular collectives that build and repair the body. In their strongest formulation, once developmental goals are completed and no new goal is maintained, cellular components may functionally disband from the original aligned state; the model therefore allows aging in the absence of accumulated damage.

**PDF anchor:** p. 2, abstract.

This is a **source theoretical claim**, not an established empirical result.

### 3.3 Morphogenesis and regeneration as setpoint-guided error reduction

The paper describes morphogenesis as pattern homeostasis: cellular collectives reduce error relative to stored anatomical setpoints through growth, remodeling, apoptosis and related effectors. It emphasizes that individual cells can pursue local metabolic / physiological goals while collectives implement larger anatomical states.

**PDF anchor:** pp. 6–7, section developing goal-seeking morphogenesis and multiscale competency.

Safe interface:

```text
local cellular goal
!=
large-scale anatomical maintenance target
```

### 3.4 Policy memory extends beyond static state memory

The review discusses imperfect-memory theory and explicitly broadens relevant memory beyond molecular, physiological or transcriptional states to include second-order memories of policies: resilient ways of responding to inputs.

**PDF anchor:** p. 7, discussion of imperfect memory and loss of goal-directedness.

This gives a useful aging distinction:

```text
state memory
!=
response-policy memory
```

but the review does not establish a single molecular carrier for such policy memory.

### 3.5 Bioelectric morphostatic information as a candidate implementation

The authors present developmental bioelectricity as a large-scale coordination layer capable of encoding / maintaining anatomical setpoints and influencing developmental and regenerative programs. They hypothesize that degradation or loss of access to these patterns can contribute to anatomical and functional aging.

**PDF anchor:** pp. 9–10, “The Bioelectrical Pattern as the Encoded Morphogenetic Goal”.

The paper also notes reciprocal relations between bioelectric state and familiar aging phenomena such as senescence, stem-cell exhaustion, inflammation, loss of proteostasis and altered nutrient sensing.

Safe compression:

```text
bioelectric pattern is a candidate morphostatic carrier / intervention interface
!=
proof that bioelectric decay is the universal cause of aging
```

### 3.6 AGED / loss of multicellular transcriptional alignment

The review discusses “Atavistic Genetic Expression Dissociation (AGED)” as a proposed transcriptional signature of increasing disagreement across tissues / cells in their inferred evolutionary-age expression patterns, interpreting this as possible evidence of loss of multicellular coordination.

**PDF anchor:** pp. 10–11, AGED section and Figure 2 discussion.

This remains an interpretive bridge; the review itself acknowledges tissue heterogeneity and exceptions.

### 3.7 Regeneration-centered therapeutic roadmap

The future-roadmap section uses planaria, Hydra and sustained regenerative systems as motivating existence examples and proposes that re-imposing or refreshing large-scale morphogenetic goals may be relevant to rejuvenation. The paper discusses bioelectric pattern renewal, regenerative contexts, partial reprogramming and related interventions as possible interfaces.

**PDF anchor:** pp. 11–12, “Future Roadmap for Therapeutics”.

The relevant source-level thesis is:

```text
aging intervention may need to restore large-scale regulatory coordination,
not only micromanage molecular hallmarks
```

### 3.8 Age-evidencing hypothesis

Late in the roadmap the authors propose an “age-evidencing” hypothesis: tissue-level age may be an output of cellular computations integrating current state and past input history, rather than a single physical or biochemical parameter.

**PDF anchor:** p. 12, paragraph immediately before Conclusion.

This is a strong speculative hypothesis. It should not be imported as:

```text
age = computation
```

without independent evidence.

## 4. What this source does not establish

Do not infer:

```text
aging is proven to be caused by goal loss
bioelectric setpoints are the unique or universal anatomical memory
morphogenetic goal = conscious intention
morphostatic control = SRT selection
cellular collective = SRT subject
loss of goal-directedness = loss of d / T_dir / Psi_f
inflammation / ROS / proteostasis failure = Psi_f
policy memory = L2 by definition
all rejuvenation requires bioelectric manipulation
planaria / Hydra immortality proves the theory
age is literally a scalar computed by cells
```

## 5. SRT consensus interface

### 5.1 Goal / setpoint supplies the maintenance-relative reference

A useful bounded synthesis is:

```text
goal / setpoint
-> specifies what counts as a maintenance-relevant deviation
-> disturbance creates a burden relative to continued realization
-> maintenance responses attempt to reduce the deviation
```

This does **not** redefine canonical `Psi_f`. It supplies a biological context in which a named mismatch may become a payability / maintenance problem.

### 5.2 Preserve AIGOAL01's goal ladder

The source's “goal-directedness” language should be cross-read with:

```text
AI/patches/SRT_AI_AIGOAL01_Goal_Selection_Completion_Separation_v0_1.md
```

SRT must preserve:

```text
goal implementation / completion
!=
goal selection among alternatives
!=
goal-space generation or revision
```

Morphogenetic setpoint following may establish goal-conditioned regulation without proving agentive goal selection, self-generated goals, subjecthood or phenomenality.

### 5.3 Multiscale bearer accounting

The paper's multiscale competency framework creates a natural interface with consequence-bearing analysis:

```text
what is locally stabilizing for a cell
may be costly for tissue / organism maintenance
```

This is especially relevant to senescence, clonal expansion, fibrosis, chronic inflammation and cancer-like local autonomy, but each case requires its own biological evidence.

### 5.4 Policy memory and AGING01 history

The source's policy-memory idea can be absorbed as a candidate carrier family for AGING01 historical writeback:

```text
past maintenance response
-> changes future response policy / accessibility
```

but “policy memory” should not become a new canonical variable or synonym for `L2`.
