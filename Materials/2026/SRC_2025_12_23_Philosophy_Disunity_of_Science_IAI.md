# SourceCard: IAI / Disunity of science as feature, not bug

## 1. One-line summary

Ragner Fjelland argues that the quest for a single reductionist "Theory of Everything" mistakes mathematical models for reality and can weaken science by preserving bloated frameworks through ad-hoc amendments; scientific strength often requires plural theories, multiple levels, and new conceptual starts.

## 2. Core claims of source

- Modern science has often been organized by a reductionist image: sciences form a hierarchy from human sciences down to physics, and diversity should eventually reduce to one final theory.
- Fjelland connects this impulse to a long Platonist lineage: from Plato's *Timaeus* to Galileo's mathematical book of nature and some modern physics attitudes toward mathematical reality.
- The article warns that mathematical models are powerful but should not be mistaken for reality itself.
- It criticizes non-empirical or weakly falsifiable Theory-of-Everything ambitions, citing Lee Smolin-style crisis critiques and Popperian falsifiability concerns via Ellis & Silk.
- It invokes Philip Anderson's "More Is Different" as an anti-reductionist pressure even within physics: new levels can require new laws, concepts, and generalizations.
- The closing claim is that complex systems should be investigated at different levels and from different perspectives; scientific disunity and diversity can be a strength rather than a defect.

## 3. Evidence / method

- Public philosophy-of-science essay on IAI News.
- The official IAI page was identified and read from the supplied text/title.
- The article is not a new empirical study, not a new physics result, and not a technical proof against reductionism.
- It functions as a public synthesis / argumentative framing around familiar references: Plato, Galileo, Weinberg, 't Hooft, Smolin, Popperian falsifiability, Ellis & Silk, and Anderson's "More Is Different."

## 4. Limits

- This source must not be treated as evidence that unification is always bad, that reduction is always invalid, or that mathematical modeling should be abandoned.
- It should not be used to promote vague pluralism, anti-science rhetoric, anti-formalism, or a license to avoid SRT formal hardening.
- The essay's treatment of emergence is high-level; it does not solve SRT's existing emergence-hygiene burden.
- Because it is public commentary, it should not be cited as primary authority for Anderson, Smolin, Ellis & Silk, Galileo, or 't Hooft. Use the primary works if those claims become load-bearing.
