# SourceCard: Mescaline, cerebellar connectivity, and frequency-selective sensory gating

## 1. One-line summary

In awake rats, mescaline produced a cerebellar-weighted BOLD pattern, widespread increases in cerebellar functional connectivity, and frequency-dependent rather than uniformly weakened acoustic gating; the result is useful as a perturbational example of **accessibility / routing reconfiguration**, not as evidence that the cerebellum is an SRT "reality filter" or that psychedelic openness equals reselectability.

## 2. Core source claims

The primary study reports:

1. awake-rat pharmacological fMRI after mescaline versus vehicle;
2. prominent cerebellar / hindbrain BOLD changes rather than a simple global activation increase;
3. increased functional connectivity between cerebellar regions and distributed areas including hippocampal, thalamic, somatosensory and midbrain territories;
4. acoustic prepulse-inhibition changes that depend on prepulse frequency rather than a single global gating direction;
5. a mechanistic interpretation in which altered cerebellar prediction / sensory-filtering may contribute to the mescaline state.

The fifth item is an interpretation, not a directly demonstrated causal chain from cerebellar gating to human psychedelic phenomenology.

## 3. Evidence / method

- Species / state: awake Sprague-Dawley rats.
- Design: mescaline versus vehicle; 24 animals total, with 12 per treatment group.
- Mescaline dose: 50 mg/kg.
- Readouts: pharmacological BOLD fMRI, resting-state functional connectivity, acoustic prepulse inhibition / sensory gating.
- Gating result: the direction of PPI change varied by acoustic frequency, supporting **frequency-selective recalibration** rather than a simple open/closed gate model.
- The paper explicitly discusses BOLD and peripheral-physiology limitations and calls for multimodal validation.

## 4. Important limits

1. **Rat-to-human translation is open.** The study does not measure human psychedelic phenomenology, ego dissolution, therapy response, or subject-position destabilization.
2. **Single-dose design.** It does not establish a dose-response curve or a general mescaline signature.
3. **BOLD is not neural firing.** No concurrent heart-rate, respiration or blood-gas monitoring fully separates direct neural effects from peripheral / vascular contributions.
4. **Connectivity is not selection.** Functional connectivity changes do not by themselves establish candidate admission, anchoring, consciousness, `d`, `Psi_f`, `T_dir`, `L_2`, or a Real Choice Moment.
5. **Gating is not uniformly reduced.** Frequency-selective PPI effects directly block the crude reading "mescaline simply opens the sensory gate."
6. The study does not establish the cerebellum as a unique or central generator of psychedelic experience.

Use only for the bounded claim that several psychedelics can reopen a **specific social-reward-learning critical period in mice**. Do not rewrite this as human personality reopening, universal plasticity, or proof of therapeutic re-selection.

### ReSPCT 2025 — context-reporting consensus

Use only to establish that psychedelic trials increasingly treat physical environment, procedure, therapeutic framework and subjective context as reportable extra-pharmacological variables. This supports a context-sensitive experimental design; it does not prove an SRT bearer–niche mechanism.

## 7. SRT relevance after novelty audit

The source does **not** create the SRT concept of reselectability. ST-A and the existing P2/P3 generative-reselectability bridge already carry that burden.

The surviving increment is narrower:

```text
perturbational reopening / routing change
!= adaptive reselectability
```

and therefore:

```text
more candidate accessibility
!= better choice
!= stronger anchoring
!= healthier L2
```

The material supplies an empirical stress-test domain for the mirror failure of over-hardening:

```text
over-hardening / lock-in
<-> bounded generative reselectability
<-> under-anchoring / failure to stabilize
```

`under-anchoring` is introduced only as a P3/P4 bridge label in the associated patch, not as a canonical SRT term.

**Evidence-role boundary:** NEURAL35's `Opening x Reanchoring` discrimination is generated from SRT's existing architecture. Cavallaro et al. provide a bounded existence example that a psychedelic perturbation can alter network organization and sensory gating in a non-uniform way; they do **not** provide the evidence base for the human clinical outcome model used in the P4 proposal.
