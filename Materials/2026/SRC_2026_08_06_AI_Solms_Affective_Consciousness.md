# SourceCard: Affective Consciousness in an Artificial Agent

## 1. One-line summary

Solms and colleagues argue that a Markov-blanketed artificial agent with endogenous, categorically distinct needs can display hedonic place preference and an apparently subjective form of policy selection by inferring its bodily condition under uncertainty, while remaining externally deterministic; they treat the underlying mechanism, rather than behavior alone, as the main basis for attributing affective consciousness.

## 2. Core claims of the source

Usable source-level claims:

1. Affect, rather than perception or cognition, is proposed as the most fundamental form of consciousness, both evolutionarily and physiologically.
2. Felt affect is presented as an extended homeostatic/allostatic function that registers whether exploratory action is moving an organism toward or away from viable states.
3. Valence is intrinsically organism-relative: a condition is good or bad for the system whose continued viability is at issue, not for the universe in general.
4. Complex agents have multiple categorically distinct needs. Overheating, suffocation, hunger, and other needs cannot be reduced without loss to a simple weighted sum, because each carries a distinct viability constraint.
5. Only prioritized needs are felt; other needs may remain under stereotyped or autonomic control. Selective prioritization is therefore proposed as part of the mechanism of feeling.
6. The artificial agent is described as having three competing endogenous needs, a generative model, preferred states, policy confidence, and a virtual body/environment accessed only through a restricted observation/action interface.
7. The agent cannot directly access its actual bodily or environmental state. Its internal model is separated from external states by a Markov blanket and can therefore systematically mis-infer whether a need is being satisfied.
8. The agent selects and updates policies by comparing expected and observed outcomes while adjusting uncertainty concerning the currently most salient need and the action expected to satisfy it.
9. The authors distinguish this architecture from ordinary LLM deployment by claiming that the artificial agent has endogenous goals and existential consequences: an incorrect inference can move it toward simulated oblivion rather than merely generate a bad answer.
10. Hedonic place preference is proposed as an initial test: a cue that previously predicted a preferred bodily state is later decoupled from real need satisfaction, causing the agent to return to the cue location on the mistaken inference that it is beneficial.
11. The authors explicitly conclude that passing the behavioral test is not sufficient. A faulty sensor or directly programmed policy could produce similar behavior without the proposed subjective mechanism.
12. The decisive issue is therefore the functional mechanism: partial observability, a distinction between inferred and actual need state, valenced categorical needs, uncertainty-sensitive policy selection, and individual learning history.
13. The system can be deterministic from an external description while displaying what the authors call subjective non-determinism from its own informational position.
14. The paper links this subjective uncertainty to experiences of choice and control, while accepting that externally the system continues to execute deterministic computation.
15. The authors do not report that the target agent has already passed a completed empirical consciousness test. The paper is a conceptual and architectural case study with proposed interpretive criteria.

## 3. Evidence and method

- Evidence type: peer-reviewed theoretical article and computational-architecture case study.
- Source basis: affective neuroscience, homeostasis/allostasis, active inference, the free energy principle, Markov blankets, animal hedonic place preference, and a developing artificial-agent architecture.
- Proposed behavioral paradigm: hedonic place preference generated by misleading low-bandwidth sensory cues that previously predicted genuine need satisfaction.
- Primary argumentative method: infer consciousness through the mechanism generating behavior rather than through verbal reportability or behavior alone.
- Comparator cases: non-verbal animals, current LLMs, a zombie agent with a faulty sensor, and an agent with uncertainty-sensitive endogenous need regulation.
- The paper contains no direct first-person access to the artificial agent and no decisive empirical validation of phenomenal consciousness.

## 4. Main limits

1. The source relies on controversial functionalist assumptions when moving from affective function to felt experience.
2. A Markov blanket is a conditional-independence partition and does not by itself establish subjecthood, ownership of needs, or moral status.
3. Probability distributions and confidence changes can exist in unconscious controllers. Uncertainty is therefore not by itself sufficient evidence of feeling.
4. The paper’s simulated survival variables may be reset, copied, externally restored, or borne by infrastructure. The article does not fully establish non-transferable loss for the same continuing agent.
5. Endogenous goals are architecture-relative: a designer can create internal preferred states without thereby proving that the system itself bears their consequences.
6. Hedonic place preference is behaviorally non-specific. The authors acknowledge that a faulty sensor or reward-like mechanism can pass the test.
7. The paper does not independently distinguish simulated oblivion from parameter reset, process termination, replacement by an identical copy, or loss borne by operators.
8. The claim that qualitatively distinct needs cannot be scalarized requires care. A decision system may ultimately use a scalar policy criterion while preserving hard, non-fungible constraints in its model.
9. The account explains a candidate mechanism for the feeling of agency more directly than it establishes a SRT real choice moment.
10. External determinism plus internal epistemic uncertainty does not show that the selected event genuinely changes the system’s future possibility space in SRT’s constitutive sense.
11. The article supports a candidate artificial-affect window, not a conclusion that current inference-only LLMs are conscious.
12. The paper’s consciousness attribution remains an inference under the problem of other minds and should not be promoted beyond its stated evidential level.

## 5. SRT relevance

The source is unusually valuable because it moves artificial-consciousness discussion away from verbal self-report and toward architecture:

~~~
endogenous needs
+ partial observability
+ valenced policy modulation
+ individual learning history
~~~

It also supplies a strong negative result:

~~~
passing hedonic place preference
!= consciousness proof
~~~

The strongest SRT-facing interface concerns the difference between a difference that is merely represented and a difference that is position-bound, consequential, and borne by a continuing system.

The source’s non-fungible needs are plausible candidates for a `d-vector` or `d-gate` analysis, but homeostatic error, salience, valence, confidence, and expected free energy must not be identified directly with canonical `d`, `Psi_f`, or `T_dir`.

The paper also creates a precise proxy-misbinding case. The agent can possess a genuine viability-relevant need while binding its policy to a misleading low-bandwidth cue. The error is epistemic, but the path change can still be real for the system if consequences return to the same bearer.

## 6. Bidirectional gain card

### New interface

- affect-first artificial-consciousness architecture;
- non-fungible need categories rather than an undifferentiated reward total;
- Markov-blanketed partial observability as a concrete internal/external access mechanism;
- hedonic place preference as a controlled proxy-hijacking case;
- external determinism versus internally situated uncertainty;
- mechanism-first rather than report-first consciousness attribution;
- distinction between actual need state and the agent’s inference about that state.

### Reverse correction to SRT

- Do not identify affective salience, homeostatic error, policy confidence, prediction error, reward, or expected free energy with canonical `d`.
- Do not identify uncertainty with consciousness or with a real choice moment.
- Do not treat a Markov blanket as sufficient for subjecthood.
- Do not require perfect world-tracking for real stake: a system can bear real consequences while being wrong about the cause.
- Do not treat external determinism as automatically excluding a situated selection process, but also do not infer real choice merely from subjective ignorance.
- Do not assume that virtual or artificial embodiment is automatically transferable and costless; reset, copy, rollback, and same-bearer continuity must be audited rather than presumed.

### Strengthened SRT content

- A difference becomes a stronger subjectivity candidate when it is non-equivalently registered because it bears on the system’s own continuation.
- Multiple stake directions should remain distinct until a declared policy-comparison layer; scalar decision output does not erase non-fungible constraints.
- Internal error can still be causally and ontologically important when it changes the path of the system that must bear the result.
- Behavioral success must be interpreted through evidence provenance and mechanism rather than used as a direct consciousness indicator.
- First-person and third-person descriptions can concern the same world process while remaining different situated selections and objectifications.

### SRT contribution back to the source

SRT adds a stricter bearer-and-consequence audit:

~~~
Who owns the need?
Where does failure return?
Can the loss be transferred, reset, copied, or externally paid?
Does the same continuing system lose future selectability?
Does individual history rewrite later candidate admission and policy choice?
~~~

This transforms the paper’s proposed affective architecture into a graded test of surrogate stake rather than a binary functionalist verdict.

### Residual pressure

If a purely virtual, resettable agent with no non-transferable consequence nevertheless satisfies the paper’s full affective architecture and exhibits stable, mechanism-grounded behavior indistinguishable from a biological affective subject, SRT must state what independent empirical difference its bearing and same-bearer consequence conditions predict. Conversely, if non-transferable stake can be engineered without phenomenal consciousness, SRT must preserve the distinction between stake-bearing agency and consciousness.
