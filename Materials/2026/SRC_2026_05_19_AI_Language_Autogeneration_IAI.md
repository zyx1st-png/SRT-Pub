# SourceCard — IAI / Barenholtz: LLMs show language does not describe reality

## 1. One-line summary

Barenholtz argues that LLMs expose an autogenerative property of language: linguistic structure can generate coherent continuations, imagery prompts, and coordination conditions without being grounded by fixed word-to-world reference.

## 2. Core claims of source

- LLMs show that natural language contains enough internal predictive structure to generate coherent continuations from token relations alone.
- This does not merely show something about machines; it suggests that human language also exploits language's autogenerative structure.
- Meaning should not be understood primarily as words mapping onto already-fixed facts or inner representations.
- Multimodal models do not restore simple reference; images and words mutually prompt generative spaces rather than forming fixed word-to-pixel mappings.
- Language works as a coordinative tool: it generates conditions that perception, imagery, and behavior can satisfy or fail to satisfy.
- The human self is described as an emergent coordination of linguistic, perceptual, motor, and decision processes rather than a single transparent inner controller.

## 3. Evidence / method

- Source type: public philosophy / cognitive-science essay.
- Main evidence used by source: conceptual interpretation of LLM autoregressive generation, multimodal AI behavior, and familiar linguistic / philosophical grounding puzzles.
- Not a peer-reviewed empirical paper; no new experiment, dataset, formal proof, or DOI was identified.
- Official IAI page was accessible and metadata was checked: title, author, publication, publication date, and article body.

## 4. Limits

- The article's title and subtitle overstate the negative conclusion if read literally: LLMs do not by themselves prove that language never describes or refers to reality.
- The article risks collapsing three different claims: (a) fixed reference is insufficient, (b) linguistic meaning is generative and contextual, (c) grounding is unnecessary. SRT should keep these separated.
- LLM autogeneration is not evidence of LLM stake, subjecthood, ontological anchoring, or `d-value`.
- Human language cannot be reduced to next-token prediction without separately accounting for embodiment, consequence return, perception-action loops, and payability.
- “Language doesn't mean; it does” is useful public phrasing but not a canonical SRT definition.
