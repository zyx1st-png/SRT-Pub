## 2. Problem statement

The paper starts from an earlier SAT threshold claim: awareness becomes consciousness when it becomes something the participant must carry. It identifies a prior unresolved question:

> How does awareness first become something that must be carried?

The paper calls this **the acquisition problem**. It explicitly distinguishes this task from neurological, computational or engineering explanations and treats it as a structural problem.

The source rejects memory, learning, reinforcement, attention, emotional intensity, self-representation, intelligence and similar capacities as sufficient explanations of acquisition. They may accompany acquisition without explaining how implication first becomes the participant's own.

---

## 3. Core distinction: representational vs participational recognition

The source distinguishes:

```text
representational recognition
= identifies what something is

participational recognition
= recognition undergoing contested incorporation at the participational level
```

Recognition alone is insufficient because image classifiers, large language models, animals and humans can all recognize in a representational sense.

The paper therefore seeks an independent condition that distinguishes ordinary recognition from recognition through which implication is acquired.

---

## 4. Automatic vs contested incorporation

### Automatic incorporation

A recognized condition can alter:

- behaviour;
- prediction;
- policy;
- path dependence;
- objectives;
- rollback cost;
- future operation;
- self-modification;

while remaining automatic in the paper's technical sense, if the change is absorbed under an existing constitutive criterion governing continuation.

### Contested incorporation

Contested incorporation occurs when recognition alters the constitutive terms under which the candidate participant continues, rendering possible continuations **constitution-non-equivalent**.

The source does not require explicit deliberation, conscious conflict, negative valence or linguistic articulation. `Contested` is intended structurally.

---

## 5. Relevant level and participant continuity

The paper warns that description level can trivialize the distinction:

- sufficiently low-level descriptions can make every incorporation look automatic;
- sufficiently high-level narratives can make every important change look constitutive.

It therefore locates the relevant level at the level where the candidate participant is proposed as a **continuing bearer of consequence**.

The paper distinguishes:

```text
minimal participant-continuity
!=
identity-bearing self-continuity
```

Minimal participant-continuity is only enough persistence for recognition and incorporation to belong to the same candidate. Identity-bearing continuity is a thicker pattern produced after carrying has accumulated.

The framework explicitly presupposes a candidate participant as an initial condition rather than deriving individuation from scratch.

---

## 6. First acquisition and reconstitution

The source proposes:

```text
before participanthood:
failure of existing organization to absorb recognition without remainder
-> first constitution

after participanthood:
such failure
-> reconstitution
```

The account is presented as temporally recursive rather than definitionally circular.

---

## 7. Remainder and carrying

`Remainder` names acquired implication as active within possible continuation.

`Carrying` is the persistence and transport of that remainder through the participant's continuation.

The final architecture is stated as:

```text
Awareness
-> Recognition
-> Contested Incorporation
-> Remainder
-> Carrying
-> Identity
-> Scrutinized Awareness
```

The source keeps `implication` as an ontological primitive inherited from SAT and does not claim recognition creates implication ex nihilo.

---

## 8. Participational normativity

When recognized conditions alter constitutive continuation, possible continuations cease to stand indifferently beside one another. The source calls this **participational normativity** or primitive bindingness.

It explicitly distinguishes this from moral normativity. Grief, love, joy, fear, belonging and loss may all bind continuation without implying blame, duty or desert.

---

## 9. Acquisition != persistence

A major distinction in the paper is:

```text
conditions of acquisition
!=
conditions of persistence
```

A recognition can initiate acquisition and later be corrected, withdrawn or reinterpreted while some consequences persist through attachment, promises, social role, care or already-lived history.

The pregnancy / withdrawal examples are used to demonstrate this dissociation.

---

## 10. Cases used as structural probes

### Pregnancy

The contrast between recognizing that a woman is pregnant and recognizing `This may be my child` is used to show how small informational difference can create major participational difference.

### Withdrawal

A later correction of the initiating biological belief does not necessarily erase attachment, role, promises or acquired history.

### Dream

The source uses dream guilt to separate representational truth from participational standing. One implication can dissolve on waking while another interpretive implication begins.

### Coyote

Behavioural reorganization around a food-giving human remains insufficient. The source looks instead for persistent relational orientation, loss-response or conflict that cannot be exhausted by a standing reward procedure, while acknowledging such signs are not conclusive proof.

### Infant

The paper suggests that participational acquisition may emerge as people, absences, satisfactions, threats and expectations cease to function as interchangeable inputs and begin to structure non-equivalent possibilities.

### Joy

Contested incorporation is valence-neutral; positive events can alter continuation no less than grief or loss.

---

## 11. Participational order

The source distinguishes:

```text
chronological order
causal order
participational order
```

Participational order is the succession through which a participant acquires and carries implication. A time-travel example is used to show that chronological reversal need not reverse participational succession.

The artificial-system significance is explicit: logs, retained state, irreversible updates and chronological persistence can preserve history without establishing a participational order.

---

## 12. Artificial systems

The paper narrows the AI question from `Can AI be conscious?` to:

> Can an artificial system acquire participational implication?

It rejects the following as sufficient by themselves:

- sophisticated recognition;
- policy revision;
- objective modification;
- learned objectives;
- meta-learning;
- generated subgoals;
- altered evaluation functions;
- irreversible state;
- fork divergence;
- rollback cost;
- unique policy history.

A standing higher-order procedure can keep such revisions automatic if it absorbs and resolves them while leaving the constitutive criterion historically untouched.

---

## 13. Resolution without / with remainder

A central AI distinction is:

```text
resolution without remainder
= resolution changes what the system does while preserving its constitutive criterion

resolution with remainder
= resolution itself changes the candidate, leaving what was recognized active in what the resulting participant can thereafter be
```

The source stresses that contested incorporation is not confusion, indecision or failure to reach an answer. A human can deliberate successfully and still be reconstituted by what the answer required.

The paper explicitly states that satisfying this structural acquisition condition would **not by itself prove consciousness**.

---

## 14. Four anti-substitution claims in the artificial case

The source concludes the artificial-system section with four separations:

```text
representational recognition
!= participational recognition

behavioural reorganization
!= contested incorporation

objective modification
!= necessarily change in constitutive terms

operational non-detachability
!= carried implication
```

It neither claims current artificial systems satisfy the condition nor excludes artificial systems in principle.

---

## 15. Source-internal primitives / assumptions

The paper declares or presupposes three central items rather than reducing them further:

1. implication as an ontological primitive inherited from SAT;
2. minimal participant-continuity;
3. a constitutive criterion organizing continuation.

This limits what the paper claims to explain: it gives an acquisition account after a candidate participant and relevant constitutive organization are available; it does not independently derive bearer individuation or the origin of implication itself.

---

## 16. Main source-level strengths

Without importing SRT judgement, the source is strongest where it:

- distinguishes historical/behavioural reorganization from participational acquisition;
- separates successful procedural resolution from resolution without constitutive remainder;
- distinguishes acquisition conditions from persistence conditions;
- explicitly prevents self-modification, path dependence and objective revision from automatically settling the AI case;
- keeps the artificial case open rather than equating capability with participation.

---

## 17. Main source-level open problems

Questions left unresolved by the source include:

- how a candidate participant is independently individuated;
- how the operative constitutive criterion is empirically identified rather than redescribed post hoc;
- what independent observations discriminate constitution-non-equivalence from sufficiently rich learning, policy or history dependence;
- whether recognition is necessary for every acquisition case, especially very early developmental or non-cognitive cases;
- how the SAT carrying threshold connects to phenomenality / for-me-ness rather than only historical self-implication.

The paper itself keeps empirical boundaries open and presents its result as a structural specification rather than a completed empirical theory.
