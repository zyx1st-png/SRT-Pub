# SourceCard: Mizrachi, Rottem & Rozenkrantz — *Voluntary attention regulates acute immune responses in humans*

## 1. One-line summary

Across three pre-registered, counterbalanced within-subject experiments, voluntarily directing attention toward sensations at a histamine skin-prick site changed the magnitude and time course of acute cutaneous inflammation relative to distraction; sensory attenuation weakened late resolution without abolishing the effect, while HRV changes were consistent with—but did not establish—parasympathetic/vagal mediation.

## 2. Core claims of source

### 2.1 Exp. 1: internal attention changes inflammatory magnitude and trajectory

Exp. 1 used `n = 37` and compared internal attention with video distraction under the same standardized histamine challenge. At 20 min, wheal size was `3.5 ± 1.1 mm` under internal attention versus `5.0 ± 0.7 mm` under distraction (`Cohen's d = 1.30`); flare was `10.6 ± 8.3 mm` versus `14.0 ± 8.0 mm` (`Cohen's d = 0.42`). Approximately 90% of participants showed greater inflammation under distraction. Wheal AUC and trajectory also differed, with between-condition differences emerging within about 3 min and increasing toward 20 min.

**PDF anchors:** p. 2, section `Internal attention versus distraction regulates the acute inflammatory response`; Fig. 1b–f and Fig. 1 caption. Full trajectory statistics route to Supplementary Tables 1.2–1.4 / Supplementary Section 2.

**Notation guard:** every `d` reported in source statistics is **Cohen's d effect size**, not the canonical SRT `d-value`.

### 2.2 Exp. 2: independent cohort, but still within-subject and counterbalanced

Exp. 2 used an **independent cohort relative to Exp. 1** (`n = 20`), while retaining a within-subject, counterbalanced comparison inside the experiment. Both attention conditions used the same continuous abstract-shape sequence and matched task structure; only the attentional instruction differed. Internal attention again produced about `1.6-fold` smaller wheal/flare responses, with about 90% of participants showing larger inflammation under distraction.

**PDF anchors:** p. 2, section `Attentional regulation of inflammation is independent of task demands`; Fig. 1a caption; p. 4, continuation of Exp. 2 results; Methods p. 8, `Participants` and `Experimental set-up and paradigm`.

The safe source-level conclusion is narrower than “all confounding is removed”:

```text
visual input, task structure and perceived task difficulty
are unlikely to explain the replicated condition effect
```

### 2.3 Exp. 3: sensory signalling contributes, especially to later resolution

Exp. 3 (`n = 17`, returning participants) added topical lidocaine while participants maintained internal attention. At 20 min, wheal was larger under internal attention + lidocaine than under intact internal attention (`4.0 ± 0.6` vs `3.1 ± 1.0 mm`, `Cohen's d = 1.04`), but remained smaller than under distraction (`4.0 ± 0.6` vs `4.9 ± 1.0 mm`, `Cohen's d = 1.19`). Sensation ratings did not significantly differ between lidocaine and distraction (`P = 0.290`), while wheal outcomes did.

**PDF anchors:** Fig. 2b–f and caption on p. 4; p. 5, section `Sensory signalling contributes to attentional regulation of inflammation`.

The source supports:

```text
intact sensory signalling contributes to the attentional effect,
with the clearest difference in the later resolution phase,
but sensory signalling does not fully account for the effect
```

It does **not** establish that subjective intensity itself is the causal currency.

### 2.4 Autonomic findings: HRV is compatible with a top-down route, not proof of vagal mediation

Skin conductance, heart rate, skin temperature and perceived anxiety did not differ between internal attention and distraction in the pooled Exp. 1/2 analyses. HRV was higher under internal attention than distraction (`P = 0.023`, `Cohen's d = 0.34`). In Exp. 3, HRV remained higher under internal attention + lidocaine than distraction and did not differ from intact internal attention.

**PDF anchors:** p. 5, section `Attention-driven inflammatory regulation involves top-down autonomic control`; Fig. 3a–h and caption on p. 6.

The authors interpret this pattern as consistent with parasympathetic/vagal engagement. However, the paper itself later states that HRV is an **indirect index** of vagal activity and that more direct autonomic measurements are required.

**Boundary anchor:** pp. 7–8, `Several limitations should be considered`.

### 2.5 The authors' stronger phenomenal interpretation is a discussion-level claim

The Discussion says the findings position subjective sensory experience as a functional component of immune regulation, and the Conclusion describes subjective sensory experience as an active contributor. Those statements are part of the authors' interpretation.

**PDF anchors:** p. 7, Discussion paragraph beginning `Our findings also extend prior evidence...`; p. 8, concluding paragraph.

The experiments manipulate attentional state and peripheral sensory signalling; they do **not** independently manipulate phenomenality while holding the relevant structural/functional variables fixed. Therefore the source does not identify phenomenal experience as an indispensable causal term.

## 3. Evidence / method

The study used three pre-registered experiments with a standardized histamine skin-prick test. Conditions were completed within individuals in counterbalanced order, allowing participants to serve as their own controls. Exp. 1 and Exp. 2 used independent participant cohorts; Exp. 3 recruited returning participants from the earlier experiments.

**PDF anchors:** p. 2, overview paragraphs before the Exp. 1 results; p. 8, Methods sections `Pre-registrations`, `Participants`, and `Experimental set-up and paradigm`.

Important registration nuance: the paper states that recovery slopes, recovery classification and moderator analyses were **not** specified in the pre-registration and should be considered exploratory; several secondary autonomic analyses listed in the pre-registration were not performed/reported.

**PDF anchor:** p. 8, `Pre-registrations`.

This matters for later SRT use: the primary attention-to-inflammatory-magnitude result is stronger than any attempt to make the exploratory recovery analyses carry a unique SRT discriminator.

## 4. Limits

The source itself identifies several important limits:

1. molecular/cellular immune mediators were not directly measured;
2. the model is localized, acute, histamine-induced inflammation in healthy participants, so generalization to infectious, chronic inflammatory or autoimmune conditions is open;
3. smaller inflammation is not universally better—optimal magnitude and duration depend on context, and excessive attenuation in infection could compromise host defence;
4. attention was manipulated from inflammation onset, so initiation versus resolution is not fully dissociated;
5. topical lidocaine does not uniformly block all afferent fibre types and residual sensory input may remain;
6. HRV is an indirect vagal proxy; humoral mechanisms such as cortisol were not assessed.

**PDF anchors:** pp. 7–8, `Several limitations should be considered`.

Therefore do not infer:

```text
attention universally improves immune function
smaller inflammation = healthier bearer in every context
HRV proves vagal mediation
lidocaine cleanly deletes sensory signalling
subjective intensity is the causal mediator
subjective phenomenality is necessary for the immune effect
```

```text

```

```text

!= subjective intensity
!= attentional/control weight
!= physiological burden
!= canonical d
!= canonical Psi_f
```

**D-track:** **not claimed**. The paper does not test a unique SRT discriminator against richer active inference / predictive-allostatic rivals. The existing `precision × stake` / matched-present-state versus bearer-specific future-consequence design remains the appropriate place to seek a discriminating increment.

### 5.3 Qualia guard

It does not strengthen the claim that phenomenality itself is indispensable. Therefore the existing PH-QUAL01 constitutive deletion test remains unresolved / not passed.
