# SourceCard: Peter Schulte, *Toward a Radical Gradualism About Representation*

## 1. One-line summary

Schulte argues that theories of representation should stop looking for a strict or fuzzy natural border between mere sensitivity and genuine representation: traditional necessary-and-sufficient demarcation conditions face a `problem of adjacent cases`, while actual scientific explanatory practice appears to move gradually toward representational modeling as sensory information-processing complexity increases; he therefore proposes a robustly realist radical gradualism in which systems possess degrees of representationality rather than crossing a single fundamental threshold.

## 3. The problem the paper addresses

Schulte begins from the `demarcation question`:

```text
Where is the border between mere sensitivity and genuine representation?
```

Organisms from bacteria and plants to insects and mammals respond adaptively to environmental conditions. The traditional question asks which systems merely respond to states of the world and which genuinely represent them.

Schulte initially uses a standard notion of a representational state: an internal state with semantic content and truth-, correctness-, or fulfillment-conditions in virtue of that content. The paper brackets phenomenal consciousness and does not attempt to determine where conscious experience begins.

The target is not anti-representationalism. Schulte assumes that sophisticated animals such as dogs, crows, apes and humans possess representational states and asks how far downward representation extends.

## 4. Explanatory constraint and the `low road`

Schulte adopts an explanatory constraint on theories of representational status. The core idea is:

```text
No representation without representational explanation, and vice versa.
```

An adequate account should attribute representational states to a system if and only if such attribution can participate in genuine, distinctively representational explanations of the system's behavior.

He distinguishes two routes for applying this constraint:

```text
high road:
first build a theory of representational explanation,
then use it as the standard;

low road:
use mature scientific explanatory practice as a fallible but fairly reliable guide
for where representational models have genuine explanatory value.
```

The paper follows the `low road`.

### 4.1 Negative and positive paradigm cases

The rifle trigger is used as a negative paradigm. It is always possible to redescribe the movement of the firing pin as a state that `represents` that the trigger was pulled, but this semantic redescription plays no serious explanatory role in firearm engineering.

Human vision is a positive paradigm. Vision science routinely constructs models in which system states carry contents such as object distance, binocular disparity or visual angle and in which causal transitions are modeled as mathematical or logical operations over those contents.

Schulte generalizes the contrast:

```text
negative paradigm cases:
rifles, thermostats, vending machines, simple reflex arcs,
hormonal mechanisms, tactic responses, tropisms;

positive paradigm cases:
human perception and control, and many forms of animal perception,
navigation and motor control.
```

The relevant contrast is not whether scientists happen to use the word `representation`; it is whether representational models are doing central explanatory work.

## 5. Liberal versus non-liberal accounts

Schulte divides existing theories into two broad groups.

`Liberal` accounts attribute representation widely, potentially to reflex arcs, hormone concentration changes, bacterial tropisms, thermostats or vending machines. These theories reject or weaken the low-road desideratum.

`Non-liberal` accounts try to exclude the negative paradigm cases by identifying a condition `C` that systems must satisfy to qualify as representational.

Schulte's main critical argument targets non-liberal accounts and asks whether any proposed condition `C` actually corresponds to a discontinuity in explanatory practice.

## 6. Tyler Burge's constancy-mechanism theory

The focal case is Tyler Burge's constancy-mechanism theory. In Schulte's reconstruction:

```text
an agentive system has representational states
iff
it contains at least one sensory system employing perceptual constancy mechanisms.
```

A perceptual constancy mechanism solves a characteristic proximal-distal underdetermination problem. For size constancy, retinal image size by itself underdetermines object size; a simple mechanism can integrate retinal size with a distance cue, such as lens-accommodation information, to yield a stable estimate or representation of distal object size.

The same general pattern appears in shape, lightness, motion, position, color and loudness constancies.

If constancy mechanisms mark the natural border of representation, scientific explanatory practice should show a corresponding `tipping point` when such mechanisms first appear.

## 7. The problem of adjacent cases

Schulte's strongest negative argument is the `problem of adjacent cases`.

For any proposed necessary-and-sufficient condition `C`, compare:

```text
(i) the most complex systems that do not satisfy C
with
(ii) the least complex systems that do satisfy C.
```

Then ask whether representational models show a corresponding explanatory discontinuity between the two sides.

If `C` is the genuine demarcation boundary, the transition across `C` should mark a real change in the explanatory usefulness or indispensability of representational modeling. Schulte argues that this is not what we observe.

### 7.1 Simple size constancy

A minimal size-constancy mechanism integrates retinal image size and an object-distance cue to produce a distal object-size representation. This clearly qualifies as representational on Burge's view, and representational descriptions are indeed used in scientific explanations of such systems.

### 7.2 Desert-ant path integration

Desert ants can return to the nest in a relatively direct line after an irregular outbound route. Their path-integration system continually updates a `home vector` by combining information about the length and direction of a newly traveled segment with the previous home-vector state.

The mechanism can be modeled as:

```text
old home vector
+ current travel distance
+ current travel direction
-> updated home vector
```

Burge explicitly denies that path integration is a perceptual constancy mechanism because it does not solve the same proximal-distal underdetermination problem. Yet scientific descriptions of path integration naturally and explanatorily employ representational language about the home vector and its updating.

Schulte therefore argues that the transition from path integration to simple perceptual constancy shows no corresponding representational `tipping point` in explanatory practice.

### 7.3 Auditory cue integration

To avoid making the case depend on whether path integration is reclassified as a constancy mechanism, Schulte gives a second adjacent case.

A simple auditory localization system combines interaural time difference (`ITD`) and interaural level difference (`ILD`), weighting the two cues according to reliability across sound frequencies, to estimate sound-source direction.

This is cue integration rather than perceptual constancy: the two cues are indicators of the same target variable with different reliability profiles. Nevertheless, empirical models characterize the resulting estimate in representational terms and use those descriptions explanatorily.

Again, no sharp explanatory discontinuity appears at the boundary defined by perceptual constancy.

## 8. Generalization of the adjacent-case challenge

Schulte does not claim to have refuted every traditional theory individually. His broader challenge is methodological:

```text
for any non-liberal theory that proposes condition C,
compare the strongest C-negative case with the weakest C-positive case;
then test whether explanatory practice changes discontinuously there.
```

He gives three reasons for taking generalization seriously:

1. Burge's account is among the best-developed and most empirically informed proposals;
2. similar problems appear plausible for other non-liberal accounts, including proposals that place the boundary at relatively small increases in cue structure;
3. scientific literature on simple sensory systems appears to show a near-continuous increase in the seriousness and explanatory importance of representational modeling rather than a discrete onset.

## 9. Radical gradualism

Schulte's positive proposal is that the standard demarcation question contains a false presupposition: perhaps there is no strict boundary and no fuzzy boundary between mere sensitivity and genuine representation.

Instead:

```text
representationality itself comes in degrees.
```

Systems may have zero, low, medium or high degrees of representationality. The transition is an extended grey region rather than a border crossing.

Schulte retains a necessary entry condition of the sensory-system kind for convenience, while noting that a producer-consumer condition could be substituted. He explicitly acknowledges that information-processing complexity alone is unlikely to be sufficient for representation.

## 10. Information-processing complexity

Schulte proposes sensory information-processing complexity as the main parameter underlying degrees of representationality.

Complexity depends on:

```text
(i) the number of families of system states carrying exploitable information
    about different environmental or bodily variables;

(ii) the number and variety of systematic causal relations among those state families.
```

The ant path-integration system, for example, contains families of states carrying information about segment length, direction of travel and the home vector, with systematic causal relationships that allow the system to be described as calculating an updated home vector.

Negative paradigm cases typically contain only one relevant family of information-carrying states and little or no interaction among distinct state families.

Schulte distinguishes this notion from mere mechanistic complexity: a biological mechanism can be mechanically elaborate without having high information-processing complexity in the relevant sense.

The proposed relation is therefore approximately:

```text
higher sensory information-processing complexity
-> higher degree of representationality
-> greater explanatory value of representational models.
```

This is the paper's principal positive hypothesis, not an experimentally established quantitative law.

## 11. Robust realism versus Dennett

Schulte distinguishes his proposal from Daniel Dennett's intentional-stance gradualism.

Dennett's intentional-systems theory is deliberately neutral about the internal mechanisms that produce behavior. Schulte instead endorses robust realism:

```text
ascribing a degree of representationality commits us to
real internal representational vehicles
and to corresponding internal information-processing organization.
```

He therefore labels his view `robustly realist radical gradualism` (`RRR gradualism`).

## 12. The modified-rifle objection

Schulte considers a modified rifle that fires only when both conditions hold:

```text
trigger pulled
AND
safety catch off.
```

Compared with the ordinary rifle, the modified mechanism integrates two information-bearing conditions and can be described as performing a simple logical operation. On Schulte's gradualist proposal, this gives it a minimally higher degree of information-processing complexity and therefore a minimal degree of representationality.

He accepts that consequence.

His response is that:

```text
x has a minimal degree of representationality
```

does not entail:

```text
x is a representational system simpliciter.
```

The gradual predicate is intended to replace the binary one. Schulte compares this to degrees of democracy: possessing a very small degree of a graded property does not automatically license ordinary binary classification.

## 13. Derivative binary representation

Schulte allows ordinary binary usage to be reintroduced derivatively:

```text
x is representational simpliciter
iff
x has a sufficiently high degree of representationality.
```

But such a threshold is not a fundamental natural boundary. It is derivative, indeterminate across a wide penumbra of intermediate cases and plausibly context-sensitive.

This is central to the paper's conceptual architecture:

```text
fundamental level:
graded representationality;

derivative practical level:
context-sensitive binary classification.
```

## 14. Author-stated limits and future work

Schulte explicitly leaves several tasks open:

- information-processing complexity requires further specification;
- the contribution of perceptual constancy, memory, learning and multisensory integration should be systematically investigated;
- the relation between representation and phenomenal consciousness remains open;
- the relation between representation and mind more generally remains open.

The paper therefore establishes a philosophical framework and a negative pressure against hard demarcation more strongly than it establishes a final measure of representational degree.

## 15. Internal evaluation for SRT

### 15.1 Strongest gain: an adjacent-case threshold audit

The most portable contribution is not the scalar `complexity -> representationality` proposal but the method for testing purported natural thresholds:

```text
choose a proposed condition C
-> identify adjacent C-negative and C-positive systems
-> compare the relevant explanatory / behavioral / structural change
-> if no corresponding discontinuity appears,
   do not treat C as an established natural boundary.
```

This is highly relevant to SRT whenever a bridge concept risks being turned into a binary bearer, subjecthood, representation or agency gate without evidence of a genuine discontinuity.

### 15.2 Existing SRT representation interface already separates evidence dimensions

Current SRT already distinguishes sensitivity, specificity, invariance and downstream functionality and rejects `decodable -> strong representation` as an automatic inference.

Schulte adds a different issue: even after evidence for representation is graded more carefully, the ontology of representationality itself may also fail to contain a natural binary border.

These two dimensions must not be collapsed:

```text
epistemic evidence strength for representation
!=
ontological degree of representationality.
```

### 15.3 Information-processing complexity is useful but insufficient as an SRT bridge

Schulte's positive parameter is best treated as a pressure hypothesis, not adopted as an SRT definition.

The modified-rifle case shows the remaining problem sharply:

```text
more interacting information-bearing state families
may increase causal / computational structure
without obviously increasing the explanatory necessity of semantic content attribution.
```

SRT should therefore not infer:

```text
more internal relations
-> more representation
```

without additional functionality, stability, consumer use and task-relative explanatory tests.

### 15.4 History-bearing recursive state as an SRT-side candidate refinement

The paper itself does not define representation as selection history, and it only lists memory as a future research factor. However, its ant home-vector case exposes a contrast worth testing on the SRT side:

```text
static multi-input integration
versus
history-bearing recursive state updating.
```

A candidate P3 refinement is:

```text
past interaction / selection history
-> retained relational state
-> retained state participates in interpreting / weighting current differences
-> current processing rewrites the retained state
-> later selectability changes.
```

This may help explain why a continuously updated home vector looks more representationally indispensable than a fixed two-input AND mechanism even when both contain multiple causal dependencies.

This is an SRT-generated hypothesis and is not attributed to Schulte.

### 15.5 Representation axis must remain distinct from bearer axis

Schulte explicitly brackets phenomenal consciousness. SRT should preserve an even stricter decomposition:

```text
representation-bearing structure
!= bearer
!= experiencer.
```

A system can instantiate strong content-carrying, consumer-usable or history-sensitive representational organization without thereby satisfying same-bearer consequence return, stake, boundary continuity or phenomenality.

### 15.6 Graded structure does not force graded actuality

Schulte's argument pressures binary structure classifications, but it does not establish that every event-level fact must also be graded.

SRT should therefore preserve the distinction:

```text
graded formation / strength of representation or bearer organization
!=
graded actuality of a particular history-producing event.
```

No change to the canonical Real Choice Moment or `d` follows from this paper.
