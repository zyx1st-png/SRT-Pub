# SourceCard: Frankish on Panpsychism and Depsychologized Consciousness

## 1. One-line summary

Frankish argues that the hard problem and panpsychism become compelling when consciousness is stripped away from psychological function and treated as pure phenomenal feel; this move leaves consciousness scientifically inaccessible, psychologically inert, and weak as a basis for ethical or personal significance.

## 2. Core claims of source

The article distinguishes a psychological conception of mind, where mental states are characterized by roles such as access, control, report, memory, and behavior, from a depsychologized phenomenal conception, where consciousness is treated as pure subjective feel.

The strongest usable claims for SRT are:

1. depsychologizing consciousness makes panpsychism look natural, because once consciousness is detached from psychological organization there is less reason to restrict it to organisms or minded systems;
2. Russellian or constitutive panpsychist strategies can relocate phenomenal properties into intrinsic natures, but this makes them hard to connect to public science;
3. if consciousness is detached from causal, psychological, and access roles, then neuroscience can at best track reports, behavior, and mechanisms, not the depsychologized residue itself;
4. hyper-ubiquitous consciousness cannot by itself carry the ethical or personal significance usually attached to conscious subjects;
5. the practical importance of pain, awareness, and experience depends on their psychological and causal embedding, not on an unreachable intrinsic remainder.
