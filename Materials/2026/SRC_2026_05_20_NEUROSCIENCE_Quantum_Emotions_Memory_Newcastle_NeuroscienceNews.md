# SourceCard: Quantum Emotions / quantum formalism for emotional memory order

## 1. One-line summary

Newcastle University's funded "Quantum Emotions" project will use quantum-cognition mathematics, especially order-sensitive / noncommutative formalism, to model how emotion reshapes temporal order and precision in human memory; this is a promising modeling interface for SRT's memory-stabilization and emotional-salience bridges, but it is not evidence that the brain is physically quantum or that quantum physics explains emotion.

## 2. Core claims of source

- Emotions can reshape and distort memory, especially the perceived temporal order and precision of remembered events.
- Temporal order gives memory structure and helps create a coherent life narrative.
- The project will use mathematical tools from quantum physics because quantum formalism is useful when the order of operations changes the outcome.
- The project sits in the field of Quantum Cognition, not necessarily in physical quantum-neural computation.
- The Newcastle team plans behavioral and neural-system work on how emotions alter memory formation, retrieval, and sequencing.
- The project has £1.2 million UKRI Cross-Council funding and includes workshops / network-building for quantum cognition researchers.
- Neuroscience News adds application claims around ageing, mental health, and eyewitness testimony, but these are prospective, not demonstrated outcomes.

## 4. Limits

- Do not treat this as evidence that the human brain is a biological quantum computer.
- Do not treat quantum formalism as direct quantum physics inside memory unless the future project produces explicit physical evidence.
- Do not treat emotional-memory distortion as direct measurement of SRT `Psi_f`, `d-value`, `T_dir`, salience, arousal, prediction error, or trauma burden.
- Clinical, ageing, psychiatric, and eyewitness-testimony applications are prospective and must remain P4/P5 until validated.
- The Neuroscience News Q&A language is more explanatory and promotional than the university press release; use the Newcastle source for project identity and the SRT guardrail for claim status.

## 5. SRT relevance

Potential B-class interface:

- Temporal-order modeling: useful for SRT memory/life-narrative questions because SRT already treats stabilized order as not merely a set of contents but an ordered relation among selections.
- Noncommutative sequence pressure: quantum-cognition mathematics may provide a formal analogy for cases where `A then B` and `B then A` produce different memory-state outcomes.
- Emotion as ordering perturbation: supports a bridge hypothesis that emotion changes not just intensity of memory but order, precision, retrieval path, and narrative alignment.
- Proxy discipline: emotional memory distortion may be a proxy for anchoring instability, altered `L1 -> L2` sedimentation, or `T_dir` readability changes, but only under stated measurement windows.
- Public guardrail: the phrase "quantum emotions" is high-risk; SRT should translate it as "order-sensitive formal modeling of emotional memory," not quantum mysticism.
