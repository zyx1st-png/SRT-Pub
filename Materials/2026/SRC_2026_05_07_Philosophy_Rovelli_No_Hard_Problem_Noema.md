# SourceCard: Rovelli on the Hard Problem of Consciousness

## 1. One-line summary

Rovelli argues that the "hard problem" arises from a false view-from-nowhere picture of science: first-person and third-person descriptions are different embodied perspectives on natural processes, not evidence for two kinds of reality.

## 2. Core claims of source

The article is a Noema essay by Carlo Rovelli, published on 2026-05-07.

The strongest usable claims for SRT are:

1. consciousness is hard to understand because it is a complex natural phenomenon, not because it is proven to be non-natural;
2. improved natural explanation of the soul, inner self, emotions, or qualia does not make those phenomena unreal;
3. the hard-problem framing often imports dualism by first treating science as an outside, third-person account of an absolute world, then asking how first-person experience could be derived from that picture;
4. knowledge, theory, perception, feeling, and experience are embodied processes inside the world, not views from outside it;
5. first-person and third-person descriptions can be two perspectives on the same process rather than two ontological domains;
6. philosophical zombies are weak as an argument when their indistinguishability already assumes the extra non-physical ingredient the argument is supposed to prove;
7. mental life can be treated as a high-level natural description without being reduced to an illusion or a transcendent substance.

## 5. SRT relevance

SRT already treats the hard problem as a dissolution problem, not a magical construction problem. Rovelli's essay sharpens one hinge:

```text
view-from-nowhere science
  -> science is imagined as outside experience
  -> experience must be derived from something "else"
  -> a metaphysical gap is generated by the setup
```

SRT-compatible compression:

```text
first-person description
  != non-natural residue
third-person description
  != view from outside reality
both are embodied perspectives on processes inside the world
```
