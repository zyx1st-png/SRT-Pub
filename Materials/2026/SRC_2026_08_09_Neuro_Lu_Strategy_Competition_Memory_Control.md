# SourceCard — Lu et al. strategy competition, memory and control

## One-line summary

In a naturalistic pup-search task, female mice transition from a previous-location win-stay strategy toward a learned sound-guided strategy; auditory-cortex signals associated with successful sound use strengthen while medial prefrontal cortex carries last-pup-location information that declines with the old strategy, and the 2025 COSYNE update reports that chemogenetic mPFC silencing accelerates adoption of the sound strategy.

## Verified source anchors

1. Kai Lu, Kelvin T. Wong, Lin N. Zhou, Yike T. Shi, Chengcheng J. Yang, Robert C. Liu. *Instinct to insight: Neural correlates of ethological strategy learning*. bioRxiv, 2023. DOI: `10.1101/2023.09.11.557240`; PMID: `37745495`; PMCID: `PMC10515821`.
2. COSYNE 2025 abstract/poster update: *Instinct vs Insight: Neural Competition Between Prefrontal and Auditory Cortex Constrains Sound Strategy Learning*.

A circulated summary supplied by the author of this repository describes a later *Science Advances* article titled *Neural competition between prefrontal and auditory cortex constrains novel sound strategy learning*. That final journal bibliographic record was not independently recovered during this pass, so final-version-only numerical details do not carry evidential load here.

## Core claims of source

- Mice initially rely on a spatial-memory/win-stay strategy when searching for pups.
- They can learn to use an artificial sound cue that more reliably predicts pup location.
- Auditory cortex is required during learning, and trial-level auditory-cortex activity contains signals related to later correct sound-guided behavior even before overt adoption of the new strategy.
- mPFC encodes the previous pup location, and this representation weakens as win-stay behavior declines.
- The 2025 conference update reports that mPFC silencing accelerates adoption of the sound strategy and supports a competition model between old and new strategies.

## Evidence / method

- Naturalistic pup-search and retrieval task in female mice.
- Chronic electrophysiology in auditory cortex and mPFC.
- Behavioral strategy analysis across learning.
- Chemogenetic manipulation and phenomenological competition modeling reported in the 2025 update.

## Limits

- The 2023 source is a preprint, not a peer-reviewed final article.
- The 2025 COSYNE source is a conference abstract/poster update and does not substitute for a complete final methods/results record.
- The task concerns a specific win-stay memory strategy and a continuously available auditory cue; it cannot establish a general theory of all memory.
- Neural decodability does not by itself establish causal control, consciousness, subjecthood, `d-value`, `Psi_f`, or canonical `L2` identity.
- mPFC must not be identified with `L2`; it is a task-specific candidate implementation component.

## SRT relevance

The strongest SRT-facing increment is not "memory is not representation." The source instead supports a narrower dissociation:

> A past-event representation can be neurally present while its current behavioral control weight changes, and a newly available representation can exist before it gains dominant path efficacy.

This motivates separating:

- representational content;
- candidate accessibility;
- current selection/control weight;
- realized path efficacy;
- longer-term historical write-back.

It therefore pressures any SRT neuroscience prose that treats memory merely as stored content and supports a bridge reading of memory as a historically induced constraint on future selection.
