# SourceCard: Ola Nilsson — *Pinning down the realm of mind*

## 1. One-line summary

Nilsson uses spatially separated duplicates, perfect restoration/time-loop reasoning, and a Big-Crunch-style erasure thought experiment to argue that space is not ontologically primary and that past/present/future reality points toward a non-spatial "realm of mind"; for SRT, the strongest value is not that idealist conclusion but the pressure to separate **actual occurrence, persistent trace, `L_2` sedimentation, bearer-indexed history, and phenomenal experience**.

## 2. Source boundary

This SourceCard is based on the first-party essay supplied in full by the user together with its published Essentia Foundation URL.

The essay is a philosophical argument / narrative, not an empirical paper. Nilsson explicitly presents it as an ontological pointer rather than a formal premise-conclusion proof. It closes a trilogy in which he had previously argued that spatial separation is insufficient to individuate experience and that time may literally loop when a system is perfectly restored.

### 2.1 Text anchors and locator limits

The Essentia Foundation web essay has no stable pagination or numbered section/paragraph scheme in the supplied text. To make load-bearing attribution auditable, this card therefore uses **unique sentence / paragraph-content anchors** rather than treating "full close-read" as a substitute for location.

Use these anchors against the first-party URL / supplied full text:

- **Trilogy / imported-argument anchor:** opening paragraphs beginning with the statement that this essay is the concluding part of *Meta-Survival: On the Incoherence of Localized, Countable Subjectivity* and *The Circle Dance of Personal Identity*; the immediately following retrospective summary is where spatial separation, numerically identical experience, time-loop and subjective-time claims are imported from the prior essays.
- **Space / experience anchor:** paragraph containing the exact sentence **"experience does not require space in order to exist, but space does require experience in order to exist."** This is the clean locator for Nilsson's strongest space-dependence claim.
- **Realm-definition anchor:** paragraph introducing the **"realm of mind"** as explicitly non-spatial and comparing it loosely with Platonic forms, Tononi-style qualia spaces and Michael Levin's Platonic spaces while warning against spatial connotations.
- **Big-Crunch / total-erasure anchor:** paragraph beginning with the thought experiment asking us to imagine the universe ultimately reaching a state in which witnesses, information, evidence and causal remnants of the past are gone; use the immediately following discussion for the claim that what happened nevertheless **has happened**.
- **Restoration / occurrence anchor:** paragraph reconciling the total-erasure argument with the earlier time-loop view by allowing restored/repeated rounds to count as the same time while still insisting that the occurrences happened.
- **Past / present / future ontological-status anchor:** later paragraph asserting that past and present share the same ontological status, with the future included conditionally if it exists, and using this to generalize the non-spatial argument beyond the past.
- **Coordinates anchor:** final paragraph ending with **"for it is we that constitute the coordinates."** This anchors the closing perspectival / constitutive formulation.

Locator limitation: the duplicate and perfect-restoration arguments are **summarized/imported here from Nilsson's prior two essays rather than fully re-derived in this essay**. This SourceCard therefore attributes the retrospective claims to Nilsson but does not treat the present essay alone as the full evidential text for the earlier arguments.

Safe source-level compression:

```text
spatial separation is claimed not to be sufficient for experience individuation
space is claimed to be a retrospective / non-primary construction
perfect restoration is claimed to restore the same time
past events are claimed to remain facts even if all witnesses, records and causal remnants vanish
past / present / future are claimed to share ontological status
these points are used to motivate a non-spatial "realm of mind"
```

These are Nilsson's philosophical claims. They are not independent evidence that idealism, a universal mind, a qualia space, Platonic realm, or non-spatial experiential substrate is true.

## 3. Three thought-experiment pressures

### 3.1 Perfect duplicate pressure

Nilsson's earlier argument, imported into the essay, treats two physically separated but otherwise identical systems as yielding a numerically identical experience.

**Text anchor:** the opening retrospective summary of *Meta-Survival* described in §2.1. This essay reports the earlier conclusion; it does not reproduce the complete prior derivation.

SRT should retain only the pressure:

```text
spatial separation
!= sufficient subject / experience individuation criterion
```

Do **not** import the stronger inference:

```text
qualitatively identical phenomenal character
-> numerically identical experience-token
```

The source does not independently establish that inference.

### 3.2 Perfect restoration / time-loop pressure

Nilsson distinguishes his time-loop claim from the claim that earlier events never happened. A perfectly restored system is said to instantiate the same time even though the prior occurrence remains a fact.

**Text anchors:** the opening retrospective summary of *The Circle Dance of Personal Identity* plus the later restoration/occurrence reconciliation paragraph identified in §2.1.

For SRT this is a direct pressure test against collapsing:

```text
state identity
=
historical identity
```

Current SRT P0-03 instead treats reversal/restoration as a new selection event with its own history.

### 3.3 Big Crunch / total-erasure pressure

Nilsson asks us to imagine a point at which all witnesses, information, records and causal remnants of earlier events are gone. He argues that what happened nevertheless still happened.

**Text anchor:** the Big-Crunch / total-erasure paragraph and its immediately following "has happened" discussion identified in §2.1.

The clean SRT-side question is:

```text
if persistent trace(E) -> 0,
can Occurred(E) remain true?
```

This is the source's strongest useful pressure. It should be used to distinguish occurrence facticity from surviving memory, record, causal efficacy or `L_2` scaffold.

## 4. Main source inference and its burden

Nilsson's central route is approximately:

```text
past event remains real
+ past event has no present spatial location
-> reality is not fundamentally spatial
-> a non-spatial realm is required
-> this is the realm of mind
```

**Text anchors:** the Big-Crunch sequence, the later paragraph generalizing past/present/future ontological status, and the paragraph explicitly naming the non-spatial "realm of mind" (§2.1).

The final step is not established by the prior steps.

SRT must preserve:

```text
non-spatial
!-> mental

space may be non-fundamental
!-> space depends on experience

past fact lacks current spatial presence
!-> past fact is stored in a mental realm
```

Possible alternatives include relational, structural, causal, informational, neutral or other non-spatial ontologies. This SourceCard does not choose among them.

## 5. Ontology / epistemology separation

A key argumentative slide in the essay moves from:

```text
we can understand / represent a bygone scene without its present spatial instantiation
```

into:

```text
the bygone scene therefore belongs to a non-spatial mental realm
```

**Text anchor:** the paragraph arguing that a bygone scene can be understood without spatial specification and using that fact to point "directly" toward the realm of mind.

SRT should separate:

```text
past event facticity
!= current representation of a past event
```

The existence of a present representation does not by itself determine the ontology of the represented past event.

## 6. Temporal-status tension

The essay uses both of the following intuitions:

```text
past no longer exists in current space
```

and

```text
past and present share the same ontological status
```

**Text anchor:** the paragraph that first treats the bygone past as no longer existing in space, followed by the later paragraph explicitly assigning past and present the same ontological status (§2.1).

These pull respectively toward presentist and eternalist/block-like readings unless further distinguished. SRT should not inherit this tension. The useful import is only the occurrence-vs-persistence question.

## 7. SRT-side discrimination generated by the source

The close read supports the following **SRT-side** ladder, not a Nilsson theorem:

```text
actual occurrence
!= persistent trace
!= L2 sedimentation
!= bearer-indexed history
!= phenomenal experiencer
```

Additional guards:

```text
state identity
!= historical identity

qualitative identity
!-> numerical identity

spatial separation
!= bearer individuation criterion

physical localization
!= bearer localization
!= phenomenal locus
```

## 8. Bearer individuation pressure

The duplicate case suggests a stronger SRT candidate than spatial counting:

```text
bearer differentiation
may be audited by
independently closable consequences
+ independently writable histories
+ independently alterable future selectability
+ maintained boundary / closure continuity
```

A test-local phrase for this is **counterfactual consequence separability**:

```text
there exists a possible event whose consequence can change B1's future selectability
without the same change closing on B2, and vice versa
```

This is a P3 SRT diagnostic candidate, not a necessary-and-sufficient theorem and not a phenomenality criterion.

## 9. Personal-identity branching pressure

A perfect-copy branch creates a clean distinction between:

```text
strict numerical identity
```

and

```text
historical continuation / inheritance
```

Two future bearers may inherit the same pre-branch history without thereby being numerically identical to each other. SRT should therefore pressure-test any wording that silently equates continuity, copying, shared memory or state similarity with same-bearer identity.

## 10. Where-question typing

Nilsson's title question hides several non-equivalent questions. SRT-side hardening should distinguish:

```text
WHERE-P: where is a process physically realized?
WHERE-C: where do causal consequences propagate?
WHERE-B: where do consequences close and history write back?
WHERE-S: from which subject-position are relations indexed?
WHERE-E: for which locus, if any, is there phenomenal for-me-ness?
```

Guard:

```text
WHERE-P != WHERE-B != WHERE-E
```

The essay is useful precisely because it shows why one spatial "where" cannot answer all of these.

## 11. What the source does not show

Do not infer:

```text
Nilsson proves idealism
Essentia Foundation's metaphysical idealism is validated by the thought experiment
space is proven to be created by consciousness
spacetime emergence implies mind-first ontology
identical phenomenal content proves one experience-token
bearer differentiation proves two experiencers
past facticity requires a cosmic memory or mental archive
L2 is a permanent world-memory store
SRT = realm-of-mind ontology
```

The strongest source-level sentence behind the third line is anchored in §2.1 at the paragraph containing **"experience does not require space in order to exist, but space does require experience in order to exist."** The SRT guard above deliberately refuses to treat that author claim as independently established by the thought experiments.

## 12. Final evidence verdict

**Assimilation value: very high as a pressure test; low as direct evidence for the essay's idealist conclusion.**

The durable SRT increment is:

> What has occurred, what still leaves a trace, what continues to constrain, what belongs to a bearer, and what is phenomenally experienced are different questions.

The material should enter through a non-canonical philosophy hardening patch and integration hook. No P0/P1 canonical owner should be changed by source fiat.
