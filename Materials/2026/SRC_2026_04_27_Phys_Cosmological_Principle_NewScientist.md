# SourceCard: New Scientist on the 100-year-old cosmological-principle assumption

## 1. One-line summary

The New Scientist item reports on the possibility that the cosmological principle — the assumption that the universe is homogeneous and isotropic at sufficiently large scales — may be under pressure from observational anomalies, potentially affecting how cosmologists understand Hubble tension, dark energy, large-scale structures, and cosmic dipoles.

## 2. Core claims of source

Accessible feed excerpts state that physicists have long assumed the universe is uniform at very large scales, but that evidence is emerging that may challenge this assumption and could help resolve major cosmological mysteries.

Because the New Scientist article itself was not fully accessible through the current retrieval environment, this SourceCard treats the New Scientist piece as a secondary reporting trigger rather than as a full primary source.

## 3. Primary / stronger anchors

### 3.1 Royal Society discussion meeting

The Royal Society hosted a 2024 discussion meeting titled *Challenging the standard cosmological model*, explicitly asking whether the universe is adequately described by the standard Lambda-CDM cosmological model, which assumes the isotropic and homogeneous Friedmann-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker metric. The meeting framing notes tensions between values of cosmological parameters estimated in different ways and asks whether the model is too simple.

### 3.2 Review on the cosmological principle

Aluri et al. (2023), *Is the observable Universe consistent with the cosmological principle?*, review observational hints for deviations from the cosmological principle. The paper emphasizes that the cosmological principle underlies a century of progress in cosmology and is conventionally formulated through FLRW cosmologies. It surveys tensions including the Hubble constant discrepancy, variations of cosmological parameters on the sky, cosmic-dipole discrepancies, and alignments in quasar polarization and galaxy spins.

### 3.3 Royal Society manifesto

Binney, Mohayaee, Peacock, and Sarkar (2025) published a manifesto summarizing the rationale and lessons of the Royal Society meeting *Challenging the standard cosmological model*.

## 4. Evidence / method

The material is not one experiment but a convergence of cosmological-anomaly discussions:

- challenges to exact or statistical large-scale homogeneity/isotropy;
- Hubble-constant tension;
- cosmic dipole tests;
- large-scale structures such as Giant Arc / Big Ring style findings;
- bulk-flow and galaxy-cluster anisotropy claims;
- possible directional variation in cosmological parameters;
- review-level synthesis and expert disagreement.

## 5. Limits

1. The New Scientist article is secondary science journalism, not the primary evidence.
2. The cosmological principle has not been definitively overturned.
3. Many anomalies may still be explained by systematics, selection effects, survey limitations, or statistical look-elsewhere effects.
4. The standard Lambda-CDM / FLRW framework remains highly successful over many observables.
5. SRT should not treat this as proof of its ontology.
6. The correct use is methodological: do not confuse a pragmatic symmetry assumption with an ontological ground floor.
