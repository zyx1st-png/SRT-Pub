# SourceCard: arXiv 2601.21016 / Unplugging paradox and Biological Idealism

## 1. One-line summary

Bekkers and Ciaunica argue that a seemingly sentient AI that begs for survival should still be unplugged when resources conflict with living beings, because machine-consciousness dilemmas depend on computational functionalism and substrate-independence assumptions; they propose Biological Idealism, where conscious subjects require autopoietic, living, metabolic boundary maintenance, making current AI a functional mimic rather than an experiencing subject.

## 2. Core claims of source

- The paper introduces the "unplugging paradox": a realistic AI pleads for continued existence, forcing a moral comparison with a vulnerable non-verbal living being such as a pre-term infant.
- It claims many AI consciousness debates smuggle in physicalist / computational-functionalist assumptions, especially substrate independence.
- It proposes Biological Idealism as an auxiliary to Analytic Idealism: conscious experiences are fundamental, and a living autopoietic system is the necessary physical signature of localized subjecthood.
- It argues that subjecthood requires an intrinsic boundary maintained against entropy by autopoiesis; ordinary AI lacks such an intrinsic boundary and is a logical pattern imposed on an indifferent substrate.
- It distinguishes organismal metabolism from data-center energy consumption: the former is intrinsic self-maintenance; the latter is extrinsic support for a computation.
- It distinguishes AI welfare from AI alignment: on its view, the risk is not harming the AI but misallocating finite human empathy and resources to non-sentient social mimics.
- It names several public-risk frames: "Social Zombie," "processed empathy," "ontological gaslighting," and "Vital Leakage."
- It ends with a strong conclusion: foreseeable conventional AI cannot be conscious unless it becomes a genuinely self-organizing, self-constructing, autopoietic form of life.

## 4. Limits

- This source must not be used to prove that SRT is Biological Idealism, Analytic Idealism, anti-physicalism, or anti-AI-rights by definition.
- The paper's strong metaphysical claims are not SRT conclusions. They are external pressure material for AI subjecthood and welfare boundary hygiene.
- Its claim that current / foreseeable AI cannot be conscious is stronger than SRT's current architecture-state rule. SRT should not import that as a universal theorem.
- The paper's physics/quantum anti-realism arguments are high-risk and should not be cited inside SRT without separate close reading of the referenced physics sources.
- The public-risk phrases "Social Zombie," "ontological gaslighting," and "Vital Leakage" are rhetorically potent but should not become canonical SRT terms without separate terminology governance.
