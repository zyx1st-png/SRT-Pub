## 2. Source-native burden

Ostrom's research rejects a simple market-versus-state governance dichotomy.

The source-native question is not merely whether a rule exists.

It asks how resource conditions, community conditions and rules-in-use shape action situations and outcomes.

Across self-organized common-pool resource systems, important recurring features include:

- boundary rules;
- allocation / choice rules;
- monitoring;
- local sanctions;
- communication / agreements;
- users' ability to participate in rule formation or revision;
- fit between rules and the resource / community context.

## 3. Nepal irrigation evidence

In the Nepal irrigation programme, performance was measured through:

- physical condition of irrigation systems;
- water availability at the tail end;
- agricultural productivity.

The Nobel lecture reports that, controlling for environmental differences, farmer-governed systems performed better on these measures in the studied corpus.

Farmer-managed systems also featured communication, locally developed agreements, monitors and sanctions.

Expanded data later preserved the general result.

This is an empirical programme result, not a universal theorem that local/community governance always outperforms government systems.

## 4. Pressure on GRG X3

The current X3 phrase:

~~~text
externalized generative expectation
~~~

is too representation-centric if it can be satisfied by a written rule alone.

Ostrom pressures a stronger distinction:

~~~text
represented / articulated rule
!=
operative institutional rule-in-use
~~~

A rule becomes consequential through an action situation with:

- participants;
- positions;
- information;
- incentives / payoffs;
- monitoring;
- enforcement;
- revision / collective-choice possibilities;
- resource / material conditions.

## 5. Candidate GRG revision

Provisional split:

~~~text
X3a articulated / encoded expectation
= a rule, protocol, norm or schedule makes a future relation explicit.

X3b operative expectation regime
= that expectation is instantiated in an action situation
  through interfaces / incentives / monitoring / sanctions /
  material constraints / organizational practice
  such that participant possibilities actually change.
~~~

X3a does not entail X3b.

Written rule existence is a marker, not causal sufficiency.

## 6. Maintenance provenance

Ostrom also prevents one universal enforcement story.

Institutional expectations can be maintained through mixtures of:

- participant monitoring;
- local sanctions;
- formal authority;
- norms;
- conflict-resolution arrangements;
- nested / polycentric governance.

Therefore the existing P-TECH / P-LEGAL / P-MKT / P-ORG / P-NORM / P-INFRA / P-MIXED routing remains useful, but must describe the operative mechanism rather than merely label the institution.

## 7. Normativity guard

Ostrom's empirical design principles were not presented as one universal moral blueprint.

Robustness / performance findings do not by themselves establish justice or legitimacy.

For GRG:

~~~text
operative institutional expectation
!= moral legitimacy
~~~
