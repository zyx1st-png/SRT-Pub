# SourceCard — Meyer & Rowan formal structure / decoupling

## 1. Primary source

John W. Meyer & Brian Rowan (1977).

Institutionalized Organizations: Formal Structure as Myth and Ceremony.

American Journal of Sociology 83(2):340–363.

DOI 10.1086/226550

Publisher route:

https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/226550

## 2. Source-native claim

The paper argues that formal organizational structures can arise partly because organizations incorporate institutionalized rules and myths that provide legitimacy, resources and stability.

Its load-bearing pressure for GRG is:

~~~text
formal structure
!=
ongoing organizational activity
~~~

The source explicitly allows formal structures to be decoupled from ongoing activities.

## 3. Pressure on X3

This is a direct negative control for:

~~~text
X3a articulated / encoded expectation
-> therefore X3b operative expectation regime
~~~

That inference is invalid.

A formal structure may exist and be symbolically important while being weakly coupled or decoupled from actual work practices.

## 4. GRG admission implication

For X3b, evidence must address:

- actual implementation;
- action-situation consequences;
- monitoring / interface / incentive / sanction / workflow;
- changed participant possibilities;
- operational coupling.

Formal adoption alone is insufficient.

## 5. Important nuance

Decoupled formal structures can still affect legitimacy, resource access, stability or external signaling.

Therefore:

~~~text
X3b failure in the intended operational channel
!= no causal consequence whatsoever
~~~

The causal channel must be typed.

## 6. Normativity guard

Formal conformity or institutional legitimacy does not establish moral legitimacy.
