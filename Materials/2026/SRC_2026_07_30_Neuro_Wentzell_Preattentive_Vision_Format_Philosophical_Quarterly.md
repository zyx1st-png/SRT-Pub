# SourceCard: What Is the Format of Pre-Attentive Vision?

## 1. One-line summary

Wentzell argues that pre-attentive vision is better characterized by a picture-like, holistically bound format than by multiple separate feature maps: rapid scene-gist perception supplies positive pressure for holistic binding, while illusory conjunctions may arise in visual short-term memory and conjunction-search costs may reflect grouping-and-rejection strategy rather than the intrinsic format of early vision.

## 2. Core source claims

Usable source-level claims:

1. Pre-attentive vision is the stage of visual processing after retinal stimulation but before the initial deployment of focused visual attention.
2. Pop-out search is treated as evidence that pre-attentive processing is parallel and high-capacity because single-feature target detection is relatively insensitive to display set size.
3. The pictorial or iconic view characterizes early visual representation through a parts principle and a holism principle: representational parts correspond to scene parts, and multiple properties are represented together rather than by separate symbols.
4. The cartographic view instead proposes multiple spatially organized feature maps, with different feature dimensions represented independently.
5. Illusory conjunctions are often used to support feature maps because subjects report features that were present while misbinding which color, shape, or orientation belonged together; spatial proximity raises the probability of misbinding.
6. Conjunction search is often used to support feature maps because finding a target defined by a conjunction of features is slower and more set-size-sensitive than finding a target defined by one unique feature.
7. Gist perception pressures the cartographic view because subjects can categorize complex natural scenes rapidly and accurately while exploiting information across spatial layout, orientation, spatial frequency, luminance, and often diagnostic color.
8. Wentzell argues that the efficiency benefit from multiple feature dimensions is more naturally explained by a holistically bound early format than by costly post hoc combination of separate feature maps.
9. Illusory-conjunction paradigms require retention, masking, divided attention, and delayed report, so the observed error may arise when visual short-term memory preserves features independently but loses their binding relations.
10. Conjunction-search asymmetries can be explained by search strategy: homogeneous distractors can be grouped and rejected in large batches, whereas heterogeneous distractors require several separate grouping-and-rejection operations.
11. Contemporary feature-integration theories can allow noisy pre-attentive conjunctions, but Wentzell argues that this creates pressure to explain why those conjunctions are unreliable in conjunction tasks yet sufficiently reliable for fast and accurate gist categorization.
12. The article concludes that the balance of reasons favors a picture-like format, while acknowledging that plural-format accounts remain a live alternative.

## 3. Evidence and method

This is a philosophy-of-cognitive-science paper, not a new experiment.

Its method is comparative explanatory inference across established empirical literatures:

- visual pop-out and set-size effects;
- feature search and conjunction search;
- illusory conjunction paradigms;
- scene-gist categorization under very brief exposure and masking;
- visual short-term memory feature-recall results;
- distractor-homogeneity manipulations;
- computational models of global scene properties and spatial envelopes.

The article's principal empirical anchors include Treisman-style feature-integration work, rapid gist studies, scene-color diagnostics, VSTM feature-store findings, and Duncan-Humphreys grouping/search results. The manuscript does not provide a new dataset, preregistration, causal intervention, or quantitative model comparison.

## 4. Main limits

1. Rapid use of multiple feature dimensions does not by itself establish a realistic-photograph-like internal format.
2. Scene-level gist reliability and object-level feature-binding reliability operate at different spatial and task scales; their contrast need not be contradictory.
3. The memorial-error account shows underdetermination but does not prove that most illusory conjunctions originate in VSTM rather than perception.
4. Delayed verbal report, immediate eye movement, online pointing, grasping, and no-report neural measures may access different stages of the perception-memory-report chain.
5. Display duration is not identical to total processing duration; 26 ms stimulus presentation does not mean categorization is completed in 26 ms.
6. Modern visual-search theories are more heterogeneous than a simple classic feature-map-versus-picture dichotomy.
7. A task may recruit different representational structures at different resolutions; the paper's framing may overstate the need for one fixed format for all pre-attentive functions.
8. The paper is forthcoming and the supplied manuscript explicitly requests citation of the eventual published version; bibliographic metadata and wording should be refreshed when the journal version appears.
9. The article cannot establish SRT ontology, `L_0`, `L_1`, `L_2`, `G_hat_theta`, `d-value`, `Psi_f`, subjecthood, or consciousness.

## 5. SRT relevance

The strongest SRT interface is not "pictorialism proves SRT." It is a staged-selection correction:

```text
pre-attentive
  != pre-selection

before focused object attention
  can still contain
wide-field, low-resolution, task-conditioned selection
```

A bounded SRT-facing chain is:

```text
L0_visual accessibility
  -> coarse scene-level L1 anchoring / gist
  -> focused high-resolution object reselection and binding
  -> VSTM retention proxy
  -> report reconstruction
```

This chain yields four useful distinctions:

```text
global scene coherence != complete local object binding
initial L1 anchoring != VSTM storage format
VSTM content != final report
report error != proof of initial perceptual error
```

The source also supports a resolution-sensitive reading of `G_hat_theta`:

```text
same visual field + different task / scale / resolution
  -> different effective representational geometry
```

Picture-like and map-like structures can therefore be treated as task- and scale-conditioned projection formats rather than mutually exclusive ontological substances.

## 6. Bidirectional gain card

### New interface

- `pre-attentive != pre-selection` as a terminology guardrail.
- coarse scene anchoring before focused object individuation and feature confirmation.
- a staged `L1 perception -> VSTM proxy -> report` interface for locating binding errors.
- scene-level versus object-level binding as a scale distinction.
- task-conditioned representational geometry instead of a single fixed early-vision format.
- gist as a candidate empirical window on wide-field, high-capacity, low-resolution selection.

### Reverse correction to SRT

- Do not call pre-attentive vision an unselected `L_0` state.
- Do not identify iconicity with `L_1` by definition.
- Do not identify feature maps with `L_0` or focused attention with the whole of `G_hat_theta`.
- Do not claim that gist establishes a complete inner photograph or full local binding.
- Do not infer original phenomenology directly from delayed report errors.
- Do not equate reaction time, set-size slope, display duration, working-memory load, or search difficulty with `Psi_f` without a typed operational bridge.
- Do not infer `d-value` from stimulus salience or task relevance alone.

### Strengthened SRT content

- selection can be wide-field and parallel before it becomes focal and serial;
- a coarse but stable scene-level `L_1` can precede determinate local object binding;
- later stabilization interfaces can transform format and introduce error;
- selection as occlusion predicts recoverable, weakly accessible detail rather than absolute deletion;
- representational format may vary with `theta`, resolution `rho`, task direction, and available resources.

### SRT contribution back to the source

SRT can replace the paper's binary format question with a staged and scale-typed question:

```text
Under which operator, task, resolution, cost, and stabilization conditions
is visual structure expressed as a global field, a spatial index,
a bound object, a memory proxy, or a reportable judgment?
```

A candidate bridge scaffold is:

```text
L0_visual
  --G_theta(global, rho_coarse)--> L1_scene/gist
  --G_theta(focal, rho_fine)----> L1_object/binding
  --retention-------------------> M_VSTM
  --reconstruction-------------> R_report
```

This does not require that each stage has one exclusive physical code. It requires that empirical claims declare which stage, scale, task, and output are being measured.

### Residual pressure

- Standard predictive-processing, recurrent-grouping, object-file, or biased-competition accounts may explain the same dissociations without SRT-specific variables.
- The distinction between coarse and fine selection risks redescribing ordinary hierarchical vision unless `d-value`, `Psi_f`, occlusion, and write-back add differential predictions.
- The memorial-error interpretation needs direct online-versus-delayed dissociation tests.
- SRT must not use a philosophy paper's explanatory preference as empirical confirmation of selection realism.
