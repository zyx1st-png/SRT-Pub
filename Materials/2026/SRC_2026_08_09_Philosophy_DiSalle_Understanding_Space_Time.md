# SourceCard: Robert DiSalle, *Understanding Space-Time* (2006)

## 1. One-line summary

DiSalle reconstructs the development from Newton to Einstein as a history in which space-time principles do more than describe already-defined quantities: they help constitute the empirical meaning of motion, force, simultaneity, inertia, geometry, and curvature, while remaining revisable when contingent physical facts expose tensions in the framework; for SRT the strongest use is methodological, not ontological.

## 3. Core claims of the source

The following are source-level claims or reconstructions, not SRT claims.

1. The philosophical development of space-time physics is not well captured by a simple absolute-versus-relational dispute.
2. Newton's definitions of space, time, and motion are part of the conceptual framework that makes dynamics empirically intelligible; Newton's successful theory requires inertial structure and absolute acceleration/rotation more clearly than it requires absolute rest or absolute velocity.
3. Some principles that appear to be ordinary laws also help define the quantities to which empirical reasoning is applied; their role is therefore partly constitutive or interpretive.
4. Kant correctly recognized a special framework role for space-time principles but wrongly treated the Newtonian/Euclidean framework as unrevisable and necessary for all possible physical experience.
5. Helmholtz and nineteenth-century geometry show that spatial intuition depends on contingent physical facts and on repeatable transformations, rigid motion, and measurement practice.
6. Poincare's "definitions in disguise" insight identifies a real constitutive role for some principles, but unrestricted conventionalism understates how empirical structure constrains admissible frameworks.
7. Einstein's analysis of simultaneity and free fall is a conceptual transformation driven by tensions between inherited definitions and contingent physical knowledge, not merely an arbitrary stipulation of new coordinates.
8. Special relativity replaces frame-dependent space/time quantities with deeper invariant space-time structure; general relativity further localizes inertial structure and turns geometry itself into a dynamical empirical field.
9. Scientific progress in this history is non-cumulative but rationally reconstructible as an enlargement of perspective: older concepts survive as local or limiting structures within broader frameworks.
10. Fundamental physical principles are not an uninterpreted formal calculus plus a later mapping to experience; in important cases the principles themselves function as principles of interpretation.
11. Space-time physics can, in a strong sense, be understood as its own theory of measurement: it identifies which physical processes count as measurements of fundamental dynamical and geometrical quantities.

## 4. Evidence / method

DiSalle's method is historical-philosophical reconstruction using primary scientific and philosophical texts from Newton, Kant, Helmholtz, Poincare, Einstein, Minkowski, Weyl, Eddington, and later foundations literature.

The book's evidential contribution is therefore:

- close conceptual reconstruction;
- historical comparison of changing measurement and interpretation practices;
- analysis of how physical principles acquire empirical meaning;
- argument against both fixed Kantian apriorism and unrestricted conventionalism;
- a rational reconstruction of conceptual change across Newtonian, special-relativistic, and general-relativistic frameworks.

It is not an experimental or mathematical proof of a selection-first ontology.

## 5. Limits

1. Constitutive interpretation does **not** imply ontological production.
2. Framework dependence does **not** imply observer-created reality or subjectivism.
3. Measurement participating in the definition of a physical magnitude does **not** imply that measurement creates the underlying event.
4. Relativity of coordinates or simultaneity does **not** imply absence of objective structure; the book repeatedly emphasizes invariant and world-structural constraints.
5. The book does not identify physical measurement with SRT `selection`, `G_hat_theta`, `L_0/L_1/L_2`, `d`, `Psi_f`, or `T_dir`.
6. DiSalle's historical argument cannot be used as empirical validation of `selection precedes stable existence`.
7. "Constitutive" must not be silently upgraded to "canonical SRT ontology"; its SRT use remains bridge/methodological unless separately hardened.

## 6. SRT relevance: surviving interfaces

### 6.1 Constitutive but revisable framework

A mature theory may contain principles that are required to identify and measure its own target quantities, while those principles remain revisable when their inherited application rules become systematically incompatible with new empirical structure.

SRT-safe use:

```text
constitutive role
!= eternal necessity
!= arbitrary convention
```

This is a useful governance pattern for SRT's own bridge and measurement concepts.

### 6.2 Interpretation is internal to theory

A formal symbol is not empirically grounded merely because a later paragraph says what it "corresponds" to. The interpretation must be carried by an operational and inferential practice that states what evidence licenses identification of the quantity or event.

SRT-safe use:

> A selection formalism needs a theory of selection measurement/identification, not only an ontology and a post-hoc mapping table.

### 6.3 Redescription versus structural change

Coordinate changes and perspectival re-expression can leave invariant structure untouched. This supports a strong negative control for selection-event claims:

```text
representation change
!= path change
```

A proposed SRT selection event should not be upgraded merely because labels, coordinates, model outputs, or descriptive frames differ.

### 6.4 Perspective enlargement

A stronger framework earns explanatory value when it can recover why an older framework worked in a bounded domain while locating the older framework's hidden overextension.

SRT-safe use:

> SRT should aim to show when existing control, inference, agency, or decision frameworks are locally sufficient and exactly where an additional selection distinction becomes necessary.

### 6.5 Objectification under constraint

The history of physical geometry provides a disciplined middle path between pre-given objects and arbitrary construction. Measurement standards, transformations, and invariants participate in stabilizing physical identity, but the world constrains which constructions remain coherent across procedures.

This is useful for the book's objectification line, with a strict guard against reading it as proof of L0 ontology.

## 7. Reverse correction / pressure on SRT

The source pressures SRT against six recurring overstatements:

1. mathematical structure first, empirical interpretation later;
2. constitutive necessity therefore independent substance;
3. observer-relative description therefore observer-created reality;
4. measurement therefore ontological selection;
5. local explanatory success therefore global ontological identity;
6. a new framework is superior merely because it sounds more metaphysically fundamental.
