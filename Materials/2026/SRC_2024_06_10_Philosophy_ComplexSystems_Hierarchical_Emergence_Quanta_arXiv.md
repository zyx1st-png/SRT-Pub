# SourceCard: Quanta / Hierarchical emergence and computational closure

## 1. One-line summary

Quanta reports on Rosas et al.'s arXiv preprint, which frames emergence as a hierarchy of macroscopic processes that can be informationally, causally, and computationally self-contained relative to lower-level details.

## 2. Core claims of source

- The Quanta feature presents emergence as a problem of when large-scale descriptions are not merely convenient summaries, but self-contained levels with their own predictive, interventional, and computational organization.
- The primary arXiv anchor proposes three closure criteria:
  - informational closure: lower-level detail does not improve prediction of macro-level dynamics;
  - causal/interventional closure: lower-level detail does not improve reliable macro-level control;
  - computational closure: macro-level causal states can be organized as nested coarse-grained computational processes.
- The paper uses computational mechanics, automata theory, epsilon-machine style causal states, and strong lumpability to formalize hierarchical emergent levels.
- Case studies include invariant quantities, Ehrenfest diffusion, Ising/Glauber dynamics, modular random walks, agent-based simulation, and memory-retrieval / attractor dynamics.
- The public article also connects the framework to earlier causal-emergence work by Hoel, Tononi, Albantakis, and Comolatti, while noting live disagreement about whether macro-level causation should be read as real causal autonomy or as a useful invented degree of freedom.

## 4. Limits

- The Quanta article is not a primary scientific source.
- The arXiv paper is a preprint and should not be treated as settled empirical evidence.
- "Software in nature" is an analogy and formal modeling stance; it must not be imported as an SRT ontology.
- Macro-level closure does not prove free will, consciousness, subjecthood, agency, `d`, `Psi_f`, `T_dir`, or `L_2`.
- Strong lumpability / causal-state nesting is a candidate formal interface for certain emergence claims, not a general proof that all wholes exert extra forces on parts.
- The framework's AI and neuroscience examples remain model / proxy domains unless specific empirical systems are separately reviewed.

## 5. SRT relevance

Potential B-class interface:

- Emergence hygiene: this material supports SRT's existing rule that "emergence" must be unpacked into a mechanism: lower-level states, coarse-graining rule, transition or order parameter, stabilized macro-pattern, and implementation channel.
- `L_2` constraint interface: informational / causal / computational closure can serve as an external formal vocabulary for when a stabilized macro-pattern becomes a useful constraint surface for future trajectories.
- Downward-causation guardrail: the material helps phrase SRT's `L_2` constraint as macro-level predictive/interventional sufficiency under specified coarse-graining, not as a new force added to microphysics.
- Cross-domain caution: life, brain, AI, and social systems may involve leaky or partial closure; SRT should preserve leakage, boundary conditions, and consequence-return instead of declaring clean macro autonomy too early.
