# SourceCard: Brain-Body Synchrony in Typical and Disordered Consciousness

## 1. One-line summary

Young, Ericson, and Schooler review evidence that cardiac, respiratory, and gastric rhythms interact recurrently with neural dynamics and can modulate perception, decision processes, interoception, affect, bodily self-consciousness, and consciousness-relevant signals; they further conjecture that brain-body coupling has a non-monotonic healthy range and may sometimes provide a shared temporal scaffold for distributed cognition.

## 2. Core claims of the source

Source-supported claims that can be used safely:

1. The review argues against treating consciousness as a brain-only phenomenon and synthesizes recurrent brain-body interactions involving cardiac, respiratory, and gastric rhythms.
2. Brain-body coupling can be analyzed through phase, frequency, amplitude, mutual information, transfer entropy, Granger-style directionality, and cross-frequency synchronization; these are distinct measurement families rather than one universal synchrony scalar.
3. Cardiac phase and heartbeat-evoked neural responses are associated with perceptual, affective, interoceptive, and self-related processing.
4. Respiratory phase can modulate sensory acquisition, voluntary action timing, and decision processes. The reviewed Brændholt et al. (2025) result is especially relevant because inspiration changed evidence-accumulation parameters rather than merely overt response timing.
5. Gastric rhythms show coupling with distributed brain activity and can participate in brain-gut coordination relevant to affective and interoceptive function.
6. Network-physiology approaches permit analysis beyond pairwise coupling. The review cites provisional brain-heart-gut triadic multidirectional coupling across slow and infra-slow timescales.
7. In post-comatose patients, mid-to-late heartbeat-evoked potential amplitude was reported to predict consciousness better than non-heartbeat-locked EEG; later work using heartbeat-evoked perturbational complexity was predictive of emergence from minimally conscious versus persistent unconscious states after brain injury.
8. Across several psychiatric or stress-related contexts, altered brain-body coupling can occur independently of simple changes in the underlying bodily rhythm magnitude.
9. The authors propose an ancillary conjecture that there may be an optimal range of brain-body coupling: excessive coupling could overweight transient bodily perturbations, while insufficient coupling could weaken interoceptive precision and bodily self-reference.
10. The authors also conjecture that increased coupling under demand or stress may sometimes use bodily rhythms as a temporal scaffold for coordinating distributed cognitive systems; they explicitly leave open whether such effects primarily reflect interoceptive awareness, coordination, or both.
11. The review extends discussion to interpersonal synchrony and suggests that nested organismal and interpersonal coupling may support a biopsychosocial account of embodiment.
12. The source does not establish that synchrony itself is consciousness, that greater synchrony is better, that a dyad constitutes a new subject, or that any physiological coupling measure is identical to an SRT canonical variable.

## 3. Evidence and method

- Article type: peer-reviewed Review Article.
- Scope: cardiac, respiratory, gastric, neural, electrovascular, interoceptive, affective, self-related, clinical-consciousness, psychiatric, and interpersonal literatures.
- Main evidence classes synthesized:
  - heartbeat-evoked potentials and heartbeat-locked neural dynamics;
  - respiratory phase effects on perception, voluntary action, and evidence accumulation;
  - gastric-brain phase and amplitude coupling;
  - cross-frequency and network-physiology measures;
  - disorders of consciousness and psychiatric/affective associations;
  - interpersonal synchrony literature.
- Important consciousness-facing evidence:
  - post-coma HEP amplitude prediction;
  - heartbeat-evoked perturbational complexity and outcome discrimination.
- Important selection-facing evidence:
  - respiratory phase effects on decision boundary and evidence-accumulation starting point.

Evidence boundary:

This paper is a review, not a single preregistered causal experiment. Many consciousness claims are approached through consciousness-related cognitive and affective functions rather than direct manipulation of consciousness itself. The optimal-coupling-window and shared-temporal-scaffold proposals are explicitly conjectural syntheses.

## 4. Main limits

1. The paper combines heterogeneous methods, organs, timescales, populations, and clinical conditions; a common term such as `synchrony` does not guarantee one shared mechanism.
2. Much of the evidence is correlational or phase-conditional. Coupling can be a marker, enabling condition, compensatory response, consequence, or common-cause correlate rather than a constitutive mechanism.
3. The review does not establish a universal scalar of brain-body coupling.
4. The proposed optimal coupling range is an ancillary conjecture, not a demonstrated universal law.
5. Stress-related coupling increases do not by themselves establish adaptive coordination; stronger coupling can coexist with distress or pathology.
6. HEP and heartbeat-locked complexity can carry consciousness-relevant information without proving that heartbeat coupling constitutes consciousness.
7. Pairwise or triadic statistical dependence does not identify a central selector or prove causal direction.
8. Interpersonal synchrony does not establish collective consciousness or a new bearer unit.
9. The paper does not measure canonical `d`, `Psi_f`, `T_dir`, `L_2`, or `G_hat_theta`.
10. The paper does not justify reviving `selective resynchronization` as a cross-domain SRT construct; physiological synchrony remains a domain-specific measurable mechanism.

## 5. SRT relevance

The stable increment is a previously under-explicit implementation layer between accessible candidates and manifest anchoring: **momentary selection eligibility**.

A safe bridge is:

```text
L0_accessible candidates
-> continuously modulated momentary eligibility
-> competition / gain / gating / stabilization
-> L1 anchoring
```

The review supports investigating whether bodily phase changes the probability that a candidate can enter or win a selection process at a given time. It does not support treating bodily rhythms as `G_hat_theta` itself.

A second stable increment is the distinction between bodily information and bodily coordination:

```text
body as represented content
!=
body as temporal constraint on distributed coordination
```

This permits bodily rhythms to matter even when their primary role is not to become an explicit conscious content.

A third increment is a guardrail:

```text
synchrony
!= coordination quality
!= selection
!= d-value
!= consciousness
!= subjecthood
```

## 6. Bidirectional gain card

### New interface

- momentary selection eligibility as a P3 implementation bridge;
- phase-dependent gating as a candidate component of neural `G_hat_theta` implementation;
- bodily rhythms as candidate shared temporal constraints rather than only interoceptive content;
- explicit separation of selection eligibility from later plasticity eligibility / `L1 -> L2` write-back;
- adaptive coupling flexibility as a stronger hypothesis than maximum synchrony.

### Reverse correction to SRT

- Do not read `L0 -> L1` as requiring a single instantaneous neural collapse; discrete anchoring can emerge from continuously changing constraints.
- Do not treat `G_hat_theta` as a hidden anatomical chooser; distributed brain-body dynamics can implement parts of effective selection without a fixed center.
- Do not revive the failed cross-domain `selective resynchronization` construct from physiological synchrony evidence.
- Do not identify coupling magnitude with `d`, `Psi_f`, health, integration quality, consciousness, or subjecthood.
- Do not infer a collective subject from interpersonal synchrony without bearer-unit, consequence-return, history, and future-selectability evidence.

### Strengthened SRT content

- `L0_accessible` can remain broad while momentary eligibility is continuously modulated before anchoring.
- `d` can be treated as stake-coupled concern while interoceptive coupling remains only a possible implementation channel through which stake changes neural selection.
- `Psi_f` interpretation should track load, budget, recovery, and future capacity rather than synchrony magnitude alone.
- The organism becomes a serious candidate bearer unit because consciousness-relevant organization can include body-referenced dynamics, while bearer-unit status still requires separate evidence.

### SRT contribution back to the source

SRT can add a differential decomposition:

```text
coupling
-> coordination
-> candidate-specific eligibility modulation
-> anchoring
-> consequence return / recovery
-> future transition accessibility
```

This makes it possible to ask whether identical coupling magnitude has different consequences depending on stake, history, recovery, or future selectability.

### Residual pressure

If arousal, interoceptive precision, predictive-processing variables, ordinary decision models, and network-physiology measures fully explain phase-dependent effects, recovery, and pathology without added predictive value from stake-sensitive eligibility or future-selectability variables, the SRT bridge should be narrowed to a descriptive implementation vocabulary.
