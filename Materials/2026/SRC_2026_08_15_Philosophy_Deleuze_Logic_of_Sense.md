# SourceCard: Gilles Deleuze, *The Logic of Sense*

## 1. One-line summary

Deleuze develops an event/sense philosophy in which incorporeal events, problematic structures, singularities, heterogeneous series and surface organization are treated as prior to many stabilized forms of proposition, individual and person; the book also distinguishes static genesis from dynamic genesis and repeatedly argues that generated structures must not be presupposed as the conditions of their own genesis.

This SourceCard is based on a close read of the **main text, Series 1–34**. The appendices were only touched at the opening and are not used as positive evidence in the associated writeback audit.

This is a philosophical monograph. Its uses of Stoicism, psychoanalysis, linguistics, mathematics, biology and psychiatry are part of Deleuze's conceptual architecture and intellectual context; they do **not** by themselves provide modern empirical validation of SRT, neuroscience, developmental psychology or clinical mechanisms.

## 3. Main-text architecture

The main text can be compressed into four linked movements.

### 3.1 Event / sense / surface

Early series distinguish bodies and states of affairs from incorporeal events/effects, then distinguish denotation, manifestation and signification from a fourth dimension, sense.

Compact structure:

```text
bodies / states of affairs
!= event / sense

proposition
!= denotation alone
!= manifestation alone
!= signification alone
```

Sense is treated as both what a proposition expresses and what is attributed to a state of affairs, without being identical to either proposition or body.

### 3.2 Problem / singularity / series

Middle series develop heterogeneous series, singularities, problematic structures, aleatory displacement, compossibility/incompossibility and the distinction between a problem and the propositions that solve it.

Stable source-side pressure:

```text
problem
!= incomplete solution

problem determination
can generate
the domain of possible solutions
```

This is especially relevant as a pressure against preformed option-space models.

### 3.3 Static genesis

The static-genesis series explain how, from pre-individual singularities and convergent/divergent series, Deleuze derives world, individual, person and logical organization.

Important source-side distinction:

```text
pre-individual differentiation
!= undifferentiated abyss
!= already-complete individual
```

The analysis also separates individual continuity from person-level identity and treats common-sense identity as downstream rather than primitive.

### 3.4 Dynamic genesis

Later series ask how bodily depth, voice, sexuality, phantasm and thought produce the surface on which event/sense/language become possible.

The useful abstract pressure is:

```text
lower-level material organization
-> generated interface / surface
-> new organizational regime
```

but the specific Freud / Klein / Lacan developmental and psychopathological sequence must remain philosophical/psychoanalytic provenance unless independently supported by contemporary empirical evidence.

## 4. Stable Deleuze-side claims relevant to SRT comparison

The main-text close read supports the following as Deleuze-side philosophical claims:

1. event and sense should not be reduced to bodily state or proposition;
2. pre-individual does not mean completely undifferentiated;
3. singularities and problematic structures may precede individuated object/person forms;
4. problem-space is not reducible to an already enumerated answer-space;
5. stable identity can be a generated organization rather than a primitive substance;
6. heterogeneous series can relate through divergence/displacement rather than prior sameness;
7. a surface/interface can be generated and can support a new regime of organization;
8. structural stability should not be confused with terminal immobility;
9. an occurrence and the event/sense extracted from it are analytically distinct in Deleuze's system;
10. counter-actualization is not the physical erasure of what occurred;
11. Aion and Chronos are distinct temporal constructions within Deleuze's event ontology;
12. the condition of genesis must not simply reproduce the form of the structure it is supposed to generate.

