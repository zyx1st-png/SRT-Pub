# SourceCard: Community First Theory and NTIC

## 1. One-line summary

Ikegami, Kojima, and Kashiwagi argue that collective organization can generate individual dynamical identity, using NTIC on tracked *Tetrahymena* populations to locate a transient embedded-individuation window.

## 2. Core claims of source

The article introduces Community First Theory: individuality is not assumed as a prior property of isolated components; it can arise from collective organization.

The usable claims for SRT are:

1. individual dynamical identity may be generated by collective relations rather than presupposed before them;
2. NTIC can compare self-predictive structure with prediction conditioned on collective context;
3. information closure should not mean isolation in collective biological systems;
4. `NTIC ~= 0` with positive collective coupling is a candidate marker of embedded autonomy;
5. positive NTIC may reflect redundancy between self- and collective-predictive channels, not necessarily "more agency";
6. information-closed cells in the reported *Tetrahymena* data diverged more from parental kinetic-energy phenotype than coupled cells;
7. the method is proposed as substrate-independent, but the empirical support is currently narrow.
