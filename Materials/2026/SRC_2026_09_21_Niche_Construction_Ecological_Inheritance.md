# SourceCard — niche construction and ecological inheritance

## 1. Primary source

Laland, K., Matthews, B. & Feldman, M. W. (2016).

An introduction to niche construction theory.

Evolutionary Ecology 30:191–202.

DOI 10.1007/s10682-016-9821-z

Public full text:

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4922671/

## 2. Source-native definition

Niche construction concerns organisms modifying their own or other organisms' evolutionary niches.

The source explicitly distinguishes:

~~~text
environmental modification
from
modification that affects selection pressures
from
demonstrated evolutionary response.
~~~

Operational criteria summarized in the source:

1. organisms significantly modify environmental conditions;
2. organism-mediated modifications influence selection pressures on recipient organisms;
3. a stronger evolution-by-niche-construction claim additionally requires an evolutionary response caused by the modification.

## 3. Ecological inheritance

Environmental states modified by prior organisms can persist and be experienced by descendants.

The source calls this ecological inheritance.

This matters because:

~~~text
constructor action
-> modified environment
-> later organisms / descendants encounter changed conditions
~~~

The modified environment can outlast the individual constructor.

## 3.1 Theoretical-status guard

The broader status of niche construction as a distinct evolutionary process is contested within evolutionary theory.

GRG does not adjudicate that debate here.

This SourceCard uses the source-native causal decomposition as pressure:

~~~text
environment modification
-> altered selection pressures
-> possible ecological inheritance / evolutionary response
~~~

without assuming that niche-construction theory has exclusive explanatory ownership over evolution.

## 4. Pressure on GRG X4

The current broad X4 candidate:

~~~text
present action reconstructs future option conditions
~~~

is too permissive.

Almost any long-run consequence could satisfy it.

Niche construction forces a stronger causal decomposition.

## 5. Candidate GRG revision

Provisional split:

~~~text
X4a environment / option-field reconstruction
= current activity materially changes later environmental or action conditions.

X4b recursive selective feedback
= the modified conditions alter selection / viability / transition pressures
  on later organisms / agents / positions.

X4c inherited reconstructed condition
= the modified condition persists or is reproduced across a later cycle /
  generation so that later participants inherit a changed generative field.
~~~

Dependencies:

~~~text
X4c normally presupposes enough X4a persistence;
X4b requires a specified feedback burden;
X4a alone does not establish selection feedback or inheritance.
~~~

## 6. False-positive guards

Forbidden shortcut:

~~~text
long-term effect
-> X4b
~~~

Also forbidden:

~~~text
ecosystem engineering
-> evolution by niche construction
~~~

without evidence that the environmental modification changes selection pressures, and for the stronger evolutionary claim, produces an evolutionary response.

## 7. Relation to beaver ecosystem engineering

Beaver dams are a useful source-native example of large environmental modification.

But the ecological engineering fact and the evolutionary niche-construction claim must remain separated.

A beaver case can strongly pay X4a while X4b/X4c require additional evidence.

## 8. Normativity guard

Biological niche construction has no built-in moral direction.

Successful environmental modification for an organism does not imply ecosystem-wide goodness or human-policy desirability.
