# SourceCard: Heil on Emergence as Bad Explanation

## 1. One-line summary

Heil argues that "emergence" often functions as a label for an explanatory gap rather than a mechanism, and that appeals to downward causation frequently confuse organized part-level interaction with an additional whole-level force.

## 2. Core claims of source

The article targets uses of emergence in life, particle physics, and consciousness debates, while setting consciousness mostly aside.

The strongest usable claims for SRT are:

1. weak emergence is often defined by failure of prediction or derivation from lower-level descriptions;
2. strong emergence adds the stronger metaphysical thought that genuinely new entities, properties, or powers appear over and above the organized lower-level base;
3. using an explanatory gap as evidence for an ontological gap risks turning ignorance into ontology;
4. many macro-level features are better treated as resultant properties of dynamically organized parts, not as extra additions to the cosmos;
5. downward-causation language is dangerous when it implies that a whole acts on its parts as an extra force rather than through the organized interactions that constitute the whole.
