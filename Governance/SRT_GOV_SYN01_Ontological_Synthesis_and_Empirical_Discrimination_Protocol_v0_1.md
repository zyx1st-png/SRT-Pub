# GOV-SYN01 — Ontological Synthesis and Empirical Discrimination Dual-Track Protocol v0.1

## 0. Role

SRT is allowed to make two different kinds of theoretical contribution. They must not be collapsed into one score.

```text
Track O — Ontological Synthesis
Track D — Discriminating / Empirical Increment
```

The central correction is:

```text
no unique SRT empirical increment
!= no theoretical value
!= failed bridge
```

and symmetrically:

```text
successful cross-domain synthesis
!= empirical validation of SRT ontology
!= permission to promote a bridge to P0/P1
```

This protocol governs how SRT reads, absorbs, compares and writes back external theories and evidence. It does not define SRT ontology.

---

## 1. Why the dual track is necessary

A mature scientific field may already contain the local mechanism SRT needs.

Examples include:

- selection and retention in evolutionary biology;
- damage, repair, senescence, clonal replacement and resilience in aging biology;
- reachability and switching constraints in control theory;
- plasticity, gating and historical state in neuroscience;
- path dependence and institutional stabilization in social theory.

SRT is **not required to invent a competing local mechanism** in each domain.

A legitimate SRT task is instead:

> ask whether already-supported mechanisms from different domains instantiate a common ontological structure, can be placed at the correct level, and can be integrated without erasing their native distinctions.

Theoretical novelty may therefore reside in the **architecture relating established results**, not in re-discovering every brick used by that architecture.

---

## 2. Track O — Ontological Synthesis

### 2.1 Goal

Track O asks:

> What common reality-forming structure, if any, becomes visible when independently supported mechanisms are placed in one SRT ontology?

Typical SRT-side questions include:

```text
what difference is manifested?
what is non-equivalently registered?
which path becomes realized or excluded?
what bearer carries the consequence?
what is written back into later conditions?
what stabilizes into a new floor / constraint?
what remains reopenable or becomes harder to reselect?
```

Track O does **not** require a new domain-specific empirical prediction as an entry condition.

### 2.2 What counts as positive synthesis

A Track O contribution must do more than rename external results. It should satisfy several of the following:

1. **Source fidelity** — preserve what the external theory / experiment actually establishes; do not make it say SRT.
2. **Level separation** — distinguish implementation mechanism, bridge interpretation and ontological claim.
3. **Structural compression** — show how multiple apparently separate mechanisms can be represented by a smaller common relational grammar without deleting their differences.
4. **Cross-domain consistency** — the same SRT relation should perform the same role across domains rather than changing meaning opportunistically.
5. **Distinction preservation** — the synthesis must retain distinctions that matter in the source domains, such as repair vs elimination, current state vs future accessibility, bearer vs environment, stability vs generative health.
6. **Explanatory payoff** — the synthesis should resolve a layer confusion, expose a shared problem, clarify a bearer / boundary / history relation, or make previously separate theories mutually locatable.
7. **Constraint backflow** — external results must be allowed to narrow, correct or reject an SRT mapping. Integration is not one-way assimilation.
8. **Recoverability** — a reader should still be able to identify what comes from the external theory and what comes from SRT after the synthesis is written.

If the only result is:

```text
external concept X can be renamed with SRT term Y
```

then Track O has failed or remains analogy-only.

### 2.3 Legitimate use of established external results

SRT may use well-supported external results as **structural material**.

For example:

```text
external biology establishes:
cell elimination + compensatory clonal expansion

SRT may ask:
does this instantiate non-equivalent path realization,
consequence-bearing redistribution and historical writeback?
```

SRT does not need to re-prove the biological finding before using it as a bounded instance.

But the support relation must remain explicit:

```text
external evidence supports the external biological claim
external biological claim may instantiate an SRT structure
instantiation does not by itself prove the SRT ontology
```

## 3. Track D — Discriminating / Empirical Increment

### 3.1 Goal

Track D begins only when SRT claims more than integration, for example:

```text
SRT predicts an outcome not required by the bounded rival
SRT changes the intervention target
SRT discriminates two cases the rival treats as equivalent
SRT identifies a future path / cost / bearer difference prospectively
SRT claims a term or mechanism is empirically irreducible to competitor vocabulary
```

### 3.2 Requirements

A Track D claim must use the relevant empirical / formal discipline, including where applicable:

- pre-frozen target and context;
- bounded rival models;
- same information / fitting budget;
- prospective or out-of-sample evaluation;
- intervention or counterfactual discrimination;
- symmetric failure criteria;
- `GOV-SUB01` subtractive audit for claimed irreducibility or indispensability.

Track D is where SRT must answer:

> What changes if the SRT-specific structure is removed?

---

## 4. Track relation

Every substantial material fusion / cross-domain bridge should be classified in prose as one of:

```text
O-track        — ontological synthesis only
D-track        — discriminating / empirical increment only
O + D          — both
source-only    — useful evidence / pressure, no current SRT synthesis or discriminator
```

These are governance reading labels, **not new claim-ladder levels and not required frontmatter enums**.

### 4.1 O without D is legitimate

A bridge can be valuable when:

```text
Ontological synthesis: strong
Empirical increment: none / not claimed
```

Such a bridge may remain P2/P3/P4 depending on its existing claim level and evidence discipline. Lack of D-track novelty is not by itself a reason to classify the material as low-value or reject it.

### 4.2 D without O is possible

A domain experiment may produce a local discriminator without contributing much to SRT's broad ontology. It should not be inflated into a universal framework claim.

### 4.3 O + D is stronger but not required

The strongest case may combine a coherent synthesis with a genuine discriminator. This is a possible outcome, not a quota.

---

## 5. Relationship to GOV-SUB01

`GOV-SUB01` remains mandatory when SRT claims:

- irreducibility;
- indispensability;
- empirical superiority;
- a new scalar / operator / mechanism;
- cross-domain identity;
- a unique prediction or intervention role.

But its result must be interpreted relative to the audited target.

```text
R2 / R3 for empirical discrimination
!= automatic deletion of a useful O-track synthesis
```

Example:

```text
"repair has costs" may be R2 because Misrepair / Disposable Soma already cover it.

Yet those external results may still be useful in an O-track account
of friction, maintenance, bearer consequence and historical writeback.
```

Conversely:

```text
strong O-track synthesis
!= protection from subtractive audit
```

If SRT later claims a unique empirical role, the D-track claim must survive the relevant rival comparison.

## 6. Material-fusion discipline

For a new paper, book, article, dataset or theory, do **not** begin with:

> What new thing can SRT claim that nobody else has?

Begin with four ordered questions:

### Q1 — Source truth

```text
What does the source itself establish or argue?
```

### Q2 — Ontological placement

```text
Can the source's supported structure be placed inside SRT
without changing the source claim or SRT definitions?
```

### Q3 — Synthesis payoff

```text
Does this placement clarify a shared structure,
resolve a level confusion,
connect previously separate domains,
or constrain SRT itself?
```

### Q4 — Discriminating increment, only if claimed

```text
Does SRT additionally predict, distinguish or intervene
beyond the bounded rivals?
```

If Q4 is `no`, do not manufacture a novelty claim to justify the material.

---

## 7. Required source / SRT separation

Every high-value material writeback must keep three layers distinguishable:

### Layer A — source-derived

```text
what the paper / theory / dataset itself supports
```

### Layer B — SRT ontological synthesis

```text
how those supported results may instantiate or constrain SRT relations
```

### Layer C — SRT discriminating claim

```text
what SRT says beyond the source / bounded rivals, if anything
```

Layer B may be strong while Layer C is empty.

Never silently convert:

```text
source supports X
```

into:

```text
source proves SRT
```

---

## 8. A/B/C material consequence

Pipeline 1 A/B/C should no longer treat "unique empirical novelty" as the only meaning of contribution.

### A may be justified by either route

A material can qualify for A when evidence and landing are adequate and it provides either:

1. **stable O-track value** — non-trivial ontological integration / correction / cross-domain structural compression; or
2. **stable D-track value** — genuine new empirical / formal discrimination; or
3. both.

### B remains appropriate when

- synthesis looks promising but source verification / mapping is incomplete;
- a discriminator is plausible but not frozen;
- the result is primarily a guardrail or future trigger.

### C remains appropriate when

- the material is redundant **and** adds no meaningful synthesis, correction or constraint;
- the mapping is only vocabulary resemblance;
- SRT must distort the source to absorb it;
- the evidence cannot support the proposed landing.

Therefore:

```text
"existing theory already explains the mechanism"
```

is not by itself a C verdict.

The next question is:

```text
"does this established mechanism contribute to a coherent SRT ontological synthesis?"
```

---

## 9. What SRT should not do

This discipline explicitly rejects two opposite failure modes.

### Failure mode A — novelty manufacture

```text
invent a new variable / mechanism / scalar merely to avoid overlap with prior art
```

Do not do this. Use external science when it already explains the local mechanism.

### Failure mode B — universal relabeling

```text
every established result can be restated in SRT words,
therefore all results support SRT
```

Do not do this either.

A valid synthesis must preserve source ownership, constrain mappings and produce a real organizational payoff.

---

## 10. Writing rule for bridges

When a bridge primarily performs Track O, its preferred framing is:

```text
External theories supply the local mechanisms / evidence.
SRT supplies a candidate ontological organization of their relations.
```

Avoid:

```text
SRT explains what the source could not explain
```

unless a D-track comparison has actually established that claim.

For Track O bridges, useful outputs include:

- common-structure maps;
- level / bearer / boundary maps;
- mechanism-to-ontology crosswalks;
- compatibility / tension matrices;
- source-to-SRT constraint maps;
- places where one source forces SRT to narrow a claim.

A new equation or scalar is **not** a default deliverable.

---

## 11. Example: aging workline

Aging provides the immediate motivating case.

Established frameworks may already explain:

- finite maintenance resources;
- damage accumulation;
- repair / misrepair;
- redundancy exhaustion;
- resilience and hormesis;
- senescence;
- inflammation;
- clonal selection and replacement.

SRT does not need a competing aging mechanism merely to remain relevant.

Track O can instead ask whether these mechanisms jointly instantiate a coherent relation among:

```text
maintenance burden
payment / repair
selective elimination or replacement where applicable
bearer-specific consequence
residual / transferred consequence
historical writeback
later accessibility / stability / generative reselectability
```

If SRT additionally claims that maintenance history predicts future pathway topology beyond pre-frozen rivals, that narrower statement becomes Track D and must face AGING01's competitor matrix.

---

## 12. Claim-ladder consequence

Track O and Track D answer different questions from the claim ladder.

- Track O value does not itself raise a claim from P3 to P2/P1/P0.
- Track D success may strengthen a scoped claim, but promotion still follows `SRT_CLAIM_LADDER.md`.
- External evidence remains evidence for its native claim unless a separate bridge argument is supplied.
- Cross-domain recurrence can motivate ontological attention but does not automatically establish necessity or fundamentality.

---

## 13. Stop rule

For each material / bridge:

1. preserve the source claim;
2. identify O-track synthesis value without demanding novelty for its own sake;
3. identify D-track increment only when one is actually claimed;
4. do not create new entities merely to survive prior-art comparison;
5. do not treat synthesis as proof;
6. stop when the remaining value is accurately classified.

The desired outcome is not maximal SRT uniqueness.

It is:

> **a coherent ontology that can absorb established knowledge without stealing its evidence, and can make additional empirical claims only where SRT truly has something extra to risk.**
