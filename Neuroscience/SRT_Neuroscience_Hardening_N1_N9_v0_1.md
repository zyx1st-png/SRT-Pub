# SRT Neuroscience Hardening N1-N9 v0.1

> Status: bridge / lab working draft.  
> Canonical caution: this document does not modify SRT primitive axioms. It proposes a neuroscience-facing implementation and pressure-test layer for SRT.

## 0. Purpose

This draft hardens the neuroscience layer of Selection-Reality Theory (SRT). Its goal is not to claim that neuroscience proves SRT's ontology. Rather, neuroscience is treated as an empirically accessible implementation domain in which SRT's selection-anchoring dynamics can be operationalized, constrained, and challenged.

The central claim is:

> The nervous system is not merely an information-processing or representational device. It is an embodied selection system that anchors candidate possibilities into actionable, reportable, and historically sedimented realities.

In SRT terms:

```text
L0_accessible -> G_hat_theta -> L1 -> L2
```

where:

- `L0_accessible` = currently accessible candidate space;
- `G_hat_theta` = embodied selection / anchoring process;
- `L1` = stabilized manifest state: percept, action, judgment, conscious content;
- `L2` = sedimented constraints: habit, schema, memory, skill, affective marking, norm internalization;
- `Psi_f` = selection / anchoring friction;
- `d-value` = concern-weighted consequence for bodily regulation, action, self-model, and future selectability.

---

## N1. Neural systems are embodied selection systems, not merely passive representational devices

### Claim

At the neural level, perception, action, and conscious content should not be treated as passive representations of external inputs. They are stabilized selections among competing latent candidates under bodily, historical, attentional, affective, and action-oriented constraints.

### Core formulation

> Representation is not prior to selection; representation is a stabilized product of selection.

### SRT mapping

| SRT term | Neuroscience-facing interpretation |
|---|---|
| `L0_accessible` | candidate percepts, actions, interpretations, memories, affordances |
| `G_hat_theta` | embodied selection process: competition, gain, gating, stabilization |
| `L1` | selected percept / action / judgment / conscious content |
| `L2` | stabilized priors, habits, schemas, skills, affective markings |

### Minimal axioms

1. Before a percept, action, or judgment stabilizes, multiple candidate states may be co-activated.
2. Candidate-to-L1 transition is shaped by bodily state, attention, action readiness, historical weights, and d-value.
3. Stabilization requires payment of selection friction `Psi_f`.
4. Repeated or high d-value L1 anchors may sediment into L2.

---

## N2. G_hat_theta is a composite neural selection architecture

### Claim

`G_hat_theta` should not be identified with a single neural mechanism, brain region, or theory. At the neuroscience level, it is better modeled as a composite selection architecture involving:

```text
competition -> gain modulation -> gating -> stabilization
```

### Components

| Stage | Role | Possible neuroscience correlates |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | multiple candidate states co-activate before L1 | lateral inhibition, multistable perception, representational competition |
| Gain modulation | candidate weights are biased by theta, attention, precision, affect, d-value | attention, precision weighting, neuromodulation, salience systems |
| Gating | candidates are permitted or blocked from action, report, working memory, conscious access | basal ganglia-thalamo-cortical loops, prefrontal gating, action selection |
| Stabilization | selected candidate persists and guides behavior | recurrent processing, working memory, global access, plasticity |

### Constraint

Not every neural process is a full instance of `G_hat_theta`. A full SRT-style anchoring event requires:

1. multiple candidates;
2. theta/d/L2/Psi_f-dependent selection bias;
3. gating into action, report, memory, or conscious access;
4. stabilization as L1;
5. possible sedimentation into L2 under repetition, reinforcement, affect, or high d-value.

## N3. Psi_f is operationalizable selection friction

### Claim

`Psi_f` denotes the multidimensional cost required to stabilize a candidate state into L1. It is not identical to reaction time, cognitive effort, prediction error, uncertainty, or any single neural marker. It should be treated as a latent selection-cost construct inferred from multiple behavioral, physiological, and neural proxies.

### Candidate decomposition

```text
Psi_f(theta) = alpha_theta*C + beta_theta*E + gamma_theta*M + delta_theta*A + eta_theta*B + lambda_theta*H + rho_theta*R
```

where:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `C` | candidate conflict |
| `E` | prediction mismatch / epistemic surprise |
| `M` | model reconstruction cost |
| `A` | action switching / gating cost |
| `B` | bodily load / interoceptive strain |
| `H` | historical inertia / L2 resistance |
| `R` | affective, social, or practical risk |

### Key distinctions

- `Psi_f` is not merely cognitive effort: effort is one expression of selection friction.
- `Psi_f` is not merely prediction error: prediction error measures mismatch; `Psi_f` measures anchoring cost.
- `Psi_f` is not merely uncertainty: uncertainty measures spread; `Psi_f` measures the cost of collapsing candidate spread into lived commitment.

### Prediction

High `Psi_f` should be reflected by a multi-indicator pattern, not a single marker alone: slower response, higher error, lower confidence, increased pupil/skin conductance/heart-rate response, greater post-decision revision, increased conflict/control signals, and reduced stabilization.

## N4. L2 is sedimented selection constraint

### Claim

`L2` denotes the sedimented structure of prior selections. At the neural level, repeated or high d-value L1 states may stabilize through plasticity, habit formation, affective marking, schema consolidation, and action-outcome learning.

Once formed, L2 lowers `Psi_f` within familiar selection basins while increasing friction for incompatible alternatives.

### Core formulation

> L2 is not merely memory. It is the historical reshaping of future selectability.

### Examples

| Phenomenon | SRT reading |
|---|---|
| Habit | repeated L1 action becomes low-friction L2 path |
| Expert intuition | trained schemas lower anchoring friction for domain-relevant candidates |
| Trauma | high d-value L1 event hardens into threat-biased L2 landscape |
| Bias | some candidate interpretations become low-friction premature anchors |
| Cultural/internalized norm | social L2 becomes part of individual selection terrain |

### Key prediction

L2 hardening produces an efficiency-flexibility tradeoff:

```text
Psi_f(trained path) decreases
Psi_f(incompatible alternative) increases
```

## N5. d-value is concern-weighted selectability

### Claim

`d-value` denotes the degree to which a candidate state matters to the system's bodily regulation, self-maintenance, action possibilities, self-model, and future selection landscape.

It is distinct from salience, attention, precision, and reward.

### Distinctions

| Concept | Question answered |
|---|---|
| Salience | What stands out? |
| Attention | What receives processing resources? |
| Reward | What is approached or reinforced? |
| Precision | What is trusted as informative? |
| `Psi_f` | What does anchoring cost? |
| `d-value` | What truly matters to the system? |

### Core distinction

> Salience makes a signal noticeable; d-value makes a signal matter.

### Neuroscience-facing implementation

`d-value` is not localized in a single region. It is a system-level relevance variable integrating interoceptive, affective, reward/threat, action-oriented, self-referential, and social-consequence systems.

Possible contributing systems include insula, ACC, hypothalamic/brainstem regulation, amygdala, striatum, vmPFC/OFC, mPFC, posterior cingulate, TPJ, and action-control circuits.

### Formal sketch

```text
d(x) = w_b*B(x) + w_a*A(x) + w_r*R(x) + w_s*S(x) + w_m*M(x) + w_f*F(x)
```

where:

- `B`: bodily relevance;
- `A`: action consequence;
- `R`: reward/threat;
- `S`: self-model relevance;
- `M`: memory / identity resonance;
- `F`: impact on future selection space.

## N6. Consciousness is stable concern-weighted L1 anchoring

### Claim

Consciousness should not be identified with mere information processing, local activation, stimulus complexity, or global access alone. In SRT, conscious content emerges when a candidate state is sufficiently activated, gain-modulated, gated, and stabilized into L1 such that it becomes available for report, action guidance, self-model coupling, or future sedimentation.

`d-value` does not by itself produce consciousness, but it modulates conscious thickness, priority, persistence, and sedimentation potential.

### Thin vs thick L1

| State | Description |
|---|---|
| Thin L1 | weak, unstable, hard to report, limited action/self coupling |
| Thick L1 | stable, reportable or action-guiding, self/bodily coupled, high sedimentation potential |

### Structural expression

```text
Conscious thickness(x) ~ Stabilization * Accessibility * Action coupling * Self coupling * d(x)
```

### Boundary cases

| Case | SRT reading |
|---|---|
| Blindsight | information can guide behavior without thick L1 anchoring |
| Bistable perception | candidates alternate in L1 stabilization |
| Pain | high d-value forces strong L1 restructuring |
| Dreaming | internally generated L1 with weak external/action validation |
| Meditation | changes d/Psi_f allocation and reduces automatic hardening |

## N7. Psychopathology as distorted reality-anchoring dynamics

### Claim

Many neuropsychiatric conditions can be interpreted as disturbances in reality-anchoring dynamics: candidate generation, d-value allocation, `Psi_f` closure, and L2 hardening.

### Comparative table

| Condition | SRT core imbalance | Compact expression |
|---|---|---|
| Anxiety | threat candidates receive excessive d-value | `d(threat) up` |
| Compulsion/OCD-like closure failure | high d-value risk candidate cannot close | `Psi_f(closure) up` |
| Trauma | high d-value L1 hardens into threat L2 | `L1_trauma -> L2_threat` |
| Depression | accessible future L0 contracts; negative L2 dominates | `L0_future_accessible down` |
| Addiction | immediate reward path becomes low-friction L2 | `Psi_f(reward path) down` |
| Delusion-like salience abnormality | low-evidence candidates over-anchor as reality | `P(candidate -> L1) up` |

### Intervention implication

> Changing a person is not merely inserting correct information. It requires enabling new low-friction L1 anchors to be repeatedly lived, stabilized, and sedimented into alternative L2 paths.

## N8. Experimental roadmap

### Claim

SRT can be experimentally operationalized by manipulating accessible candidate spaces, d-value, selection friction, and L2 hardening. Experiments should ask not only whether the brain processes information, but under what conditions a candidate becomes selected, anchored, paid for, concern-weighted, and historically sedimented.

### Minimal variable set

| SRT variable | Possible manipulation | Possible measures |
|---|---|---|
| `L0_accessible` | ambiguous images, multistable stimuli, polysemous words, action choices | candidate reports, choice distribution, eye movement |
| `d-value` | self-relevance, health risk, money, threat, identity, reputation | memory, action change, physiological arousal, self-report |
| `Psi_f` | conflict, rule switch, uncertainty, closure demand, responsibility | RT, error, pupil, skin conductance, confidence, revision |
| `L2` | training, repetition, affective marking, reinforcement | transfer, bias, reversal cost, persistence |

### Flagship experiments

1. **Ambiguous perception x d-value x L2 training**  
   Test whether high d-value candidates anchor more readily under ambiguity and whether training lowers anchoring friction.

2. **Rule hardening and reversal**  
   Test whether learned L2 reduces friction inside trained basins while increasing friction for incompatible alternatives.

3. **Closure-cost task**  
   Test whether high responsibility or risk increases `Psi_f(closure)` even when objective probability is low.

4. **Safety re-anchoring**  
   Test whether repeated embodied/action-level safety experience changes L2 more than explicit information alone.

5. **Future L0 accessibility**  
   Test whether depression-like states reduce accessible future action candidates and increase action anchoring friction.

6. **Addiction and alternative-path friction**  
   Test whether effective intervention lowers `Psi_f(adaptive alternative)` rather than merely suppressing impulse.

### Falsification handles

SRT neuroscience is weakened if:

1. d-value has no effect after controlling salience and task difficulty;
2. L2 training improves familiar paths without any alternative-path friction;
3. high d-value L1 does not sediment more strongly than low d-value L1;
4. `Psi_f` proxies fail to form any stable latent cost structure;
5. explicit information alone reliably rewrites deep L2 without repeated embodied/action anchoring;
6. psychopathologies fail to show distinguishable anchoring failure modes.

## N9. SRT and mainstream neuroscience theories: hard distinctions

### Claim

SRT does not deny predictive processing, free-energy minimization, active inference, global workspace access, information integration, reinforcement learning, or embodied cognition. It treats them as partial mechanisms within a broader selection-anchoring architecture.

SRT's distinct contribution is to explain how candidate possibilities become stabilized as lived realities under concern-weighted, cost-constrained, and historically sedimented conditions.

### Comparison table

| Theory | Core question | SRT absorbs as | SRT distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Predictive processing | How does the brain predict and update models? | candidate generation, error, precision/gain | SRT explains reality anchoring, not only model updating |
| FEP | How does a system maintain itself by minimizing free energy? | viability substrate, self-maintenance constraint | SRT adds lived anchoring, d-value, and L1-to-L2 sedimentation |
| Active inference | How does action reduce expected uncertainty/error? | action-gating and policy selection layer | SRT treats action as reality commitment and sedimentation path |
| Global workspace | How does information become globally accessible? | one L1 stabilization/access route | SRT includes pre-access competition and post-access hardening |
| IIT | How does integrated information relate to experience? | possible structural integration constraint | SRT emphasizes concern-weighted anchoring rather than integration alone |
| Reinforcement learning | How does reward update policy/value? | one L2 learning mechanism | d-value is broader than reward; Psi_f broader than prediction error |
| Embodied cognition | How do body and environment shape cognition? | theta, bodily d-value, action affordances | SRT says reality-selection itself is bodily |

### Compact distinction

> Predictive processing explains how the brain guesses the world. FEP explains how systems maintain themselves. Global workspace explains access. RL explains value updating. Embodied cognition explains bodily dependence. SRT explains how a candidate possibility, under bodily, concern-weighted, cost-constrained, and historically sedimented conditions, becomes real for the system.

## 11. One-paragraph abstract

SRT's neuroscience layer treats the nervous system as an embodied selection system rather than a passive representation device. Candidate percepts, actions, judgments, and conscious contents emerge from accessible latent spaces through a composite process of competition, gain modulation, gating, and stabilization. `Psi_f` captures the multidimensional friction required to anchor a candidate into L1; `d-value` captures the degree to which a candidate matters to bodily regulation, action, self-model, and future selectability; and `L2` captures the sedimentation of prior selections into future constraints. This framework interprets consciousness as stable concern-weighted L1 anchoring, psychopathology as distorted anchoring dynamics, and experimental neuroscience as the manipulation of candidate spaces, concern weights, friction costs, and hardening effects. Existing theories such as predictive processing, FEP, active inference, global workspace, IIT, reinforcement learning, and embodied cognition are treated as partial mechanisms within a broader selection-anchoring architecture rather than as direct equivalents of SRT.
