## 1. One-paragraph abstract

Selective Reality Theory (SRT) introduces concepts such as selection friction (`Psi_f`), concern-weighted stake (`d-value`), manifest anchoring (`L_1`), and hardening (`L_2`) that are not directly observable as single objective variables. This has an obvious methodological risk: if such concepts are not reducible to simple measurements, SRT may appear unfalsifiable or merely interpretive. This paper argues that non-reduction need not imply immunity from evidence. It develops a validation framework based on proxy measurement, structural convergence, differential prediction, baseline comparison, failure conditions, and withdrawal or narrowing rules. The framework is applied to five SRT claims: `d-value` versus salience, `Psi_f` versus prediction error, `L_2` hardening versus memory or habit, subjecthood versus AI self-report, and institutional legitimacy versus stability. The paper proposes that SRT should be judged not by whether its core concepts are photographed directly, but by whether they generate structured consequences that outperform nearby explanations and accept empirical risk.

---

## 2. Core thesis

Non-reductive concepts can be testable if they generate constrained consequences.

Core SRT validation formula:

```text
validation = proxy measurement
           + structural convergence
           + differential prediction
           + baseline comparison
           + failure / narrowing condition
```

Paper thesis:

> SRT should not claim that its concepts are directly measurable in a single objective scale. Instead, it should make them scientifically and philosophically accountable by specifying proxy families, structural signatures, competing baselines, and conditions under which the claim must be narrowed or withdrawn.

---

## 3. Contribution claim

The paper contributes six claims:

1. **Anti-immunity claim**: non-reductive theory does not mean evidence-free theory.
2. **Proxy discipline claim**: SRT concepts must specify proxy families before being treated as empirical.
3. **Differential prediction claim**: SRT must state what it predicts beyond nearby theories.
4. **Baseline claim**: SRT must beat simpler explanations such as salience, prediction error, memory, reward, self-report, or stability.
5. **Withdrawal claim**: every strong SRT claim needs a narrowing or failure condition.
6. **Research-program claim**: SRT becomes a research program only when its bold claims are converted into validation packages.

## 5. Section plan

### Section 1 — The verification problem

Aim:

> State the strongest objection clearly.

Objection:

```text
If SRT concepts such as L0, Psi_f, d-value, and L2 hardening are not directly measurable,
then SRT risks becoming unfalsifiable metaphysics.
```

Response:

```text
Direct measurement is not the only form of validation.
Many scientific constructs are inferred through structured consequences.
But SRT must accept empirical risk by specifying proxies, baselines, and failure conditions.
```

---

### Section 2 — What non-reductive verification is not

Aim:

> Prevent misuse of “non-reductive.”

Non-reductive verification is not:

```text
exemption from measurement;
explaining any outcome after the fact;
refusing baselines;
using metaphors as proof;
claiming every proxy confirms the theory;
protecting core claims from possible narrowing.
```

Guardrail:

> A non-reductive claim can still be weak, wrong, overbroad, or in need of narrowing.

### Section 4 — Proxy measurement without proxy reduction

Aim:

> Explain why proxies matter but do not exhaust concepts.

Example:

```text
d-value may be proxied by memory persistence, cost-bearing, non-substitutability,
identity impact, and future decision weighting.
```

But:

```text
d-value is not identical to any single proxy.
```

Principle:

> Use multi-proxy convergence. Do not collapse the construct into its easiest measurement.

---

### Section 5 — Structural convergence

Aim:

> Define what counts as stronger evidence when no single measurement is decisive.

Structural convergence means:

```text
different proxy families point toward the same SRT structure;
nearby baselines do not fully explain the pattern;
the pattern recurs across contexts or scales;
the theory predicts where the pattern should fail or narrow.
```

Example structures:

```text
high-d content -> memory persistence + action reorganization + non-substitutability;
hardening -> reduced local selection cost + increased global constraint + hysteresis;
friction export -> institutional efficiency + hidden burden + low appeal capacity.
```

---

### Section 6 — Differential predictions

Aim:

> Make SRT compete with nearby theories.

Key examples:

| SRT claim | Nearby baseline | SRT differential prediction |
|---|---|---|
| `d-value` | salience / reward / arousal | high-d predicts memory and action beyond salience |
| `Psi_f` | prediction error / task difficulty | self-relevant reframing has extra transition cost |
| `L2` hardening | memory / habit | local ease plus global rigidity / hysteresis |
| subjecthood | self-report / benchmark performance | self-report dissociates from continuity / boundary / stake indicators |
| legitimacy | stability / efficiency | stable systems can export hidden friction and narrow futures |

---

### Section 7 — Case package 1: `d-value` vs salience

Validation package:

```text
Claim: d-value is not reducible to salience.
Proxy: memory persistence, cost-bearing, identity relevance, action reorganization.
Baseline: salience, arousal, confidence, reward.
Prediction: d-value proxies predict later action beyond salience.
Failure: salience/arousal fully explain downstream effects.
Narrowing: restrict d-value to identity / non-substitutability / future-selectability contexts.
```

---

### Section 8 — Case package 2: `Psi_f` vs prediction error

Validation package:

```text
Claim: Psi_f is not reducible to prediction error.
Proxy: reaction time, reframing resistance, stress, recovery time, switching cost.
Baseline: prediction error, task difficulty, cognitive load.
Prediction: identity/norm-revising updates show extra transition friction.
Failure: PE and task difficulty fully explain friction.
Narrowing: keep Fisher-style Psi_f only in information-geometric slices.
```

---

### Section 9 — Case package 3: `L2` hardening vs memory / habit

Validation package:

```text
Claim: L2 hardening is not ordinary memory alone.
Proxy: reduced deliberation, violation cost, hysteresis, reversal cost.
Baseline: memory strength, practice, habit frequency.
Prediction: hardening shows local ease plus global rigidity.
Failure: memory/habit fully explain automation and rigidity.
Narrowing: treat L2 as integrative vocabulary where no extra signature appears.
```

---

### Section 10 — Case package 4: subjecthood vs AI self-report

Validation package:

```text
Claim: AI self-report is not sufficient for subjecthood.
Proxy: boundary maintenance, continuity, counterfactual self-model, own-future-state preservation.
Baseline: self-report quality, benchmark performance, memory length, tool-use success.
Prediction: self-report/performance dissociate from S4/S5 threshold indicators.
Failure: self-report and performance fully predict all threshold indicators.
Narrowing: restrict SRT subjecthood threshold to moral/existential subjecthood.
```

---

### Section 11 — Case package 5: legitimacy vs stability

Validation package:

```text
Claim: institutional stability is not sufficient for legitimacy.
Proxy: hidden compliance cost, appeal success, burden distribution, correction latency.
Baseline: stability, efficiency, compliance rate, formal legality.
Prediction: some stable systems show high friction export and low future selectability.
Failure: stability/efficiency reliably predict low friction and high affected-agent selectability.
Narrowing: apply friction-export diagnostics mainly to asymmetric or contested institutions.
```

---

### Section 12 — Objections and replies

Key objections:

1. **Proxy looseness objection**: too many proxies make confirmation easy.
2. **Post-hoc explanation objection**: SRT can explain any result after the fact.
3. **Construct inflation objection**: SRT renames existing constructs.
4. **Measurement objection**: core terms cannot be objectively measured.
5. **Baseline objection**: existing theories already explain the effects.
6. **Scale-jump objection**: cross-domain convergence is analogy, not evidence.
7. **Metaphysics objection**: validation cannot establish ontology.
8. **Over-narrowing objection**: failure conditions will make SRT trivial.

Required replies:

```text
pre-register proxy families;
state baselines before testing;
allow narrowing;
separate conceptual from empirical claims;
require differential predictions;
do not treat analogy as evidence unless structural signatures match.
```

---

### Section 13 — Conclusion

Return to core claim:

> SRT can be non-reductive without being unfalsifiable if it accepts structured empirical risk.

Final line:

```text
A theory that cannot be reduced to one measurement can still be constrained by many measurements,
provided it says in advance what patterns should appear, what alternatives must be beaten,
and what results would force retreat.
```

## 6. Key definitions for the paper

| Term | Paper definition | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| non-reductive validation | testing a construct through structured proxy consequences | not exemption from evidence |
| proxy | measurable signature linked to a construct | not identical to the construct |
| structural convergence | multiple proxy families supporting one pattern | not loose analogy |
| differential prediction | prediction that distinguishes SRT from nearby theories | not generic explanation |
| baseline | simpler competing explanation | must be named before evaluation |
| failure condition | result that weakens the claim | not optional |
| narrowing condition | disciplined retreat after failure | not ad hoc escape |

## 11. Abstract variants

### 11.1 More philosophical-of-science

This paper argues that non-reductive theories need not be unfalsifiable. Using Selective Reality Theory as a case, it develops a validation framework based on proxy measurement, structural convergence, differential prediction, baseline comparison, and narrowing conditions. The framework allows concepts such as selection friction, concern-weighted stake, hardening, and subjecthood to be empirically constrained without reducing them to single-variable definitions.

### 11.2 More interdisciplinary

SRT proposes concepts that cross ontology, cognition, AI, ethics, and social theory. Such breadth creates a methodological challenge: how can these concepts be tested without flattening them? This paper proposes a practical answer: turn every strong SRT claim into a validation package with proxies, baselines, differential predictions, and failure conditions.

### 11.3 More provocative

A theory is not unfalsifiable because its central concepts are complex. It becomes unfalsifiable only when it refuses to say what would count against it. This paper shows how SRT can avoid that failure.
