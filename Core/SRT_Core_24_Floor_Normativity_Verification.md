# SRT Core 24 — Floor Replacement, Normativity, and Non-Reductive Verification

> **Status note**: This file records a theory-hardening supplement. It does not replace canonical definitions of `L0/L1/L2`, `Ψ_f`, `d-value`, or `Ĝθ`. Its role is to clarify why SRT has cross-scale explanatory power, how normativity can be presented dynamically, and how SRT can remain experimentally tractable without collapsing into reductive measurement.
>
> **PH-SS-11 validation pointer**: Read this file with `Philosophy/SRT_Philosophy_Objection_Ledger_PH_SS_Extension.md` (`O-Phil-19`) and `Philosophy/PH_SS_Hardening_Audit_2026-04-27.md`. Non-reductive verification is not a license to avoid empirical risk. SRT claims should specify proxy measurements, structural convergence, differential predictions, and withdrawal / narrowing conditions.

## 0. Why This File Was Added

Recent evaluation of SRT showed that an important layer had not been sufficiently represented in the repository: SRT's explanatory power should not be defended as “it can explain everything.” That framing is too weak and too vulnerable to the criticism of unfalsifiable over-extension.

The stronger claim is this:

> SRT gains cross-disciplinary, cross-scale, and cross-temporal explanatory force because it challenges the object-first ontological floor on which many current theories are built.

In other words, SRT is not merely adding another explanatory layer to existing theories. It attempts to replace a deeper presupposition: that objects, facts, values, goals, and frameworks are already given before selection occurs.

This supplement therefore clarifies three points:

1. SRT's explanatory power comes from **floor replacement**, not arbitrary generalization.
2. Purpose, value, morality, and frameworks can be expressed as **forms of selection dynamics**.
3. SRT can be experimentally tested through **structural consequences and convergent proxies**, not through a single reductive objective ruler.

---

## 1. Floor Replacement: Why SRT Explains Across Scales

SRT's explanatory power should not be described as the ability to attach its vocabulary to many unrelated domains. The stronger position is that many domains appear disconnected because they share an unexamined ontological floor.

That floor may be called:

> **object-first ontology** — the assumption that reality first consists of already-given objects, and only afterward do perception, choice, evaluation, and action occur.

Under object-first ontology, one tends to assume:

- the world is composed of completed objects;
- the subject enters the world after the fact;
- choice is merely preference among already-existing options;
- value is a subjective assignment;
- morality is a social overlay;
- purpose is an emergent appearance inside an otherwise purposeless mechanism;
- frameworks are descriptions imposed after reality is already there.

SRT reverses this floor.

It does not begin from already-completed objects. It begins from a latent field of possibilities (`L0`), a constrained selection operator (`Ĝθ`), the cost or friction of actualization (`Ψ_f`), manifest actualization (`L1`), and repeated stabilization or sedimentation (`L2`).

The core movement is therefore:

```text
L0 potentiality
  -- constrained selection / Ĝθ, paying Ψ_f -->
L1 manifestation
  -- repetition, stabilization, sedimentation -->
L2 constraint domain
  -- downward constraint -->
future Ĝθ
```

This means that SRT's cross-scale explanatory power is **vertical**, not merely horizontal. It does not explain many fields by stretching a metaphor sideways. It explains why those fields can be connected at all: each domain involves latent possibilities being selected into manifest realities and then hardened into constraints.

Examples:

| Scale | Latent field | Selection / actualization | Hardening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neural | competing representational possibilities | inhibition, attention, stabilization | trained circuits, habits, attractors |
| Cognitive | possible interpretations | framing, categorization, salience | beliefs, schemas, expectations |
| Behavioral | possible actions | choice under cost and stake | habit, skill, routine |
| Social | possible coordination patterns | norm adoption and enforcement | institutions, law, roles |
| Cultural | possible narratives | symbolic selection | tradition, identity, worldview |
| AI | output/action/search space | optimization or agentic selection proxy | memory, policy, tool-use constraints |

Thus:

> SRT's explanatory power does not come from claiming that it explains everything. It comes from reopening the deeper question of how anything becomes a stable reality in the first place.

This is the sense in which SRT attempts to break an incorrect floor rather than repair a building constructed upon it.

## 2. Purpose, Value, Morality, and Frameworks as Selection Dynamics

A central promise of SRT is that it can express purpose, value, morality, and frameworks dynamically without reducing them to subjective preference or treating them as supernatural additions.

The claim is not that purpose, value, or morality are “merely mechanisms.” Rather, SRT asks how these phenomena obtain real constraint power inside selection processes.

### 2.1 Purpose

In SRT, purpose is not primarily a static end-state represented by a subject.

> **Purpose is directional stabilization among possible futures.**

A system has purpose when certain future paths repeatedly gain selection priority across time. A living system does not need an explicit abstract concept of “survival” before behaving purposively. Its selection structure can continuously privilege paths that maintain organization, preserve viability, or expand future reachable states.

Purpose therefore appears as a dynamic structure in which some future possibilities are repeatedly made more selectable than others.

### 2.2 Value

Value is not equivalent to reward, utility, preference intensity, or subjective liking.

> **Value is the non-substitutability weight of a selection path for a system's future continuity and concern structure.**

This connects directly to `d-value`.

`d-value` should not be interpreted as “how much a system likes something.” It marks the degree to which a selection matters for the system's continuing organization, future reachability, self-continuity, and stake-bearing structure.

A system's values are therefore revealed not only by declared preference but by what the system is willing to pay for, preserve under pressure, reorganize around, or refuse to exchange for short-term reward.

In compact form:

> Value is the dynamic expression of selection non-substitutability.

### 2.3 Morality

Morality is neither a merely subjective opinion nor a physical object-like entity.

> **Morality is an `L2` boundary formed through cross-subject coupling of `d-value` and stabilized selection constraints.**

This avoids two extremes:

1. moral realism as if moral facts were object-like substances;
2. moral relativism as if morality were arbitrary preference.

In SRT, morality becomes real as a constraint domain. It is sedimented through responsibility, trust, shame, promise, punishment, education, reputation, law, and cultural memory. These are not merely labels. They alter the future space of acceptable selections.

Thus morality is an `L2` reality: not object-like, but genuinely constraining.

> **Normativity guardrail**: `L2` moral reality is not automatically moral legitimacy. Use the Philosophy ethics guardrails for friction-export, future-selectability, cross-subject `d-value` bandwidth, and correction-channel tests.

### 2.4 Frameworks

Frameworks are not neutral descriptions applied after reality is already present.

> **A framework is a preconfiguration of what can become visible, selectable, costly, and manifest.**

Different frameworks do not merely interpret the same world. They change which possibilities enter the actual selection field.

For example:

- a medical framework sees symptoms, diagnosis, intervention, recovery;
- a business framework sees cost, conversion, margin, channel, leverage;
- a scientific framework sees variables, models, causality, testability;
- a moral framework sees harm, responsibility, dignity, obligation;
- an SRT framework sees latent domain, selection operator, selection cost, manifestation, and hardening.

Therefore:

> A framework is not merely an explanation of reality after the fact. It is a condition of reality-selection before the fact.

In SRT terms, frameworks partially parameterize `Ĝθ` by changing the visibility, availability, and friction landscape of `L0` possibilities.

## 3. Non-Reductive Experimental Verification

SRT can be experimentally tested, but its significance is not reducible to lower-level entity explanation.

It does not claim that purpose, value, morality, consciousness, or agency can be reduced to one measurable physical or neural variable. SRT is better understood as a cross-level constraint theory.

It asks:

> How do systems generate stable realities through selection, cost, concern, and hardening across levels?

Therefore, SRT's core concepts should not be tested only by searching for a single objective ruler that directly measures `L0`, `Ψ_f`, `d-value`, or `L2`.

Instead, SRT should be tested through:

- structural consequences;
- convergent operational proxies;
- cross-level signatures;
- failure conditions;
- comparative predictions against adjacent theories.

This is not an escape from verification. It is a different verification logic.

> **PH-SS-11 guardrail**: A non-reductive validation strategy must still include empirical risk. Each strong claim should state at least one proxy class, one structural consequence, one comparison target, and one withdrawal / narrowing condition. If no differential prediction beyond nearby theories can be specified, classify the claim as metaphysical program or bridge hypothesis rather than empirical theory.

Many scientific constructs are not directly observed as substances. Fields, curvature, latent variables, constraints, phase transitions, and information geometry are often inferred through their effects. SRT concepts should be treated similarly.

## 4. Operationalizing `Ψ_f`

`Ψ_f` should not be treated as a directly visible substance.

> `Ψ_f` is an inferred constraint from the difficulty, cost, or friction of selection-path transition.

Possible operational projections include:

- increased reaction time;
- increased error rate;
- task-switching cost;
- representational competition;
- neural or metabolic load;
- Fisher-information curvature;
- model-update path length;
- recovery time after prediction failure;
- irreversibility or asymmetry of transition paths;
- early non-monotonic volatility during learning or reframing.

None of these indicators is identical to `Ψ_f` itself. Each is a projection in a particular system and measurement context.

Therefore:

> SRT does not require a single absolute measurement of `Ψ_f`; it requires convergent evidence that transition difficulty behaves as selection friction under specified conditions.

---

## 5. Operationalizing `d-value`

`d-value` should not be reduced to preference strength, pain, salience, reward, or self-report intensity.

> `d-value` marks concern-weighted non-substitutability: the degree to which a selection matters for the system's future continuity, organization, and stake structure.

Possible operational signatures include:

- how much cost a system will bear to preserve a selection;
- how much structural reorganization occurs when that selection is blocked;
- whether the selection remains stable across time;
- whether it affects identity continuity;
- whether it is maintained under stress or perturbation;
- whether it organizes other downstream choices;
- whether it spreads across subjects and sediments into institutional or cultural form.

This is especially important for social theory. A society may not possess individual consciousness in the ordinary sense, but it can exhibit supra-individual `d`-structures: collective non-substitutabilities around identity, law, sacred values, dignity, territory, roles, or moral boundaries.

Thus:

> SRT does not require an absolute ruler for concern. It requires convergent operational signatures of non-substitutability.

## 6. Operationalizing `L2` Hardening

`L2` should not be tested by asking where an object called “L2” is located.

Instead, `L2` should be inferred from the structural consequences of hardening:

- path dependence;
- hysteresis;
- early high cost followed by later low cost;
- transition from explicit selection to automation;
- resistance or backlash when a norm is violated;
- cross-subject and cross-context replication;
- lower local selection cost but stronger global constraint.

A compact definition:

> `L2` hardening = reduced local selection cost + increased global constraint + hysteresis under perturbation.

This makes `L2` experimentally and computationally tractable without reducing it to a single material substrate.

## 7. Three Meta-Claims to Preserve

### Claim A — Floor Replacement

SRT's cross-scale explanatory power comes from replacing object-first ontology. It does not treat reality as a pre-given set of objects, but as latent possibility selected into manifestation and hardened into constraints.

### Claim B — Dynamic Ontology of Normativity

Purpose, value, morality, and frameworks are not subjective additions placed on top of reality. They are stable forms of selection dynamics: purpose as directional selection, value as concern-weighted non-substitutability, morality as cross-subject `L2` boundary, and framework as preconfiguration of `Ĝθ`.

### Claim C — Non-Reductive Verification

SRT can be experimentally examined without reducing its core concepts to single objective measurements. `Ψ_f`, `d-value`, and `L2` hardening function more like inferred constraints, fields, curvatures, or latent variables: they are known by the structured consequences they produce.

Guardrail:

> Non-reductive does not mean non-falsifiable. The minimum package is proxy measurement + structural convergence + differential prediction + withdrawal condition.

## 8. Reviewer-Risk Guardrails

This supplement strengthens SRT, but it also introduces risks. The following guardrails should be observed in papers and public writing.

### 8.1 Avoid “SRT explains everything”

Use:

> SRT explains why different layers can be connected through selection, manifestation, and hardening.

Avoid:

> SRT explains everything.

### 8.2 Avoid claiming old theories are simply wrong

Use:

> Many existing theories may remain locally valid while resting on an incomplete object-first floor.

Avoid:

> All existing theories are wrong.

### 8.3 Avoid reducing morality to mechanism

Use:

> SRT explains how moral normativity gains constraint power through cross-subject hardening.

Avoid:

> Morality is just dynamics.

### 8.4 Avoid using “not directly measurable” as a shield

Use:

> SRT requires structural, convergent, and comparative validation.

Avoid:

> SRT is beyond measurement.

### 8.5 State failure conditions

For scientific credibility, SRT must preserve possible failure conditions:

- If `Ψ_f` produces no distinguishable transition-cost signatures beyond existing models, its operational role weakens.
- If `d-value` cannot predict non-substitutability better than preference, reward, or salience, its distinctiveness weakens.
- If `L2` hardening cannot be distinguished from ordinary memory, habit, or social convention, its theoretical role weakens.
- If SRT produces no comparative prediction beyond FEP, predictive processing, RL, IIT, GNW, or social construction theory, it risks being absorbed as relabeling.
