## 2. Hardened Definitions

### D24.1 — Object-First Ontology

**Object-first ontology** is the background assumption that reality is primarily composed of already-given objects, and that perception, selection, valuation, agency, and normativity occur only afterward.

**Guardrail**: This is a diagnostic category, not a claim that all existing theories are wholly false. Existing theories may remain locally valid while inheriting object-first assumptions.

### D24.2 — Selection-First Ontology

**Selection-first ontology** is the SRT position that stable reality is downstream of constrained selection: possibilities in `L0` become manifest through `Ĝθ` under cost or friction `Ψ_f`, and repeated manifestations sediment into `L2` constraints.

Compact form:

```text
L0 -- Ĝθ / Ψ_f --> L1 -- stabilization --> L2 -- downward constraint --> future Ĝθ
```

### D24.3 — Dynamic Normativity

**Dynamic normativity** names the SRT treatment of purpose, value, morality, and frameworks as stabilized selection structures rather than subjective overlays on an already-complete world.

### D24.4 — Framework

A **framework** is a preconfiguration of visibility, availability, cost, and admissibility over `L0` possibilities. It partially parameterizes `Ĝθ` by shaping what can be seen, selected, paid for, and stabilized.

### D24.5 — Non-Reductive Verification

**Non-reductive verification** is the method of testing SRT concepts through convergent structural consequences rather than through a single direct objective ruler. It applies when the target concept functions as a constraint, field, curvature, latent variable, or cross-level relation.

## 3. Merge-Ready Core Claims

### C24-1 — Floor Replacement Claim

SRT's cross-scale explanatory power does not come from explaining everything. It comes from replacing object-first ontology with selection-first ontology: stable realities arise when latent possibilities are selected into manifestation and repeated manifestations harden into constraints.

**Recommended insertion**: `Core/SRT_Core_21_Minimal_Axioms.md` or `Core/SRT_Core_21_Formal_Axioms.md`, as a framing note after the minimal ontology loop.

**Do not write**: “SRT proves all previous ontologies are wrong.”

---

### C24-2 — Vertical Explanatory Power Claim

SRT explains across domains because many domains instantiate the same vertical loop: potentiality, constrained selection, manifest stabilization, and downward constraint. The theory's unifying power is structural rather than metaphorical.

**Recommended insertion**: `Core/SRT_Core_21b_Constitutive_Theorems.md` or `_SRT_DEEP_THEORY_MAP.md`, as a cross-scale explanatory guardrail.

**Failure condition**: If the same loop cannot generate domain-specific discriminating predictions, this claim collapses into analogy.

---

### C24-3 — Dynamic Normativity Claim

Purpose, value, morality, and frameworks are not external additions to reality. In SRT they are stable forms of selection dynamics: purpose is directional stabilization, value is concern-weighted non-substitutability, morality is cross-subject `L2` boundary formation, and framework is preconfiguration of `Ĝθ`.

**Recommended insertion**: `Core/SRT_Core_12b_Ontology_L2.md`, `_SRT_D_VALUE_CANONICAL.md`, and philosophy-domain files.

**Guardrail**: Describing morality as `L2` constraint does not automatically endorse any particular moral order.

---

### C24-4 — Value / d-Value Link Claim

Value is not identical to reward, utility, preference, or salience. In SRT, value is the dynamic expression of non-substitutability: the degree to which a selection matters for a system's future continuity, concern structure, and stake-bearing organization.

**Recommended insertion**: `_SRT_D_VALUE_CANONICAL.md` as a bridge note, not as a replacement for canonical `d-value` definition.

**Failure condition**: If `d-value` cannot predict non-substitutability better than preference, reward, or salience, its distinct theoretical role weakens.

---

### C24-5 — Non-Reductive Verification Claim

SRT does not require its core concepts to be measured by one direct objective ruler. It requires structural, convergent, and comparative validation through the observable consequences of selection friction, non-substitutability, and hardening.

**Recommended insertion**: `SRT_EXP_MEASURE_MAP.md` and `Governance/SRT_LAB_HYPOTHESES.md`.

**Do not write**: “SRT is beyond measurement.”

---

### C24-6 — L2 Hardening Operational Signature

`L2` hardening can be operationally recognized by the conjunction of three signatures:

```text
reduced local selection cost
+ increased global constraint
+ hysteresis under perturbation
```

This signature does not define the whole ontology of `L2`, but it gives an empirical handle for detecting hardening across cognitive, neural, social, institutional, and artificial systems.

**Recommended insertion**: `Core/SRT_Core_12b_Ontology_L2.md`, `Core/SRT_Core_23_IG_Complexity_Neuro_Hardening.md`, and `SRT_EXP_MEASURE_MAP.md`.

**Failure condition**: If `L2` hardening cannot be distinguished from ordinary memory, habit, or convention, the bridge claim weakens.

## 4. Canonical正文 Insert Blocks

The following blocks are short enough to be inserted into existing canonical or near-canonical files.

### 4.1 Insert Block for Minimal Axioms / Formal Axioms

```markdown
### Selection-First Framing Note

SRT does not treat stable reality as a pre-given set of objects to which selection is later applied. Its starting point is selection-first: latent possibilities become manifest through constrained selection, and repeated manifestations harden into future constraints. The theory's cross-scale explanatory power comes from this floor replacement, not from an unrestricted claim to explain everything.
```

---

### 4.2 Insert Block for L2 Ontology

```markdown
### Operational Signature of L2 Hardening

Although `L2` is not reducible to any single substrate, its hardening can be operationally recognized when repeated selections lower local selection cost while increasing global constraint and producing hysteresis under perturbation. This signature allows `L2` to be studied across neural, cognitive, social, institutional, and artificial systems without reducing it to mere memory or convention.
```

---

### 4.3 Insert Block for d-Value Canonical

```markdown
### Value as Non-Substitutability

In SRT, value should not be reduced to reward, utility, preference strength, or salience. Value names the dynamic expression of non-substitutability: the degree to which a selection matters for a system's future continuity, concern structure, and stake-bearing organization. This use of value is a bridge to `d-value`, not a replacement for the canonical definition of `d-value`.
```

---

### 4.4 Insert Block for Ψ_f Canonical

```markdown
### Ψ_f as Inferred Selection Friction

`Ψ_f` need not be directly measured as a substance-like variable. It can be inferred from structured transition difficulty: increased switching cost, representational competition, update path length, recovery burden, irreversibility, or early non-monotonic volatility during learning and reframing. These are projections of `Ψ_f`, not identities with `Ψ_f` itself.
```

---

### 4.5 Insert Block for Experimental Measure Map

```markdown
### Non-Reductive Validation Rule

SRT concepts should be tested through convergent structural consequences rather than a single direct objective ruler. A proxy may support an SRT construct only when it helps distinguish selection friction, concern-weighted non-substitutability, or hardening from simpler alternatives such as loss, reward, salience, memory, or convention.
```

---

### 4.6 Insert Block for Open Tensions

```markdown
### Core 24 Review-Risk Guardrail

Core 24 introduces a major framing advantage but also a review risk: if floor replacement is presented as “SRT explains everything,” the theory becomes vulnerable to the charge of unfalsifiable over-extension. The correct framing is narrower: SRT explains why multiple domains can share a selection-manifestation-hardening loop, and it remains accountable to comparative predictions and failure conditions.
```

## 5. Failure Conditions

To avoid becoming merely rhetorical, Core 24 requires explicit failure conditions.

### F24-1 — `Ψ_f` Distinctiveness Failure

If `Ψ_f` produces no transition-cost signatures distinguishable from ordinary loss, prediction error, energy expenditure, or task difficulty, its operational role weakens.

### F24-2 — `d-value` Distinctiveness Failure

If `d-value` does not predict concern-weighted non-substitutability better than reward, preference, salience, or pain, its distinct theoretical role weakens.

### F24-3 — `L2` Hardening Failure

If `L2` hardening cannot be distinguished from ordinary memory, learned habit, convention, or environmental stability, its bridge role weakens.

### F24-4 — Cross-Scale Loop Failure

If the selection-manifestation-hardening loop cannot generate domain-specific discriminating predictions, SRT's cross-scale explanatory claim collapses into analogy.

### F24-5 — Normativity Failure

If purpose, value, morality, and frameworks cannot be modeled as selection constraints with identifiable consequences, the dynamic normativity claim remains philosophical interpretation rather than an operational bridge.

## 9. Do-Not-Merge Warnings

Do not merge the following formulations into canonical正文:

- “SRT explains everything.”
- “All previous theories are built on a wrong floor and therefore false.”
- “Morality is just dynamics.”
- “SRT is not objectively measurable, therefore it cannot be falsified.”
- “`Ψ_f` is exactly Fisher information.”
- “`d-value` is exactly preference intensity.”
- “`L2` is exactly memory, habit, or social convention.”

Preferred replacements:

- “SRT explains cross-domain connectivity through selection, manifestation, and hardening.”
- “Existing theories may remain locally valid while inheriting incomplete object-first assumptions.”
- “SRT explains how moral normativity gains constraint power through cross-subject hardening.”
- “SRT requires structural, convergent, and comparative validation.”
- “Fisher geometry is a local projection or proxy of selection friction.”
- “Preference intensity is one possible projection, not the definition, of concern-weighted non-substitutability.”
- “Memory, habit, and convention are domain projections of hardening, not the full ontology of `L2`.”
