# Claim Level Guide

This guide explains how to read claim roles in the frontstage structure. For full governance rules, use [`../Governance/SRT_CLAIM_LADDER.md`](../Governance/SRT_CLAIM_LADDER.md).

## Quick Distinctions

| Term | Meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| canonical | Current internal priority source for a definition, variable, or usage rule. | Repository-canonical does not automatically mean externally established natural law. |
| primitive axiom | Minimal starting commitment within the formal core. | Do not derive it from a bridge or public summary. |
| constitutive theorem | Core-level theorem generated from primitive commitments. | Requires formal anchor support; not supplied by analogy. |
| bridge hypothesis | Cross-domain mapping between SRT and another framework. | A bridge hypothesis is not canonical. |
| operational proxy | Measurable or model-facing approximation of an SRT construct. | A proxy is not canonical. |
| lab hypothesis | Test-facing claim or measurement proposal. | It remains a hypothesis until tested and interpreted through formal anchors. |
| public summary | Reader-facing simplification or outreach formulation. | A public summary is not canonical. |
| governance rule | Editing, citation, or claim-discipline rule. | It constrains repository work; it is not itself theory content. |

## Hard Boundaries

- Bridge hypotheses do not define the core.
- Operational proxies do not replace canonical variables.
- Public summaries do not upgrade claim level.
- Governance rules keep edits disciplined, but they do not create new theory commitments.
- External convergence can strengthen or pressure a formal anchor, but it should not bypass the anchor.
