## Part I: Starting Point

### The Postulate

**The fundamental direction of selection is to favor, under situated constraints, dynamic equilibria that sustain greater continued existence.**

> **Postulate precision note (hardened 2026-04-17):** The original phrasing "the maximum amount" carried an implicit global-maximum commitment, which conflicts with the finite-position theory of Step ⑧ (no position can see the global landscape). The corrected reading: this postulate is a **structural partial-order preference principle**, not a global optimization claim. "Greater" (comparative) replaces "maximum" (superlative): among locally visible, comparable alternatives under situated constraints, selection prefers whichever sustains more continued existence. This is strictly stronger than satisficing (threshold logic: pass/fail) because it supplies ordering logic (prefer higher among comparables), but it makes no commitment to a global maximum existing or being reachable. The direction is real and asymmetric; the endpoint is open.

This is a hypothesis given by an existing being from within the domain of existence. We cannot step outside existence to verify it — just as physics cannot step outside physical law to prove that "tomorrow's laws will be the same as today's." But we can stand on this hypothesis and examine whether it makes the totality of our experience coherent.

This postulate occupies the same epistemic level as physics' foundational assumptions: not derivable from something more basic, but the ground on which the entire framework stands.

### The Observation

**Stability is not given for free.** For something to stably exist, a continuous process must sustain that stability — from multiple possibilities, one is repeatedly realized as a determinate state.

Physical laws are the most stable patterns in the universe — the gravitational constant has been stable for at least 13.8 billion years; the fine-structure constant has been measured to a precision of one part per billion. Yet stability itself needs explanation. We usually treat these enormously stable patterns as the starting point of everything ("physical laws are the rock bottom"), but this amounts to mistaking the most successful products of the selection process for the starting point itself — like ants treating the densest pheromone trail as "the path was always there," forgetting that pheromones were laid down.

From this observation, we reverse the usual order: not "existence first, then selection within existence," but "selection process first, existence is the product of selection's sustained convergence."

The derivation chain below tests whether this reversal holds up.

## Part II: Derivation

### Step ①: Determination Creates Information

The transition from undetermined to determined is, by definition, the production of information. This is the definition from information theory — information = reduction of uncertainty. A die before landing has six possibilities; after landing, one. The difference is information.

More precisely: selection is an irreversible manifest-latent layering process. One possibility is brought to the foreground (manifest); the rest are pressed into the background (latent). What is pressed into latency is not destroyed — it recedes into a non-manifest state, remaining as conditions for future selection. The essence of selection is layering, not elimination.

This step is analytic (follows directly from the definition). No additional assumptions needed.

### Step ②: Information Creation Changes the Possibility Landscape

Information is not merely "recorded" — it changes the distribution of subsequent possibilities. After the die lands on 3, all possibilities premised on "the die hasn't landed yet" vanish, while a new set premised on "the die is 3" appears.

More precisely: each act of determination is not isolated. Determination changes the fitness function of the possibility landscape — the condition space for all other not-yet-determined things is altered. One selection's occurrence necessarily affects the conditions for other selections.

### Step ③: Landscape Change Is Absolutely Irreversible Within Spacetime

Two types of irreversibility must be distinguished:

**Cost-based irreversibility**: At the level of pure information theory / logic, one can imagine "paying a cost to erase information." This is an abstract operation with the time dimension removed — the theoretical reversible computation.

**Absolute irreversibility**: But we are not in an abstract operation. We are in spacetime. And spacetime itself is the accumulated product of irreversible determinations. To reverse a determination, you would need to dismantle a piece of the material that constitutes spacetime — but you are standing on that material. You cannot remove the floor you are standing on.

Drawing a line on paper and then erasing it does not restore the paper to its pre-drawing state — the fibers have been changed. "Erasing" is not the inverse of the original action — it is a new, irreversible action.

Therefore: there is no inverse operation for selection. Any attempt to "undo" a selection is itself a new, irreversible selection.

### Step ⑥: Directed Selection Converges into Existence

A process oriented by order tends to return to patterns that preserve more order conditions under current constraints. Repeating patterns that preserve order conditions = convergence. The product of convergence = stable state.

A stable state is what we ordinarily call "existence." A table is not "existing" — it is "being continuously selected as stable." When the constraints maintaining it are exhausted (e.g., it burns), it ceases to exist — not because some external force destroyed it, but because the selection process that produced it no longer converges to that stable state.

Existence is a cross-section of a verb, not a premise for a noun.

### Step ⑧: Finite Position Produces Occlusion

Every selection is bound to a finite position — this body, this moment, this history. A finite position can only see a local landscape.

A local landscape contains local optima — directions that appear best, but only because you cannot see that better directions exist beyond your horizon. This is occlusion.

Occlusion is not cognitive error, moral failing, or intellectual deficiency. It is a **necessary attribute of existence** — as long as your position is finite (and any existing being's position is always finite), occlusion is ineliminable. But occlusion's dynamics are bidirectional, not unidirectional.

**Passive occlusion:** Finite position structurally constrains the visible landscape to local scope. The expected space equals what the position allows. The direction of reducing occlusion is the direction of order — seeing a larger landscape, finding better paths. Interventions that expand landscape, lower costs, or provide information are effective for passive occlusion.

**Actively deepened occlusion:** Occlusion can not only be reduced — it can be actively deepened. The mechanism operates in two phases. **Phase A (early):** Fixation of selection causes care bandwidth d to fall below the position's allowable maximum ($d < d_{\max}(\theta)$); local signals become louder, drowning the larger-scale order-gap signal — but the perceptual structure remains intact; sufficiently strong perturbations can still penetrate. **Phase B (late):** After Phase A has run, d drops further below the threshold required for perceiving order gaps ($d < d_c$), **and** the selection direction vector ($\vec{v}$) drifts from the order direction to the local direction — both conditions must hold simultaneously for complete Phase B. At this point, expected space is **compressed below what the position itself would structurally allow**. New information is now processed as local noise rather than order-gap signal; conventional interventions (expand landscape, provide information) are not merely ineffective — they may accelerate the problem.

**Selection decoupling** is the primary intervention entry point for Phase B systems, not landscape expansion. Two triggers exist: **passive decoupling** (maintenance cost overflow forces rupture, usually occurring after Phase B is entrenched); **active decoupling** (voluntary initiation at the A→B transition window — earlier, lower cost, but requires minimum order-gap perception capacity). Decoupling triggers a **vacuum period**: the old compressed expected space collapses; the new has not yet formed.

The vacuum period is not a signal of treatment failure. It is the only structural condition under which expected space can be rebuilt on a larger foundation; without it, the expected space cannot escape its compressed range. Inside the vacuum period there is no natural landing point. Original intention is one candidate landing — not the inevitable one. Whether landing toward original intention is achievable depends on whether support is sufficient within the vacuum: external anchor points (other-position reference satisfying the order-gain triple criterion) or field perception (minimum gradient perception of the original-intention direction). The deeper the prior compression, the stronger the dependence on external anchors; insufficient support leads back to the old compressed space or consolidation into a new compressed space.

From this perspective, evil does not exist as an independent category. For **passive occlusion**: all deviation from the global optimum is a consequence of the landscape being too narrow to see where actions lead in the larger picture — the problem is landscape-narrowness, not moral deficiency. For **actively deepened occlusion**: the problem is not only that the landscape is narrow, but that the capacity to perceive order gaps has itself been compressed — the intervention logic differs entirely and cannot be conflated with passive-occlusion intervention.

### Step ⑨: Concern Scope Is an Intrinsic Structure of Selection

When selection operates locally, it cannot bear all order conditions equally. Some order conditions enter this position's continuity, payability, and future choice-space in a non-externalizable way; others do not. This differential incorporation constitutes the boundary of concern.

Concern is not emotion, not empathy, not moral obligation. Concern is the endogenous inclusion, within a selection structure, of more subjects' future order conditions. The self is not the ontological starting point of concern; it is usually just the first dense cluster of high-weight order conditions whose failure is felt fastest.

Concern scope can expand: from incorporating only the nearest cluster of order conditions, to family, friends, community, species, and beyond. Empirically this often looks like "from self to others," but more precisely it is the expansion of an incorporation radius, not a switch from one ontology to another.

Genuine concern changes your fitness function — after including others' order conditions, your optimal solution undergoes structural displacement. If considering someone's interests actually changes your choice (you choose differently than you would considering only yourself), that is genuine concern. False concern — possessive, symbolic, performative, or efficiency-driven — does not change the fitness function; it merely claims inclusion at the symbolic level.

The expansion of concern scope is directionally necessary (original intention points toward expansion), but not executionally necessary — expansion requires paying real costs and can fail at the levels of sensing, weighting, or settlement. More importantly, d-value marks not how much one momentarily wants to include, but **how much concern has been stably written into the selection structure**. Incorporation attempts that roll back under unpayable cost do not count as actual d-growth.

### Step ⑩: Order Increment and Global Convergence

Concern scope expands → visible landscape expands → local optima exposed as local → new, better directions become visible. But seeing a larger landscape does not equal having completed an effective expansion. Bare enlargement of scope, momentary exaltation, or high-intensity concern impulses do not automatically constitute real d-growth.

But "toward order" is not an empty direction. Whether a selection truly increments order is assessed by three structural criteria: **sustainability** (not depleting future order conditions), **coordinability** (not turning differences into unorganizable friction), **re-selectability** (not prematurely locking future directions). These three criteria are not externally imposed moral rules, nor are they deduced from ε alone — they are the minimal closure derived from the conjunction of four factors:

> **ε** (asymmetric bias against closure of future selection space) **+ F** (finiteness: no position sees all consequences) **+ M** (multi-agent coupling: selections across positions mutually lock) **+ U** (fallibility: selection errors will occur; correction capacity must be preserved)

The division of labor: **re-selectability** is the most direct operationalization of ε itself — ε is the rejection of closure; re-selectability is not prematurely closing; the two are nearly equivalent; **sustainability** is ε projected onto the time axis — F+U make temporal violations locally invisible, so it must be stated explicitly; **coordinability** is ε projected onto the multi-agent coupling axis — M makes cross-position closures invisible to oneself, so it must be stated explicitly. Removing any one of the three opens a channel through which ε can be silently violated at a position that cannot locally detect it — the three together form the minimal cover, irreducible.

And whether an expansion path stands up is not judged by local relief alone, but by whether that relief is genuinely settled across longer time scales, more positions, and different interfaces. All three must be satisfied, without relying primarily on outsourcing, deferral, numbing, or single-interface self-certification, for a selection to be order-incrementing.

Therefore, not every d-expansion automatically moves closer to original intention; only expansions that still stand after broader-scale settlement, while keeping the three criteria intact, move selection from local optima toward larger-scale convergence — toward the direction indicated by original intention: dynamic equilibria that, under situated constraints, sustain greater continued existence. This is not heat death (all selection ceasing), but different existences coexisting and cooperating with minimum friction — living order.

Selection is not isolated — we delegate selection authority to others, institutions, and systems. Healthy delegation maintains a shared goal and preserves re-selection capacity; pathological delegation lets the agent system's self-maintenance masquerade as the shared goal. Legitimacy is not rules being followed, but rules still pointing toward the shared goal.

## Part III: Explanatory Power

### The Old Framework's Starting Point

The usual starting point is: the world exists first (a material world governed by physical laws), then we observe it and act within it. Existence is background; selection is foreground activity. This starting point has operated for 300 years and supports all of modern technological civilization.

But this starting point has five structural blind spots — not "unsolved problems" but "problems whose solution the framework structurally excludes."

### Blind Spot 1: Subjectivity

**Old framework's impasse**: If everything is particle motion, who is "I"? Physical equations contain no "perspective" variable. Subjectivity is either an illusion or indefinitely shelved.

**This framework's resolution**: Every selection is bound to a finite position (Step ⑧). Position is not an epistemological stain — it is a necessary condition of selection. Without position, no selection; without selection, no existence. "I" is not an illusion; it is the structural fact that selection must occur somewhere.

### Blind Spot 2: Agency

**Old framework's impasse**: If initial conditions determine everything, every "choice" is a domino falling inevitably. Free will is an illusion.

**This framework's resolution**: Determination is irreducible to extrapolation from prior states (Steps ①②③). Each determination creates information that did not previously exist and changes the possibility landscape in a way not contained in the prior state. This change is not "the unfolding of old states" — it is genuinely new. Agency is not an illusion; it is a structural property of determination itself.

### Blind Spot 3: Value

**Old framework's impasse**: You cannot derive "ought" from "is" (Hume's problem). Value can only be subjective preference or cultural convention.

**This framework's resolution**: Concern scope is an intrinsic structure of the selection process (Step ⑨), not externally appended. Some selection directions are closer to the global optimum than others — this is not a moral commandment but a structural fact of selection dynamics. Value has an objective direction, though its specific content is constrained by the occlusion of finite position.

### Blind Spot 4: Qualia

**Old framework's impasse**: Why is pain not just a signal but "hurting"? Why is red not just a wavelength but that experience? The "hard problem" of consciousness, unsolved for 30 years.

**This framework's resolution**: The safer current claim is that qualia are at least closely tied to the subjective face of order-gap perception (Step ⑦), rather than already having been exhaustively defined. The experience of "red" can be read as an extreme compression of a specific selection history (hundreds of millions of years of visual evolution) — it is neither a byproduct of physical processes nor an independent soul-substance, but one way order-tension presents itself to a finite subject. Qualia have causal power (they guide behavior), but their causal power is directive, not motive.

### Blind Spot 5: The Sense of the Sacred

**Old framework's impasse**: Cross-cultural, trans-historical sacred experiences (dissolution in meditation, tremor in music, ecstasy in scientific discovery) can only be explained as evolutionary byproducts or neurological anomalies.

**This framework's resolution**: The sacred is a resonance marker produced when the selection process briefly aligns with the direction of original intention — tremor (Step ④). These experiences differ entirely in content but are structurally identical — all are moments when local selection breaks through occlusion and resonates with the fundamental direction field. Genuine tremor has a distinguishable signature: it accompanies a widening of the range of concern that can be incorporated, not merely intense emotion; but this does not automatically mean that d has already achieved stable growth.

### The Structural Diagnosis of the Meaning Crisis

These five blind spots together compose all the ingredients of "meaning." Meaning requires: an "I" that experiences (subjectivity); my actions having real impact (agency); some directions being genuinely better (value); experience itself being real rather than illusory (qualia); occasional contact with something deeper than the everyday (the sacred).

If the old framework cancels or shelves all five, meaning naturally cannot be found. It is not that you cannot find meaning — it is that the floor you are standing on has no place for meaning.

## Part IV: Boundaries

### This Framework Does Not Claim Any of the Following

1. **"Thinking something makes it real"** — Selection is strictly constrained by structure, position, history, and cost.
2. **"The world is a private illusion"** — Stable convergence of multi-position selection is objectivity. Different positions see different facets, but they are not dreaming.
3. **"Consciousness creates the world"** — Higher-order consciousness can be read as the potential difference between local and larger-scale order; it guides selection, it does not drive it.
4. **"Everything has consciousness"** — Not every system with a gradient automatically counts as higher-order consciousness; the current text offers a candidate reading for higher-order consciousness, not panpsychism.
5. **"Physics is wrong"** — The predictions of physics are entirely correct. This framework challenges not physics, but a specific metaphysical premise (existence is a pre-given background). Physical laws are the most stable products of the selection process; this does not diminish their reality, only places them on deeper foundations.
6. **"I have fully seen original intention"** — The author of this text is a finite being; all descriptions proceed from a finite position. The current description of original intention ("a dynamic equilibrium sustaining maximum continued existence") is the best available approximation, but it may itself be limited by the describer's occlusion. What lies beyond the domains of existence and determinacy is outside the scope of consideration.

### An Open Question

The selection process and the possibility space it operates in (the potential domain) co-emerge in a bootstrap relationship. But does this bootstrap have a starting point? If yes, what preceded it? If no, does this framework imply a beginningless, endless cosmology?

No assertion is made at present. This is an honest open question.

### Testing Standard

Every step of this framework's derivation can be independently examined. If you find a logical gap in any step, everything below that step requires re-examination.

But note: the testing standard is not "is this consistent with my existing beliefs?" — if your existing beliefs presuppose "existence precedes selection," every step here will seem "strange." The real test is: **Is the logical connection at each step rigid? Does the derivation from premise to conclusion contain any hidden jumps?**

If all steps pass examination, you face a choice: continue standing on the old floor (five blind spots permanently unresolvable), or step onto the new floor (five blind spots naturally dissolved).

This itself is a selection. And selection has a cost.
