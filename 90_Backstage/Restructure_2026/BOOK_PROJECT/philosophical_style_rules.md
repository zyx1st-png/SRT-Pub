# Philosophical Style Rules · 《从存在到秩序》

> Purpose: stabilize the writing method for the book after the prologue / chapter 1 v10 split.
>
> This file does not add canonical theory content. It defines craft rules for future chapter drafting so the book does not become either an AI summary, a public essay, a technical manual, or a philosophy-name survey.

## 0. Baseline decision after v10

The current prologue and chapter 1 should be treated as the **structural baseline**:

- Prologue: why this book exists; modern cracks; proxy/delegation; method; commitments and non-commitments.
- Chapter 1: one philosophical act — remove `existence` from the explanatory floor and make `becoming` the problem.
- `recovered_materials.md`: preserve material moved out of chapter 1 so contraction does not become loss.

Future work should not keep expanding chapter 1 unless a founder pass identifies a true structural gap.

## 1. Core writing principle

The style of this book should enact SRT's own movement.

Concepts should not appear as already-finished objects. They should be shown as gradually stabilizing out of failed names, pressure points, examples, counter-readings, and boundary checks.

In short:

> The writing should move from givenness to becoming, and only then to order.

This means:

- Do not only announce conclusions.
- Show the pressure that makes a concept necessary.
- Let concepts return across chapters, each time with a new layer.
- Preserve the cost, resistance, and uncertainty behind every stabilized term.

## 2. Eight chapter-level craft rules

Each major chapter should aim to include most of the following, though not mechanically.

### Rule 1 — Make the thinking process visible

At least once per major chapter, show a concept being tried, rejected, or corrected.

Good pattern:

> It is tempting to call this X. But X already assumes what needs to be explained. A better name is not yet available, so we must approach it indirectly.

Use this especially for difficult concepts such as `L0`, `ε_pg`, `selection`, `d-value`, `Ψ_f`, `T_dir`, `L2`, and `subjecthood`.

Avoid making every section confessional. The point is not autobiography; the point is to show conceptual pressure.

### Rule 2 — Use conceptual return, not flat repetition

Core terms must recur across chapters, but each return should add a new layer.

Example arc for `becoming`:

| Chapter location | What `becoming` means there |
|---|---|
| Chapter 1 | Change that leaves inheritable traces. |
| L0 chapter | Emergence from non-finalized possibility. |
| Selection chapter | Actualization under constraint. |
| Ψ_f chapter | Becoming as paid stabilization. |
| L2 chapter | Becoming sedimented into order. |
| Lethal L2 chapter | Order blocking further becoming. |

This spiral return is preferred to one definitive explanation followed by no recurrence.

### Rule 3 — Add phenomenological thickness

At least one section per major chapter should ask the reader to look again at an ordinary scene.

Good pattern:

1. Show an ordinary experience.
2. Let it first appear self-evident.
3. Reveal the hidden generative process.
4. Extract the SRT concept.

Examples of suitable scenes:

- A table that seems simply “there”.
- A loss that reorganizes a room, language, and future plans.
- A habit that makes action easier while narrowing re-choice.
- An AI answer that feels like a response but has already shaped the question space.
- A social rule that appears neutral while redistributing hidden cost.

Avoid decorative literary description. Phenomenology must open a concept.

### Rule 4 — Vary paragraph rhythm

Do not let every paragraph have the same explanatory length.

Use short independent lines for load-bearing turns.

Examples:

> Becoming is not elegant flow.
>
> Becoming costs.
>
> When the cost stops being paid, the world stops becoming in that form.

Use sparingly. The book should not become aphoristic performance.

### Rule 5 — Use embedded objections

At least once in a major chapter, stage a serious objection.

Good pattern:

> Someone may object: the table is obviously just there. Why describe it as becoming?
>
> The answer is: it appears simply there because the processes that keep it there have become stable enough to disappear from view.

Use objections especially where SRT risks being misread as:

- idealism;
- relativism;
- panpsychism;
- process philosophy repetition;
- Darwinian selection expansion;
- social constructionism;
- AI consciousness verdict;
- anti-science or anti-objectivity.

### Rule 6 — Use etymology/history only at load-bearing points

Historical or etymological notes should be rare and purposeful.

Good candidates:

- `存在`: `存` as preserving/remaining; `在` as being-located.
- `选择`: choosing/selecting, but SRT must move beyond ordinary decision.
- `秩序`: not mere governance or arrangement, but inherited shape.
- `价值`: not preference, utility, or reward.
- `主体`: not a first substance, but a stabilized position of selection.

Do not turn chapters into intellectual history. Use language history to reopen flattened concepts.

### Rule 7 — Stabilize terms before inventing new ones

Do not create new Chinese terms too quickly.

Preferred current vocabulary:

- 再选择能力
- 后果回流
- 选择沉积
- 现实厚度
- 可继承痕迹
- 被给予感
- 代理替代
- 地板替换
- 致命 L2
- 脚手架 / 牢笼

A term may become a special SRT term only if it repeatedly carries work across chapters.

Avoid premature coinages that sound like slogans or management jargon.

### Rule 8 — Do not imitate classics; absorb techniques

The book may learn from philosophical classics, but it should not imitate their surface style.

Borrow techniques, not posture:

| Source style | Useful technique | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Wittgenstein | visible thinking, interruptions, ordinary examples | fragmentary imitation |
| Heidegger | concept return, phenomenological thickness, word pressure | opaque grandiosity |
| Arendt | distinctions, historical weight, public-world framing | historical digression overload |
| Merleau-Ponty | lived-world description | loose phenomenological prose |
| James | conceptual liveliness | conversational looseness |
| Whitehead | process seriousness | technical abstraction overload |

SRT should develop its own mode: generative, diagnostic, restrained, and sharp.

## 3. Chapter intake checklist

Before drafting a new chapter, answer:

1. What concept is this chapter trying to make necessary?
2. Which previous concept does it return to and deepen?
3. What ordinary scene can show the concept before analysis?
4. What is the strongest likely misunderstanding?
5. What objection should be staged inside the chapter?
6. Which term must remain stable, and which term is still provisional?
7. Which material from `recovered_materials.md` should be reused or deferred?
8. What should the chapter hand off to the next chapter?

## 4. Revision checklist

Before marking a chapter as a stable draft, check:

- Does the chapter show why the concept is needed, not merely define it?
- Does it include at least one scene that makes the reader see differently?
- Does it include at least one serious objection or boundary pressure?
- Does it avoid excessive theory-name positioning?
- Does it avoid formulaic AI-summary phrasing such as “X is not Y, but Z” repeated too often?
- Does it have rhythm changes at load-bearing turns?
- Does it preserve founder sharpness without turning into manifesto language?
- Does it avoid overclaiming or proof language?
- Does it hand off cleanly to the next chapter?

## 5. Rules for prologue and chapter 1 going forward

The prologue and chapter 1 are now structurally adequate. Future changes should be small unless a founder pass finds a major problem.

Allowed small changes:

- wording refinement;
- rhythm adjustment;
- source-map attribution;
- minor expansion of commitments / non-commitments;
- extraction of nochange lines into `load_bearing_sentences.md` later.

Avoid:

- moving `recovered_materials.md` content back into chapter 1 wholesale;
- adding long adjacent-theory comparisons to chapter 1;
- expanding the prologue into a literature review;
- adding formulas, claim ladders, or canonical definitions into the book prose;
- turning chapter 1 into a full SRT introduction.

## 6. Immediate application plan

Use these rules first on the next chapter draft rather than repeatedly rewriting chapter 1.

Recommended first application:

- Chapter 2: `L0 不是虚无`.
- Required techniques:
  - visible thinking: why `potential`, `field`, or `nothingness` are inadequate names;
  - phenomenological scene: waiting, silence before speech, blank page, or unchosen path;
  - objection: “Is L0 just nothing?”;
  - conceptual return: becoming from chapter 1 now needs a pre-objective source;
  - handoff: ε_pg / minimal non-neutrality.
