# Objecthood as Reselectability —— 对象性作为再选择能力的元标准

> **File status**: non-canonical research seed.  
> This file does **not** create a new canonical definition, theorem, metric, or moral doctrine.  
> It records a candidate SRT advantage-line: evaluation should not stop at an object's physical properties, performance, utility, or social value; it should audit whether the object still retains the capacity to continue as an object by remaining reselectable, reorganizable, consequence-bearing, and reopenable.

---

## 0. One-line thesis

> **表现只是对象的输出；再选择能力才是对象性的生命线。**

More precise:

> SRT does not merely add robustness or long-term performance to an ordinary performance metric. It treats reselectability as a **meta-standard** for judging whether an object remains capable of continuing as an object: distinguishable, consequence-bearing, recoverable, reorganizable, and open to reselection.

This is not a replacement for all concrete standards. Physical properties, causal laws, functional performance, social value, and long-term utility still matter. SRT asks a prior audit question:

> Are those properties, performances, values, and utilities enhancing the object's reselectability, or are they being purchased by compressing, replacing, externalizing, or hiding the loss of reselectability?

---

## 1. What is being shifted

Many evaluation regimes begin from an already-given object and ask:

- What properties does it have?
- What laws govern it?
- How well does it perform?
- What utility or social value does it produce?
- How robust is it under perturbation?

SRT shifts the question downward:

> What allows this to continue being an object at all?

An object is not merely a bundle of properties or a performance unit. It is a maintained closure that remains distinguishable through perturbation, receives consequence return, carries some continuity, and can still be reorganized rather than merely replaced by a script.

Therefore, the deepest evaluative question is not only:

> Does it work?

but:

> Does it remain reselectable while working?

---

## 2. Layer reading: L0 / L1 / L2

### L0 — existence as non-self-erasing sustainable difference

At the L0 side, the claim is **not** that every existent thing has moral status. The safer claim is:

> Existence is not a neutral given; what exists is a sustainable difference that has not immediately erased the conditions of its continued determinability and differentiation.

This is a proto-normative floor only in the constitutive sense: selection-as-selection requires a minimum non-self-erasing direction. It is **not** proto-morality, proto-experience, or cosmic moral bias.

### L1 — objecthood as maintained closure

At the L1 side, objecthood begins to matter as objecthood when a pattern can maintain closure through perturbation:

- distinguishability;
- consequence return;
- identity continuity;
- recoverability;
- reorganization;
- reopening of paths after partial foreclosure.

Here reselectability is not a superficial option count. It is the capacity of the object to continue as a consequence-bearing closure without being reduced to a dead script, absorbed by an external scaffold, or locked into a single unreopenable path.

### L2 — performance, utility, value narratives, and social scripts

At the L2 side, systems can become highly functional, stable, efficient, or socially valued while quietly compressing reselectability. This is the zone where ordinary performance metrics are most likely to mislead.

A system can be:

- high-performing but brittle;
- stable but unreopenable;
- efficient but appeal-proof;
- socially valued but externally costly;
- low-friction but no longer connected to live selection.

SRT's advantage is to reread L2 success through the L1/L0 question:

> Has this success preserved or destroyed the conditions under which the object can continue to reselect?

## 3. Ontology, value, and morality

### 3.1 Ontology

> **Objecthood is not a given entity-status; it is maintained reselectable closure.**

This does not mean every object chooses like an agent. It means that objecthood itself depends on maintained conditions of continued distinction, consequence, and reorganization.

### 3.2 Value theory

> **Value is not reducible to current performance, utility, preference, or social recognition.**

A thing becomes value-relevant when its loss, replacement, blockage, or compression forces a non-trivial reorganization of future choice, identity continuity, or consequence return. This converges with the d-value line, but this file does not redefine canonical d-value.

### 3.3 Morality

> **Morality opens when one position's selection can irreversibly compress another position's reselectability.**

This is not a claim that all beings have equal moral status. It is a claim that all evaluative orders must pass an anti-foreclosure audit: whose future reselectability is being preserved, compressed, hidden, or externalized?

---

## 4. Meta-standard, not the only answer

Reselectability should be treated as a **meta-standard**, not a mechanical maximization rule.

It does **not** mean:

- more options are always better;
- all closure is bad;
- every stable structure is pathological;
- every object has equal moral standing;
- destructive or parasitic systems must be preserved merely because they can continue;
- reselectability replaces all other concrete criteria.

The right question is:

> Which reselectability, at which scale, for which closure, under which consequence-return path, and with what externalization to other positions?

A healthy closure may reduce local option count while increasing real future reselection capacity. A pathological closure may maximize current performance while quietly killing future reopening.

---

## 5. Evaluation audit questions

A practical SRT audit should ask:

1. **Real option-field**: Are the remaining options real, reachable, and actionable, or merely nominal?
2. **Recoverability**: Can foreclosed paths be restored at payable cost?
3. **Consequence return**: Do consequences return to the selecting closure, or are they absorbed / displaced by external scaffolds?
4. **Reorganization**: Can the object change its structure after failure, or only repeat a fixed script?
5. **Re-entry / appeal**: Can excluded positions re-enter the process, trigger review, or reopen the boundary?
6. **Externalization**: Is current performance purchased by transferring foreclosure, cost, or risk to other positions?
7. **Hidden debt**: Is friction being lowered by real support, or merely by masking future Ψ_f burden?
8. **T_dir availability**: Can the system read and reorient its own direction of selection, or is it blind to its lock-in?

These questions are not a finished metric. They are a bridge toward one.

---

## 6. Relation to RL / toy simulations

The RL-style toy result is useful only as an illustration of the dynamics half:

> reward-health can dissociate from reselectability-health.

A system may achieve high current reward while its option-diversity / re-sampling capacity has already collapsed. Under future shift, that system may become brittle. This supports the narrow hypothesis that re-sampling capacity can act as an early warning signal for performance purchased by foreclosure.

But this file is not an RL theory. It does not claim:

- SRT = RL;
- RL lacks exploration or robustness concepts;
- diversity = d-value;
- toy simulation validates SRT ontology or morality;
- the dynamics half proves the moral half.

The RL interface is a measurement wedge, not the foundation.

---

## 7. Relation to universal morality

This line supports a restrained version of universal morality:

> Not every object has equal moral status, but every evaluative system must answer to the meta-question of reselectability.

The universal element is not a universal moral property attached to all things. It is a universal audit:

> Does this evaluation preserve, compress, or externalize the conditions under which relevant positions can continue to become objects, bear consequences, and reselect?

This avoids both extremes:

- **cosmic moral realism overload**: "the universe itself has a moral direction";
- **pure social construction**: "morality is only whatever a society calls moral".

SRT's middle claim:

> moral constraint arises when selection across positions threatens irreversible foreclosure of reselectability.

---

## 8. Why this is not ordinary pragmatism

Ordinary pragmatism asks whether something works. Long-term pragmatism asks whether it keeps working. Robustness theory asks whether it keeps function under perturbation.

SRT asks a deeper question:

> Does the thing retain the conditions for continuing as a reselectable object while it works?

This matters because a system can keep working by ceasing to be alive to selection. It can become a high-performance dead script.

SRT therefore distinguishes:

- performance from object-health;
- stability from reopenability;
- efficiency from consequence-bearing closure;
- social value from non-externalized reselectability;
- robustness of output from reorganization of objecthood.
