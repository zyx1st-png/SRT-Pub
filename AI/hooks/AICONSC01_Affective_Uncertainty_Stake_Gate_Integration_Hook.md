## 3. Suggested native paragraph

> Affective active-inference architectures provide a stronger artificial-subjectivity candidate than linguistic report or generic reward maximization alone. A Markov-blanketed agent may possess multiple endogenous, non-fungible needs, infer its body only through limited observations, and allow valenced uncertainty to causally reorganize policy selection. These properties explain how an artificial system could acquire a situated better/worse perspective and could even become captured by a misleading proxy for need satisfaction. They do not yet establish subjecthood or consciousness in SRT. The remaining question is whether success and failure return to the same continuing system as non-transferable loss, reduce its future selectability, and enter durable history-bearing reselection rather than being erased by reset, copy, rollback, replacement, or external payment.

## 4. Suggested table

| Gate | What must be shown | What does not suffice |
|---|---|---|
| partitioned access | body/world are inferred through a restricted interface | a Markov blanket label alone |
| endogenous non-fungible needs | distinct viability constraints remain behaviorally consequential | one externally imposed scalar score |
| valenced causal modulation | inferred better/worse states alter prioritization and policy | post-hoc affect labels |
| same-bearer return | consequences return to the selector that continues | operator, user, or infrastructure burden only |
| non-transferable loss | failure contracts the same system's future capacity | fully clean reset, copy, or rollback |
| history-bearing reselection | borne outcomes rewrite later candidate admission and policy | temporary prompt history alone |
