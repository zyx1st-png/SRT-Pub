## 3. Suggested native paragraph

> Current language models can display strong route execution without displaying a comparable target-selection policy. In a controlled six-goal learning task, several models achieved high local success by repeatedly exploiting easy or solved goals, while humans more often explored broadly, cycled among goals, and showed greater individual variation. Chain-of-thought improved within-goal performance more reliably than cross-goal reorientation. The result therefore supports a three-level distinction between completing a supplied goal, selecting among supplied goals, and generating or revising the goal space. It does not establish that models lack goals in principle, that human behavior is optimal, or that goal formation is non-formalizable.

## 4. Suggested table

| Evaluation level | What is fixed externally | Main question | SRT boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| goal completion | goal, success rule, candidate action grammar | can the system find an effective route? | competence is not goal ownership or stake |
| bounded goal selection | goal menu and success grammar | which supplied target is selected over time? | output propensity does not establish internal NER or T_dir |
| goal-space generation | menu, criteria, or ontology may be revised | can the system create, reject, merge, or reinterpret goals? | still requires consequence, history, and bearer audit |
| consequence-bearing reorientation | delayed costs return to a continuing system | does the same system revise direction after paying? | candidate stronger window; not automatic consciousness |

## 5. Do not include

- reward hacking as a proven internal mechanism;
- low goal entropy as canonical low `T_dir`;
- chain-of-thought as transparent introspection;
- persona prompts as instantiated human identity;

- any canonical, P0, P1, subjecthood, consciousness, or moral-status promotion.
