# Evidence Card: Electromagnetic Field Resonance as a Candidate Interface for Binding, Criticality, and SRT Field-Anchoring

This draft card records an external convergence and pressure case from electromagnetic-field theories of consciousness, computational neuroscience, neural criticality, bioelectricity, and philosophy of mind. It is not canonical, not accepted evidence, and not proof of SRT.

The source article is a peer-reviewed hypothesis-and-theory paper. Its role here is structural convergence and bridge-building, not direct validation of SRT ontology.

## 1. External finding

Tam Hunt's article, *The goo that binds us: how field resonance solves neuroscience's binding and criticality problems* (*Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience*, 2026), argues that two major neuroscience puzzles should be reframed through continuous electromagnetic field dynamics rather than discrete spike-centered computation:

1. the binding problem: how distributed neural processes produce unified conscious experience;
2. the criticality problem: how the brain maintains scale-free, near-critical dynamics at the edge between order and chaos.

The paper contrasts two styles of explanation:

- `prickly` neuroscience: discrete spikes, synapses, modules, digital computation, binding circuits, and point-to-point transmission;
- `gooey` neuroscience: continuous electromagnetic fields, resonance, volumetric propagation, cross-frequency coupling, field coherence, and analog field computation.

The central claim is that binding and criticality become less puzzling if conscious processing is treated as a continuous field pattern generated by brain-body dynamics. The article argues that electromagnetic field patterns can naturally integrate information through spatial overlap, superposition, interference, cross-frequency coupling, and resonance.

Key source claims include:

- weak electric fields can causally entrain neural spike timing at physiologically relevant thresholds;
- ephaptic field propagation through brain tissue may be hundreds to thousands of times faster than ordinary neural transmission;
- cross-frequency coupling provides a natural field-level mechanism for integrating slow global coordination and fast local processing;
- neural avalanches and 1/f-like scaling may arise from multi-scale electromagnetic resonance cascades rather than from fine-tuned synaptic weights alone;
- Michael Levin's bioelectric pattern-memory work provides an evolutionary and conceptual bridge from morphogenetic bioelectric coordination to neural consciousness;
- artificial consciousness may require field-like, embodied, continuous integration infrastructure rather than pure digital computation alone.

For SRT, the paper is best treated as a candidate external bridge for explaining how an `L1` phenomenal slice might be field-like, continuous, body-supported, and globally integrated without being assembled from isolated local informational parts after the fact.

## 3. SRT construct involved

Primary SRT anchors:

- `L1` field anchoring: the article supports a cautious bridge in which unified conscious manifestation is closer to a continuous field pattern than to a late-stage binding of discrete representational fragments.
- `L0/L1/L2`: the paper helps separate latent dynamical possibility, manifest field-stabilization, and longer-term infrastructure / pattern-memory constraints.
- `L2` hardening: Levin-style bioelectric pattern memory provides a candidate bridge for stable morphogenetic setpoints, embodied memory, and target-state maintenance across cellular turnover.
- `Psi_f`: maintaining coherent field dynamics, cross-frequency coupling, and critical balance may provide a physiological projection of friction, payability, or organizational burden.
- `d-value`: field patterns become SRT-relevant only when they are coupled to stake, concern, consequence return, or future-option preservation for an embodied system.
- `T_dir`: phase, amplitude, cross-frequency coupling, field gradients, and critical-state transitions may provide direction-readable traces of how a system is being steered.
- `G_hat_theta`: the operator-state interface may be partly implemented through brain-body field infrastructure rather than through spikes alone.
- relational selection: field superposition and resonance provide an external model of behaviorally relevant relations that are not reducible to isolated node activations.
- subjecthood boundary: the article pressures SRT to distinguish mere physical field coherence from subject-involving concern and reselectability.
- AI consciousness boundary: the article supports a cautious contrast between digital access / report and embodied field-like integration, without proving that digital AI cannot ever be conscious.

This card is especially relevant to the SRT claim that conscious reality is not simply a set of discrete contents later combined into unity. A selected L1 slice may be generated as an already-unified, concern-conditioned field of manifestation.

## 4. Support type

Structural convergence / bridge-support candidate / operational-proxy candidate / pressure case.

The structural convergence is:

> conscious unity may arise from continuous, body-supported, multi-scale field dynamics rather than from post-hoc assembly of discrete neural contents.

This maps naturally onto SRT's distinction between a selected / anchored reality slice and a later reportable description of that slice.

The operational-proxy candidate is that measurable field variables may serve as proxies for some SRT-facing interfaces:

- cross-frequency coupling as a candidate proxy for multi-scale integration;
- phase / amplitude relationships as candidate `T_dir`-like directional traces;
- field coherence and perturbation sensitivity as candidate L1 anchoring / stability indicators;
- criticality and avalanche structure as possible signatures of open-yet-stabilized option-space dynamics;
- metabolic / energetic maintenance of coherent field regimes as a candidate physiological projection of `Psi_f`.

The pressure case is that electromagnetic field theory may explain binding and criticality through ordinary biophysics without requiring SRT's stronger selection-first ontology.

## 5. Evidence level: E0-E5

E2 = structural convergence.

Reason for E2: the source provides a strong conceptual and mechanistic bridge between continuous field dynamics, binding, criticality, bioelectric memory, and AI-consciousness boundaries. It also collects empirical anchors from field entrainment, ephaptic propagation, cross-frequency coupling, neural avalanches, and bioelectric pattern memory.

Reason not to rate E3 yet: the source is primarily a hypothesis-and-theory synthesis. It does not define a validated SRT-specific measurement protocol for `d-value`, `Psi_f`, `T_dir`, subjecthood, reselectability, or L1 anchoring. It also does not directly test SRT predictions.

## 6. Why it matters

This card matters because it gives SRT a plausible neuroscience-facing interface for the unity of conscious manifestation.

The SRT translation is:

> Electromagnetic field resonance provides a candidate implementation layer for L1 field-anchoring: a selected phenomenal slice may be generated as a continuous, globally integrated, body-supported field pattern rather than assembled from independent representational fragments.

This helps SRT express several difficult ideas more concretely.

### 6.1 Binding as field-anchoring rather than post-hoc assembly

The paper argues that the binding problem arises partly because standard neuroscience begins with separate modules and then asks how they are combined. SRT can translate this into its own terms: unity is not necessarily produced by combining already-independent contents; it may arise because L1 manifestation is anchored as an already-relational field.

The useful SRT move is:

> binding is not late integration of discrete objects; binding is the field-like form of manifestation itself.

This supports an SRT-friendly distinction between:

- reportable parts of experience;
- the prior phenomenal field in which those parts are already jointly present.

### 6.2 Field dynamics as a possible `G_hat_theta` implementation layer

The article does not define SRT's operator, but it gives a candidate biological substrate for operator-state coupling. Neural spikes may maintain and modulate field infrastructure, while the field dynamics perform rapid integration across the brain-body system.

For SRT, this suggests a cautious implementation route:

> `G_hat_theta` may not be reducible to spikes, symbolic computation, or local node activity; in biological consciousness, it may depend on a field-infrastructure regime that allows global integration, local modulation, and state-dependent reanchoring.

### 6.3 `Psi_f` as field-maintenance burden, not identical to field energy

The article's emphasis on metabolic support, resonance, and critical balance gives SRT a possible projection for `Psi_f`: coherent field regimes must be maintained, stabilized, and repaired against noise, perturbation, and pathological runaway.

But the mapping must remain scoped:

- field energy is not identical to `Psi_f`;
- neural effort is not automatically `Psi_f`;
- criticality is not automatically selection cost;
- pathological disruption is not automatically foreclosure.

A stronger SRT bridge would require showing that changes in field coherence, cross-frequency coupling, or perturbational recovery track future-option burden, consequence return, or reselectability loss.

### 6.4 `d-value` cannot be inferred from field coherence alone

The article is valuable precisely because it pressures SRT not to overclaim. A coherent field may integrate information without thereby having concern. A standing wave, an antenna field, or a synchronized circuit is not automatically a subject.

For SRT, the `d-value` test should ask whether a field regime is coupled to:

- non-substitutable system continuity;
- future-option preservation or loss;
- consequence return to the same embodied system;
- recovery cost paid by the system itself;
- concern-weighted reselectability.

This makes the paper useful for AI consciousness boundaries: digital systems may show access, report, and integration, but SRT would still ask whether they have stake-coupled, self-maintaining, consequence-bearing field / body infrastructure.

### 6.5 Bioelectric pattern memory as `L2` hardening

The article's Levin section is especially important for SRT. Bioelectric patterns are described as ancient, cross-scale pattern-memory systems that guide morphogenesis and can persist across cellular changes.

SRT translation:

> bioelectric pattern memory is a candidate biological projection of `L2` hardening: a stabilized selection-history / target-state regime that constrains future L1 generation paths without being reducible to any single cell or molecule.

This makes the article useful for the book's life-consciousness bridge:

- morphogenetic setpoints as `L2` target-state constraints;
- regeneration as re-entry into a stabilized L2 attractor;
- cancer / malformation as possible governor or closure disruption;
- field-mediated repair as a biological example of future-shaping reselectability.

### 6.6 Criticality as open-yet-stabilized option-space

The source's account of criticality is useful for SRT because criticality resembles an open-yet-stabilized regime: not rigid order, not noise, but a region where transitions remain possible and coordination remains coherent.

A cautious SRT interpretation:

> neural criticality may be a physiological regime in which a system preserves high reselectability while paying the field-maintenance burden required for coherent L1 anchoring.

This should remain a lab hypothesis, not a canonical claim.

### 6.7 AI consciousness boundary

The article argues that pure digital computation may be insufficient for consciousness if biological consciousness depends on continuous field-like, embodied, nested, multi-scale infrastructure.

SRT should not use this as a blanket anti-AI-consciousness claim. The safer SRT use is:

> digital access and report are not the same as subject-involving L1 anchoring. Artificial consciousness would require more than high-dimensional representation or workspace access; it would need a self-maintaining architecture in which selection has consequence return, non-substitutable continuity, and payable future burden.

This pairs naturally with existing SRT cards on AI access and high-capacity / low-stake systems.

## 7. Alternative explanations

The same external ideas can be explained without SRT:

- EM field theories may explain binding through standard biophysics without requiring selection-first ontology.
- Cross-frequency coupling may be a communication / coordination mechanism rather than a consciousness substrate.
- Neural criticality may arise from generic dynamical systems, network topology, homeostatic plasticity, excitation-inhibition balance, or self-organized criticality.
- Field entrainment may modulate spike timing without making fields primary for consciousness.
- Binding may be explained by predictive processing, active inference, global workspace, recurrent processing, attention, or dynamical systems theory.
- Levin-style bioelectric pattern memory may already explain morphogenetic stability without SRT's `L2` vocabulary.
- Artificial consciousness debates may remain unresolved; current digital architectures may be insufficient for reasons unrelated to EM fields.
- The article's `goo vs prickles` framing may overstate the contrast between discrete spikes and continuous fields, since biological neural systems contain both.

The strongest alternative is field-theory reduction:

> electromagnetic field theory may explain binding, criticality, and bioelectric coordination through ordinary continuous biophysics, leaving no need for SRT-specific constructs such as `d-value`, `Psi_f`, reselectability, or selection-first ontology.

## 8. What would weaken this

This card should be downgraded toward E1 if:

- field effects remain modulatory but not functionally central to conscious binding;
- weak-field entrainment fails to generalize in vivo or does not affect cognitive / phenomenal organization;
- cross-frequency coupling is better explained as a correlate of task coordination rather than a substrate of conscious unity;
- neural criticality is fully explained by synaptic, homeostatic, or network-topology mechanisms;
- field coherence does not track consciousness level, reportable unity, perturbational complexity, or recovery from disruption;
- bioelectric pattern memory remains relevant to morphogenesis but not to neural consciousness;
- SRT cannot distinguish `L1` anchoring from ordinary field coherence;
- `Psi_f` collapses into generic energy cost, metabolic load, or noise suppression;
- `d-value` collapses into information integration, synchrony, salience, or task relevance;
- future artificial systems implement equivalent integration and stake-coupling without biological EM fields.

This card should be split if future SRT work separates:

1. EM field theories of consciousness;
2. ephaptic coupling and weak-field entrainment;
3. cross-frequency coupling and EEG / MEG field signatures;
4. neural criticality and avalanche dynamics;
5. Levin-style bioelectric pattern memory;
6. AI consciousness and field-infrastructure requirements.

## 9. Boundary: what this does not prove

This card does not establish any canonical SRT definition.

Specifically:

- It does not prove SRT.
- It does not prove that consciousness is literally identical to electromagnetic fields.
- It does not prove that all field coherence is conscious.
- It does not prove `L1` anchoring, `d-value`, `Psi_f`, `T_dir`, or subjecthood.
- It does not prove that binding is fully solved by EM field dynamics.
- It does not prove that criticality is caused by field resonance alone.
- It does not prove that neural spikes are secondary in all brain functions.
- It does not prove that artificial consciousness is impossible in digital systems.
- It does not override existing SRT boundaries between access, report, concern, subjecthood, and moral status.
- It should not be used as accepted evidence; it remains a draft bridge card.
