# Evidence Card: Sensing Qualia / Grounded Functionalism as a Candidate Interface for SRT Embodied Detection and Sensory Grounding

This draft card records an external convergence and pressure case from philosophy of mind, cognitive science, and systems neuroscience. It is not canonical, not accepted evidence, and not proof of SRT.

The source article is a peer-reviewed hypothesis-and-theory paper. Its role here is structural convergence and bridge-building, not experimental validation of SRT.

## 1. External finding

Paul Skokowski's article, *Sensing Qualia* (Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience, 2022), argues that many standard theories of mind fail to account for sensory qualia because they look in the wrong place.

The paper reviews and criticizes:

- behaviorism, for excluding internal states and reducing mentality to observable behavior;
- identity theory, for making mental states too tightly identical to particular brain states and thereby weakening multiple realizability;
- functionalism, for preserving multiple realizability but failing to explain qualitative contents such as colors, tastes, pains, and temperatures;
- Integrated Information Theory, for identifying qualia with intrinsic internal cause-effect structures while still failing, on the author's view, to reveal or explain the felt content of qualia.

The proposed alternative is `Grounded Functionalism`:

1. Senses should be understood as physical detectors.
2. Detectors are intentional in the broad physical sense: they detect properties beyond themselves.
3. A detector's internal state is the vehicle or detecting mechanism, not the detected property itself.
4. Sensory qualia should be grounded in the physical properties detected by sensory systems.
5. The relevant qualia are not found by searching only inside the brain; they are tied to the external or bodily properties that sensory systems have the biological function to detect.

Examples discussed in the paper include:

- color qualia as grounded in surface reflectance properties;
- heat / temperature qualia as grounded in material temperature detected by thermosensitive systems;
- taste qualia as grounded in chemical properties of food;
- pain-related qualia as grounded in bodily damage or noxious disturbance detected by nociceptive systems;
- detector analogies such as Stern-Gerlach devices, tachometers, barometers, and calorimeters.

For SRT, the finding is best treated as a candidate external bridge for sensory grounding: L0 physical properties are selected through embodied detectors and become L1 phenomenal contents only under the right system-level conditions.

## 2. Source domain

Cognitive science / philosophy of mind / systems neuroscience / sensory science / representationalism / externalism / functionalism.

## 3. SRT construct involved

Primary SRT anchors:

- `G_hat_theta`: sensory systems can be interpreted as embodied selection operators that couple a system to specific classes of world or body properties.
- `L0/L1/L2`: the article supports a layered distinction between physical properties, detector-mediated phenomenal contents, and conceptual / report-level classification.
- sensory grounding: qualia contents are not purely intrinsic computational states; they depend on properties detected through embodied sensory channels.
- `T_dir`: detector outputs can act as direction-readable traces that bias future action, avoidance, report, or reorientation.
- `d-value`: SRT must explain when detected properties enter concern bandwidth rather than remaining mere physical detection.
- qualia boundary: the paper helps separate detection, internal vehicle, phenomenal content, and verbal report.
- misrepresentation: hallucination, dream, error, or failed detection pressure SRT to explain internally generated L1 slices.
- embodiment: genuine sensory grounding requires a channel that couples the system to physical or bodily properties, not merely linguistic reconstruction.

This card is especially relevant to SRT's distinction between mere representation and embodied reselectability.

## 4. Support type

Structural convergence / bridge-support candidate / operational-proxy candidate / pressure case.

The structural convergence is:

> sensory qualia should be analyzed as world/body properties detected through embodied channels, rather than as purely intrinsic brain states or merely functional roles.

This converges with SRT's view that a phenomenal slice is not an isolated inner picture, but a selected and stabilized interface between a system, its embodied operator, and the world.

The operational-proxy candidate is that sensory channels can be studied as detector systems: what property they are tuned to, what range they discriminate, what actions they bias, and under what errors they misrepresent.

The pressure case is that detector-grounding may explain sensory qualia without SRT's additional machinery; SRT must show why embodied detection alone is not sufficient for subject-involving phenomenal concern.

## 5. Evidence level: E0-E5

E2 = structural convergence.

Reason for E2: the paper provides a clear structural bridge from external / bodily physical properties to sensory contents through embodied detector systems. This helps SRT articulate the transition from L0 physical property to detector-mediated L1 phenomenal slice.

Reason not to rate E3: the article is a hypothesis-and-theory paper, not a validated SRT measurement protocol. It does not operationalize SRT-specific `d-value`, `Psi_f`, subjecthood, or reselectability. It should therefore remain a bridge-support candidate rather than a confirmed operational proxy.

## 6. Why it matters

This card is important because it helps SRT avoid three common errors in qualia theory:

1. **Brain-only error**: treating qualia as if they must be found only by inspecting internal neural states.
2. **Function-only error**: treating input-internal-output causal role as enough to explain qualitative content.
3. **Report-only error**: treating verbal or conceptual report as equivalent to the sensory content itself.

The SRT translation is:

> Sensory qualia can be treated as L0 world/body properties selected through embodied detector structures into an L1 phenomenal interface, with L2 naming, classification, and report added later.

This supports a useful chain:

`L0 physical property -> embodied detector / G_hat_theta -> L1 phenomenal slice -> L2 concept or report`

### 6.1 `G_hat_theta` as embodied detector-selection

Grounded Functionalism gives SRT a way to make `G_hat_theta` more concrete in the sensory domain: the operator is not an abstract representational function alone, but an embodied detection channel tuned to some range of world or body properties.

### 6.2 `L0/L1/L2` separation

The paper helps separate:

- `L0`: surface reflectance, material temperature, chemical composition, tissue damage;
- detector interface: sensory receptors, pathways, and cortical processing;
- `L1`: phenomenal content such as yellow, hot, bitter, pain;
- `L2`: statements, concepts, categories, and reports such as “this is yellow” or “this hurts.”

### 6.3 AI consciousness boundary

The card constrains overclaims from language-model cases. A system may have reportable internal states or J-space-like cognitive access without having first-order detector-grounded sensory qualia. Textual reference to red, heat, taste, or pain is not equivalent to embodied sensory grounding.

### 6.4 Engineering route for sensory qualia

The paper suggests a practical engineering question:

> What kind of detector system can stably discriminate a class of world/body properties and make that discrimination available for future action, avoidance, report, or recombination?

SRT can absorb this while adding that detection must enter reselectability, concern, and consequence-return structures before it supports subject-involving experience.

## 7. Alternative explanations

The same external findings can be explained without SRT:

- Grounded Functionalism itself may provide the required account of sensory qualia without SRT.
- Externalist / representationalist theories may explain qualia as represented external properties.
- Teleosemantics may explain sensory content through biological function and natural selection.
- Neuroscience may explain detection and discrimination through sensory pathways and coding without `G_hat_theta`.
- Predictive processing may explain sensory content through active inference and error minimization.
- Enactivism may explain perception through sensorimotor coupling without SRT's L0/L1/L2 structure.
- Ordinary functionalism plus external content may explain multiple realizability and sensory content sufficiently.

The strongest alternative is detector reduction:

> sensory qualia may be reducible to externally grounded detection of physical properties, leaving no need for SRT-specific reselectability, `d-value`, or subjecthood machinery.

## 8. What would weaken this

This card should be downgraded toward E1 if:

- sensory qualia cannot be consistently mapped to externally or bodily detected physical properties;
- internally generated experiences such as dreams, hallucinations, imagery, and afterimages cannot be handled without abandoning the detector model;
- affective qualia, mood, pain-as-suffering, or concern-laden states resist the same grounding model;
- detector-grounding collapses into ordinary representationalism without adding useful structure to SRT;
- SRT cannot distinguish subject-involving phenomenal detection from mere physical detection by instruments;
- empirical neuroscience shows that qualia contents are better explained by intrinsic generative dynamics than by externally grounded detector function;
- the card is used to infer subjecthood from detection alone.

This card should be split if future SRT work separates color, temperature, pain, taste, proprioception, affect, and internal imagery into different grounding classes.

## 9. Boundary: what this does not prove

This card does not establish any canonical SRT definition.

Specifically:

- It does not prove SRT.
- It does not prove that qualia are fully reducible to external physical properties.
- It does not prove that mere detectors have experience.
- It does not prove that a thermometer, barometer, camera, or sensor has subjecthood.
- It does not solve affective qualia, mood, suffering, or concern-laden experience.
- It does not solve dreams, hallucinations, imagery, or internally generated phenomenal slices by itself.
- It does not prove that LLMs lack every possible kind of experience; it only pressures claims about first-order sensory qualia without embodied detectors.
- It does not override SRT's need to distinguish detection, representation, reselectability, concern, and subjecthood.

## 10. Upgrade path

Possible next steps:

1. Create a bridge note: sensory grounding as an interface for `G_hat_theta` and the `L0 -> detector -> L1 -> L2` chain.
2. Define a lab hypothesis: detector-grounded signals should produce different reselectability profiles than purely symbolic or report-only representations.
3. Define a subjecthood boundary test: mere physical detection is not enough unless the detected property enters concern bandwidth, action selection, memory, and consequence return.
4. Compare domains: color / temperature / taste / nociception / proprioception / affective qualia / internal imagery.
5. Create a pressure entry: `CL-COG-SENSING-QUALIA-DETECTOR-REDUCTION`.
6. Keep the card at E2 until SRT defines measurable criteria distinguishing detector-grounded content from subject-involving phenomenal concern.
