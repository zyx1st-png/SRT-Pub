# Pressure Note: Kakeya-to-SRT Metaphor and Theorem-Transfer Risk

This note records downgrade and scoping pressure on [`EC-MATH-KAKEYA-DIRECTION-NONCOLLAPSE`](../Mathematics_Information/EC-KAKEYA-DIRECTION-NONCOLLAPSE.md).

It is not a refutation of SRT. Its purpose is to prevent a rigorous theorem about Euclidean line segments and tubes from being presented as direct support for an ontological theory of selection.

## 1. Primary pressure

The Wang-Zahl theorem concerns Kakeya sets and families of approximately straight `delta`-tubes in Euclidean three-space. SRT's candidate objects are selection states, executable trajectories, stake-gated directions, and historical constraint structures. No theorem-transfer is valid until a specified SRT model class supplies:

- a metric state space;
- a defensible notion of direction;
- a family of nontrivial executable trajectories;
- a finite-resolution tube or uncertainty neighborhood;
- a concentration functional;
- scale maps and non-degeneracy assumptions;
- a proof that the relevant trajectories satisfy Kakeya-type hypotheses.

Without these items, the relation remains a structural analogy.

## 2. Construct-conflation pressure

The following identifications are prohibited:

```math
\text{Kakeya direction} \equiv T_{dir}
```

```math
\dim_H(\text{trajectory support}) \equiv d_{canonical}
```

```math
\text{convex tube container} \equiv L_2
```

```math
\text{full dimension} \equiv \text{high stake, high }\Psi_f\text{, or subjecthood}
```

At most, Kakeya geometry may inform a capacity-side question about whether rich directional coverage can coexist with low-dimensional realized support.

## 3. Alternative mathematical explanations

The intended SRT question may be better modeled by established tools that do not require Kakeya geometry:

- nonlinear controllability and reachable-set dimension;
- geometric control theory;
- sub-Riemannian geometry;
- metric entropy and covering numbers;
- manifold learning and intrinsic-dimension estimation;
- dynamical-systems attractor dimension;
- information bottleneck and rate-distortion theory;
- incidence geometry for curved or branching trajectories.

If one of these frameworks captures the proposed domain more directly, the Kakeya bridge should remain historical or heuristic rather than being treated as a preferred SRT formalism.

## 4. Curvature and branching pressure

SRT selection trajectories may be:

- curved;
- stochastic;
- branching;
- discontinuous at thresholds or phase transitions;
- dependent on path history and `L2` writeback;
- nonlocal or only locally parameterizable.

These features can destroy the straight-tube assumptions behind the classical Kakeya formulation. Local straightening is not enough unless curvature, multiplicity, overlap, and scale errors are quantitatively controlled.

## 5. Coordinate-artifact pressure

A high apparent direction count may result from:

- redundant parameters;
- gauge freedom;
- arbitrary embeddings;
- measurement coordinates;
- correlated features counted as independent;
- local Fisher rank that does not survive reparameterization or intervention.

Therefore, a Kakeya-style non-collapse claim cannot begin from raw coordinate dimension. The direction family must be operationally distinguishable and stable under the declared equivalence or normalization rules.

## 6. Stake and payability pressure

Even a valid full-dimensional trajectory support would establish only geometric richness. It would not establish:

- irreversible consequence return;
- stake-coupled concern;
- closure or identity continuity;
- payability of selection burden;
- self-readable direction;
- consciousness or subjecthood.

The full-dimension / zero-measure possibility is itself a warning that geometric dimension and existential burden can diverge sharply.

## 7. Downgrade conditions

The related evidence card should be downgraded toward E1 or retained only as a methodological analogy if:

1. no concrete domain yields a stable direction-to-trajectory mapping;
2. standard controllability or intrinsic-dimension tools dominate the Kakeya formulation;
3. the result changes materially under harmless reparameterization;
4. `L2` concentration cannot be defined independently of the desired conclusion;
5. no observable consequence follows from the proposed non-collapse quantity;
6. the mapping is used rhetorically to support SRT's P0 ontology;
7. full dimension is treated as evidence for canonical `d`, `Psi_f`, `T_dir`, freedom, or subjecthood.

## 8. Promotion gate

Before any upgrade beyond E2, require:

- one explicit model class;
- formal definitions of direction, trajectory, width, scale, and concentration;
- comparison with controllability and intrinsic-dimension baselines;
- a theorem, simulation, or falsifiable empirical result;
- an ablation showing that the proposed Kakeya-type structure predicts something not already explained by simpler geometry;
- continued separation between capacity-side dimension and stake / payability variables.

Until then, the safe wording is:

> Kakeya geometry supplies a candidate mathematical language for directional non-collapse and multi-scale concentration. It does not prove SRT or directly instantiate SRT constructs.
