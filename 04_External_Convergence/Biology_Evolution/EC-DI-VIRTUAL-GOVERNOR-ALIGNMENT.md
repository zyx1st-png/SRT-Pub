# Evidence Card: Virtual Governor Alignment as a Candidate Interface for SRT `L2`, `Psi_f`, `d-value`, and Multiscale Coordination

This draft card records an external convergence and pressure case from diverse intelligence, basal cognition, biology, social coordination, and control theory. It is not canonical, not accepted evidence, and not proof of SRT.

The source article is a preprint marked as not peer-reviewed. Its role here is therefore limited to structural convergence and bridge-building.

## 1. External finding

Benjamin Lyons, Léo Pio-Lopez, and Michael Levin's preprint, *Alignment Is to a Virtual Governor: A Theory of Coordination in Diverse Intelligence* (posted 3 July 2026), argues that alignment in decentralized coordinated systems is necessarily alignment to a `virtual governor`.

The paper defines a virtual governor as an abstract, relationally embodied governing structure formed by coordinating relationships among components. It is not a localized physical object or an explicit central controller, but it can still exert causal influence by shaping incentives, constraints, enablements, and local stress signals.

The central mechanism is:

1. A coordinated system must satisfy system-level constraints.
2. Violations of those constraints generate system-level stress.
3. A signaling architecture translates system-level stress into component-level stress, cost, incentive, or constraint.
4. Components locally minimize their own stress or pursue their own local goals.
5. Because local stress is coupled to global stress, local action collectively realizes the system-level objective embodied by the virtual governor.

Examples discussed in the source include:

- center of gravity as a non-material but intervention-relevant controller;
- algorithms as higher-level causal governors in digital computers;
- bioelectric networks as morphogenetic governors that coordinate cells toward target anatomy;
- stress sharing among cells during morphogenesis;
- motor behavior as rapidly assembling and dissolving virtual governors over muscles, joints, and neural signals;
- electrical-grid frequency as a shared signal translating supply-demand imbalance into local generator adjustment;
- the price system as an economic virtual governor that translates scarcity into local production and consumption incentives;
- universal properties in mathematics as relationally defined structures constraining valid constructions;
- taxes, subsidies, markets, and institutional design as methods of steering virtual governors;
- cancer, multiscale biological organization, and planarian bistability as cases of exit, competition, or conflict among virtual governors.

For SRT, the finding is best treated as a candidate external model for how higher-level relational structures govern lower-level selectable options by translating global constraint violations into local pressure.

## 2. Source domain

Diverse intelligence / basal cognition / biology / bioelectricity / control theory / economics / social coordination / multiscale systems.

## 3. SRT construct involved

Primary SRT anchors:

- `L2` hardening: virtual governors provide an external model of relationally embodied higher-order structures that constrain and stabilize component behavior without being reducible to one local object.
- `Psi_f`: the global-stress-to-local-stress translation offers a candidate operational interface for friction, payability, and pressure distribution.
- `d-value`: local concern weights can be modeled as projections of system-level constraint pressure into component-level cost, incentive, risk, or attention.
- `T_dir`: shared signals such as voltage patterns, grid frequency, prices, or institutional incentives provide direction-readable traces of how the system is steering component behavior.
- `G_hat_theta`: bioelectric and embodied coordination structures provide a candidate bridge to operator-state interfaces in living systems.
- consequence return: the article helps distinguish local component goals from system-level consequences that return through the signaling architecture.
- foreclosure / exit: cancer and competing governors provide cases where components exit, defect from, or reconfigure the higher-level coordination regime.

This card is especially relevant to SRT's multiscale and social-institutional direction: high-level selection structures can govern local options without being explicit beliefs of any single component.

## 4. Support type

Structural convergence / operational-proxy candidate / bridge-support candidate / falsification-pressure candidate.

The structural convergence is strong at the level of form:

> decentralized coordination becomes stable when a relational higher-order structure converts system-level constraint violations into local action pressures.

This maps naturally onto SRT's distinction between local capacity and higher-order selection governance.

The operational-proxy candidate is that measurable signals such as bioelectric potentials, grid frequency, prices, tax incentives, stress-sharing signals, or institutional rules may approximate how an `L2` selection structure distributes `Psi_f` and `d-value` across components.

The pressure case is that virtual-governor theory may explain much of SRT's multiscale coordination territory through ordinary control theory, signaling architecture, and emergent governance without requiring SRT-specific ontology.

## 5. Evidence level: E0-E5

E2 = structural convergence.

Reason for E2: the paper offers a clear structural mapping between higher-level relational governance, stress translation, local incentive reshaping, multiscale coordination, exit, and competing attractors. These structures converge with SRT's `L2`, `Psi_f`, `d-value`, and consequence-return concerns.

Reason not to rate E3 yet: this card does not yet define a validated SRT-specific measurement protocol. A virtual governor may be measurable in particular systems, but SRT must still specify what distinguishes `Psi_f` from ordinary stress, `d-value` from incentive, and `L2` selection governance from generic control-theoretic feedback.

## 6. Why it matters

This card is important because it gives SRT an external, cross-domain vocabulary for the problem of multiscale coordination:

- individual components often do not explicitly know the system-level goal;
- the higher-order structure need not be a physical object or central controller;
- system-level constraint violations are translated into local stress or incentive;
- local minimization can implement a global policy;
- components can defect, exit, or align with competing governors;
- the relevant governor can be reshaped by editing the signaling architecture rather than commanding every component.

The SRT translation is:

> A virtual governor is a candidate external model of an `L2` selection structure: it is relationally embodied, causally instructive, and capable of reshaping the local option-space of components by translating higher-level constraint violations into local pressures.

This helps SRT express several difficult ideas more concretely:

### 6.1 `L2` as relational governance, not object-substance

The source supports the idea that a higher-order structure can be real in its effects without being a localized object. In SRT terms, `L2` hardening can be interpreted as the stabilization of relational constraints, signals, and expectations that govern local selectable realities.

### 6.2 `Psi_f` as translated organizational burden

The article's system-level stress / component-level stress mechanism gives SRT a candidate bridge for explaining how friction or burden becomes locally payable. A system does not need each component to understand the global problem; it needs a signaling architecture that makes the relevant part of the problem locally actionable.

### 6.3 `d-value` as local concern-weight projection

A component's concern is shaped by what the governor's signal architecture makes locally costly, risky, urgent, or rewarding. This provides a candidate interface for interpreting `d-value` as a position-specific projection of system-level constraint pressure into local reselectability burden.

### 6.4 `T_dir` as direction-readable governance signal

Prices, frequencies, bioelectric gradients, and institutional incentives are not merely measurements; they are directional traces that reshape future behavior. This provides a concrete family of `T_dir`-like probes for social, biological, and engineered systems.

### 6.5 Foreclosure, exit, and competing governors

Cancer, market failure, institutional breakdown, and planarian bistability show that alignment is not always peaceful. A component can leave one governor, be captured by another, or participate in competing higher-order regimes. This is directly relevant to SRT's concern with foreclosure, consequence return, and whose selectable future is being preserved or sacrificed.

## 7. Alternative explanations

The same external findings can be explained without SRT:

- Control theory may explain virtual governors as ordinary feedback systems.
- Cybernetics may explain alignment through signaling architecture and error correction.
- Economics may explain the price system through incentives and scarcity without invoking `d-value`.
- Active inference / FEP may explain stress minimization as prediction-error or free-energy minimization.
- Causal emergence theory may explain macro-level governance without SRT's selection-first ontology.
- Dynamical systems theory may explain attractor switching, planarian bistability, and morphogenesis without `L2` or `Psi_f`.
- Institutional theory may explain social governance through rules, incentives, and legitimacy without SRT.
- Levin-style diverse-intelligence theory may already cover multiscale agency, basal cognition, bioelectric setpoints, and synthetic morphology in a way that overlaps strongly with SRT.

The strongest alternative is cybernetic reduction:

> virtual governor theory may explain multiscale coordination through signaling, feedback, stress translation, and attractor dynamics without requiring SRT-specific `Psi_f`, `d-value`, or ontological selection claims.

## 8. What would weaken this

This card should be downgraded toward E1 if:

- virtual governors reduce to ordinary feedback loops with no useful mapping to `L2` selection governance;
- the stress translation mechanism cannot distinguish SRT-relevant `Psi_f` from generic error, cost, or difficulty;
- local incentives explain behavior fully without requiring `d-value` as concern-weight projection;
- `T_dir` cannot be operationally separated from ordinary signal direction or control input;
- examples such as prices, bioelectric gradients, and grid frequency are too heterogeneous to support a unified SRT bridge;
- the theory remains purely metaphorical and does not yield measurable predictions about foreclosure, exit, competing governors, or reselectability loss;
- peer review or later versions materially weaken the claim that alignment in decentralized systems is necessarily to a virtual governor.

This card should be split if future SRT work separates biological governors, economic governors, institutional governors, and AI coordination governors into different empirical classes.

## 9. Boundary: what this does not prove

This card does not establish any canonical SRT definition.

Specifically:

- It does not prove SRT.
- It does not prove that virtual governors are identical to SRT `L2` structures.
- It does not prove that all stress signals are `Psi_f`.
- It does not prove that all incentives are `d-value`.
- It does not prove subjecthood at the level of cells, markets, institutions, or AI systems.
- It does not prove top-down causation in an unrestricted metaphysical sense.
- It does not prove that decentralized alignment is always beneficial or morally legitimate.
- It does not show whose future selectable space should be prioritized when governors compete.
- It does not override existing SRT canonical boundaries around `d-value`, `Psi_f`, or subjecthood.
- Because the source is a non-peer-reviewed preprint, it should not be cited as accepted evidence.

## 10. Upgrade path

Possible next steps:

1. Create a bridge note: `Virtual Governor` as an external bridge for `L2` governance, `Psi_f` translation, and `d-value` projection.
2. Define an SRT lab hypothesis: if a virtual governor is present, system-level stress signals should predict local option-space reshaping better than local incentives alone.
3. Define a foreclosure test: when a component exits or is excluded from a governor, measurable reselectability loss or stress displacement should appear somewhere in the system.
4. Separate domains: biological bioelectric governors, economic price governors, institutional rule governors, AI coordination governors.
5. Create a bridge with `T_dir`: directional governance signals should be readable as future behavior-biasing gradients, not merely descriptive measurements.
6. Create a pressure entry: `CL-DI-VIRTUAL-GOVERNOR-CYBERNETIC-REDUCTION`.
7. Keep the card at E2 until SRT-specific measurement conditions distinguish `Psi_f` from stress, `d-value` from incentive, and `L2` from generic feedback governance.
