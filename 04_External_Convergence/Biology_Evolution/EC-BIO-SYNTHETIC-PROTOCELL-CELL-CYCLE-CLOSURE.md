# Evidence Card: Synthetic Protocell Cell-Cycle Closure as a Candidate Bridge for Minimal Life, `Psi_f`, and Biological Reselectability

This draft card records an external convergence and pressure case from synthetic biology, minimal-cell research, and origin-of-life engineering. It is not canonical, not accepted evidence, and not proof of SRT.

The primary source is a science-journalism report on a bioRxiv preprint that was not peer-reviewed at the time of reporting. The card should therefore remain E2 draft unless the underlying work is peer-reviewed and SRT-specific measurement criteria are defined.

## 1. External finding

Yasemin Saplakoglu's Quanta Magazine article, *For the First Time, a Cell Built From Scratch Grows and Divides* (1 July 2026), reports on Kate Adamala's synthetic-biology team building a membrane-bounded synthetic cell-like system from nonliving biomolecular components.

The reported system, nicknamed a `spudcell`, combines several life-like modules:

- lipid membrane / liposome boundary;
- a small synthetic genome;
- DNA replication machinery;
- transcription / translation support, including a commercial enzyme pack;
- feeder liposomes that deliver sugar, lipids, enzymes, tRNA, ribosomes, and other required components;
- membrane fusion machinery that lets supply vesicles merge with the synthetic cell;
- a division mechanism based on membrane protein tags that recruit proteins to bend and pinch the membrane;
- observed growth, DNA replication, and division into daughter cells;
- preliminary population-level selection-like behavior when artificially introduced genetic variants alter growth and daughter-cell production.

The source stresses that the system is not alive by ordinary definitions. It requires constant external deliveries of food and ribosomes, lacks defenses and a robust waste-removal system, cannot make its own ribosomes, and does not yet undergo natural selection from internally generated random mutations.

For SRT, the finding is best treated as a candidate bridge for staged biological closure: nonliving chemical components can be organized into a bounded system that begins to execute growth, information preservation, replication, division, and weak selectable variation, while still externalizing much of its maintenance burden.

## 2. Source domain

Synthetic biology / origin of life / minimal cell / protocell engineering / biological closure / abiogenesis studies.

## 3. SRT construct involved

Primary SRT anchors:

- `L0/L1`: nonliving chemical components are organized into a bounded, life-like process loop.
- `Psi_f`: the system's maintenance cost remains largely externally scaffolded through supplied ribosomes, nutrients, enzymes, and complex molecules.
- `d-value`: growth and daughter-cell production begin to affect future representation in the population, but do not yet establish intrinsic concern or full biological stake.
- `G_hat_theta`: the membrane-bound synthetic cell is a candidate minimal embodied operator boundary, but still heavily dependent on external engineering.
- biological closure: growth, DNA replication, and division form a partial cell-cycle closure.
- reselectability: replicated genetic information and daughter-cell production create a primitive biological reselectability channel.
- consequence return: larger synthetic cells producing more daughter cells suggests the beginning of a consequence-return loop at population scale.
- boundary formation: the liposome membrane provides a physical closure surface that holds processes together.
- minimal life threshold: the system helps separate life-like process loops from full self-sustaining life.
- evolution precursor: artificial genetic variation and differential daughter-cell output approximate a first step toward selection but not natural selection.

This card is especially relevant to SRT's boundary between chemical possibility, biological closure, and subjecthood.

## 4. Support type

Structural convergence / bridge-support candidate / boundary-case candidate / falsification-pressure candidate.

The structural convergence is:

> life-like organization appears when nonliving chemical components are enclosed in a boundary and coordinated into growth, information-copying, protein expression, feeding, and division loops.

This maps to SRT's claim that stable existence requires staged closure of selectable processes rather than a single binary jump from nonlife to life.

The boundary-case value is strong: the system performs several cell-cycle-like functions but remains nonliving and externally scaffolded. This helps SRT distinguish:

- life-like behavior from autonomous life;
- replication from intrinsic stake;
- division from subjecthood;
- engineered supply chains from internally paid `Psi_f`;
- artificial variation from natural selection.

## 5. Evidence level: E0-E5

E2 = structural convergence and boundary-case support.

Reason for E2: the report describes a concrete synthetic-biology system that combines boundary, genetic information, expression machinery, feeding, growth, DNA replication, and division in one engineered loop. This provides a strong conceptual bridge for SRT's minimal-life and biological-closure vocabulary.

Reason not to rate E3 yet: the card is based on a Quanta report about a bioRxiv preprint, and the underlying system is not yet self-maintaining, metabolically autonomous, or naturally evolving. SRT also has not yet defined a validated measurement protocol for `Psi_f`, `d-value`, or biological reselectability in protocell systems.

## 6. Why it matters

This card is important because it supplies SRT with an empirical boundary object: a system that is more than passive chemistry but less than autonomous life.

The SRT translation is:

> Synthetic protocells show that biological closure is not a single switch from nonlife to life, but a staged accumulation of boundary, information, expression, feeding, replication, division, and selectable variation. In SRT terms, life begins to appear when `L0` chemical processes are organized into a bounded, self-reproducing, future-bearing selection loop, while this case remains incomplete because much of the loop's friction cost is externally scaffolded.

### 6.1 Boundary formation as first closure surface

The liposome membrane creates a physical boundary that keeps the relevant components together. For SRT, this supports the idea that a selection loop requires a closure surface: a distinction between inside, outside, component, supply, and daughter unit.

### 6.2 `Psi_f` as internally paid versus externally scaffolded maintenance burden

The strongest SRT value of this case is not that the system is alive, but that it shows how life-like behavior can be produced while much of the maintenance burden is still paid from outside.

The system grows and divides only because external feeder liposomes provide key components such as ribosomes, tRNA, enzymes, sugars, and lipids. In SRT terms, it has partial closure, but its `Psi_f` remains heavily externalized.

### 6.3 Reselectability through replication and division

DNA replication and daughter-cell formation create a minimal biological reselectability channel: internal information can be copied into future bounded units. This is not yet subjecthood, but it is a primitive future-bearing loop.

### 6.4 `d-value` precursor without full concern

Artificially introduced variants that make some cells grow larger and produce more daughter cells create a weak precursor to `d-value`: structural differences begin to affect future persistence in the population. However, because variation is externally introduced and the cells lack full autonomous maintenance, this should not be read as intrinsic concern.

### 6.5 Minimal life threshold

The case helps SRT define a layered threshold:

1. passive chemistry;
2. bounded chemistry;
3. bounded growth;
4. bounded information copying;
5. bounded division;
6. heritable variation;
7. autonomous metabolism and waste handling;
8. self-maintenance and natural selection;
9. subjecthood or concern-bearing agency.

The reported system appears to occupy an intermediate zone: it has boundary, growth, DNA replication, and division, but lacks full autonomous metabolism, ribosome production, waste management, and natural evolutionary dynamics.

## 7. Alternative explanations

The same external findings can be explained without SRT:

- synthetic biology may explain the result as modular engineering of known biochemical functions;
- origin-of-life research may treat it as a protocell milestone without SRT's selection ontology;
- systems chemistry may explain the system through compartmentalization and reaction networks;
- autopoiesis theory may explain the case through self-production and boundary maintenance thresholds;
- minimal-cell biology may explain it through metabolic, genetic, and translation-system requirements;
- evolutionary theory may treat the selection-like results as artificial differential reproduction, not natural selection;
- engineering accounts may regard the system as externally powered biochemical machinery rather than an autonomous biological unit.

The strongest alternative is engineered-scaffold reduction:

> the system may be an impressive externally scaffolded biochemical machine that grows and divides without establishing autonomous life, intrinsic `d-value`, or SRT-specific biological closure.

## 8. What would weaken this

This card should be downgraded toward E1 if:

- the preprint does not survive peer review or key observations fail to replicate;
- growth and division depend on artifacts of liposome fusion, imaging, or experimental selection;
- DNA replication and division are not reliably coupled into a repeatable cell-cycle-like loop;
- feeder liposomes supply so much of the system's organization that the protocell itself has little internal closure;
- selection-like population changes cannot be reproduced without manually introduced variation;
- metabolic autonomy, waste handling, ribosome production, or sustained reproduction remain absent;
- SRT cannot distinguish partial biological closure from ordinary engineered biochemical assembly.

This card should be split if future work separates protocell boundary formation, genome replication, metabolism, division, and evolution into distinct empirical cards.

## 9. Boundary: what this does not prove

This card does not establish any canonical SRT definition.

Specifically:

- It does not prove SRT.
- It does not prove that scientists have created fully living cells from scratch.
- It does not prove autonomous life.
- It does not prove intrinsic `d-value`.
- It does not prove subjecthood, consciousness, qualia, or moral status.
- It does not prove natural selection, because reported variation is synthetically introduced.
- It does not prove internally paid `Psi_f`, because the system depends heavily on external supplies.
- It does not prove that growth and division alone are sufficient for life.
- It does not override SRT's distinction between boundary, replication, metabolism, self-maintenance, concern, and subjecthood.

## 10. Upgrade path

Possible next steps:

1. Create a bridge note: synthetic protocells as a minimal-life boundary case for `L0 -> biological closure`.
2. Define an SRT lab hypothesis: a protocell moves closer to biological closure as it internalizes `Psi_f` currently supplied by external feeder systems.
3. Define a `Psi_f` test: quantify which maintenance burdens are internally paid versus externally scaffolded, including metabolism, ribosome production, waste handling, membrane repair, and genome integrity.
4. Define a reselectability test: measure whether replicated information changes future daughter-cell production without manual intervention.
5. Define a natural-selection test: introduce endogenous mutation and environmental pressure to see whether heritable variants alter persistence without synthetic design of variation.
6. Keep subjecthood separate: biological closure, even if achieved, does not automatically imply consciousness or morally relevant concern.
7. Pair this card with `CL-BIO-SYNTHETIC-CELL-LIFE-OVERCLAIM` before any public-facing claim.
