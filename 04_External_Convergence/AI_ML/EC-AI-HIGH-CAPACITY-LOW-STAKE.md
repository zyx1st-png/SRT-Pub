# Evidence Card: AI / High Capacity Low Stake as a Candidate Interface for `d-value`, Consequence Return, and Subjecthood Threshold

This draft card records a sourced structural / conceptual contrast candidate. It is not canonical, not accepted evidence, and not proof of SRT.

## 1. External finding

Recent AI systems can show high inference capacity, fluent language behavior, tool use, planning-like behavior, and increasingly agentic task performance. At the same time, the checked literature separates these capabilities from several stronger conditions:

- RLHF and preference modeling train models toward human-preferred outputs or reward-model objectives; they do not by themselves establish stake-coupled concern.
- Agentic-system evaluations measure task completion, autonomy, or long-horizon capability; they do not by themselves establish consequence-bearing subjecthood.
- LLM self-reports and self-assessments are limited evidence for internal state, subjecthood, or consciousness.
- AI consciousness / welfare assessment work generally treats consciousness as an open assessment problem using theory-based indicators, not as a conclusion from fluency alone.
- Persistent memory, embodiment, self-maintenance, and consequence-bearing loops are live architectural pressure points for future classification.

For SRT, this makes current AI a candidate structural contrast: high capacity can appear without clearly demonstrated `d-value`, consequence return, or subjecthood threshold. That contrast is not a universal conclusion about all current or future AI systems.

## 2. Source domain

AI / machine learning / RLHF / agentic systems / AI consciousness and welfare assessment.

This card concerns a contrast between high inference / task capacity and direct stake-coupled consequence return. It does not classify AI systems as a whole.

## 4. Support type

Structural / conceptual differential-support candidate.

This is not an operational proxy yet. It becomes operational only if a later card specifies architecture-level criteria for persistent memory, self-maintenance, embodiment, consequence return, and non-substitutable future burden.

## 5. Evidence level: E0-E5

E2 = structural convergence / conceptual contrast.

Reason for E2: The AI literature supports a careful separation between performance capacity, reward optimization, preference following, agentic task behavior, and stronger claims about internal stake or subjecthood. That separation is compatible with SRT's distinction between capacity-like proxies and `d-value` as stake-coupled concern.

Reason not to rate E3: this draft card does not provide a validated operational proxy for `d-value`, consequence return, or subjecthood threshold in AI systems.

## 6. Why it matters

SRT treats `d-value` as stake-coupled concern, not merely bandwidth, fluency, reward sensitivity, or task competence. AI systems are therefore a useful test case for separating capacity from concern.

Current dominant LLM-style systems appear to separate high inference capacity from direct stake-coupled consequence return under many deployment conditions, but future architectures with persistent memory, embodiment, self-maintenance, or consequence-bearing loops may require revised classification.

The relevance is structural:

- RLHF and preference modeling can generate helpful or aligned behavior without showing that consequences return to the system as non-substitutable stake.
- Agentic task evaluations can show tool use, planning, and long-horizon competence without deciding whether the agent bears the cost of its outcomes.
- Self-reports may be fluent and context-sensitive without being reliable evidence of subjecthood.
- AI consciousness assessment work suggests that architecture and functional indicators matter more than ordinary language fluency alone.
- Persistent-agent and embodied-agent research shows why the boundary must remain open rather than frozen around present LLM deployments.

This makes AI / high capacity low stake a candidate interface for clarifying `d-value`, consequence return, and subjecthood threshold. It does not settle the status of current or future systems.

## 7. Alternative explanations

The same external findings can be explained without SRT:

- Functionalist accounts may argue that sufficiently organized behavior or information processing is already enough for relevant mental status.
- Reward-centered accounts may treat RL reward, reward modeling, or preference satisfaction as a sufficient proxy for concern.
- Behaviorist evaluations may treat fluent language and task performance as enough for practical attribution.
- Tool-use and agent-benchmark failures may reflect engineering immaturity rather than absence of stake.
- Deployment context may create external stakes for users, firms, or institutions even if those stakes do not return to the model itself.
- Future AI systems with persistent identity, self-maintenance, embodiment, resource constraints, or stable memory may satisfy stronger conditions than present systems.

## 8. What would weaken this

This card should be downgraded toward E1 if:

- high-capacity AI behavior cannot be cleanly separated from architecture-level consequence return;
- a future system shows persistent self-maintenance, non-substitutable future burden, and outcome loops that return to its own continuity;
- reward / preference-model objectives become directly tied to system-level self-preservation, repair, or irreversible loss;
- self-report methods become independently reliable indicators of internal state rather than ordinary language behavior;
- the card is used to make a universal class conclusion about AI subjecthood rather than a scoped contrast.

It should be routed to a pressure ledger if future architectures make the high-capacity / low-stake contrast too coarse.

## 9. Boundary: what this does not prove

This card does not establish any canonical SRT definition.

Specifically:

- High capability does not by itself establish `d-value`.
- Fluent language behavior does not by itself establish subjecthood.
- RL reward, preference modeling, or instruction following are not automatically stake-coupled concern.
- Current AI systems should not be classified as universally lacking or possessing subjecthood as a class conclusion.
- This card is a structural / conceptual differential-support candidate, not proof of SRT.
- It does not treat present LLM deployments as the final form of AI agency.
- It does not deny that future systems with persistent memory, embodiment, self-maintenance, or consequence-bearing loops may require revised classification.
- No evidence card may override the canonical `d-value` file.

## 10. Upgrade path

Possible next steps:

1. Create a bridge note separating capacity, reward, preference following, agency, stake-coupling, and subjecthood.
2. Define a scoped lab hypothesis for architecture-level consequence return: persistent memory, self-maintenance, embodiment, resource constraints, and non-substitutable future burden.
3. Upgrade toward E3 only if operational criteria distinguish high capacity from stake-coupled concern better than simpler behavioral or functional accounts.
4. Add a pressure-ledger entry in a later pass for future AI systems that blur the high-capacity / low-stake boundary.
5. Keep public summaries cautious: this card is about a diagnostic contrast, not an AI sentience verdict.
