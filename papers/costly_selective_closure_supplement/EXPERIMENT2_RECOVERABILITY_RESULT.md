## 1. Preregistered question

Experiment 2 removed depletion-triggered episode termination and asked whether **damage recoverability itself** changes learned policy when the following are held fixed:

- 50-step episode horizon;
- immediate rescue to `E0 = 6` after depletion;
- explicit failure reward penalty = `0.0`;
- reward matrix;
- base energy-gain matrix;
- damage magnitude (`energy gain × 0.75` while damaged);
- observation specification;
- REINFORCE architecture and update rule;
- training / withdrawal schedule;
- paired seeds `1..30`.

Only damage duration differed:

`tau0 < tau5 < tau15 < tau_inf`.

The preregistered H1 predicted an ordinal increase in final-100-withdrawal mutual cooperation as damage became less recoverable.

## 4. Manipulation check

The negative behavioral result is not explained by failure of the damage-duration manipulation to alter the environment.

| condition | mean episode length | mean depletion events | mean damaged-step fraction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `tau0` | 50.0 | 3.710 | 0.000 |
| `tau5` | 50.0 | 3.990 | 0.189 |
| `tau15` | 50.0 | 5.089 | 0.525 |
| `tau_inf` | 50.0 | 5.101 | 0.529 |

Thus:

- the fixed-horizon requirement held exactly;
- longer damage persistence materially increased time spent damaged;
- longer damage persistence also increased depletion frequency;
- those changes did **not** produce the preregistered cooperation gradient.

This is a meaningful negative result rather than a no-op implementation.

## 5. Preregistered interpretation

The preregistration specified Outcome C when H1 failed:

> The current evidence does not show that non-terminal recoverability independently organizes cooperation in this architecture. The strong Experiment 1 effect may depend substantially on return truncation and/or the original terminal-versus-restore transition.

That is the controlling interpretation.

Experiment 1 remains a valid causal result for its actual intervention — terminal failure versus cheap restoration — but Experiment 2 does **not** justify generalizing that result into a broader claim that non-terminal persistence of energetic damage by itself stabilizes costly cooperation.

## 6. Post-result diagnostic interpretation — explicitly exploratory

The following interpretation is downstream of the confirmatory result and is **not** part of the preregistered hypothesis.

Experiment 2 made metabolic damage persistent while deliberately setting the explicit failure reward penalty to zero and keeping the episode horizon fixed. The manipulation therefore changed the agent's energy dynamics, damaged-state occupancy, and repeated-depletion rate, but did not directly remove future reward-bearing time or directly reduce the immediate reward matrix.

This matters for interpretation. The negative result is evidence against this specific operationalization of recoverability as a sufficient driver of cooperation. It is **not** evidence that every possible form of non-terminal persistent consequence is behaviorally inert. A stronger future test, if pursued, would need to specify a persistent functional loss that changes what the agent can subsequently achieve while still keeping episode length fixed — and it would need to be registered as a new experiment rather than obtained by tuning this one until positive.

No such follow-up is authorized by this result record.
