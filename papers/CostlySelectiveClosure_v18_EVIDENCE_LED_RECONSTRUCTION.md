# Costly Selective Closure v18 — Evidence-Led Reconstruction Decision

Date: 2026-09-15
Target venue: *Artificial Life*
Status: publication reconstruction / no submission yet

## 1. Why v18 is necessary

The v17 candidate was written before the preregistered Experiment 2 and Experiment 3 results existed. Its empirical interpretation treated terminate-versus-restore as one operational probe of a scalar-like fourth dimension, `V = irreversible vulnerability`, and described a non-terminal irreversibility test as future work.

That framing is no longer adequate.

The completed evidence sequence is now:

```text
Experiment 1 — terminal depletion versus cheap restoration
= strong positive cooperation effect

Experiment 2 — non-terminal persistent metabolic impairment
= preregistered positive H1 not supported

Experiment 3 — non-terminal reversible recovery latency / forced Rest
= preregistered positive H1 not supported;
  strong opposite-direction association observed
```

Experiment 2 therefore blocks the inference that persistence of internal damage is sufficient to reproduce the Experiment 1 effect. Experiment 3 further blocks the broader inference that increasing unit-borne recovery burden or temporary action-opportunity loss monotonically stabilizes costly cooperation.

The publication must change rather than hiding or minimizing those follow-ups.

## 2. Controlling conceptual revision

### v17 formulation

```text
B = selective breadth
M = maintenance burden
H = historical retention
V = irreversible vulnerability
```

This was presented as a four-part profile, with V asking what is lost under failure and how cheaply the loss can be reversed.

### v18 formulation

Keep three profile dimensions:

```text
B = selective breadth
M = maintenance burden
H = historical retention
```

Replace scalar-like `V` with a structured descriptor:

```text
C = consequence architecture
```

`C` is not an ordered scalar and must not be interpreted as “more consequence = more life-likeness.” It records how failure changes the declared unit’s subsequent organization, including where relevant:

- termination / continuation;
- reset or restoration;
- persistent impairment;
- action availability;
- recovery latency;
- future reward-bearing or interaction opportunity;
- replacement / copying / lineage continuation;
- which organizational level bears the consequence.

The purpose of `C` is decomposition, not ranking.

The declaration rule remains:

```text
organizational unit
+ boundary
+ timescale
+ recovery regime
```

This rule is strengthened by E1/E2/E3 because superficially similar “failure severity” manipulations produced qualitatively different learning effects.

## 3. What survives unchanged

The following v17 commitments remain defensible:

- CSC is not a definition of life;
- CSC is a comparison and intervention protocol rather than a universal scalar score;
- unit / boundary / timescale / recovery regime must be declared;
- episode-token, controller-lineage, and training-process levels must be separated;
- Experiment 1 is a valid causal result for terminate-versus-restore in the reported RL architecture;
- the zero-penalty ablation, lives gradient, payoff sweep, and common-state probe remain valid robustness evidence for Experiment 1;
- no novelty is claimed for precariousness, multidimensional life-likeness, artificial mortality, reset costs, or action constraints themselves.

## 4. What must be removed or downgraded

The following v17 statements cannot control the new manuscript:

1. “We test one dimension, irreversible vulnerability.”
   - Replace with: the experiments test three distinct consequence architectures originally hypothesized to lie on a shared vulnerability axis; the follow-ups reject that simple axis interpretation.

2. “Terminate-versus-restore is one operational way to vary V.”
   - Downgrade to: it is one consequence-architecture intervention with a strong effect in this testbed.

3. “Cheap restoration lowers operational V.”
   - Remove. The data do not establish an ordered V scale.

4. High/low V assignments in cross-system illustrative tables.
   - Replace with qualitative descriptions of consequence architecture.

5. Non-terminal irreversible damage as an unperformed future test.
   - Remove. E2 partially tested that generalization and returned no support; E3 tested a different non-terminal functional consequence and returned the opposite direction.

6. “Only the recovery-architecture hypothesis receives direct support.”
   - Replace with the narrower result: recovery architecture matters, but the direction is not monotonic across consequence mechanisms.

7. `real-stake` as the main condition name.
   - Replace manuscript-facing terminology with `terminal` and `restore`; retain historical code labels only in reproducibility notes.

## 5. New empirical story

The publication should treat the evidence as a sequence of discriminating tests rather than three independent demonstrations.

### Experiment 1 — terminal versus restore

Question: does terminating the current episode-token rather than cheaply restoring it alter learned costly cooperation?

Answer: yes, strongly.

This establishes a real architecture-sensitive effect but is compatible with a standard RL mechanism: termination changes the future return stream and state occupancy.

### Experiment 2 — persistent impairment without termination

Question: if episode length and immediate rescue are matched, does making metabolic impairment less recoverable produce the same positive cooperation gradient?

Answer: no.

This rejects a simple “persistent damage is enough” generalization.

### Experiment 3 — recovery latency / temporary action loss

Question: if failure creates a fully reversible but unit-borne period of reduced action opportunity, does greater recovery burden increase learned cooperation?

Answer: no; the preregistered positive hypothesis fails and the observed association is strongly negative.

This rejects a broader “more recovery burden => more cooperation” interpretation.

## 6. Surviving publication claim

The strongest evidence-led claim is:

> Failure consequences in adaptive artificial systems are architecture-specific and cannot be represented safely by a single severity or recoverability axis. Terminal failure, persistent impairment, and temporary recovery latency can produce qualitatively different learned policies even when they are all intuitively described as making failure more consequential. Cross-system life-likeness comparisons should therefore declare the organizational level and recovery regime and decompose consequence architecture rather than treating vulnerability as a scalar.

This claim is narrower than v17 but better supported.

## 7. Novelty guard

The manuscript must not claim discovery of any of the following:

- precariousness or adaptivity;
- mortality constraints in artificial systems;
- the fact that termination changes RL returns;
- reset cost as an RL variable;
- stochastic or constrained action availability;
- dimensional approaches to life or life-likeness.

Candidate contribution:

```text
unit / boundary / timescale / recovery-regime declaration
+
B / M / H profile with structured consequence architecture C
+
matched E1 terminate-versus-restore result and robustness package
+
preregistered E2 and E3 attempts to generalize the consequence interpretation
+
transparent negative / opposite-direction evidence that forces theoretical narrowing
```
