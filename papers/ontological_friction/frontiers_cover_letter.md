Dear Editors of *Frontiers in Neuroscience*,

Please consider the manuscript, **"A Translational Cross-Modal Control-Cost Framework for Executive Breakdown,"** for publication as a **Hypothesis and Theory** article in the **Translational Neuroscience** specialty section.

This manuscript proposes a falsifiable translational framework for executive breakdown across neurological and neuropsychiatric conditions. The core construct is a latent cross-modal control-cost factor ($\\Psi_f$) designed to formalize the knowing-doing gap and to support direct empirical adjudication. The manuscript centers a low-cost validation chain suited to translational neuroscience: behavioral control tasks, autonomic physiology (HRV/SCR), and a language-derived modal-ratio biomarker, with neural and biochemical measures positioned as optional adjudication layers.

The work is structured for empirical use rather than narrative theorizing. It provides:

- Explicit model equations with dimensionalization and identifiability strategy.
- Pre-registered hypothesis structure with hard falsification criteria.
- Competing-model decision rules and model-pruning pathways.
- Clinical boundary-use predictions (MDD, OCD, PD) with decisive contrast tests.

This submission reports no new empirical data. Instead, it offers a theory-to-experiment blueprint for biomarker development and model comparison in executive dysfunction. The manuscript is particularly suited to the Translational Neuroscience section because it links computational theory to clinically accessible measurements and disorder-differentiating predictions.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authorship and AI-assistance disclosures are included in the manuscript. The author declares no conflicts of interest.
