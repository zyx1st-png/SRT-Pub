# Artificial Life submission-policy check — v19.1

Checked against the current MIT Press *Artificial Life* submission-guidelines page on 2026-09-16.

## Confirmed current requirements relevant to this manuscript

- Regular **Article** manuscripts are typically `6,000–12,000` words.
- Initial manuscripts are submitted electronically as PDF through the journal's online submission system.
- Initial submission requires a cover letter explaining how the article fits the scope of *Artificial Life*.
- The article must be substantially unique and not under review elsewhere.
- Review-stage supplementary material necessary for review must be uploaded with the paper or referenced through a URL; access to that URL must not facilitate or potentially undermine **reviewer anonymity**.
- The accepted-version cover page includes title and author details.
- The journal requires `5–6` keywords.
- Citations and bibliography follow APA 7 style.
- Vector figures are preferred for line art/graphs/diagrams.

## Author-anonymity boundary

The current journal guideline page does **not** state that initial manuscripts must remove author identities or that journal review is double blind. Therefore the v19.1 candidate retains author name/affiliation/e-mail rather than importing ALIFE conference anonymity rules into the journal workflow.

This should still be checked against the live submission interface immediately before upload in case the platform presents a newer journal-specific instruction.

## Supplementary-material boundary

The manuscript's current plan for a static reviewer reproduction package is compatible in principle with the journal's requirement that supplementary access not compromise reviewer anonymity. The final review route should avoid private repository permissions, access requests, individualized download links, or other mechanisms that reveal reviewer identity to the author.

## AI-use disclosure

MIT Press publication-ethics guidance requires authors using AI tools for text, graphics, or data-related work to inform editors and be transparent in the manuscript. An *Artificial Life* editorial additionally states that generative-AI use in submitted work should be prominently and unambiguously labeled, with the source identified.

The manuscript already contains an `AI Assistance Disclosure` describing ChatGPT-assisted literature organization, restructuring, wording, code-review support, governance checks, consistency review, and post-hoc analysis scripting, while assigning responsibility to the author and distinguishing preserved experimental outputs from model-generated text.

Before actual upload, perform one final disclosure-format check against the live journal instructions. If the editorial's strongest source-identification language is enforced literally, add model/source/version/access-date details in the disclosure or references without changing scientific claims.

## Submission action

External journal upload/submission = **NOT PERFORMED**.
