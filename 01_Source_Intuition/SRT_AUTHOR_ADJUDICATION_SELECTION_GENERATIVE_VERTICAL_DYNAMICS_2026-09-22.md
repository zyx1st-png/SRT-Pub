# Author adjudication — Selection as generative differentiation and GRG vertical dynamics

This record captures the 2026-09-22 author dialogue after the BCTB-0 T1 calibration.

It is intentionally broader than the bounded canonical edits made alongside it. The author confirms the direction below as the current intuition to preserve and pressure-test. It must influence later GRG / ontology work, but it must not be mistaken for a completed formal theory.

## A. Selection is an SRT term of art, not ordinary option-choice

The author explicitly rejects the conventional default reading:

~~~text
pre-given alternatives
-> choose one / keep one / eliminate the others
~~~

SRT Selection is more primitive.

Author-confirmed direction:

> **Selection is a real, non-neutral actualising process in which a difference becomes generatively effective and leaves a consequence that cannot be fully erased from subsequent generation.**

Selection therefore does **not** require a completed option set in advance. Alternatives, positions, relations and even the reachable possibility structure may themselves become distinguishable only through prior Selection.

Hard guard:

~~~text
Selection
!= conscious choice
!= decision among a pre-given menu
!= Darwinian winner-picking by default
!= mere filtering
!= bare state transition
!= any arbitrary change
~~~

A change that is fully reversible in the strong generative sense — leaving no retained or inheritable consequence for later generation — does **not** count as Selection under the current author clarification.

The retained consequence need not be a localized record, memory object or persistent carrier. It may remain diffusely in the generative field, alter later reachability / friction / support, or be inherited as generative capacity by a later or higher-order formation.

Working compression:

~~~text
Selection
= actualised non-neutral differentiation
+ not-fully-erasable generative consequence
+ alteration of what later generation can become / do
~~~

Open reconciliation:

~~~text
the strengthened retained-generative-consequence criterion
vs
legacy "terminal Selection remains genuine" wording
= OPEN / requires bounded owner reconciliation.
~~~

Do not resolve this tension by silently weakening either side.

## B. Object and relation are cuts, not primitive furniture of reality

The author confirms:

~~~text
object = a cognitive / research binding of relations;
relation = also a cut on an underlying generative process.
~~~

Therefore:

~~~text
object is useful
!= object is ontologically primitive;

relation is useful
!= relation is ontologically primitive.
~~~

The problem with conventional object-first research is not that objects are invalid. The problem is treating the chosen objectification as the bottom-level given.

A cut is valuable when it preserves load-bearing generative dependencies and supports better intervention / reconstruction / prediction of later generation.

~~~text
cut validity
!= correspondence to an independently self-contained thing;

cut validity
~ preservation of generatively load-bearing structure.
~~~

Multiple object / relation cuts may be simultaneously useful at different questions or scales.

## C. Diffuse Selection sediment can precede vertical structure

Selection does not have to enter a clear object, proxy or vertical structure immediately.

Current direction:

~~~text
Selection
-> diffuse sediment / generative-field modification
-> altered friction / pressure / reachability
-> altered later Selection
-> repeated / organized sediment
-> possible stable proxy
-> possible vertical structure
~~~

The critical transition is not "enough sediment by quantity." It is when distributed Selection history becomes compressible into a stable proxy that actively reorganizes future Selection.

## D. Vertical structure is an asymmetric but closed generative loop

The author rejects a traditional hierarchy reading.

Vertical structure is provisionally characterized by the simultaneous presence of:

~~~text
proxy
+ support
+ stability through friction / homeostatic reconstitution
~~~

The foreground / background distinction is generated through this relation; positions are not prior coordinates.

A vertical relation is asymmetric because foreground and background carry different generative roles, but closed because:

~~~text
background supports foreground;
foreground proxies / mediates background into later generation;
later consequences / friction reconstruct background;
reconstructed background alters later support and proxy.
~~~

The asymmetry remains live only because Selection continually reconstitutes it.

## E. Proxy is selective compression, not a transparent pipe

Proxy has both world-side and cognition-side senses.

World-side:

> a foreground can mediate / stand in for a wider background in later generation.

Cognition-side:

> a foreground can compress a wider background into a tractable presentation.

The two may overlap but must not be conflated.

A proxy is necessarily selective. It preserves some differences, suppresses others and thereby reshapes future generative reachability.

Hence:

~~~text
proxy efficiency
<-> generative fidelity
~~~

is a recurring tension.

Proxy identity is generative-role identity, not object identity.

Therefore a proxy may be distributed:

~~~text
distributed proxy
= a set of relations jointly carrying a generative role
  that no single local object can substitute for on its own.
~~~

Its boundary is a generative-role boundary, not necessarily a spatial or object boundary.

The same lower-level relations may participate simultaneously in multiple proxies / vertical structures.

## F. Prediction and expectation are different

The author distinguishes:

~~~text
prediction
= description / forecast of foreground-background differentiation;

expectation
= the vertical structure's active shaping of later generation.
~~~

Expectation is not a static endpoint and not merely a probability weighting over a fixed future menu.

The stronger reading is:

> **Expectation reshapes reachability itself: it can make some transitions easier, harder, newly available or no longer available.**

Probability / cost reweighting over a fixed state space is only a weaker special case.

Expectation belongs to the whole foreground-background loop, not to a foreground object acting alone.

## G. Attractor is field-level; stable form is Selection sediment

The author distinguishes:

~~~text
attractor
= a convergence structure of the wider generative field;

stable form
= a historically sedimented result of Selection.
~~~

Environmental change may change local expectation and convergence path without changing the attractor.

Longer Selection sediment can reconstruct the background / generative field deeply enough to shift, split, dissolve or create attractors.

Therefore Selection is not merely motion within a fixed attractor landscape. Over longer histories it may change the landscape itself.

Lower-order attractor transformation is typically constrained by higher-order generative structures, while higher-order structures remain themselves revisable through longer Selection histories.

This cross-level mediation is a central role of proxy.

## H. Friction is both maintenance cost and transformation pressure

Friction is not only an error after expectation failure.

Two coupled roles are retained:

~~~text
1. maintaining a non-neutral vertical structure already costs / excludes / coordinates;
2. mismatch with current expectation redistributes or amplifies that friction.
~~~

Persistent friction that current proxy / expectation cannot absorb can become pressure.

~~~text
friction
-> pressure
-> altered Selection
-> altered sediment
-> new proxy / support configuration
-> possible new vertical structure / attractor
~~~

Thus friction is both the cost of maintained asymmetry and a possible source of structural novelty.

Selection does not simply minimize friction.

## I. Support is generativity-restructuring, not mere resource provision

The author corrects the earlier "support = keeping the proxy alive" reading.

Support more fundamentally:

> **closes / suppresses some possibilities and opens / organizes others such that the higher-order whole gains generativity.**

Therefore:

~~~text
local possibility count decrease
can coexist with
higher-order generativity increase.
~~~

Do not equate support with possibility maximization.

Do not equate generativity with novelty count, complexity, or number of possible states.

Working meaning of generativity:

> **capacity, under changing conditions, to reorganize Selection, relations, proxies, support structures and even attractors so that effective generative difference can continue.**

## J. Higher-order means broader generative integration, not "more hierarchical"

A higher-order attractor / vertical structure is higher because it can proxy and reorganize a broader generative background.

It must not merely expand scope by flattening lower generativity.

Author-confirmed burden:

~~~text
retain effective prior generative capacities
+ reorganize their realization / positioning
+ absorb previously unresolved friction
+ add new generativity
= candidate higher-order gain.
~~~

What must be retained is **generative capacity and generative-role / dependency continuity**, not old form.

~~~text
preserve generative capacity
!= preserve old object / carrier / rule / form.
~~~

Likewise:

~~~text
functional equivalence alone
!= generative continuity.
~~~

The relevant continuity is in generative role and dependency structure, even when mechanism / substrate / objectification changes.

## K. Selection reorganizes relations, not merely winners

When overlapping vertical structures conflict, the more basic Selection question is usually not:

~~~text
which structure survives?
~~~

but:

~~~text
how are their generative relations / positions reorganized?
~~~

Possible outcomes include repartition, partial decoupling, altered proxy roles, new support structures or a higher-order coordinating proxy.

Hence:

> **Selection acts more fundamentally on generative positioning / relation-reconfiguration than on object survival.**

## L. Lower-level autonomy is a generative requirement

A higher-order proxy should not pre-decide all lower Selection.

Lower positions need enough generative autonomy to:

~~~text
produce Selection not fully encoded by the higher proxy;
generate new consequence / friction;
allow those consequences to return upward;
force higher expectation / proxy / attractor revision when necessary.
~~~

Autonomy here is not "absence of constraint." It is retained capacity to generate consequential novelty inside a vertical relation.

A higher-order structure that blocks all such feedback may become stable yet generatively closed.

## M. Wholeward direction begins weakly inside Selection

The author confirms a weak wholeward tendency is already implicit in Selection, but not as a prewritten goal or moral endpoint.

Lowest-strength reading:

~~~text
Selection consequence remains generatively recruitable / inheritable
rather than merely persisting inertly.
~~~

Therefore:

~~~text
persistence / survival
!= generative inheritance.
~~~

A structure may persist for a long time yet become a generative dead end.

Stronger wholeward organization emerges when inherited capacities are recombined into higher generativity and future higher-order attractors.

Wholeward is therefore not:

~~~text
complexity maximization;
stability maximization;
friction minimization;
possibility-count maximization;
a fixed cosmic destination.
~~~

It is a historical direction in which generative capacities can be retained, reorganized and made available for further higher-order generation.

## N. Methodological consequence for GRG

The current BCTB T1 result clarified a programme mismatch.

Relation-transfer calibration can ask whether a known relation can be recovered in another domain. That is useful, but it is not the central GRG burden identified in this author dialogue.

GRG's core research direction is now better stated as:

> **construct and test a cross-domain generative dynamics grammar for how cuts, proxies, vertical structures, expectations, Selection sediment, friction, support and attractors form and transform — while preserving source-native mechanisms and allowing distributed proxies that object-first methods may miss.**

The core target is not "same rule in many domains."

The stronger target is:

~~~text
source-native generative dynamics
-> local reconstruction
-> generative-role / dependency extraction
-> cross-realization comparison
-> candidate generative grammar
-> held-out transformation / reachability tests.
~~~

Therefore:

~~~text
BCTB relation-transfer work
= secondary calibration surface;

BCTB
!= core proof of GRG value.
~~~

Further BCTB folds should not automatically proceed as the main programme before this role is realigned.
