# X Thread (EN): The Three-Step Test

**1/**
"It was your own choice."
That sentence shows up in exit interviews, family arguments, courtrooms.
But "choice" might be one of the most carelessly used words we have.
Here's a three-step test that pulls any "choice" apart.

**2/**
Start with an odd question: when a snowflake forms its six-fold shape, did it *choose* that?
Your gut says no — snowflakes have no minds.
But slow down. It pushed other shapes aside, settled into this one, and that shape changed how later snow landed on it.
Some things, with nobody choosing, behave more like choice than choice does.

**3/**
The test. For something to count as a genuine selection, three steps must all hold:
① Exclusion — other possibilities got pushed aside;
② Formation — one definite shape got fixed;
③ Inscription — the result wrote into later conditions and changed what came next.
Miss any step and it's mere happening, not choice.

**4/**
Run it on "the wind blew dust to another spot":
Exclusion? Shift the wind and the dust drifts back — nothing was really pushed aside.
Formation? No. Inscription? Tomorrow's wind blows the same dust the same way.
Zero of three. Just happening.
So not every change is a choice. The word has a threshold.

**5/**
Now run it on "the examination system":
It excluded other ways of measuring people; fixed a format for "what counts as talent"; wrote the result into a generation's career terrain.
Three of three — a large-scale selection.
But notice: **no single person is doing the "choosing."**

**6/**
Back to "it was your own choice."
The test asks three questions:
How was the menu generated? (who arranged the options you see)
Who got excluded? (which possibilities never entered your view)
What got inscribed? (what later conditions this result changed)

**7/**
You'll find: you really are choosing — but those three seconds are only the last frame of a long chain.
The real question was never "did you choose,"
but: **how many choices, with nobody choosing, happened before you began?**
