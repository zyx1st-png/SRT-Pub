# GRG downstream derivation trace — from Selection non-neutrality to ontology / epistemology / intervention / civilization

## 0. Purpose and provenance

This file preserves the substantive assistant-visible analytical development that branched out from the 2026-09-23 FRR / Grammar Card discussion.

It is a structured reconstruction of what was explicitly presented in the dialogue, not a hidden chain-of-thought transcript.

The author subsequently stated that the entire divergent analysis should be written back and that the analysis was accepted by default. The bounded author-acceptance record is the companion adjudication file.

The material below is intentionally noncanonical and should be treated as a research reservoir for later bounded drill-down.

---

## 1. Starting point: clarify Selection without inventing a new occurrence mechanism

The dialogue returned to the OPEN anti-tautology problem around SRT Selection.

The main correction was to avoid writing:

`Selection -> Actualization`

as though Selection were an extra force causing actuality.

A cleaner research decomposition was proposed:

- actualization = actuality status;
- Selection = actuality considered as genuine non-neutral differentiation.

Working formulation:

> Selection is not an added cause of actualization; it is the ontological structure of actualization-as-differentiation.

This allowed the research programme to distinguish:

- candidate-space organization;
- occurrence;
- retention;

without turning GRG into a new collapse theory.

A further distinction was introduced between:

- causal closure problem: why this actuality occurs;
- individuation problem: what makes an actual event count as Selection rather than a merely descriptive/modelled change.

The dialogue proposed that SRT can remain primitive at the occurrence level while still owing non-circular membership / individuation conditions.

---

## 2. Three kinds of non-neutrality

The phrase “non-neutral differentiation” was decomposed because it had been carrying several burdens at once.

### 2.1 Ontological non-neutrality

The minimum contrast:

`actual != merely potential / probable / modelled`.

The claim is not that actual outcomes require indeterminism.

A deterministic event can still be actual rather than merely entailed.

This means:

`Selection != randomness`.

### 2.2 Relational non-neutrality

A difference matters only relative to a declared position / relational structure.

Two descriptions are neutral relative to a position when they are safely substitutable for the source-native burden in question.

Working notation:

`d_i ~_P d_j`.

A genuine relationally non-neutral difference requires that the actualized distinction cannot be removed by a legitimate structure-preserving redescription.

This motivated representation-invariance as a Selection audit burden.

### 2.3 Historical / generative non-neutrality

Some actualities later alter reach, constraints, support, future Selection burden or higher-order organization.

This is retention / sedimentation.

The dialogue preserved the hard distinction:

`Selection occurrence != sedimentation / retained historical efficacy`.

A terminal Selection can remain genuine even when no later process exists in which it is retained.

---

## 3. Proxy as a neutrality allocator

The proxy concept was sharpened.

Rather than treating a proxy only as a compressed representation, the dialogue proposed:

> a proxy provisionally allocates relational neutrality.

A proxy determines, for a declared position / burden, which lower-order differences can currently be treated as equivalent and which must remain distinct.

Analytical shorthand:

`D -> D / ~_P`.

This produced a stronger working statement:

> proxy organizes Selection burden, but does not replace Selection.

The proxy changes what enters current burden, not which actuality must occur.

This also supplied a bridge to reach:

- a difference can be flattened at one proxy boundary;
- remain latent;
- later regain reach when conditions change or the proxy is reopened.

---

## 4. Scaffold and cage through neutrality

The earlier scaffold / cage distinction was recast through neutrality assignment.

### 4.1 Scaffold-like organization

A healthy scaffold can:

- background repetitive / already-stabilized distinctions;
- lower repeated Selection burden;
- preserve enough reopening / provenance for consequential differences to regain reach;
- release capacity for higher-order Selection.

Working intuition:

> good higher-order structure reduces repetitive Selection without eliminating consequential re-Selection.

### 4.2 Cage-like organization

A cage emerges when a formerly useful equivalence remains operative after actuality has made the compressed distinction consequential again.

Working pattern:

`d_1 ~_P d_2` was once adequate;

later actual consequences repeatedly indicate:

`d_1 !~_P d_2`;

the higher-order structure still forces:

`d_1 ~_P d_2`.

The deeper failure is not merely “too much compression”.

It is:

> forced neutrality after the difference has become generatively consequential.

This led to the stronger formulation:

> a cage prevents already-occurring non-neutrality from revising the higher-order neutrality map.

---

## 5. Generative debt as false-neutrality maintenance burden

Generative debt was reinterpreted through the same structure.

When an old equivalence is failing but the system refuses to recut it, increasingly costly support may be required:

- subsidies;
- enforcement;
- exception handling;
- information suppression;
- manual workaround;
- extra control;
- narrative repair.

The dialogue proposed:

> generative debt is the accumulated support required to prevent recurrent lower-level non-neutrality from revising a higher-order equivalence relation.

This preserves the earlier narrower intuition:

> debt accumulates when current stability is borrowed by postponing already-needed re-Selection / recutting.

The research guard remains:

`generative debt != ordinary cost`.

---

## 6. Normativity as an expected neutrality map

Generative normativity was connected to neutrality.

A higher-order grammar can be read as an expectation concerning which differences should remain equivalent and which should become consequential under specified conditions.

Working abstraction:

`G_t -> expected ~_(t+1)`.

This reframed `ought-to-expect`:

- not a command to reality;
- not moral obligation;
- a fallible expectation generated by the current higher-order grammar.

When actuality repeatedly violates the expected equivalence structure, the grammar incurs friction and should reopen / recut.

The resulting dynamic was summarized as:

`compression -> neutrality assignment -> ought-to-expect -> actuality test -> retain or recut`.

---

## 7. Boundary stress tests for Selection

The dialogue then tested the provisional Selection concept against several edge cases.

### 7.1 Deterministic motion

A deterministic transition can still be an actual differentiation.

Therefore Selection should not require stochasticity or epistemic uncertainty.

Pressure exposed:

- if an analyst can arbitrarily cut continuous motion into infinitely many “events”, the theory needs source-defensible event individuation.

### 7.2 Symmetry breaking

A symmetric possibility / solution space is not yet Selection.

An actual symmetry-broken realization can qualify at a declared position.

Pressure exposed:

- Selection may be position-indexed;
- local Selection can coexist with ensemble-level symmetry.

### 7.3 Random noise

A real fluctuation may constitute lower-level actual differentiation even if it leaves no durable sediment.

Pressure exposed:

- Selection != meaningful change;
- Selection != adaptation;
- Selection != historical importance.

### 7.4 Coordinate change / variable relabeling

A redescription that preserves the relevant relational structure must not create a new Selection.

Pressure exposed:

- representation invariance is necessary;
- raw “state changed” is insufficient.

### 7.5 Terminal event

A real event may be ontologically eligible as Selection even when no trace persists.

Pressure exposed:

- ontological eligibility must be distinguished from empirical warrant.

### 7.6 AI internal state update

A hardware / implementation-level difference can be real while remaining functionally neutral at a higher position.

If it changes downstream routing / output, it can gain higher-level reach.

Pressure exposed:

- Selection is typed / position-indexed;
- one event can have different Selection status at different analytic / generative positions.

---

## 8. Provisional Selection audit

The stress tests yielded a research-level audit, not a canonical definition.

For a candidate event `e` at position `P`:

- occurrence / actuality must be source-supported;
- event individuation must be defensible;
- the difference must be relationally non-neutral;
- the claimed difference must survive legitimate redescription.

Working shorthand used in the dialogue:

`Sel_P(e) = O_P(e) AND I_P(e) AND N_P(e) AND V_P(e)`.

This is not a canonical SRT equation.

It is a research audit schema.

Retention remains a separate later question.

---

## 9. Reach redefined across equivalence boundaries

The neutrality analysis sharpened reach.

Working definition:

> generative reach is the capacity of a distinction to remain or regain relational non-neutrality across one or more proxy / equivalence transformations and to enter consequence-producing Selection paths.

This is stronger than “propagation distance”.

A lower-level distinction can:

- be neutralized at one higher-order layer;
- remain latently reconstructible;
- regain reach under changed conditions.

This connects reach directly to:

- reconstructibility;
- friction;
- re-Selection;
- higher-order recutting.

---

## 10. Higher-order formation: aggregation is not enough

The dialogue then asked when many lower-level Selections form a genuinely new higher-order structure.

A hierarchy of increasingly stronger conditions was proposed.

### 10.1 Aggregation

`A = f(x_1, ..., x_n)`.

A statistic or aggregate descriptor can be useful without being a new Selection position.

### 10.2 Objectification

A compression can be stabilized and repeatedly invoked as a unit.

This creates identity compression but still may be observer-side.

### 10.3 Load-bearing proxy / organization

The higher-order unit begins changing reach, routing, support or later transition conditions.

### 10.4 New Selection position candidate

A genuinely stronger formation appears when the higher-order organization forms its own load-bearing neutrality / burden architecture and reconditions later Selection.

Working emergence candidate:

> a higher-order structure emerges when retained compression of lower-order actualities becomes a new position from which distinctions are jointly neutralized or rendered consequential, and this new structure reconditions future Selection.

The dialogue explicitly rejected the need for substance dualism or “mystical extra properties”.

Verticality was treated as generative dependency plus reconditioning.

---

## 11. Generative re-entry and One-formation

A powerful criterion emerged:

> a higher-order unit should be able to re-enter later relations as a unit.

If lower-level elements `a,b,c,d` are compressed into `H`, strong higher-order evidence appears when:

`H + X -> new Selection / relation`

without the analysis having to expand the entire lower-level set each time.

This was called:

> generative re-entry.

A provisional One-formation candidate was then formulated:

> multiplicity forms an operationally closed yet reconstructibly open unit that can re-enter subsequent relations as a new Selection position.

Important guard:

- objectification can be necessary for complexity;
- objectification closure becomes pathological when the unit cannot be reopened after consequential lower-level distinctions return.

---

## 12. Operational closure and reconstructive openness

The dialogue proposed that mature higher-order units need both:

- operational closure: usable as a unit;
- reconstructive openness: reopenable when consequences invalidate the current compression.

This supplied a more precise scaffold / cage boundary.

Without operational closure:

- no higher-order complexity accumulates.

Without reconstructive openness:

- higher-order structure can become cage-like.

---

## 13. Endogenous perspective

The next question was when a Selection position is not merely analyst-defined.

The term “ownership” was deliberately softened to:

> position endogeneity / endogenous anchoring.

A perspective candidate becomes endogenous when differences are organized relative to the internally maintained relational organization of the formed unit rather than solely relative to an external cut.

Working formulation:

> perspective is a locus of neutrality assignment.

This is not yet a phenomenal point of view.

The dialogue separated:

- Selection position;
- operational One;
- endogenous One-level perspective;
- Bearer;
- subject / observer / agent / consciousness.

Hard guards:

`position != perspective automatically`;

`perspective != Bearer automatically`;

`Bearer != consciousness automatically`.

---

## 14. Ontology and epistemology as a continuous generative route

The author explicitly highlighted that perspective and endogeneity may offer a way to unify ontology and epistemology.

The dialogue developed that intuition as follows.

An endogenous perspective is first an ontological fact:

- reality itself has formed a position from which distinctions are organized.

Epistemic organization begins when that position constructs reusable proxies of relations that are:

- absent;
- hidden;
- past;
- possible;
- future.

Those proxies then participate in present Selection and remain revisable by later actuality.

Working synthesis:

> epistemology is a reflexive development within ontology rather than a viewpoint outside reality.

The dialogue rejected two shortcuts:

- this does not imply idealism;
- this does not imply naive direct realism.

Instead, an epistemic bearer encounters reality through its own formed localization / proxy / neutrality architecture, while reality can still push back through actuality.

---

## 15. Epistemic organization and error

A minimal epistemic loop was proposed:

`reality -> proxy -> expectation -> Selection/intervention -> consequence -> proxy revision`.

This led to a generative definition of error:

- mismatch between proxy-generated expectation and actualized consequence;
- crucially, the mismatch must be able to gain enough reach to revise the proxy.

Working health criterion:

> a healthy epistemic structure is not one that never errs; it is one in which error can obtain sufficient generative reach to modify the structure that produced it.

This connected scientific method to GRG:

- blinding;
- measurement protocols;
- replication;
- criticism;
- preregistration;
- instrumentation;

can be read as external epistemic scaffolds that protect consequential re-Selection against identity / expectation / incentive closure.

---

## 16. Bearer as historical self-conditioning

The dialogue next thickened the formed One.

A Bearer candidate was proposed as:

> a formed One whose retained actualizations recursively participate in constituting its own later Selection position.

The key is not memory as archive.

The stronger burden is:

`L2(H_t) -> conditions L0(H_(t+1))`.

Past actuality participates in determining what later becomes relevant / non-neutral for the same generative lineage.

This is stronger than persistence alone.

---

## 17. Anticipation as future-proxy reach

Anticipation was split into at least two layers.

### 17.1 Structural / thin anticipation

A current organization can already be sensitive to future-compatible states without a rich explicit model.

### 17.2 Proxy-mediated anticipation

The system forms current proxies of possible future consequences:

`F_hat_1, F_hat_2, ...`.

These present structures can change current Selection burden.

Hard guard:

`future itself does not cause the present`.

Rather:

`present proxy of possible future -> present Selection`.

This allowed the dialogue to describe a temporally extended Selection position:

- past is present as retention;
- possible future is present as anticipation proxy;
- Selection occurs now.

---

## 18. Self as temporal generative lineage

The dialogue then distinguished:

- persistence;
- Bearer continuity;
- self continuity.

A self candidate was developed as an endogenous temporal equivalence relation across Selection positions.

Working idea:

`P_t ~_self P_(t+1)`

does not mean state identity.

It means that temporal differences are treated as variations within one operative generative lineage, and that lineage relation itself changes current Selection.

A stronger formulation:

> a self emerges when a Bearer treats its past, present and anticipated future Selection positions as belonging to one generative lineage and this relation itself participates in current Selection.

Hard guard:

`self != self-model`.

A self-model is a higher-order epistemic proxy about that lineage.

---

## 19. Memory, anticipation and modelling under one grammar

The dialogue noticed that several cognitive functions share a common GRG role:

- memory: past absent difference -> current reach;
- anticipation: possible future difference -> current reach;
- model / representation: hidden or nonlocal relation -> current reach.

This led to a broader research formulation:

> epistemic organization enables currently non-present differences to retain or acquire present generative reach.

This was one of the strongest ontology / epistemology synthesis moves in the dialogue.

---

## 20. Stake

Stake was introduced only after a temporally extended formed position exists.

A candidate stake exists when an actual or anticipated difference bears on:

- the future integrity of the formed position;
- its continuity;
- its future Selection capacity;
- its ability to reconstruct / respond.

Working formulation:

> stake is the relation by which possible future differences become non-neutral with respect to the future viability / generative capacity of a formed position.

This produces the stronger structure:

`for-this-position`.

The dialogue emphasized:

> “for whom” is downstream, not primitive in Selection.

---

## 21. Valuation, salience and attention

Binary neutral / non-neutral organization was judged insufficient for complex endogenous systems.

### 21.1 Valuation

Candidate role:

> directional organization of actual or anticipated consequences relative to an endogenous position.

Valuation asks not merely whether a difference matters but in which direction it biases later Selection.

### 21.2 Salience

Candidate role:

> tendency of a difference to acquire priority / current reach within the formed burden landscape.

### 21.3 Attention

Candidate role:

> dynamic allocation of finite Selection capacity among multiple simultaneously non-neutral differences.

The dialogue explicitly separated:

`salience != attention`.

A highly salient difference may lose current attention to a more urgent burden, and trained / institutional structure can allocate attention to low-salience differences.

---

## 22. Affect-like organization

The dialogue cautiously introduced an affect-like generative role without equating it with feeling.

Candidate:

> distributed stakes / valuations are compressed into a lower-dimensional global modulation that reweights many Selection processes simultaneously.

This can be described as a broad Selection-burden field.

Hard guard:

`affective organization != phenomenal affect`.

Reward signals, threat-like routing or global priority modulation do not establish feeling.

---

## 23. Subject-level organization and consciousness OPEN

A possible subject-level organization was discussed as a further thickening involving:

- endogenous perspective;
- Bearer / self-lineage;
- stake;
- valuation;
- attention;
- integrated global burden modulation.

However the dialogue deliberately retained a hard OPEN boundary:

> generative perspective is not yet phenomenal perspective.

The programme may explain much of:

- memory;
- self;
- anticipation;
- attention;
- valuation;
- agency-like behaviour;

without thereby explaining why there is something it is like to undergo the process.

This was explicitly preserved as an OPEN frontier rather than filled by inference.

## 24. Constitutive stack as dependency graph, not rigid ladder

A provisional stack was assembled:

`Selection/Actualization`

`-> Retention/L2`

`-> Operational One`

`-> Endogenous Perspective`

`-> Epistemic Organization`

`-> Bearer`

`-> Self`

`-> Anticipation`

The dialogue then corrected its own presentation:

> this should be treated as a constitutive dependency graph / thickening map, not a strict chronological ladder.

Attention-like routing, for example, may arise without a rich self-model.

## 25. Intervention as epistemology writing back into ontology

The next major move was intervention.

Generic causation was distinguished from intervention.

Candidate intervention structure:

`epistemic proxy -> anticipated consequences -> valuation/stake -> intervention Selection -> modified L0 / generative conditions -> later actuality -> revision`.

An agent does not simply choose a future actuality.

It modifies:

- reach;
- constraints;
- support;
- routing;
- probability / admissibility;
- possibility architecture.

Later actuality then tests whether the intervention grammar was adequate.

This closed a larger loop:

`ontology -> epistemology -> action/intervention -> ontology`.

---

## 26. Science and engineering under one generative loop

The dialogue used intervention to connect science and engineering.

Working roles:

- science: reconstruct generative grammar from actuality;
- engineering: use generative grammar to reshape later Selection conditions.

The mature loop:

`observation -> grammar -> intervention -> actuality -> grammar revision`.

This connected the author's long-term intuition about accumulating experience, laws and architecture to deliberate world-building without treating theory as detached representation.

---

## 27. Intervention depth

Interventions were provisionally typed by what they alter:

- state intervention;
- reach intervention;
- proxy intervention;
- grammar intervention;
- position-forming intervention.

The deeper interventions do not merely move a system within a fixed option space.

They change:

- what distinctions matter;
- what can become reachable;
- what can become an operative unit;
- what future Selection positions can form.

This led to the working definition:

> creation can be the construction of a new possibility architecture, not merely the actualization of a pre-given possibility.

---

## 28. Technology as sedimented intervention capacity

A one-off successful intervention can be retained, standardized and objectified.

Working candidate:

> technology = sedimented intervention capacity.

Technology allows later positions to invoke a previously learned intervention grammar without rediscovering the entire lower-level burden.

This makes technology a civilization-scale L2 scaffold.

The dialogue also identified a danger:

> strong intervention capacity can keep a bad grammar alive by controlling reality around the grammar rather than recutting the grammar in response to reality.

This produced a distinction between:

- control capacity;
- generative capacity.

High control can coexist with low reconstructive openness.

---

## 29. Generative intelligence

The dialogue proposed that a mature generative intelligence differs from a fixed-goal optimizer.

A strong control system may optimize under:

- fixed objective;
- fixed proxy;
- fixed success criterion.

A more generative system can also revise:

- proxy;
- intervention grammar;
- possibly the relevant higher-order question / frame.

The higher form of agency was described as:

> not merely choosing among given options, but restructuring what can become an option.

This remains a research direction, not an AGI architecture claim.

---

## 30. Intervention across Bearer boundaries -> power

When an intervention changes shared generative conditions, it also changes other positions' future Selection landscapes.

Power was therefore proposed as:

> the relational capacity of one Selection position to alter another position's reach, neutrality map, support conditions or available re-Selection pathways.

Money, force, law, authority and platform control were treated as possible power carriers / implementations rather than definitions of power itself.

---

## 31. Influence, coordination and coercion

The dialogue distinguished several cross-position relations.

### 31.1 Influence

A can make a difference available to B while leaving B's own evaluation / rejection / reconstruction substantially intact.

### 31.2 Coordination

A and B can create shared proxies that reduce repeated alignment burden without requiring complete internal sameness.

### 31.3 Coercion candidate

A stronger candidate boundary appears when A's intervention materially reduces B's capacity for consequential re-Selection, exit or reconstruction.

Working research formulation:

> coercion is power exercised through foreclosure of another position's consequential re-Selection.

This is not a legal definition.

---

## 32. Consent and autonomy

Consent was thickened beyond a yes-token.

A candidate consent structure includes:

- the receiving position can form a proxy of the proposed intervention;
- can relate it to its own stakes;
- retains meaningful refusal / exit / reopening routes;
- incorporates the proposed intervention into its own Selection process.

Working research formulation:

> consent is endogenous incorporation of another position's proposed intervention under preserved consequential re-Selection.

Autonomy was correspondingly reframed:

> autonomy is not isolation from influence; it is the capacity to preserve endogenous re-Selection while participating in externally coupled generative structures.

---

## 33. Multi-agent alignment as bounded compatibility

The dialogue rejected the need for internal sameness across agents.

Large-scale coordination instead requires shared proxies that create bounded compatibility.

Agents can preserve:

- different histories;
- different valuations;
- different goals;

while accepting a shared operational settlement relation for a bounded domain.

This made alignment closer to:

> bounded generative compatibility

than identity of internal state or value.

---

## 34. Externality

A GRG-style structural definition was proposed.

An externality occurs when:

> a consequence crosses a Selection-position boundary but does not gain corresponding reach in the proxy that governed the original intervention.

This captures the familiar intuition behind “not internalized” while grounding it in proxy / reach structure.

The general response is not merely to add a number.

It is to make previously excluded consequential differences obtain appropriate reach in the governing decision structure.

---

## 35. Money

Money was preserved as a strong candidate example of a multi-agent settlement proxy.

Working formulation:

> money is not value itself; it is a transferable proxy for settling bounded cross-agent Selection burdens.

Its generative power comes from reducing the need for deep alignment.

Two agents do not need identical values or histories to coordinate a bounded exchange.

The danger appears when the proxy expands its jurisdiction:

`price ~= value`

or market settlement is treated as exhaustive of all relevant stakes.

The dialogue introduced:

> proxy jurisdiction expansion

as a useful failure mode.

---

## 36. Institutions, law and rights

### 36.1 Institution

Candidate:

> institution = retained multi-agent intervention / coordination grammar.

Institutions sediment:

- roles;
- permissions;
- defaults;
- consequence routing;
- dispute handling;
- exit / re-entry conditions.

They reduce repeated coordination burden.

### 36.2 Law

Candidate:

> law often functions as a meta-intervention grammar.

It structures:

- who may intervene on whom;
- which intervention paths are recognized;
- which consequences trigger recourse;
- how disputes reopen;
- who can change the rules.

### 36.3 Rights

A bounded structural candidate was proposed:

> rights can function as anti-closure scaffolds that protect specified re-Selection capacities against certain external interventions.

This does not derive a rights catalogue or moral theory from GRG alone.

---

## 37. Conflict and institutional reconstructibility

The dialogue rejected:

`less conflict = necessarily healthier system`.

Conflict can sometimes indicate:

- a consequential difference has regained reach;
- an obsolete equivalence is being challenged.

A mature multi-agent scaffold therefore may not eliminate conflict.

It may instead provide low-destruction channels for:

`conflict -> information about failed equivalence -> reopening / recutting`.

Dispute-resolution institutions were thus interpreted as possible reconstructibility infrastructure.

---

## 38. Power cage

A dangerous multi-agent pattern appears when:

`power increases`

while:

`feedback reach decreases`;

`exit decreases`;

`reconstructibility decreases`.

The controlling position can rewrite others' Selection landscapes while consequences from those positions cannot revise the controlling grammar.

Working candidate:

> power becomes cage-like when asymmetry hardens into one-way grammar authority.

Again this is an analytical research formulation, not a political label to apply without evidence.

---

## 39. Civilization as sedimented coordination architecture

The dialogue generalized the multi-agent structure.

Civilization can be viewed as repeated multi-agent Selection burden sedimented into shared scaffolds:

- language;
- money;
- law;
- standards;
- organizations;
- science;
- protocols;
- technologies.

A candidate direction:

`coordination burden per interaction decreases`

while

`reachable coordination scale increases`.

This was called a form of generative elevation.

The systemic risk is not only disorder.

A civilization can accumulate obsolete but historically successful proxies whose jurisdiction continues expanding after their generative validity has changed.

This connected civilization-scale failure to generative debt and reconstructibility.

---

## 40. Multi-agent generative health

A provisional synthesis was proposed:

> a multi-agent system is generatively healthy when shared proxies compress repeated coordination burden while consequential differences from participating positions can still regain reach, trigger renegotiation and revise the shared grammar.

This deliberately avoids equating health with:

- total agreement;
- conflict elimination;
- maximum control.

It remains a research criterion, not a complete normative theory.

---

## 42. Why this analysis should not be inserted wholesale into the Grammar Card

After returning to the original topic, the dialogue recognized that this development had become too expansive for the immediate Card task.

The author agreed that the main line should return to:

`FRR -> improve Card -> run more fusion cases -> revise Card`.

Therefore the present downstream stack is preserved separately.

Reasons:

- later domains should not be forced to instantiate self, perspective, power or civilization concepts;
- these concepts need source-native fusion cases of their own;
- the Card should remain minimal enough that heterogeneous sources can reshape it;
- speculative downstream implications should not become template requirements.
