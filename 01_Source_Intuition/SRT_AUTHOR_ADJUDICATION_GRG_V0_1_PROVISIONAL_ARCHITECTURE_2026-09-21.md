## 2. Formation

For v0.1:

~~~text
formation
= mandatory cross-cutting burden
!= independent primitive GRG relation family yet
~~~

Every vertical relation must state how the relevant organization / relation forms.

An independent R-FORM family remains OPEN and may be promoted later if a non-trivial recurring formation grammar is discovered.

## 3. Operative positionality

For v0.1:

~~~text

-> where additional burden is paid,

~~~

R-POS remains important but is provisionally downstream of local generative-locus formation rather than a peer primitive beside R-LOC.

Guard:

~~~text
R-LOC != R-POS automatically
R-POS != SRT Selection-position automatically
~~~

## 4. History / recurrence

For v0.1 keep two named burdens:

~~~text
R-HIST-a
retained efficacy / later conditioning

R-HIST-b
recurrent reconstitution / recurring conditioning role
~~~

This preserves the distinction between:

- prior organization remaining effective;
- the relevant conditioning organization being regenerated / recurrently reconstituted.

## 5. Reconstruction

For v0.1 split:

~~~text
R-RECON-a
typed generative-field / accessibility / condition reconstruction

R-RECON-b
realized downstream generative consequence of that reconstruction
~~~

R-RECON-a requires an actually changed typed generative condition, not merely a hypothetical future.

R-RECON-b requires evidence that the changed condition altered later generation / transition / accessibility / viability / differentiation as claimed.

## 6. Grammar status

For v0.1:

~~~text
GRG
= revisable typed proto-grammar architecture
!= mature closed formal grammar
!= completed universal grammar
~~~

The programme may use "GRG" as its research name while preserving the proto-grammar qualification at the current architecture level.

A mature formal grammar would require stronger composition syntax, compatibility constraints, derivation rules and revision semantics than are currently established.
