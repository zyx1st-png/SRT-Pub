# Author adjudication — GRG mainline is framework construction, not single-pilot progression

## 0. Trigger

After the first GRG-R1 calibration cycle, the author corrected the programme emphasis.

The GRG programme should now primarily attempt to establish a research framework capable of organizing and learning from:

- natural sciences;
- life sciences;
- neuroscience / cognition;
- artificial intelligence;
- economics;
- social science;
- institutions and law;
- ethics;
- culture / philosophy / religion;
- historical and civilizational experience.

The programme must not make one bounded experiment or one data-access route the blocker for the whole GRG mainline.

## 1. Main adjudication

~~~text
GRG programme mainline
= FRAMEWORK CONSTRUCTION + CROSS-DOMAIN / CIVILIZATIONAL GRAMMAR LEARNING

bounded experiments / source-specific causal tests
= CALIBRATION WORKSTREAMS

calibration workstreams
!= programme-wide blocking gate
~~~

The RNN and HKB lines remain important because they teach GRG what it may not infer.

They do not define the whole programme.

## 2. Why calibration remains necessary

The calibration track exists to prevent GRG from becoming retrospective relabeling.

It should test:

- semantic invariance;
- source fidelity;
- causal burdens;
- failure conditions;
- objectification sensitivity;
- transfer value.

Negative results are productive if they narrow the grammar.

But:

~~~text
one failed candidate
!= whole GRG failure

one blocked dataset
!= whole GRG programme blocked
~~~

unless the failed relation was explicitly declared indispensable to the whole framework.

## 3. Framework target

The desired GRG architecture should support:

~~~text
source-native experience / theory / law / historical record
-> typed evidence
-> objectification declaration
-> candidate generative relation
-> cross-domain burden comparison
-> generative expectation
-> intervention / institutional response
-> consequence
-> revision of grammar
~~~

The architecture must preserve differences in mechanism, scale, evidence and participant position.

It must be able to reject false unifications.

## 4. Civilizational target

The programme treats civilization as a distributed, historically accumulated generative-learning process.

Relevant inputs include:

- empirical science;
- formal knowledge;
- engineering practice;
- institutional success and failure;
- economic history;
- social conflict and cooperation;
- cultural and philosophical traditions;
- individual and collective lived experience.

These inputs are not epistemically equal.

GRG must type their evidential role rather than flatten them into one dataset.

## 5. Normativity direction

The author's existing direction is retained:

~~~text
generative relation
-> structural generative expectation
-> indexed comparison of actual trajectory
-> downstream normativity
~~~

Civilizational use therefore asks not merely:

~~~text
what happened?
~~~

but also:

~~~text
what generative relations were strengthened, weakened,
externalized, hollowed out or destroyed,
for which positions / relations / scales?
~~~

Full moral legitimacy is not solved by this relation alone.

## 6. Architectural consequence

The new programme should distinguish:

~~~text
MAINLINE A — GRG Framework Construction
MAINLINE B — Domain / Civilizational Atlas and Relation Library
MAINLINE C — Generative Expectation / Normativity / Institution interface

CALIBRATION — bounded experiments, historical cases, source-fidelity tests,
              transfer pilots and adverse-result preservation
~~~

The exact number of tracks is operational, not ontological.

## 7. Canonical guard

This adjudication is a programme-direction source.

It does not rewrite:

- primitive Selection;
- L0;
- One / Bearer;
- canonical normativity owners;
- Level / scientific-distinctiveness status.

Canonical promotion remains separately gated.
